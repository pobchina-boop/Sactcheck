When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact the team promptly for new visual symptoms, chest pain, breathlessness, palpitations, faintness, severe muscle pain or weakness, dark urine, bleeding, severe rash, persistent diarrhoea or severe abdominal pain. Do not continue one medicine at an unchanged dose if the other has been held without a specific team instruction. Report markedly reduced urine, persistent vomiting, new muscle cramps or weakness, or palpitations promptly; the team may need to check salts, kidney function and tumour lysis risk.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Medicines in this regimen

Encorafenib and binimetinib block two points in the BRAF-MEK growth pathway to help control eligible advanced melanoma. The team reviews response, symptoms and side effects to decide whether the treatment continues.

Oral BRAF inhibitor

Encorafenib

Braftovi
Blocks altered BRAF signalling

Encorafenib blocks altered BRAF signalling in eligible melanoma. Binimetinib acts at a different step in the same growth pathway. Each medicine retains its own dosing and safety instructions.

BRAF V600 confirmed↓BRAF signal blocked↓Partner MEK medicine

What to watch for
A change to binimetinib can change the encorafenib plan. Have interactions checked and avoid grapefruit.

Oral MEK inhibitor

Binimetinib

Mektovi
Blocks downstream MEK signalling

Binimetinib inhibits MEK proteins in the growth-signalling pathway. Alongside effects on cancer cells, it can affect muscle, heart and retinal tissue. The prescribed blood and heart checks and prompt review of new eye symptoms help detect these effects.

MEK inhibited↓Cancer-growth signal reduced↓Muscle / heart / eye checks

What to watch for
Report vision change or muscle pain/weakness promptly; keep creatine kinase (CK), kidney and heart checks even when you feel well.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Taking or receiving treatment

The usual method is to swallow the prescribed capsules/tablets whole with water, with or without food. If swallowing is difficult, ask the pharmacist for the source-permitted preparation: Braftovi capsules may be opened into about 20 mL of apple puree for immediate use, and Mektovi 15 mg tablets may be dispersed in about 10 mL of water, orange juice or apple juice, taken immediately, followed by a further 10 mL rinse. Do not assume this applies to other tablet strengths or improvise a preparation. Keep doses at consistent times. Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit juice, and have all medicines and supplements checked.

STEP 2

Missed, vomited or held treatment

For a missed encorafenib dose, take it only if more than 12 hours remain before the next dose. For binimetinib, do not take a missed dose if fewer than six hours remain before the next; ask the pharmacist if timing is uncertain. Do not double doses. If either medicine is vomited, do not take an extra dose; take the next scheduled dose unless the team advises otherwise.

STEP 3

Appointments and supply

Plan blood tests, travel and medicine collection in advance. If you need an interpreter or someone to help at home, tell the team before the appointment.

STEP 4

Paper copies and contact information

Ask for your latest schedule and contact details on paper. Replace old versions when treatment changes so everyone helping you uses the same plan.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

An individual symptom plan

Ask which anti-sickness or other symptom medicines are prescribed, when to take them and when to call if they do not help. Do not start a borrowed medicine or alter treatment to manage a side effect yourself.

Your urgent contacts

Keep your actual oncology, out-of-hours and pharmacy contacts on the wallet card. Example numbers on a template must be replaced before you use it.

Pregnancy and fertility precautions

Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least one month after the last dose. Hormonal contraception needs an additional or alternative barrier method because interactions can reduce effectiveness. Discuss fertility beforehand and follow the team’s breastfeeding instructions. Report a possible pregnancy promptly.

Eating, activity and practical help

Let the team know if cramps, altered taste or poor appetite make eating or moving difficult. Ask for practical food and activity advice that fits your symptoms and any other illness.

Hydration and tumour lysis assessment

The team checks whether tumour burden, kidney problems or dehydration call for additional precautions and blood tests. Keep the individual fluid plan; do not force fluids if you have a restriction. Report reduced urine, persistent vomiting or marked weakness promptly.

Treatment reference · 01

Understanding your diagnosis and treatment goal

Use these questions with your own pathology report and agreed treatment plan.

What the diagnosis says

Record the original melanoma site and pathology findings. Breslow thickness and ulceration describe features of the skin tumour; stage describes its extent. Melanoma arising in the eye or a mucosal surface may need a different pathway.

BRAF and other tumour tests

A BRAF V600 result identifies whether the targeted medicines in this plan can act on the tumour. Keep the laboratory result and date. A tumour change is not, on its own, evidence of an inherited condition.

Surgery, radiotherapy and medicine have different roles

Ask whether medicine is intended to reduce recurrence after complete surgery or control cancer that remains. Add any operation or radiotherapy to the plan with its own purpose, timing and risks.

Stage and response are different

Stage describes the extent of disease. Response describes a change in measurable cancer during treatment. After complete removal there may be no visible tumour to measure; follow-up then looks for recurrence and treatment effects.

A new skin change still needs review

Report a new or changing mole, a sore that persists, a new lump or an unexplained symptom. A photo or a good response in one area cannot rule out a separate problem.

Practical preparation

Bring your medicine list, allergy information and questions. Tell the team about practical problems with travel, swallowing, wound care or remembering doses before treatment starts.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests: what is required and what each result means

Safety tests, tumour tests and response scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Confirm BRAF V600 and treatment setting. Full blood count, kidney/liver tests, creatine kinase (CK) and creatinine, blood pressure, ECG, a heart-pumping assessment by echo or MUGA, and skin examination.

During treatment

Blood count, kidney/liver tests monthly. CK and creatinine monthly for the first six months, then as indicated. ECG at four weeks, then every 12 weeks and after dose changes; heart-pumping assessment every three months. Skin assessment every 60 days, head/neck examination every three months and chest CT every six months. Response scans follow the individual plan.

Scans and tumour markers

Ask the team to write the next assessment and who will arrange it. Bloods used to monitor kidneys, liver, hormones or muscle are not automatically measures of cancer response. There is no universal tumour-marker blood schedule for these skin-cancer regimens; a scan or extra blood test is requested for a specific clinical reason.

After a pause or finish

Continue skin surveillance for six months after stopping as specified by the regimen. Agree continuing symptom/organ checks, the next melanoma assessment and medicine-specific pregnancy precautions.

Getting the result

Keep a named contact for bookings and results. If an expected appointment or result is missing, call that service; do not take silence as confirmation that everything is complete.

What to expect between visits

You can feel well and still need the planned checks. New symptoms deserve attention even on a day without an appointment.

Your usual pattern

Record the start and change in symptoms, temperature if advised, bowel pattern, drinking, urine and the effect on normal tasks. A simple few-line record is enough. Use it to describe a change rather than trying to grade or diagnose a toxicity yourself.

When to call

Contact the team promptly for new visual symptoms, chest pain, breathlessness, palpitations, faintness, severe muscle pain or weakness, dark urine, bleeding, severe rash, persistent diarrhoea or severe abdominal pain. Do not continue one medicine at an unchanged dose if the other has been held without a specific team instruction. Eating, drinking and activity

Report nausea, mouth discomfort, diarrhoea or weight loss before they make eating and drinking difficult. The team can adjust symptom support and arrange dietitian or rehabilitation advice.

Treatment reference · 06

Skin checks, sun protection and optional photographs

This complements clinical assessment and does not replace it.

Protect your skin

Protect exposed skin with clothing, a hat and shade. Use broad-spectrum sunscreen with sun protection factor (SPF) 30 or higher and reapply as directed. Avoid sunbeds. Cloudy weather does not remove ultraviolet exposure.

Know the pattern of your skin

Ask the team to show you how to check your skin and treated area. Use a mirror or a helper for areas you cannot see. Report a new lump, a changing lesion or a sore that does not heal.

Photographs are optional

A private, dated photograph can help describe a change if your team finds it useful. Keep the lighting and size reference consistent. Photographs support a discussion; they do not replace examination and need not be uploaded.

Wounds, nails and sensitive skin

Follow the wound and skin-care instructions for the treated area. Ask before adding a cream or remedy. Increasing redness, discharge, pain, bleeding or difficulty using the area needs assessment.

Treatment reference · 07

Pregnancy, fertility and protecting others

Discuss these precautions before treatment even if pregnancy is not currently planned.

Medicine-specific precautions

Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least one month after the last dose. Hormonal contraception needs an additional or alternative barrier method because interactions can reduce effectiveness. Discuss fertility beforehand and follow the team’s breastfeeding instructions. Report a possible pregnancy promptly.

Planning ahead

Ask early about fertility preservation, sexual health and symptoms affecting intimacy. Tell the team about any chance of pregnancy, breastfeeding or a partner’s pregnancy so that the correct medicine-specific advice can be given. Keep the precautions and their end dates in the written plan.

Safe handling and supply

Do not share anticancer medicines. Keep oral medicines in their original labelled packaging, away from children and anyone who could take them accidentally. Return unused medicines to the supplying pharmacy and follow its handling advice. A treatment hold does not remove pregnancy precautions.

Treatment reference · 08

Questions, decisions and practical support

Your priorities and the burden of treatment belong in the discussion.

Benefits and uncertainties

Ask what outcome this treatment aims to improve for your diagnosis and what alternatives exist. Results from a study group help a discussion but cannot promise your own result.

Alternatives and review points

Agree the next decision point and what would make the plan change. Ask about other suitable treatments, trials and symptom care. Support for comfort and function can continue alongside cancer treatment.

Help with daily life

Tell the team what is hardest in daily life: travel, work, caring duties, costs, sleep or confidence. Ask who can help and how to contact them.

Shared understanding

Before going home, check that you can describe your medicine schedule, next tests and urgent-contact route. Ask for an interpreter, larger print or a supporter if that would help.
