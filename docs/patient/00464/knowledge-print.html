When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Follow your unit’s fever threshold and urgent-contact instructions; call immediately if you develop fever, shaking chills or feel suddenly unwell, even without a high temperature. Also report unusual bleeding, persistent vomiting, inability to drink, reduced urine, jaundice or severe abdominal pain. Fever with abdominal pain and jaundice needs urgent assessment for rare severe liver injury. Tell the nurse immediately about infusion-site burning or swelling.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

To control metastatic melanoma with chemotherapy, with benefit, blood counts and tolerability reviewed before each treatment.

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

Dacarbazine

DTIC
Damages cancer-cell DNA

Dacarbazine is converted in the body into substances that damage DNA and make it harder for cancer cells to divide. It also affects some healthy cells, including blood-forming cells. This explains why nausea prevention and blood-count checks are central to the plan.

Tiredness, anaemia, poor sleep and nausea can affect concentration. Report severe dizziness, confusion or a major change in function; do not drive if unwell.

3Breathlessness / anaemia
Dacarbazine

Low red cells can cause tiredness or breathlessness. New or worsening breathlessness needs assessment; severe breathlessness or chest pain is an emergency.

5Liver
Dacarbazine

Rare severe liver injury can cause abdominal pain, fever and jaundice. Seek urgent assessment; routine liver tests do not replace symptom reporting.

7Stomach / appetite
Dacarbazine

Nausea and vomiting need preventive medicines and a take-home plan. Report persistent symptoms, severe abdominal pain or failure of rescue treatment.

9Bruising / bleeding
Dacarbazine

Low platelets can cause bruising, nosebleeds or bleeding gums. Persistent bleeding, black stools or blood in urine require prompt assessment.

2Blood / infection
Dacarbazine

Low white cells increase infection risk and may occur late in the cycle. Fever or suddenly feeling unwell needs urgent contact, even near the next planned dose.

4Skin / hair
Dacarbazine

Hair loss or sun sensitivity can occur. New severe rash, swelling or breathing difficulty may indicate allergy and needs urgent help.

Dacarbazine

Vomiting and reduced intake can lead to dehydration. Report reduced urine, persistent vomiting or inability to drink; kidney tests also help guide dosing.

Complete timing and symptom notes

Day 1: Treatment day

The NCCP dose is dacarbazine 850 mg/m² on day 1 in one litre of saline over one hour, with the infusion protected from light. The individual dose is prescribed using body size and clinical review. Allow extra time for blood results, anti-sickness medicine, preparation and any other fluids. Tell the nurse immediately about pain, burning, swelling or redness around the infusion. Report symptoms and previous reactions before treatment.

Day 2–21: Between treatment visits

Follow your unit’s fever threshold and urgent-contact instructions; call immediately if you develop fever, shaking chills or feel suddenly unwell, even without a high temperature. Also report unusual bleeding, persistent vomiting, inability to drink, reduced urine, jaundice or severe abdominal pain. Fever with abdominal pain and jaundice needs urgent assessment for rare severe liver injury. Tell the nurse immediately about infusion-site burning or swelling. Do not wait for the next appointment to report urgent symptoms.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Taking or receiving treatment

The NCCP dose is dacarbazine 850 mg/m² on day 1 in one litre of saline over one hour, with the infusion protected from light. The individual dose is prescribed using body size and clinical review. Allow extra time for blood results, anti-sickness medicine, preparation and any other fluids. Tell the nurse immediately about pain, burning, swelling or redness around the infusion.

STEP 2

Missed, vomited or held treatment

If treatment is delayed for low blood counts, illness or another problem, the team sets the new date and dose. Do not mark a planned but cancelled dose as received. The six-cycle limit is conditional on benefit and tolerability, not a requirement to give unsafe treatment.

STEP 3

Appointments and supply

Arrange blood tests, transport, medicine supply and interpreter or carer support. Check who reviews results and how you receive the decision before the next dose.

STEP 4

Paper copies and contacts

Keep the full handbook, three-page summary and wallet card together. The QR code is optional. Ask the team to replace every labelled example contact with your real oncology and out-of-hours details; ask for a new printed plan when treatment changes.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines and symptom plan

Dacarbazine has high nausea-and-vomiting risk, so a planned preventive anti-sickness regimen is needed under the local protocol, including clear take-home instructions. The NCCP line that routine premedication is usually unnecessary does not remove this antiemetic requirement. Ask what to take regularly, what is rescue treatment and when to call if it fails.

Skin and daily care

Hair loss and sun sensitivity can occur, though they are not inevitable. Use gentle skin care and sun protection. Report redness, pain or swelling at a previous infusion site, or a spreading rash or signs of an allergic reaction.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

The current HPRA product information advises effective contraception during treatment and for six months after the final dose for women, and during treatment and for three months afterwards for men. Discuss fertility preservation and, when relevant, genetic counselling before treatment. Do not breastfeed during treatment; ask the team about the appropriate interval afterwards.

Understanding your diagnosis and treatment goal

Use your own pathology, stage and agreed plan alongside this guide.

Your diagnosis and stage

Keep the pathology report, original melanoma site, stage and known sites of spread. This guide concerns advanced disease that cannot be removed completely by surgery or has spread; it does not describe a postoperative recurrence-prevention course.

Sources: nccn-patient

The tumour test

A BRAF V600 test helps identify whether BRAF-targeted medicines are an option. A tumour mutation does not by itself mean an inherited family condition. Keep the exact result and ask how it changes your choices.

Sources: nccn-patient

Scans and local treatments

Record the scans used to follow known disease. Surgery or radiotherapy may have a separate role for a particular site; ask for their timing and risks to be added to the overall plan.

Sources: nccn-patient

New changes still matter

Report a new lump, changing mole, persistent skin sore or unexplained symptom. A response at one site does not establish that every other symptom is harmless.

How treatment works

Dacarbazine is converted in the body into substances that damage DNA and make it harder for cancer cells to divide. It also affects some healthy cells, including blood-forming cells. This explains why nausea prevention and blood-count checks are central to the plan.

Sources: nccp · dacarbazine-pi

Important boundaries

This is single-agent dacarbazine at the NCCP 850 mg/m² dose. It is not an immune-checkpoint or BRAF-targeted regimen. Historical studies often used 1000 mg/m² and different treatment settings; their response or survival figures are not a precise forecast for this 850 mg/m² course.

Sources: nccp · dacarbazine-pi

Evidence and choices

The dacarbazine comparator response rate was 13.9%; this is not a validated efficacy estimate for NCCP dacarbazine 850 mg/m². These are group results from that study, not a prediction for you. Dacarbazine dose differs from NCCP 00464, and the population was BRAF wild type. The study primarily established benefit of nivolumab over a historical comparator. No exact survival or response prediction is assigned to the NCCP 850 mg/m² course.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests, results and treatment decisions

Safety tests and scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Full blood count, kidney and liver function, medicines and prior treatment history. Discuss pregnancy prevention and fertility before the first dose. Record symptoms and the disease sites being followed on scans.

Sources: nccp · dacarbazine-pi

During treatment

Full blood count, kidney and liver function before every cycle. Blood-count suppression can be delayed: the product information describes the lowest counts often three to four weeks after a dose, so a 21-day appointment does not guarantee recovery. Extra blood tests are arranged when clinically needed.

Sources: nccp · dacarbazine-pi

What a result means

Ask which result is abnormal, whether the finding needs a repeat test and whether treatment is held, changed or stopped. Do not use a reference range or one normal result to decide independently that a dose is safe.

Sources: nccp · dacarbazine-pi

Response and follow-up

Keep reporting fever, bleeding, worsening fatigue or other symptoms after the last infusion while counts and organs recover. Response scans, later blood tests and the next treatment decision are individual; the NCCP source gives no universal imaging or tumour-marker schedule.

Sources: nccp · dacarbazine-pi
