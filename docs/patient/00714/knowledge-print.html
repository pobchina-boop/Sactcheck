When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Follow your oncology team’s emergency instructions for fever, shivering or sudden illness, including with normal temperature. Report new breathlessness/cough, diarrhoea or abdominal pain, reduced urine, severe rash, unusual weakness, headache or marked thirst promptly. Severe chest pain, collapse, confusion or major breathing difficulty needs emergency care.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Three medicines. Different jobs.

This treatment aims to control metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer, help symptoms and extend life. It is usually given without an expectation of cure. The team checks the cancer subtype, molecular results, your health and your preferences before recommending this particular chemotherapy backbone.

Immunotherapy

Nivolumab

Opdivo
Blocks the PD-1 immune checkpoint

Nivolumab binds PD-1 on T cells. It prevents signals that can reduce the immune attack on cancer. This can also let immune cells inflame healthy tissues. It is infused every 21 days in this plan.

T cell↓PD-1 blocked↓Immune response

What to watch for
New breathing, bowel, skin, hormone or other symptoms need prompt advice during or after treatment. Seek urgent help for new chest pain, severe muscle weakness, drooping eyelids or difficulty swallowing or breathing.

Sources: nivo · nccp · opdivo-current-v27

Immunotherapy

Ipilimumab

Yervoy
Blocks the CTLA-4 immune checkpoint

Ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4, another control on T-cell activity. It works alongside nivolumab through a different immune checkpoint. It is given every 42 days, so it is absent from each Day 22 visit. Healthy organs can also become inflamed.

T cell↓CTLA-4 blocked↓Immune activity

What to watch for
Report diarrhoea, tummy pain, rash, severe tiredness, headache or other new symptoms promptly. Keep your immune alert card.

Sources: ipi · nccp

Chemotherapy

Pemetrexed

Alimta
Disrupts deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)/RNA building

Pemetrexed blocks enzymes needed to make DNA and RNA building blocks. Dividing cancer cells are affected, but healthy blood-forming and mouth-lining cells can also be injured. Prescribed folic acid and B12 reduce toxicity; do not omit them because pemetrexed is an antifolate. There are two planned doses in this regimen.

DNA building blocks↓Supply disrupted↓Cell division slows

What to watch for
Low blood counts, sore mouth, rash, sickness and kidney problems can occur.

Sources: pem · nccp

Chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Damages DNA

Cisplatin forms links in DNA that hinder copying and repair. Healthy tissues can also be harmed. Kidney injury, changes in salts in the blood, hearing damage and nerve symptoms are important risks. Extra fluids and electrolyte checks are part of treatment. There are two planned doses in this regimen.

DNA↓Platinum links↓Copying disrupted

What to watch for
Report reduced urine, vomiting, ringing in the ears, hearing change or numbness. Hydration instructions are individual.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Day 1 visit

Nivolumab and ipilimumab are due. In Cycle 1, pemetrexed and cisplatin are also due. Checks, preparation and observation add to the infusion time. Report symptoms before treatment and confirm your support prescription.

Day 2–21: Between the first and second visits

Follow the prescribed support plan after chemotherapy. Symptoms can occur at any time; do not use the date to decide they are safe. Contact your team for new diarrhoea, breathing changes, fever or sudden illness.

Day 22: Day 22 visit · no ipilimumab

In Cycle 1 this is nivolumab and the second/final chemotherapy administration. In later cycles this is nivolumab only. Blood count, kidney and liver checks remain due. Confirm the active medicines and support plan; do not repeat ipilimumab at this visit.

Day 23–42: Before the next 42-day cycle

After the final pemetrexed, folate continues for 21 days. In immune-only cycles, continue to report symptoms between visits. Confirm the next Day 1 appointment and glucose/thyroid as well as usual blood checks.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Alternating visits and duration

The first cycle lasts 42 days: all four medicines on Day 1, then nivolumab, pemetrexed and cisplatin on Day 22. Chemotherapy ends after those two visits. From Cycle 2, nivolumab continues on Days 1 and 22 and ipilimumab on Day 1 of each 42-day cycle. Immune treatment continues while appropriate until the cancer progresses, side effects require stopping, or up to 24 months without progression. Plans can change after review.

STEP 2

Time in the Day Ward

Nivolumab over 30 minutes first; ipilimumab over 30 minutes on Day 1 only; pemetrexed over 10 minutes; cisplatin over 60 minutes, starting 30 minutes after pemetrexed, with pre/post hydration. Ipilimumab requires vital signs every 30 minutes during infusion and for one hour afterwards. Clinic time also includes assessment, preparation, observation and support.

STEP 3

Medicine and monitoring appointments

Folic acid starts before pemetrexed, continues through both chemotherapy visits and for 21 days after the last pemetrexed dose. B12 is given in the preceding week. The source repeats B12 every three cycles if pemetrexed continues; this planned course has only two pemetrexed administrations, so do not automatically continue B12 in immune-only cycles. Your team reconciles the steroid used to prevent pemetrexed rash with the anti-sickness steroid into one clear prescription. Do not add the two schedules together or use leftover steroids yourself.

STEP 4

Transport and access

intravenous (IV) treatment through agreed venous access; no home chemotherapy pump. Arrange transport, lab appointments and prescriptions. Allow extra time for pre/post hydration and review fluid restrictions, urine and electrolyte needs individually. Keep immune alert information available for every healthcare contact.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Cycle 1 Days 1 and 22 · chemotherapy-containing visits

Use generic high-risk NCIS row including olanzapine. Cisplatin requires pre/post hydration and electrolyte review; follow individual/local fluid policy. Folic acid starts before pemetrexed, continues through both chemotherapy visits and for 21 days after the last pemetrexed dose. B12 is given in the preceding week. The source repeats B12 every three cycles if pemetrexed continues; this planned course has only two pemetrexed administrations, so do not automatically continue B12 in immune-only cycles. Your team reconciles the steroid used to prevent pemetrexed rash with the anti-sickness steroid into one clear prescription. Do not add the two schedules together or use leftover steroids yourself. Nivolumab and ipilimumab can inflame healthy organs. Report new symptoms promptly, including after treatment stops; follow-up vigilance is required for at least five months after the last dose.

Cycle 2 onwards Day 1 · nivolumab + ipilimumab every 42 days

Ipilimumab is low risk and nivolumab minimal; this combined visit is low risk. No further pemetrexed or platinum: stop/review induction antiemetics and rash steroid; complete the folate tail only. Continue immune symptom education, bloods each treatment and glucose/TFT each 42-day cycle.

Cycle 2 onwards Day 22 · nivolumab alone, between ipilimumab visits

No routine prophylactic antiemetic course for nivolumab-only minimal-risk treatment; prescribe symptom-directed rescue under local policy if needed. Ipilimumab is due at the next 42-day cycle Day 1, not every nivolumab visit. Immune toxicity can still occur between ipilimumab visits; the joint hold rule remains relevant.

Treatment reference · 01

Why this combination is offered

This treatment aims to control metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer, help symptoms and extend life. It is usually given without an expectation of cure. The team checks the cancer subtype, molecular results, your health and your preferences before recommending this particular chemotherapy backbone.

What do the cancer terms mean?

Metastatic means cancer has spread beyond where it started. NSCLC is a broad lung-cancer group; non-squamous describes one histology, often adenocarcinoma. First-line means the first systemic treatment for this setting. The pathology and molecular results help determine whether this exact pemetrexed combination suits your cancer.

Sources: nccp

What does the evidence mean for me?

In CheckMate 9LA, the strategy of two immune medicines with a short chemotherapy course helped people live longer than chemotherapy alone. The study included different histologies and platinum medicines. It cannot tell exactly what benefit you will have, or prove that this particular platinum is better for you.

Sources: trial

What are the alternatives?

Ask how tumour tests, fitness and your priorities affect the options, including other drug combinations, targeted treatment when relevant, a clinical trial or symptom-focused care. Support for breathlessness, pain, appetite and emotional concerns can accompany anticancer treatment.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 02

Before the first visit

The preventive medicines and baseline review are part of the treatment.

Describe your usual health

Tell the team about your usual breathing and bowel pattern, autoimmune illness, lung inflammation, infections, nerve or hearing symptoms, diabetes, and all prescription or non-prescription medicines. This helps identify a new problem promptly.

Sources: nccp

Folate, B12 and steroid plan

Folic acid starts before pemetrexed, continues through both chemotherapy visits and for 21 days after the last pemetrexed dose. B12 is given in the preceding week. The source repeats B12 every three cycles if pemetrexed continues; this planned course has only two pemetrexed administrations, so do not automatically continue B12 in immune-only cycles. Your team reconciles the steroid used to prevent pemetrexed rash with the anti-sickness steroid into one clear prescription. Do not add the two schedules together or use leftover steroids yourself.

Sources: nccp

Prepare contact and practical support

Keep your oncology and out-of-hours instructions, a thermometer, treatment names and immune alert card available. Arrange blood tests, transport and help at home where needed. Ask what to do if a support medicine is missed; do not double a dose without advice.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 03

Two chemotherapy visits, then continuing immunotherapy

The first cycle lasts 42 days: all four medicines on Day 1, then nivolumab, pemetrexed and cisplatin on Day 22. Chemotherapy ends after those two visits. From Cycle 2, nivolumab continues on Days 1 and 22 and ipilimumab on Day 1 of each 42-day cycle.

Day 1 and Day 22 differ

Cycle 1 Day 1 includes all four medicines. Day 22 is three weeks later and includes nivolumab plus the second and final planned chemotherapy doses, but no ipilimumab. A 42-day cycle is six weeks.

Sources: nccp

The next 42-day cycles

From Cycle 2, Day 1 includes nivolumab and ipilimumab. Day 22 includes nivolumab only. No pemetrexed or platinum is planned in this continuation phase. The support medicines change too; check the updated prescription.

Sources: nccp

How long does treatment last?

Immune treatment continues while appropriate until the cancer progresses, side effects require stopping, or up to 24 months without progression. Plans can change after review. The team reviews benefit, symptoms and scans. A delay can allow investigation or recovery. It does not mean you have failed treatment.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 04

What the checks are looking for

Different checks assess safety today, possible toxicity and cancer response.

Blood count, kidney and liver

Blood counts and kidney/liver tests are checked before each Day 1 and Day 22 treatment. Blood counts show infection-fighting cells, oxygen-carrying cells and platelets. Kidney results influence chemotherapy safety. Normal results from a previous visit do not rule out a new problem.

Sources: nccp

Hormones and immune effects

Glucose and thyroid tests are checked before each 42-day cycle. The baseline assessment includes morning cortisol and hepatitis tests. New symptoms can require extra tests between planned visits; routine blood tests alone cannot exclude all immune side effects.

Sources: nccp

Scans and decisions

Your consultant sets the scan schedule. The team considers symptoms, examination, results and what matters to you. Side effects are not a reliable measure of how well treatment is controlling cancer.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 05

Effects around the chemotherapy visits

These are selected possibilities, not a forecast or a complete medicine leaflet.

Sickness, appetite and constipation

Take preventive anti-sickness medicines as prescribed. Tell the team if they do not control symptoms. Persistent vomiting, difficulty keeping fluids down or much less urine need prompt advice. Some anti-sickness and pain medicines cause constipation; ask for a bowel plan.

Sources: nccp

Blood counts and infection

Fever, shivers or suddenly feeling unwell need immediate oncology advice, even with a normal temperature. Counts can fall between visits and cannot be judged from the calendar. Report unexpected bruising, bleeding or worsening breathlessness.

Sources: nccp

Mouth, skin and tiredness

Use gentle mouth care and report ulcers, painful swallowing, rash or reduced intake. Balance rest with manageable activity. Marked or sudden weakness needs assessment: fatigue can also be caused by infection, anaemia or immune hormone problems.

Sources: nccp

Kidneys, hearing and nerves

Cisplatin can injure kidneys and affect blood salts, hearing and nerves. Fluids before and after the infusion reduce particular risks, and your fluid advice is individual. Tell the team about reduced urine, new ringing in the ears, hearing loss, tingling or numbness before another chemotherapy dose.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 06

Immune effects need early reporting

Nivolumab and ipilimumab can inflame healthy organs. Report new symptoms promptly, including after treatment stops; follow-up vigilance is required for at least five months after the last dose.

Breathing, bowel, skin and urine

New cough or breathlessness, diarrhoea, blood or mucus in stool, severe abdominal pain, yellow eyes, dark urine, reduced urine or swelling need prompt assessment. Do not assume these are ordinary chemotherapy effects. Blistering or peeling skin or sores in the eyes or mouth need urgent care.

Sources: nccp

Hormones, heart, muscles and nerves

Report unusual weakness, persistent headache, dizziness, marked thirst, frequent urination, palpitations, muscle weakness or vision changes. Severe chest pain, collapse, confusion or major breathing difficulty needs emergency care. Some hormone effects require long-term replacement.

Sources: nccp

Treatment may need to pause

The team may withhold both immune medicines, investigate and prescribe steroids or other treatment. This is different from the short preventive steroid course around chemotherapy. Do not start leftover steroids, change their dose or abruptly stop a prescribed course.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 07

Living with treatment

The aim is to fit care around the things that matter to you.

Food, fluids, work and movement

Ask for help with weight loss, reduced appetite, work adjustments, transport or household tasks. A fixed fluid target is not suitable for everyone. Do not drive if dizzy, very tired or affected by sedating medicines; cisplatin anti-sickness treatment can include olanzapine.

Sources: nccp

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility before treatment where relevant. Ask for a written contraception plan covering all four medicines and the period after treatment. Report possible pregnancy promptly and do not breastfeed unless your team confirms it is safe.

Sources: nccp

Support and choices

Ask for an interpreter, accessible information, psychological support or a trusted person to join appointments. Discuss alternatives, expected benefits and lasting harms. You can revisit your preferences as treatment continues.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 08

Care after a drug stops

Stopping chemotherapy or immunotherapy changes the plan but does not immediately remove all risks.

After the second pemetrexed dose

Finish the prescribed 21-day folic-acid tail after the last pemetrexed. Review anti-sickness and rash-steroid stop dates. B12 is not automatically continued just because immune therapy continues.

Sources: nccp

After the last immune dose

Nivolumab and ipilimumab can inflame healthy organs. Report new symptoms promptly, including after treatment stops; follow-up vigilance is required for at least five months after the last dose. Keep the alert card and tell other healthcare professionals about both immune medicines. Ask who will arrange follow-up and review any unresolved symptoms.

Sources: nccp

Use your team’s urgent instructions

When you call, state your treatment names, last treatment date, what changed, temperature if known, and whether you can drink and pass urine. Do not delay calling while gathering every detail.

Sources: nccp

Why this treatment is offered

The combined strategy improved survival compared with chemotherapy in the trial population, with some durable responses. Benefits and harms vary between people.

The study tested the combined strategy across different lung-cancer histologies and chemotherapy choices. Your own expected benefit depends on your cancer and health.

Sources: trial

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Will chemotherapy continue for months?

The planned pemetrexed/platinum course is two administrations, on Days 1 and 22 of Cycle 1. Immune treatment can continue afterwards with a different support plan.

Why is ipilimumab absent from some visits?

Nivolumab is every 21 days; ipilimumab every 42 days. Day 22 has no ipilimumab. A withheld immune dose is a separate safety decision requiring team review.

How will we know whether it is helping?

Ask about the goals, scan plan and what would lead to continuing, changing or stopping. Side effects do not reliably show treatment response.

Can I decide from this guide whether treatment is safe today?

No. Your team must interpret symptoms, bloods, previous reactions and the current prescribing protocol. Omitted results are not presumed normal.

What if I miss folic acid or a steroid dose?

Contact your treatment team or pharmacy for an individual plan. Do not double doses or combine separate steroid schedules yourself.

Which risks continue after treatment?

Nivolumab and ipilimumab can inflame healthy organs. Report new symptoms promptly, including after treatment stops; follow-up vigilance is required for at least five months after the last dose. Ask about follow-up for any lasting nerve, hearing, kidney, hormone or other problems.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are you having now? What changes after induction? What would make you call today? Ask your team to fill any gaps.
