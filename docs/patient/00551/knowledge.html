When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology immediately for new breathlessness, persistent diarrhoea, severe tummy pain, yellow skin or eyes, much less urine, spreading or blistering rash, severe headache, marked weakness, confusion, unusual thirst or passing much more urine. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizure needs emergency care. Effects can begin after treatment ends.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Three medicines. Different jobs.

These two immunotherapy medicines aim to control bowel cancer with a confirmed MSI-H/dMMR deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)-repair feature. This pathway follows previous fluoropyrimidine-based combination chemotherapy.

Immunotherapy

Nivolumab

Opdivo
Blocks PD-1

Nivolumab blocks PD-1, a brake on immune T cells. This can help immune cells attack cancer, but can also inflame healthy organs.

Immune T cell↓PD-1 brake blocked↓Immune attack

What to watch for
Report new breathing, bowel, hormone, skin, heart or nerve symptoms promptly.

Immunotherapy

Ipilimumab

Yervoy
Blocks CTLA-4

Ipilimumab blocks another immune brake, CTLA-4, helping activate T cells. Combining two immune medicines can increase anti-cancer activity and also the risk of serious immune inflammation.

Immune activation↓CTLA-4 brake blocked↓T-cell response

What to watch for
Report diarrhoea, abdominal pain, skin changes, headache, weakness or other new symptoms immediately.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Combination appointments

During the first four cycles, nivolumab is infused first over about 30 minutes, then ipilimumab over about 30 minutes. Allow extra time for checks and observation. Nivolumab alone: 240 mg IV or 600 mg under the skin every two weeks, or 1200 mg under the skin every four weeks.

STEP 2

Changing phase

The first nivolumab-only dose is due three weeks after the final combination dose, subject to clinical review. Nivolumab alone: 240 mg IV or 600 mg under the skin every two weeks, or 1200 mg under the skin every four weeks.

STEP 3

Tests and reviews

Before starting and before every cycle: blood count, kidney and liver profile, glucose and thyroid function. Cortisol is checked if clinically indicated. Disease assessment follows the consultant plan. Continue monitoring for late immune effects for at least five months after the final dose.

STEP 4

Alert card and home care

There is no take-home chemotherapy pump. Keep the card, medicine list, symptom record and urgent contact numbers accessible.

STEP 5

Treatment duration

Continue while helping and tolerable, until progression or unacceptable toxicity. This colorectal regimen does not set a fixed overall two-year limit.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Sickness prevention and premedication

Nivolumab has minimal and ipilimumab low emetogenic potential. Routine premedication is not usually required; the team individualises anti-sickness treatment and care after any previous reaction.

Steroids and other treatment for immune effects

Do not self-start or stop steroids. If prescribed, follow the exact taper and any infection-prevention plan. Do not restart cancer treatment until the team explicitly authorises it. If the team changes the interval between nivolumab-only doses, they will give you a new appointment plan: the first four-weekly dose is two weeks after the last two-weekly dose, or the first two-weekly dose is four weeks after the last four-weekly dose. Do not change the schedule yourself. This does not change which formulations are permitted for your colorectal treatment.

Pregnancy and feeding

Discuss fertility and effective contraception before treatment. Effective contraception continues for at least five months after the last nivolumab dose. Do not breastfeed during treatment; agree any later feeding plan with the team.

Treatment reference · 01

Your cancer and treatment aim

A validated tumour result helps select this treatment.

MSI-H and dMMR

These describe a feature of the cancer’s DNA-repair system. The result helps identify colorectal cancers that may respond to immunotherapy. Ask whether a genetics assessment is relevant.

Your treatment setting

These two immunotherapy medicines aim to control bowel cancer with a confirmed MSI-H/dMMR DNA-repair feature. This pathway follows previous fluoropyrimidine-based combination chemotherapy.

Choices and goals

Discuss the likely benefit, uncertainty, other treatment options, clinical trials and symptom-focused care in relation to your priorities.

Treatment reference · 02

How the two medicines work

They act at two different immune-control points.

Nivolumab

Nivolumab blocks PD-1, a brake on immune T cells. This can help immune cells attack cancer, but can also inflame healthy organs.

Ipilimumab

Ipilimumab blocks another immune brake, CTLA-4, helping activate T cells. Combining two immune medicines can increase anti-cancer activity and also the risk of serious immune inflammation.

Why side effects need prompt assessment

Immune activity may inflame healthy organs. The combination can cause serious effects, and symptoms may continue or begin after ipilimumab or all treatment stops. Pauses, steroids or permanent discontinuation may be needed.

Treatment reference · 03

Combination first, then nivolumab alone

Keep both phases in your written plan.

Four combination cycles

Nivolumab 3 mg/kg and ipilimumab 1 mg/kg are given on Day 1 every 21 days for four cycles. Nivolumab is infused first, then ipilimumab; each infusion takes about 30 minutes, with additional preparation and observation.

First nivolumab-only treatment

The first nivolumab-only dose is due three weeks after the final combination dose, subject to clinical review.

Maintenance options

Nivolumab alone: 240 mg IV or 600 mg under the skin every two weeks, or 1200 mg under the skin every four weeks.

How long treatment continues

Continue while helping and tolerable, until progression or unacceptable toxicity. This colorectal regimen does not set a fixed overall two-year limit.

Under-the-skin maintenance

The team may select the specific nivolumab formulation given under the skin over about 3–5 minutes. Tell staff about sore, red or bruised skin at the injection site. Dose and route must match the prescription; they are not interchangeable by simple volume.

Changing maintenance appointments

If the team changes the interval between nivolumab-only doses, they will give you a new appointment plan: the first four-weekly dose is two weeks after the last two-weekly dose, or the first two-weekly dose is four weeks after the last four-weekly dose. Do not change the schedule yourself. This does not change which formulations are permitted for your colorectal treatment.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests and treatment reviews

Checks continue in both phases.

Blood and thyroid tests

Before starting and before every cycle: blood count, kidney and liver profile, glucose and thyroid function. Cortisol is checked if clinically indicated. Disease assessment follows the consultant plan. Continue monitoring for late immune effects for at least five months after the final dose.

Scans and markers

The supervising consultant determines imaging, tumour-marker and other disease-monitoring tests. Ask when each test is due and who will review the result.

Treatment delays

If a dose is held, the team decides whether and when either medicine can restart. Do not assume one medicine is safe to receive when the other has been withheld.

Treatment reference · 05

Between visits and practical support

Keep an early-reporting plan.

Bowels and hydration

Report new diarrhoea, blood in stools or abdominal pain immediately. Immune bowel inflammation can worsen and needs assessment; do not rely on self-treatment alone.

Energy, hormones and daily life

New marked fatigue, headache, dizziness, weakness, thirst or extra urine can need hormone/glucose tests. Ask for help with eating, activity, sleep, work and caring responsibilities.

Medicine list and steroids

Tell the team about all medicines, supplements and steroid use. Follow any steroid taper exactly and carry information about hormone replacement.

Pregnancy and feeding

Discuss fertility and effective contraception before treatment. Effective contraception continues for at least five months after the last nivolumab dose. Do not breastfeed during treatment; agree any later feeding plan with the team.

Treatment reference · 06

Urgent symptoms and care after treatment

Do not wait for the next appointment.

What to report

Contact oncology immediately for new breathlessness, persistent diarrhoea, severe tummy pain, yellow skin or eyes, much less urine, spreading or blistering rash, severe headache, marked weakness, confusion, unusual thirst or passing much more urine. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizure needs emergency care. Effects can begin after treatment ends.

During infusions

Alert the nurse immediately if you feel itchy, flushed, breathless, dizzy, tight-chested or suddenly unwell.

Late effects

Keep the alert card and report symptoms after treatment stops. NCCP specifies continuous monitoring for at least five months after the last dose. Some hormone changes need longer follow-up.

Evidence for this colorectal setting

CheckMate 142 followed 119 previously treated people receiving nivolumab 3 mg/kg plus ipilimumab 1 mg/kg, followed by nivolumab alone.

The four-year follow-up reported sustained responses in this single-arm cohort. Without a randomised control group, it cannot establish how much longer a person lives compared with another treatment.
