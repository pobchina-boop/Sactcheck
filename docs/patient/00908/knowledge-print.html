Understand your treatment

Three medicines. Different jobs.

This treatment aims to control extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer, relieve symptoms and help people live longer. The balance of benefit, side effects and daily life is individual; it is generally not offered with an expectation of cure.

Immunotherapy

Serplulimab

Hetronifly
Blocks the PD-1 immune brake

Serplulimab is an antibody that blocks PD-1, a control switch on immune T cells. Some cancers use PD-L1 or PD-L2 to engage this switch and reduce the immune response. Blocking it can help immune cells attack cancer. The same immune activation can inflame healthy organs, so new symptoms need assessment even after treatment stops.

Immune cell↓Brake blocked↓Cancer cell

What to watch for
The immune system can also inflame healthy organs. New cough, diarrhoea, unusual weakness, severe headache, thirst or a spreading rash need prompt advice. Effects can start during treatment or after it finishes.

Sources: nccp · ema · serplulimab-smpc

Chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Damages cancer-cell deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)

Carboplatin is a platinum-containing chemotherapy. Inside cells, it forms chemical attachments to DNA, including links that stop DNA strands working normally. This disrupts copying and repair and can lead to cell death. Healthy cells can also be injured, especially blood-forming cells in the bone marrow. The kidneys help remove carboplatin, so kidney function is used to calculate the dose. AUC 5 describes a target drug exposure over time; it is not a fixed dose of 5 mg.

DNA strands↓Platinum links↓Copying disrupted

What to watch for
Low blood counts, nausea and vomiting can occur. An allergic reaction is also possible: tell the nurse immediately if you feel flushed, itchy, tight-chested or unwell during the drip.

Sources: nccp

Chemotherapy

Etoposide

Chemotherapy; intravenous (IV) or a separately prescribed capsule alternative
Interferes with DNA repair during cell division

Etoposide blocks topoisomerase II, an enzyme that helps untangle DNA. It can leave DNA breaks unrepaired so that dividing cancer cells die. Healthy dividing cells are affected too, which can lower blood counts, irritate the mouth and bowel and cause hair loss. Kidney function, liver tests and albumin help the team choose a safe dose. Capsules have different dosing instructions from the drip.

DNA strands↓Repair disrupted↓Cell division stops

What to watch for
Report fever or sudden illness immediately. During a drip, tell the nurse about dizziness, flushing, breathing difficulty or pain at the cannula. If capsules are prescribed, do not take an extra dose after vomiting or a missed dose without advice.

Sources: nccp · etoposide-smpc

The whole treatment journey

Serplulimab, carboplatin and etoposide: treatment and checks

First-line extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer

Induction lasts four planned 21-day cycles unless treatment needs to stop earlier. On Day 1 you receive serplulimab, carboplatin and etoposide by drip. With the standard IV schedule, etoposide is repeated on Days 2 and 3. After induction, carboplatin and etoposide stop. Serplulimab alone can continue every three weeks while it is helping and its risks remain acceptable. There is no fixed universal number of maintenance cycles in this NCCP regimen. Your team may instead prescribe etoposide by drip on Day 1 and capsules twice daily on Days 2 and 3. This is a separate alternative, not extra treatment. Oral and IV doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Confirm which plan you have; take the capsules on an empty stomach as prescribed.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Day 1: three medicines during induction

Serplulimab is given first over about 60 minutes at the first visit. If tolerated, later doses may take about 30 minutes. Carboplatin takes about 30 minutes and IV etoposide about 60 minutes. Assessment, preparation and observation add time. Days 2–3 need further etoposide visits unless the separate oral alternative has been prescribed. Before leaving, confirm Days 2–3 etoposide arrangements, your written support prescription and urgent contact number.

Day 2–5: Etoposide Days 2–3 and early recovery

Your team may instead prescribe etoposide by drip on Day 1 and capsules twice daily on Days 2 and 3. This is a separate alternative, not extra treatment. Oral and IV doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Confirm which plan you have; take the capsules on an empty stomach as prescribed. Anti-sickness support must cover the actual treatment days and afterwards. Take only the written plan; ask before adding duplicate medicines. Contact the team for persistent vomiting, inability to drink, new diarrhoea or a problem taking prescribed capsules.

Day 6–14: Keep checking how you feel

Blood counts may be reduced between treatments. You cannot tell your count from the date or from how well you feel. Fatigue or mouth symptoms may affect eating and daily activity. Use the urgent advice below for fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, including with a normal temperature.

Day 15–21: Prepare for your next review

Some symptoms may settle; others can continue or appear for the first time. Improvement does not prove your blood counts have recovered. The next treatment depends on your review and tests. Bring your symptom record and questions. Confirm the next appointment: the next Day 1 is usually 21 days after this cycle's Day 1, if treatment proceeds.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Induction: plan each treatment day

Day 1 carboplatin has a stronger anti-sickness plan. Days 2–3 etoposide also need planned cover, with review for two days after the final chemotherapy dose. Ask for one reconciled schedule; do not add another steroid or anti-sickness course yourself.

Maintenance: a different prescription

Serplulimab alone usually does not need the routine chemotherapy anti-sickness course. Continue the alert card and immune symptom checks; new nausea or vomiting may need investigation, not just extra anti-sickness tablets.

Treatment reference · 01

Understanding the treatment aim

Take one section at a time and discuss what matters to you.

What do the words mean?

Small-cell describes the cancer cells seen under the microscope. Extensive-stage means it is too widespread for the limited-stage treatment approach. First-line means the first drug treatment for this stage. This regimen combines two chemotherapy medicines with serplulimab immunotherapy.

Sources: nccp

What is treatment trying to achieve?

The usual aims are cancer control, symptom relief and longer life while considering quality of life. It is generally not given with an expectation of cure. Some people have a prolonged response; others do not. Your team uses your cancer tests, general health and preferences to choose a plan.

Sources: nccp

What are the alternatives?

Ask about other appropriate systemic treatments, a clinical trial and symptom-focused care. Palliative care can support symptoms and daily life while you receive active cancer treatment. This regimen has no required PD-L1 cutoff; that does not guarantee benefit.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 02

Before the first infusion

Your hospital’s instructions take priority.

Tell the team your starting point

Describe usual breathing, bowel pattern, energy, skin and nerve symptoms. Mention autoimmune illness, previous lung inflammation, infection and all medicines including steroids and supplements. Changes are easier to recognise when the team knows what is normal for you.

Sources: nccp

Prepare the practical details

Keep the oncology and out-of-hours numbers, a thermometer and the immunotherapy alert card available. Check blood-test appointments, transport and who can help at home. Ask for written medicine directions and accessible information.

Sources: nccp

What happens on the day?

Serplulimab is given first over about 60 minutes at the first visit. If tolerated, later doses may take about 30 minutes. Carboplatin takes about 30 minutes and IV etoposide about 60 minutes. Assessment, preparation and observation add time. Days 2–3 need further etoposide visits unless the separate oral alternative has been prescribed. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, itching, chest pain, dizziness, breathing changes or pain around the drip.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 03

Etoposide: confirm the route

There are two NCCP schedules. Your prescription chooses one.

Standard IV schedule

Etoposide is given by drip on each of Days 1, 2 and 3 during induction. Serplulimab and carboplatin are given on Day 1. The next cycle is planned 21 days after Day 1, if your team confirms it.

Sources: nccp

Separate IV and capsule alternative

Your team may instead prescribe etoposide by drip on Day 1 and capsules twice daily on Days 2 and 3. This is a separate alternative, not extra treatment. Oral and IV doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Confirm which plan you have; take the capsules on an empty stomach as prescribed. Capsules are supplied in different strengths; your label specifies the number to take. Do not open, crush or share capsules. Ask the pharmacist about handling, storage and returning unused doses.

Sources: nccp · etoposide-oral

Treatment reference · 04

What tests and scans check

A normal earlier test does not rule out a new problem.

Blood counts

Neutrophils help fight infection, platelets help stop bleeding, and red cells carry oxygen. Counts can fall between visits. You cannot know a count from the day of the cycle or from feeling well.

Sources: nccp

Kidneys, liver and hormones

Kidney function helps set carboplatin dosing and may affect etoposide dosing. Liver tests and albumin also matter for etoposide. Serplulimab can inflame organs and hormone glands, so blood glucose and thyroid checks continue during maintenance. Report new symptoms even if an earlier test was normal.

Sources: nccp

Scans and treatment review

The team sets the scan schedule and reviews scans with symptoms and examination. Stable cancer can be a worthwhile result. Report new symptoms before the next scan; do not assume worsening is evidence that immune treatment is working.

Sources: nccp

Delays and changed doses

Chemotherapy may be delayed or reduced for toxicity. Serplulimab is held or stopped for relevant toxicity rather than dose-reduced. Ask which medicine is changing and why. Omitted checks are not the same as normal results.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 05

Side effects and what to do

Selected possibilities are shown; this is not a prediction or a complete medicine leaflet.

Nausea, vomiting and appetite

Take prescribed anti-sickness medicines as directed. Small meals and regular sips may help. Report poor control early; persistent vomiting, inability to keep fluids down or much less urine needs prompt advice.

Sources: nccp

Fatigue and weakness

Balance rest with manageable activity. Sudden severe weakness, dizziness, breathlessness or inability to manage basic tasks needs assessment: infection, anaemia or hormone problems can resemble ordinary tiredness.

Sources: nccp

Mouth and bowels

Use gentle mouth care and report painful ulcers or difficulty drinking. Report new diarrhoea, abdominal pain or blood in stools promptly because immune bowel inflammation is possible. Ask about constipation from anti-sickness drugs or pain medicines; severe pain, swelling or vomiting needs assessment.

Sources: nccp

Hair, skin, hearing and nerves

Chemotherapy can cause hair loss. Report new hearing changes, numbness or weakness, and tell the team if these affect balance or daily tasks. Serplulimab can cause immune skin reactions: blistering, peeling or sores around the eyes or mouth need urgent advice.

Sources: nccp

Bruising and bleeding

Report unusual bruising, nosebleeds or bleeding gums. Heavy bleeding, black stools with weakness or severe breathlessness needs urgent assessment. Do not change prescribed blood thinners without advice.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 06

Serplulimab: symptoms that deserve special attention

Immune treatment can cause inflammation in healthy organs, during treatment or after it ends. Keep your immunotherapy alert card and tell other healthcare professionals you have received serplulimab.

Lungs, bowel, liver and kidneys

New cough or breathlessness, diarrhoea, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, reduced urine or swelling can signal inflammation. These symptoms also have other causes, including infection and cancer. Contact the team promptly for assessment rather than trying to identify the organ or diagnosis yourself.

Sources: nccp

Hormones, heart and nervous system

Report unusual weakness, persistent headache, dizziness, marked thirst, frequent urination, palpitations, muscle weakness or changes in vision. Severe chest pain, collapse, confusion, seizures or major breathing difficulty require emergency care. Some hormone effects need ongoing replacement treatment even after immunotherapy stops.

Sources: nccp

Why early reporting matters

The team may pause treatment and investigate. Some reactions need steroids or other immune-suppressing treatment; others need hormone replacement. These are different from the short planned steroid course around chemotherapy. Do not start leftover steroids, change their dose or stop a prescribed course yourself. Carry the treatment name when attending emergency care.

Sources: nccp

Muscle weakness and vision changes

New muscle weakness, tiring much more easily, drooping eyelids, swallowing difficulty or new visual symptoms need urgent advice. Difficulty breathing or swallowing needs emergency care. Serplulimab can cause serious nerve-muscle or eye inflammation; do not wait for the next appointment.

Sources: serplulimab-smpc

Treatment reference · 07

Living with treatment

The aim is a plan that fits your life as well as your treatment. Bring practical concerns to the team early.

Food, fluids and weight

Aim for enough food and fluid to maintain strength, using foods you tolerate. Tell the team about weight loss, swallowing problems or difficulty shopping or cooking; ask for dietitian support. A fixed fluid target is not right for everyone, particularly with heart or kidney disease. “Anti-cancer” diets, fasting and high-dose supplements are not substitutes for treatment and can create nutritional or interaction problems.

Sources: nccp · hse

Movement, work and rest

Try activity that feels manageable and increase or reduce it according to symptoms. Short walks or simple daily tasks may be more realistic than your usual exercise. Discuss work adjustments and travel plans. Do not drive if dizzy, unusually tired or affected by sedating medicines. Sudden deterioration should prompt assessment, not an attempt to push through exercise.

Sources: nccp · hse

Infection precautions without isolation

Wash hands, practise food hygiene and avoid close contact with people who are unwell. Ask about vaccinations and their timing; some vaccines are unsuitable during treatment. You can usually still spend time with family. Keep your urgent contact plan available: precautions reduce risk but do not make fever or sudden illness safe to watch at home.

Sources: nccp · hse

Intimacy, fertility and pregnancy

Discuss fertility preservation before starting if relevant. Do not become pregnant or breastfeed during this treatment. NCCP specifies effective contraception during serplulimab and for at least six months after the last dose for women of childbearing potential; the team must reconcile all medicines into one written contraception and breastfeeding plan. Report a possible pregnancy promptly.

Sources: nccp · hse

Emotional support and choices

It is reasonable to feel worried, angry, uncertain or overloaded. Ask for psycho-oncology, counselling, social work or family support. You can request information in smaller pieces, an interpreter or a written summary. Tell the team what you most want to preserve in daily life. You can revisit treatment decisions and ask for more time or another explanation.

Sources: nccp · hse

Treatment reference · 08

A short treatment dictionary

Terms you may hear in clinic.

Cycle · induction · maintenance

A cycle is a planned treatment-and-interval period. Induction is the initial combination phase. Maintenance is ongoing treatment after that phase; it does not mean side-effect-free treatment.

Sources: nccp

Response · stable disease · progression

Response means the cancer has reduced by the assessment used. Stable disease means it has not changed enough to meet response or progression criteria. Progression means growth or spread meeting the assessment criteria. Your team interprets these alongside how you are feeling.

Sources: nccp

Neutropenia · anaemia · thrombocytopenia

Neutropenia means low infection-fighting neutrophils. Anaemia means low haemoglobin. Thrombocytopenia means low platelets. These are different results with different implications; one normal number does not establish that all treatment risks are low.

Sources: nccp

Immune-related · colitis · pneumonitis

Immune-related effects come from immune activity against healthy tissue. Colitis means inflammation of the colon; pneumonitis means inflammation in the lungs. A symptom alone cannot establish either diagnosis.

Sources: nccp

Why this treatment is offered

A randomised study found that adding serplulimab to carboplatin and etoposide helped people with previously untreated extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer live longer than chemotherapy alone.

These results describe groups. They cannot predict your own response or length of life. Ask what benefit is realistic for you and what matters most in your daily life.

Sources: trial-v08423 · trial-original

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

How will we know if it is helping?

Ask your oncologist what the treatment is aiming to achieve for you, when scans are planned and what would lead to continuing, changing or stopping it. How you feel and the amount of side effects do not reliably show how well treatment is working.

Why might a cycle be delayed?

A delay can give time to recover or investigate a new symptom. It is a safety decision, not a personal failure. Ask which medicines are affected, what needs to improve and when you will be reviewed.

Will I get every side effect?

No. The body map shows selected possibilities, not a prediction. Ask about common effects, serious less common effects, and any long-term risks that matter particularly to you. The same symptom can have several causes.

What should I organise before starting?

Confirm blood-test and Day Ward arrangements, transport home, your medicines schedule and a thermometer. Keep the oncology contact instructions and immunotherapy alert card available. Ask for an interpreter or accessible information if useful.

What else belongs in the consent conversation?

Your priorities, expected benefit, alternatives including supportive care, fertility, contraception, pregnancy or breastfeeding, work, driving and the possibility of lasting side effects. You can ask more questions or reconsider treatment at any stage.

Do more side effects mean the treatment is working better?

No. Side effects are not a reliable personal measure of cancer response. Report them so they can be treated; do not tolerate serious symptoms in the hope that they are a sign of benefit.

Do all three medicines continue indefinitely?

Induction lasts four planned 21-day cycles unless treatment needs to stop earlier. On Day 1 you receive serplulimab, carboplatin and etoposide by drip. With the standard IV schedule, etoposide is repeated on Days 2 and 3. After induction, carboplatin and etoposide stop. Serplulimab alone can continue every three weeks while it is helping and its risks remain acceptable. There is no fixed universal number of maintenance cycles in this NCCP regimen.

What should I say when calling about a symptom?

Give the current medicines or say you are in maintenance, the last treatment date, what changed and when, your temperature and whether you can eat, drink and pass urine. Do not delay calling to collect every detail.

Which checks continue afterwards?

Immune effects can arise after the final serplulimab dose. Keep the alert card and urgent contact plan. Follow-up of blood counts, organ changes and scans follows the team’s plan; no universal scan interval is imposed by this regimen.

Will I come to hospital on all three treatment days?

Usually with the standard IV induction plan, yes. Some people receive a separate capsule prescription for Days 2 and 3. Confirm the route and exact schedule before arranging transport; never substitute capsules for a drip yourself.

What if I miss or vomit an etoposide capsule dose?

Contact the oncology team or pharmacist for instructions. Do not double the next dose or repeat a vomited dose without advice. Store and handle capsules exactly as the pharmacy advises.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are you having now? What changes after induction? What would make you call today? Ask your team to fill any gaps.
