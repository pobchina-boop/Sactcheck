01 / what is it?

Three parts, one treatment

Oxaliplatin and 5-FU are chemotherapy. Folinic acid helps 5-FU act on cancer cells. The first treatment is on day 1; 5-FU then continues through a portable pump for 46 hours.

Oxaliplatin

Platinum chemotherapy

Given through a drip on day 1. Cold-triggered tingling and changes in sensation are distinctive effects. Numbness may build after repeated cycles.

Folinic acid

Helps 5-FU work

Given as an infusion before 5-FU. It is part of the chemotherapy combination; it is not a vitamin supplement to take yourself.

5-fluorouracil

Injection + pump

Given by a short injection on day 1 and then a 46-hour continuous infusion. Bowel, mouth, blood-cell and occasionally heart problems can occur.

Why am I receiving it, and for how long? NCCP 00209 covers several gastrointestinal cancers and different treatment purposes. In adjuvant colon cancer, the source describes 12 cycles, or 6 cycles for selected lower-risk disease; advanced disease follows a different plan. Ask your team to write your own goal and expected review date in the passport below. A cycle may be delayed or adjusted.
02 / the chronology

One cycle, from day 1 to the next

This sequence is based on the NCCP 14-day schedule. Symptom windows are examples: they vary from person to person and from cycle to cycle.

Day 1

Review and bloods; oxaliplatin and folinic acid by drip, then 5-FU injection and pump connection.

Days 2–3

Pump continues. Disconnect after 46 hours, usually on day 3. Cold sensitivity or nausea may start.

Days 3–6

Bowel changes, poor appetite or mouth soreness may appear. Follow your prescribed symptom plan.

Days 7–14

Blood counts may be lower. There is no reliable personal nadir date: respond to fever on any day.

Day 15

First day of the next 14-day cycle if your team confirms bloods, symptoms and recovery allow.

A useful distinction The pump disconnect is timed from when the 46-hour infusion actually starts. The chart is a guide to experience, not a schedule for self-assessing blood counts or delaying a call.

Before the first cycle

Your team checks blood count, kidney and liver function and DPD status before 5-FU. Tell them about heart conditions, existing numbness, bowel symptoms, pregnancy possibility and all medicines you use.

Before every later cycle

Blood count, kidney and liver blood tests; check numbness, diarrhoea, mouth symptoms and hands/feet. New problems may change the dose or timing after clinician review.

03 / where effects can occur

Read the signs your body gives you

Each numbered area points to a symptom group. The colour signals the action: teal means report and manage with your team; red means seek urgent advice.

1Mouth and throat

Mouth ulcers can reduce eating and drinking. A suddenly tight throat after cold exposure needs prompt assessment.

3Gut

Nausea, vomiting or diarrhoea may occur. Report persistent symptoms and dehydration.

5Hands, feet and nerves

Cold sensitivity may start early; numbness between cycles may build. Tell the team before the next dose.

2Heart and breathing

Chest pressure or pain, fainting or sudden breathlessness needs urgent help. Rare 5-FU cardiac effects can begin during infusion.

4Blood and infection

White cells may fall later. Fever, shivers or suddenly feeling unwell requires immediate contact, on any day.

!Infusion and skin

Report infusion reactions and pain, redness, swelling or leakage at the line or pump immediately.

Cold and the throat Avoid cold drinks, freezer items and cold air when they trigger tingling after oxaliplatin. The NCCP says not to suck ice chips during the 5-FU injection. A tight throat can have several causes; seek advice rather than assuming it is harmless.
04 / the action plan

When to call for help

Call your oncology SOS service immediately Temperature of 37.5°C or higher, or 36°C or lower; hot/cold shivers or flu-like illness; four or more bouts of diarrhoea in a day; vomiting despite anti-sickness tablets; mouth ulcers stopping eating or drinking; bleeding or unusual bruising; or a sudden change in how well you feel. Do not wait until the next appointment.

First check the temperature

If you feel unwell, measure your temperature before taking paracetamol or ibuprofen. They may lower a fever and hide a warning sign. Contact the oncology team for advice before using them to treat a suspected fever. They may still be appropriate for other reasons when your team advises.

Emergency symptoms

Severe chest pain, marked breathlessness, collapse or a medical emergency: call 112 or 999. Tell the emergency team you are receiving chemotherapy and whether the 5-FU pump is attached.

5-FU pump problem A leak, wet dressing, damaged tubing, disconnection or persistent alarm needs the specific pump instructions and immediate contact with the day ward or the emergency oncology number. Do not try to repair a leaking line yourself.
05 / care between visits

Your support medicines have a purpose

The CUH moderate emetogenic-risk proforma below is a source for discussion, not a prescription. Follow the labelled medicines actually supplied to you; your team may adapt the list.

Prevent nausea

Dexamethasone + omeprazole

The local sheet lists dexamethasone on days 2–3 and omeprazole on days 1–3. Omeprazole is included deliberately. Confirm your individual directions.

Look after the mouth

Nystatin + chlorhexidine

The local sheet lists both during chemotherapy. Check timing and technique with your nurse or pharmacist, and report sores that affect drinking or eating.

Use when needed

Cyclizine, metoclopramide, loperamide

These are on the local sheet for nausea and diarrhoea as prescribed. Seek help promptly if symptoms continue despite them; never let tablets delay a call.

Medicine reconciliation matters Bring your actual prescription and a current medicine list. The oncology team must confirm any interaction, contraindication, dose and timing. Do not substitute this information for the charted local proforma.

Food, fluids and mouth care

Take small regular drinks if you can, use the mouth care supplied, and tell your team if swallowing or drinking becomes difficult. Do not rely on home management for worsening diarrhoea or vomiting.

Pregnancy, fertility, medicines

Discuss pregnancy avoidance, contraception, fertility and vaccination with your team. Ask before starting non-prescribed medicines or supplements; tell other healthcare professionals that you are receiving FOLFOX.

06 / common questions

What if…?

My fingers tingle only when I open the fridge.

Cold sensitivity is characteristic of oxaliplatin. Avoid the trigger and tell your team. If symptoms persist between cycles, interfere with tasks, or include a new tight throat, report them promptly.

I have diarrhoea but took loperamide.

Follow the exact instructions you were given. Call your SOS service if you have four or more bouts in a day, are unable to drink, have blood in the stool, fever or symptoms that keep worsening.

I feel shivery but my temperature is normal.

Contact the oncology service. Serious infection can present without a high reading, and the action plan includes suddenly feeling unwell or shivery.

Will every cycle be identical?

No. Blood tests and symptoms may mean a delay, dose change or change to a drug. Your team should explain what changed and why. The 46-hour pump disconnect is timed from connection.

Is this immunotherapy?

No. This exact regimen is oxaliplatin, folinic acid and 5-FU. If another medicine is added to your plan, ask for its separate information. This guide does not describe added bevacizumab, cetuximab or immunotherapy effects.

07 / take it to the day unit

My FOLFOX cycle passport

Print the 14-page passport: one setup page, individual pages for cycles 1–12 and a reusable blank cycle page. Each cycle page covers days 1–7 and 8–14 and includes the treatment and pump disconnect dates. Print only the pages you need and bring completed sheets to each visit.

Call first; write later. Fever, new chest pain, breathlessness, persistent diarrhoea, bleeding, or a leaking or disconnected pump needs action when it happens. A low symptom score must not delay your oncology SOS plan.
