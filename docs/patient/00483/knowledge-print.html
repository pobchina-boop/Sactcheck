When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact the oncology team immediately for new or worsening breathlessness, chest pain, persistent diarrhoea, severe tummy pain, yellow skin/eyes, much less urine, severe headache, marked weakness, confusion, unusual thirst or passing much more urine, or a spreading/blistering rash. For severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or other immediately dangerous symptoms, seek emergency care. Do not wait for the next infusion.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Your cancer and treatment aim

Previously treated locally advanced or metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

Eligibility

Adults with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC after prior chemotherapy; recurrence or progression during or after one prior platinum-containing doublet for advanced/metastatic disease; ECOG 0–1.

Choices

Discuss other treatment, trials and symptom-focused care with your team.

How nivolumab works

Immunotherapy supports immune attack.

PD-1 blockade

Nivolumab blocks PD-1 on immune cells, helping them recognise and attack cancer. It can also cause the immune system to inflame healthy organs.

Healthy organs

Inflammation can affect lungs, bowel, liver, kidneys, skin, glands, muscles, heart and nerves. Some effects are serious, delayed or permanent.

Why this treatment may be offered

Nivolumab can help the immune system control some previously treated lung cancers. Some people benefit for a long time; others do not benefit.

Trial results describe groups, not an individual prognosis.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 3

Tests and review

Blood count, kidney/liver tests and glucose before every 2-week cycle; thyroid once a month; cortisol if clinically indicated. Review cancer progression before cycle 8. Continue immune-toxicity monitoring for at least 5 months after the last dose.

STEP 4

Home records

Carry your immune alert card, medicine list and urgent contact details. Follow prescribed steroids or hormone replacement carefully; do not stop steroids suddenly.

STEP 5

After treatment

Keep reporting symptoms and attend agreed follow-up for at least 5 months after the final dose; some hormone replacement continues longer.

Your treatment course

Confirm the formulation with your team.

Pauses

Treatment may be held or stopped for side effects. Nivolumab is not routinely dose-reduced. Tell the team about missed appointments.

Previous permanent-stop decisions

If your team has permanently stopped nivolumab because of a serious immune reaction, improvement does not automatically make restarting safe. Keep that decision in your treatment record; only your specialist team can review further treatment.

Tests and review

New symptoms matter even when recent tests were normal.

Before starting

Blood count, kidney/liver tests, glucose and thyroid tests establish a baseline; discuss existing illness and all medicines.

Response review

Your team assesses progression before cycle 8; later scans follow the consultant plan.

There are no guaranteed “safe days”. Immune effects can happen on any day and after treatment ends. Carry your immunotherapy alert card and tell any healthcare team that you receive nivolumab.

Between-treatment notes

Day 1: Your treatment visit

The team checks blood results, symptoms and medicines before the prescribed infusion or injection. Tell the nurse immediately about itching, flushing, breathlessness, dizziness or feeling suddenly unwell. Before leaving, confirm the next tests, treatment visit and urgent contact number.

Day 2–14: Between visits

You may feel well between infusions, but immune inflammation can begin at any time. Keep a short symptom record and take medicines exactly as prescribed. Contact your team about new symptoms instead of waiting for your next appointment. Confirm monthly thyroid tests and who arranges them; report new symptoms without waiting for the next treatment.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Sickness medicines

Nivolumab alone has minimal sickness risk. Routine preventive anti-sickness medicines or premedication are not usually required; the team may prescribe symptom relief.

Immune alert card

Carry your alert card during treatment and afterwards. Show it when seeking urgent care; symptoms can be mistaken for an infection or an ordinary stomach upset.

Hormones and steroids

Some immune effects need steroids or hormone replacement. Follow the prescribed plan and do not stop steroids suddenly. Ask for help if symptoms worsen or you cannot take essential medicines. Hormone replacement alone does not always mean nivolumab must stop. Your team checks symptoms and recovery and reviews any medicines that suppress the immune system before deciding whether treatment can continue or restart.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding before treatment. Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 5 months after the last dose. Breastfeeding requires a specialist decision; contact your team promptly if pregnancy occurs.

Practical support

Tell your team what matters to you.

Energy and activity

Balance activity and rest. New marked fatigue with dizziness, headache, thirst or weakness needs assessment.

Medicines and pregnancy

Bring all medicine/supplement details. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding. Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 5 months after the final dose.

Sickness

Minimal sickness risk: routine preventive sickness medicines are not usually required. Your team may prescribe symptom relief.

When to get help

Use your oncology urgent contact plan.

Report promptly

Report new breathlessness, persistent diarrhoea, severe pain, yellow eyes, reduced urine, severe headache, weakness, unusual thirst or a spreading/blistering rash immediately. Use your oncology contact plan; severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency care.

During treatment

Tell the nurse immediately about a reaction during the infusion or injection.

After treatment

Continue monitoring for at least 5 months after the final dose. Show your immune alert card to anyone treating you.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Why am I having nivolumab?

Previously treated locally advanced or metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC). It is used after previous chemotherapy to help control cancer; ask how the benefits and alternatives apply to you.

What are my other options?

Ask about other anticancer treatment, trials and symptom-focused care suited to your cancer and preferences. Support remains available if you decline treatment.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are you having now? What changes after induction? What would make you call today? Ask your team to fill any gaps.

Additional phase-specific support

  • Minimal emetic risk; no routine antiemetic prophylaxis or premedication. Prescribe rescue/support individually.
