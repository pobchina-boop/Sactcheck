When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Immunotherapy can cause inflammation in healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new diarrhoea, blood in stools, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell any clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Medicines in this regimen

Cemiplimab helps immune cells act against advanced cutaneous squamous cell cancer when curative surgery or radiotherapy is not suitable. The team reviews cancer control, symptoms, side effects and your priorities to decide whether to continue.

PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy

Cemiplimab

Libtayo
Releases a PD-1 immune brake

Cemiplimab blocks PD-1 signalling that can limit T-cell activity. This may help the immune system attack cutaneous squamous cell cancer. Increased immune activity can also inflame healthy organs, so new symptoms need attention even if the visible skin tumour appears to improve.

PD-1 blocked↓Immune response activated↓Organ inflammation possible

What to watch for
Carry an immunotherapy alert card and report new diarrhoea, cough, breathlessness, rash, weakness or endocrine symptoms promptly.

Treatment reference · 01

Understanding your diagnosis and treatment goal

Use these questions with your own pathology report and agreed treatment plan.

Confirm that this is skin SCC

This guide concerns cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma arising in the skin. Squamous cancers beginning inside the mouth, throat, lung or cervix have different treatment pathways. Record the original skin site, pathology date and whether the cancer has returned or spread.

Sources: nccn-patient

Why a cancer is considered high risk

The size, depth, site, growth pattern and involvement of nerves, lymph nodes or other structures can affect the plan. Prior treatment, recurrence and reduced immunity also matter. Ask the team to explain the risk features in your report in ordinary language; a risk category is not itself a treatment response.

Sources: nccn-patient

Why medicine is being offered

The cited NCCP cemiplimab setting is advanced skin SCC when curative surgery or radiotherapy is not appropriate. Record the multidisciplinary team’s reason and the aim of treatment. This guide does not make a general promise that immunotherapy will avoid surgery or cure the cancer.

Sources: nccn-patient

Immunity and transplantation

Tell the oncology team about an organ transplant, immune-suppressing medicines, autoimmune illness and previous immune treatment. These can substantially change the balance of benefit and harm. Never stop transplant or steroid medicines yourself; the relevant specialists need a shared plan.

Sources: nccn-patient

Lesions, wounds and daily function

A skin tumour or treatment can affect pain, bleeding, wound care, movement, eating, appearance or sleep. Ask for a wound and symptom plan that names who to contact. Worsening bleeding, infection symptoms, new nerve pain or weakness should be assessed rather than waiting for a photograph comparison.

Sources: nccn-patient

Treatment reference · 04

Tests: what is required and what each result means

Safety tests, tumour tests and response scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Full blood count, kidney/liver tests, glucose and thyroid tests; hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis B core antibody and hepatitis C testing. Review autoimmune conditions, organ transplantation, previous immune reactions and medicines.

Sources: nccp

During treatment

Full blood count, kidney/liver tests and glucose each cycle; thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) every three to six weeks. Review new symptoms at every visit and arrange additional tests when indicated. Response assessment and scans follow the individual plan.

Sources: nccp

Scans and tumour markers

Ask the team to write the next assessment and who will arrange it. Bloods used to monitor kidneys, liver, hormones or muscle are not automatically measures of cancer response. There is no universal tumour-marker blood schedule for these skin-cancer regimens; a scan or extra blood test is requested for a specific clinical reason.

Sources: nccp

After a pause or finish

Immune effects can begin or persist after treatment stops. Keep the agreed follow-up, steroid or hormone-replacement instructions and alert card, and tell clinicians about previous cemiplimab. The next scan, skin/lesion review and any further treatment are individually planned.

Sources: nccp

Getting the result

For each test, record its purpose, due date, result and action. Agree how results reach you and who checks a result from another hospital. If an appointment or result has not arrived, contact the named service rather than assuming no news means no action is needed.

Sources: nccn-patient

What to expect between visits

You can feel well and still need the planned checks. New symptoms deserve attention even on a day without an appointment.

Your usual pattern

Record the start and change in symptoms, temperature if advised, bowel pattern, drinking, urine and the effect on normal tasks. A simple few-line record is enough. Use it to describe a change rather than trying to grade or diagnose a toxicity yourself.

Treatment reference · 06

Skin checks, sun protection and optional photographs

This complements clinical assessment and does not replace it.

Protect your skin

Use shade, protective clothing, a hat and broad-spectrum sunscreen of at least SPF 30, and avoid tanning beds. Reapply sunscreen according to its instructions. Protection matters even when it is cloudy; choose a practical approach that lets you remain active safely.

Sources: nccn-patient

Know the pattern of your skin

Ask how to examine your skin, scars and relevant lymph-node areas and how often your own team recommends doing so. A mirror or a trusted helper can make hard-to-see areas easier to check. Report a new or changing lesion, a non-healing sore or a new lump rather than waiting for a routine visit.

Sources: nccn-patient

Photographs are optional

If useful, keep a dated photograph with consistent lighting and a size reference, following your team’s advice. Photographs are optional and do not diagnose a cancer or replace examination. Keep them privately; this printable passport does not require you to upload images or other personal health information.

Sources: nccn-patient

Wounds, nails and sensitive skin

Use the dressings and skin-care plan recommended for your lesion and treatment. Ask before applying new creams or remedies to an open or treated area. Worsening pain, discharge, redness, bleeding or loss of function needs advice; routine skin-care instructions are not a substitute for assessment.

Sources: nccn-patient

Treatment reference · 07

Pregnancy, fertility and protecting others

Discuss these precautions before treatment even if pregnancy is not currently planned.

Medicine-specific precautions

Use effective contraception during cemiplimab treatment and for at least four months after the last dose. Discuss fertility before treatment, and follow the medicine-specific breastfeeding restrictions. Report a possible pregnancy promptly.

Sources: nccp

Planning ahead

Ask early about fertility preservation, sexual health and symptoms affecting intimacy. Tell the team about any chance of pregnancy, breastfeeding or a partner’s pregnancy so that the correct medicine-specific advice can be given. Keep the precautions and their end dates in the written plan.

Sources: nccp

Safe handling and supply

Do not share anticancer medicines. Keep oral medicines in their original labelled packaging, away from children and anyone who could take them accidentally. Return unused medicines to the supplying pharmacy and follow its handling advice. A treatment hold does not remove pregnancy precautions.

Sources: nccp
