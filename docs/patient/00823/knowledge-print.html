How this medicine supports your treatment

Tepotinib blocks the MET growth signal in selected lung cancers with a MET exon 14 skipping change. In advanced cancer the aim is to control the cancer and its symptoms. The tumour test and previous treatment help determine whether it is suitable.

MET targeted therapy

Tepotinib

Blocks abnormal MET growth signalling
MET exon 14 change identified↓MET signal blocked↓Cancer growth slowed

Tepotinib inhibits the MET receptor. A MET exon 14 skipping change can keep this growth signal active; blocking it can slow or shrink selected lung cancers.

Report new cough or breathlessness, swelling, severe bowel symptoms, jaundice, palpitations or fainting promptly.

Before and during treatment

Tests and symptoms guide treatment decisions.

Before starting

Confirm the MET result, previous treatment, dose, other medicines and emergency contact. Check blood count, electrolytes, kidney and liver function; ECG if clinically indicated. Discuss pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding, and perform a pregnancy test when applicable.

NCCP 00823 v2 · official regimen · EMA Tepmetko product information, updated 08/10/2026

Ongoing reviews

Repeat blood count, electrolytes, kidney and liver tests at the team’s agreed reviews. ECG is used when clinically indicated. Report new lung symptoms, swelling, diarrhoea or vomiting between visits. A rise in creatinine needs assessment because the medicine can change the test result without necessarily damaging the kidneys. Scans follow the Consultant’s plan.

Taking tepotinib

Follow your own prescription.

Food and missed doses

Take at the same time each day with food and swallow whole. If a dose is missed, take it when remembered on the same day unless the next dose is due within 8 hours; then skip it. Do not double a dose. Ask your pharmacist if the timing is uncertain or if you vomit; do not take a replacement without advice. If swallowing is difficult, ask about the approved water-dispersion method.

NCCP 00823 v2 · official regimen · EMA Tepmetko product information, updated 08/10/2026

Medicine interactions

Ask the pharmacist to check all prescribed medicines, non-prescription medicines and supplements before changes. Tepotinib can affect medicines including dabigatran, digoxin, rosuvastatin and metformin. Supportive medicines may also affect heart rhythm; individual advice is needed.

Support during treatment

Report changes early so your team can help.

Swelling and weight changes

Report swelling early and discuss changes in weight, movement or breathing. The team will assess the cause and agree treatment; do not start a water tablet or change tepotinib yourself.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Agree a diarrhoea treatment plan before starting. Begin the prescribed anti-diarrhoeal medicine at the first loose stool as instructed, and maintain fluids if able. Persistent diarrhoea, weakness or reduced urine needs prompt advice.

Nausea medicines

Tepotinib has minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Agree an as-needed anti-sickness plan with the team, including medicine-interaction and heart-rhythm checks; no routine infusion premedication is specified.

Pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding

Avoid pregnancy during treatment. Effective contraception is needed during treatment and for at least one week after the last dose; women using hormonal contraception should also use a barrier method. Men with a partner who could become pregnant should use barrier contraception for the same period. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for at least one week afterwards. Human fertility effects are not known; discuss fertility before starting.

When to get urgent help

Do not wait for the next appointment.

Call urgently

Contact the oncology team urgently for new or worsening cough, breathlessness or fever; marked swelling or sudden weight gain; severe diarrhoea, repeated vomiting or inability to drink; yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or severe abdominal pain; or palpitations or fainting. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or swelling of the mouth or throat needs emergency help. Use your unit’s temperature and 24-hour contact instructions.

What studies tell us

VISION found that tepotinib could shrink some advanced MET exon 14 skipping lung cancers, including cancers previously treated with other medicines. It was a single-arm study, so it does not show how much longer people live compared with another treatment. Study results describe groups and cannot predict your own outcome.

Evidence for this treatment

VISION found that tepotinib could shrink some advanced MET exon 14 skipping lung cancers, including cancers previously treated with other medicines. It was a single-arm study, so it does not show how much longer people live compared with another treatment. Study results describe groups and cannot predict your own outcome.

Single-arm evidence does not establish comparative survival benefit; first-line trial patients are outside this NCCP indication.

Questions to ask

Is this a fixed course of chemotherapy?

No. These targeted tablets are taken daily without a planned rest week, while they are helping and your team considers treatment appropriate.

Why does a trial mention 500 mg?

The VISION trial’s 500 mg salt dose corresponds to 450 mg active tepotinib. The prescribed tablets contain 225 mg active tepotinib each; follow your own prescription.

What if I forget or vomit a dose?

Take a forgotten dose when remembered on the same day unless the next dose is due within 8 hours; then skip it. Never double up. Ask your pharmacist about vomiting or uncertain timing before taking any replacement.

Why might my creatinine test change?

Tepotinib can change how creatinine is handled by the kidneys, but real kidney problems can also occur. The team must assess the change; neither explanation should be assumed.

Can I change the dose when I feel better?

Only your team can authorise restarting or changing the dose after side effects. Feeling better does not establish that blood tests or other checks have recovered.
