01 / treatment identity

Why these medicines are paired

NCCP 00831 covers adults with advanced or unresectable HCC who have not had prior systemic treatment. Its purpose is to control cancer; your oncologist can explain the expected benefit and review plan for your situation.

ATEZOLIZUMAB / TECENTRIQ

Immune checkpoint treatment

It blocks PD-L1, allowing immune cells to act. An activated immune system can also inflame healthy organs. Those effects may begin during treatment or after it stops. Some hormone problems require lasting replacement treatment.

BEVACIZUMAB / AVASTIN

Anti-VEGF treatment

It blocks a vessel-growth signal. Watch for bleeding, high blood pressure, protein in the urine and impaired wound healing. In liver cancer, screening for oesophageal varices before starting is particularly important.

Which form of atezolizumab will I receive? The NCCP protocol includes IV atezolizumab (1200 mg) or subcutaneous atezolizumab (1875 mg), with IV bevacizumab (15 mg/kg), all on day 1 every 21 days. The route and appointment time on your own plan should be confirmed with your team. The combination continues while it offers clinical benefit and side effects remain acceptable; there is no preset cycle count.
02 / cycle chronology

C1 → C2 → each further review

The diagram shows treatment and monitoring events. It deliberately gives no fixed onset day for immune toxicity or bleeding; new symptoms matter whenever they appear.

Before C1

Varices screening; blood count, liver/kidney, glucose, thyroid, BP and urine protein assessment.

C1 · Day 1

Atezolizumab IV or SC, then bevacizumab IV. Tell the nurse immediately about an infusion or injection reaction.

Days 2–21

Notice new symptoms. Follow any home BP instructions and keep your SOS number accessible.

C2 · Day 22

Repeat review, bloods, BP and urine; thyroid testing is scheduled every six weeks. Treatment only if appropriate.

Later / after

Each cycle follows the same 21-day pattern. Immune effects may still appear after treatment stops.

Do not wait for a marked cycle day A new cough, diarrhoea, black stools, severe headache or other worrying symptom needs action when it occurs.

Also report new jaw or tooth pain (rare jaw-bone damage). A severe headache, vision change, confusion or seizure needs emergency assessment (rare PRES).

Atezolizumab: immune-related effects

Hormone or sugar changes (endocrine problems)

Unusual tiredness, dizziness, new headache, thirst or passing much more urine. Some hormone changes need long-term treatment.

Lung inflammation (pneumonitis)

A new cough or breathlessness needs prompt advice. Chest pain, racing heartbeat or fainting needs urgent assessment for a rare heart problem (myocarditis).

Liver inflammation (hepatitis)

Yellow eyes or skin, dark urine or worsening fatigue. Blood tests can show a change before symptoms.

Bowel inflammation (colitis)

New or worsening diarrhoea, abdominal pain or blood in stools. Contact oncology rather than waiting for the next cycle.

Kidney inflammation (nephritis)

Usually found on blood tests; report passing less urine or new swelling.

Skin, muscle or nerve changes

New spreading rash, blisters, weakness, new numbness or changes in vision need assessment.

Bevacizumab: blood vessels and healing

High blood pressure (hypertension)

Often has no symptoms, so your team checks readings. A severe headache, vision change or confusion needs urgent assessment.

Bleeding (haemorrhage)

Report nosebleeds that do not settle, black stools or new bruising. Vomiting blood or major bleeding is an emergency.

Clots in veins or arteries (thromboembolism)

A clot in a calf vein may cause one-sided swelling or pain; one in the lungs may cause sudden breathlessness or chest pain. Stroke-like symptoms are an emergency.

Protein leaking into urine (proteinuria)

Usually found on urine tests. Report new swelling or passing much less urine.

A hole or abnormal connection in the gut (perforation / fistula)

Sudden severe or persistent belly pain, often with fever or feeling very unwell, needs emergency assessment.

Slow wound healing (impaired healing)

Tell oncology before surgery, dental extraction or another procedure; the team will plan treatment timing.

Jaw-bone damage (osteonecrosis of the jaw)

Rare: new jaw or tooth pain, swelling, a loose tooth or a mouth wound that will not heal. Tell oncology and your dentist promptly.

Rare brain swelling syndrome (PRES)

Severe headache, new vision change, confusion or a seizure, with or without high blood pressure, needs emergency assessment.

Get help for a new neurological symptom A sudden severe headache, new vision loss or change, confusion or a seizure may have several causes, including the rare bevacizumab-associated condition PRES. Follow your emergency instructions immediately; PRES can occur without high blood pressure.

04 / action language

Know when to call

Emergency: call 112/999 Collapse, severe bleeding or vomiting blood, severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, seizure, sudden new vision loss or a major neurological change. Tell responders you receive atezolizumab and bevacizumab.

Contact oncology urgently

Black stools, any concerning bleeding, severe or persistent diarrhoea, new cough or breathlessness, yellow eyes, reduced urine, new severe headache, new vision changes, marked weakness, confusion, a temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, or feeling suddenly unwell. Follow your centre’s SOS instructions even if a symptom begins after the last cycle.

Medicines and fever

Measure your temperature before paracetamol or ibuprofen if you feel unwell, and contact your team for advice. Do not self-start steroids for suspected immune toxicity. Ask before using anti-inflammatory painkillers, herbal products or new medicines, particularly if you have liver disease or bleeding risk.

What about anti-sickness or a PPI? The NCCP lists both medicines as having minimal emetogenic potential and says premedication is not usually required. No routine antiemetic or PPI script is invented here. Continue medicines actually prescribed to you and ask for a reconciled list if you are unsure.
05 / what the team checks

Tests have a purpose

Before starting

Bleeding and liver context

Oesophageal varices screening, BP, urine protein, blood count, liver/kidney profile, glucose and thyroid tests. Discuss past bleeding, surgery, autoimmune illness and medicines with the team.

Each cycle

Blood, urine, BP

NCCP specifies blood count, liver/kidney profile, glucose and urine-protein dipstick before each cycle, plus BP before and after treatment. Results are assessed alongside symptoms.

Every 6 weeks

Thyroid tests

Thyroid function tests are scheduled every six weeks. Your team may order additional endocrine tests when symptoms suggest adrenal, pituitary or glucose problems.

Why screen for varices? Liver disease can produce swollen veins in the oesophagus that may bleed. Bevacizumab can increase bleeding risk, so the source protocol includes varices screening before treatment. Ask the team whether screening was completed and whether any finding needs treatment.
06 / practical situations

What if…?

I develop diarrhoea after my infusion.

Contact oncology rather than assuming a food infection. Immune colitis and other causes require different treatment. Seek urgent assessment for severe diarrhoea, blood or abdominal pain; follow the plan your team gave you.

My blood pressure is high at home.

Repeat it as your team instructed and report the reading and symptoms to oncology. Severe headache, confusion, chest pain, weakness or visual change with high BP needs urgent assessment. Do not alter prescribed BP medicines on your own.

I need a dental extraction or surgery.

Tell the treating team and the dentist or surgeon that you receive bevacizumab before the procedure. Bevacizumab may delay wound healing. Your team decides how treatment and procedure timing should be arranged.

I finished immunotherapy months ago. Can it still be involved?

Yes. Immune effects can develop after stopping. Tell any healthcare professional when your last atezolizumab dose was and keep the alert card or passport available.

Is this chemotherapy? Do I need routine anti-sickness tablets?

This exact pair is immunotherapy plus anti-VEGF treatment, without cytotoxic chemotherapy. The NCCP calls both minimally emetogenic and does not usually require premedication. Follow only your own medicine list.

07 / take it to the day unit

My cycle symptom passport

Print the eight-page passport: one setup page, six pages for cycles 1–6, and a reusable blank page for further cycles. Each cycle page covers days 1–7, 8–14 and 15–21. Record the worst symptom in each week, then write dates, stool counts, BP readings, how it affected you and advice received.

Call first; write later. New bleeding, black stools, chest pain, breathlessness, diarrhoea or another worrying change needs your oncology team’s advice when it happens. The score is a record for conversation, never a reason to delay help.
