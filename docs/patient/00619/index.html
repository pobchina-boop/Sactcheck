01 / treatment identity

What this treatment is doing

Abemaciclib with endocrine therapy for selected adults with HR-positive, HER2-negative, node-positive early breast cancer at high risk of recurrence.

Abemaciclib

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

Endocrine therapy

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

Regimen-specific point
Because this is continuous oral treatment, early diarrhoea management, medication-interaction review and the first months of blood monitoring are central to safe treatment.
02 / treatment journey

How the schedule fits together

Every day

Abemaciclib 150 mg twice daily, with or without food, at approximately the same times each day unless your dose has been changed.

Alongside it

Continue the endocrine treatment prescribed for you.

First 2 months

Blood count, kidney/liver and bone profile are checked every 2 weeks.

Next 2 months

These tests are usually monthly, then as clinically indicated.

Treatment duration

Abemaciclib is taken continuously for 2 years or until progression/unacceptable toxicity, according to the NCCP source and your treatment plan.

A delay, dose change or omitted medicine can be appropriate. Your team should explain what changed and why.

03 / visual side-effect guide

Where changes may appear

This larger body map is a memory aid. The cards below are separated by the medicines or treatment phase that make each risk relevant. A picture cannot diagnose the cause of a symptom: report new or worsening changes to your oncology team.

Abemaciclib

Diarrhoea

Can begin early. Follow the team’s diarrhoea plan, maintain fluids and contact oncology for persistent/severe symptoms or dehydration.

Low neutrophils / infection

Fever, shivering or sudden illness needs urgent oncology contact.

Venous blood clot (VTE)

One-sided leg swelling/pain, sudden breathlessness or chest pain needs urgent assessment.

Lung inflammation (ILD/pneumonitis)

New or worsening cough or breathlessness needs prompt assessment.

Liver toxicity

Liver blood tests are monitored; jaundice, dark urine or marked worsening fatigue should be reported.

Endocrine therapy

Agent-specific effects

The endocrine medicine may be an aromatase inhibitor or tamoxifen depending on the plan. Hot flushes, joint symptoms, vaginal symptoms and bone-health issues vary by agent.

Do not assume all risks are identical

Ask which endocrine agent you are taking and use its medicine-specific information alongside this portal.

This is a regimen-specific safety summary, not an exhaustive adverse-effect list. Follow the current treatment plan, medicine leaflets and your oncology team’s SOS instructions.

04 / monitoring and support

What helps make treatment safer

Before and during treatment

  • Start anti-diarrhoeal treatment at the first sign of loose stools exactly as advised by your oncology team.
  • Swallow tablets whole; do not chew, split or crush.
  • Avoid grapefruit/grapefruit juice. If a dose is missed or vomited, take the next dose at the scheduled time rather than an extra dose.

Bring to each review

  • Your current medicines, including non-prescription products.
  • A note of new symptoms, when they started and what they stopped you doing.
  • Any temperature, blood-pressure or bowel-count readings your team asked you to record.
  • Questions about the purpose of treatment, scan/review timing or changes to the plan.
05 / when to call

Do not wait for the next appointment

Use your oncology SOS instructionsCall oncology urgently for fever, persistent or severe diarrhoea, dehydration, new breathlessness, one-sided leg swelling, jaundice, significant bleeding or marked weakness. Severe chest pain or breathing difficulty: call 112/999.

If your team has given you different local thresholds or emergency instructions, follow those instructions.
