When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call urgently for blistering or peeling skin, mouth sores with rash or fever, severe muscle pain or weakness, or dark urine. Seek immediate advice about suspected pregnancy or exposure. Report taste change, reduced eating and weight loss early so support can start.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Medicines in this regimen

Vismodegib is used to help control advanced basal cell skin cancer when the source-specific criteria are met. Discuss whether the aim is shrinkage, symptom relief, longer disease control or another agreed goal, and how surgery or radiotherapy has been considered.

Oral targeted capsule

Vismodegib

Erivedge
Blocks the Hedgehog growth pathway

Vismodegib blocks Smoothened, a protein in the Hedgehog signalling pathway. Abnormal activity in this pathway can drive basal cell cancer. Blocking it can slow tumour growth, while effects on normal tissues help explain cramps, taste changes and hair loss.

Keep four-week blood tests and the pregnancy-prevention programme. Report cramps, loss of strength, reduced intake, weight loss and severe skin symptoms.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Taking or receiving treatment

Swallow the prescribed capsule whole with about 200 mL of water, with or without food, at the same time each day. Do not open, crush or chew it. Keep it in its labelled pack, away from children, and return unused capsules to the pharmacy.

STEP 2

Missed, vomited or held treatment

If a dose is missed, skip it and take the next dose at the usual time. Do not double up. If you vomit after taking it, ask the oncology team or pharmacist before taking more; do not assume that the dose needs replacing.

STEP 3

Appointments and supply

Plan blood tests, travel and medicine collection in advance. If you need an interpreter or someone to help at home, tell the team before the appointment.

STEP 4

Paper copies and contact information

Ask for your latest schedule and contact details on paper. Replace old versions when treatment changes so everyone helping you uses the same plan.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Formal pregnancy-prevention programme

The programme materials, supervised tests, contraceptive methods and after-treatment restrictions must be agreed and documented before the first dose; they apply even during a treatment pause.

An individual symptom plan

Ask which anti-sickness or other symptom medicines are prescribed, when to take them and when to call if they do not help. Do not start a borrowed medicine or alter treatment to manage a side effect yourself.

Your urgent contacts

Keep your actual oncology, out-of-hours and pharmacy contacts on the wallet card. Example numbers on a template must be replaced before you use it.

Pregnancy and fertility precautions

Vismodegib can cause severe harm to an unborn baby. Women who could become pregnant need the formal pregnancy-prevention programme, including two recommended contraceptive methods (one highly effective method plus a barrier method), unless the agreed alternative is continuous abstinence, during treatment and for 24 months after stopping. Pregnancy tests are supervised before treatment and monthly, even with absent periods. Men must use a condom, with spermicide if available, during sex with a female partner during treatment and for two months afterwards, including after a vasectomy. Report a suspected patient or partner pregnancy immediately. Do not breastfeed or donate blood during treatment or for 24 months afterwards; do not donate semen during treatment or for two months afterwards. Ask the team to explain and document the full programme; this summary does not replace its materials.

Eating, activity and practical help

Let the team know if cramps, altered taste or poor appetite make eating or moving difficult. Ask for practical food and activity advice that fits your symptoms and any other illness.

Treatment reference · 01

Understanding your diagnosis and treatment goal

Use these questions with your own pathology report and agreed treatment plan.

Confirm the diagnosis and extent

Basal cell carcinoma starts in the skin. It usually grows locally; spread to distant organs is uncommon. Your team assesses the affected area and whether surgery or radiotherapy can treat it.

Why tablets are being considered

Vismodegib is used in the advanced basal cell carcinoma settings specified in your treatment protocol. Ask the team to explain why this medicine is suitable and why an operation or radiotherapy alone is not the agreed option.

Record earlier local treatment

Record previous operations, radiotherapy and other treatment to the same area. These details help the team plan further treatment and interpret changes in the skin.

Inherited conditions and multiple cancers

Several basal cell cancers at a young age or a suggestive family history may warrant a genetics assessment. Gorlin syndrome is one possible inherited cause. Testing is selected for the person; it is not required for every skin cancer.

A visible lesion is only one part of assessment

The team may measure or photograph a visible lesion and examine deeper tissue or lymph nodes. Scans are used when they answer a specific question. A photograph alone cannot show the full extent of disease.

Treatment reference · 03

Taking treatment and preparing for visits

Keep the written medicine plan beside this guide; it records your own dose and dates.

Taking or receiving the medicine

Swallow the prescribed capsule whole with about 200 mL of water, with or without food, at the same time each day. Do not open, crush or chew it. Keep it in its labelled pack, away from children, and return unused capsules to the pharmacy.

Missed, vomited or held treatment

If a dose is missed, skip it and take the next dose at the usual time. Do not double up. If you vomit after taking it, ask the oncology team or pharmacist before taking more; do not assume that the dose needs replacing.

Other medicines and supplements

Show the pharmacist a current list of prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, herbal products and supplements. Include anticoagulants, heart medicines, steroids and recent antibiotics. Check before adding, stopping or changing anything; an apparently harmless product can alter treatment or confuse a symptom assessment.

Practical preparation

Bring your medicine list, allergy information and questions. Tell the team about practical problems with travel, swallowing, wound care or remembering doses before treatment starts.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests: what is required and what each result means

Safety tests, tumour tests and response scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests; medicine review and pregnancy-prevention programme assessment for women and men. If pregnancy is possible, a supervised pregnancy test is needed within seven days before starting.

During treatment

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests every four weeks. Review cramps, strength, taste, eating, weight, hair/skin changes and treatment tolerance. Pregnancy testing is monthly when applicable, even if periods stop.

Scans and tumour markers

Ask the team to write the next assessment and who will arrange it. Bloods used to monitor kidneys, liver, hormones or muscle are not automatically measures of cancer response. There is no universal tumour-marker blood schedule for these skin-cancer regimens; a scan or extra blood test is requested for a specific clinical reason.

After a pause or finish

Agree continuing clinical/skin review, any response imaging and support for ongoing effects. The pregnancy-prevention, donation and breastfeeding restrictions continue after the last dose for their specified periods.

Getting the result

Keep a named contact for bookings and results. If an expected appointment or result is missing, call that service; do not take silence as confirmation that everything is complete.

Treatment reference · 05

What to expect between visits

You can feel well and still need the planned checks. New symptoms deserve attention even on a day without an appointment.

Your usual pattern

Record the start and change in symptoms, temperature if advised, bowel pattern, drinking, urine and the effect on normal tasks. A simple few-line record is enough. Use it to describe a change rather than trying to grade or diagnose a toxicity yourself.

When to call

Call urgently for blistering or peeling skin, mouth sores with rash or fever, severe muscle pain or weakness, or dark urine. Seek immediate advice about suspected pregnancy or exposure. Report taste change, reduced eating and weight loss early so support can start.

Eating, drinking and activity

Report nausea, mouth discomfort, diarrhoea or weight loss before they make eating and drinking difficult. The team can adjust symptom support and arrange dietitian or rehabilitation advice.

A held dose and a new phase

If a dose is missed, skip it and take the next dose at the usual time. Do not double up. If you vomit after taking it, ask the oncology team or pharmacist before taking more; do not assume that the dose needs replacing.

Treatment reference · 06

Skin checks, sun protection and optional photographs

This complements clinical assessment and does not replace it.

Protect your skin

Protect exposed skin with clothing, a hat and shade. Use broad-spectrum sunscreen with sun protection factor (SPF) 30 or higher and reapply as directed. Avoid sunbeds. Cloudy weather does not remove ultraviolet exposure.

Know the pattern of your skin

Ask the team to show you how to check your skin and treated area. Use a mirror or a helper for areas you cannot see. Report a new lump, a changing lesion or a sore that does not heal.

Photographs are optional

A private, dated photograph can help describe a change if your team finds it useful. Keep the lighting and size reference consistent. Photographs support a discussion; they do not replace examination and need not be uploaded.

Wounds, nails and sensitive skin

Follow the wound and skin-care instructions for the treated area. Ask before adding a cream or remedy. Increasing redness, discharge, pain, bleeding or difficulty using the area needs assessment.

Treatment reference · 07

Pregnancy, fertility and protecting others

Discuss these precautions before treatment even if pregnancy is not currently planned.

Medicine-specific precautions

Vismodegib can cause severe harm to an unborn baby. Women who could become pregnant need the formal pregnancy-prevention programme, including two recommended contraceptive methods (one highly effective method plus a barrier method), unless the agreed alternative is continuous abstinence, during treatment and for 24 months after stopping. Pregnancy tests are supervised before treatment and monthly, even with absent periods. Men must use a condom, with spermicide if available, during sex with a female partner during treatment and for two months afterwards, including after a vasectomy. Report a suspected patient or partner pregnancy immediately. Do not breastfeed or donate blood during treatment or for 24 months afterwards; do not donate semen during treatment or for two months afterwards. Ask the team to explain and document the full programme; this summary does not replace its materials.

Planning ahead

Ask early about fertility preservation, sexual health and symptoms affecting intimacy. Tell the team about any chance of pregnancy, breastfeeding or a partner’s pregnancy so that the correct medicine-specific advice can be given. Keep the precautions and their end dates in the written plan.

Safe handling and supply

Do not share anticancer medicines. Keep oral medicines in their original labelled packaging, away from children and anyone who could take them accidentally. Return unused medicines to the supplying pharmacy and follow its handling advice. A treatment hold does not remove pregnancy precautions.

Testing and medicine supply

When pregnancy is possible, the initial prescription and dispensing must occur by day seven, counting the pregnancy-test date as day one. Supplies are limited to 28 days and need a renewed prescription. Ask the team to coordinate supervised monthly testing and supply so that these safeguards are maintained.

Evidence in advanced basal cell carcinoma

ERIVANCE studied vismodegib without a comparison treatment. At an analysis 21 months after the last person joined the study, independent reviewers found a confirmed response in 11 of 33 people with metastatic basal cell carcinoma (BCC) (33.3%; 95% CI 19.2-51.8%) and 30 of 63 with locally advanced BCC (47.6%; 95% CI 35.5-60.6%). The analysis date does not mean everyone took treatment for 21 months.

A response means that the study's cancer-response criteria were met. The assessment combined scans for metastatic disease and lesion measurements, ulcer healing and biopsy information for locally advanced disease. Response is not the same as cure and does not prove longer survival compared with another treatment.
