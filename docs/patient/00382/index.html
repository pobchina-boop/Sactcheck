01 / treatment identity

What this treatment is doing

Oral treatment for previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer when this NCCP regimen is appropriate.

Trifluridine/tipiracil (Lonsurf)

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

Regimen-specific point
The active treatment days and the rest days are both part of the 28-day cycle. A calendar or Treatment Passport can help prevent dosing on the wrong day.

03 / visual side-effect guide

Where changes may show up

The body map is a memory aid, not a prediction. A symptom can have more than one cause, so report new or worsening changes rather than trying to diagnose the cause yourself.

Blood + infection

Low white cells are a major risk. Fever, shivers or suddenly feeling unwell requires immediate oncology contact.

Stomach + bowel

Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea or reduced appetite can occur. Persistent symptoms can cause dehydration.

Mouth

Mouth soreness can affect eating and drinking. Report ulcers that limit intake.

This regimen-focused illustration anchors the treatment site. The symptom cards show other body systems to discuss. It is not a prediction of which effects you will experience.
Fatigue

Tiredness is common; a sudden or marked change can also reflect anaemia, infection or dehydration.

Skin

Rash or other skin changes should be reported if persistent or severe.

Home medicines

Keep the tablets in their original packaging and follow the handling/storage instructions from your oncology pharmacy.

04 / monitoring and support

What helps make treatment safer

Before and during treatment

  • Take doses after the morning and evening meals exactly as prescribed.
  • Do not double a dose for a missed or vomited dose; follow your oncology/pharmacy instructions.
  • Blood counts are particularly important because neutropenia can be significant.

Bring to each review

  • Your current medicines, including non-prescription products.
  • A note of new symptoms, when they started and what they stopped you doing.
  • Any temperature, blood-pressure or bowel-count readings your team asked you to record.
  • Questions about the purpose of treatment, scan/review timing or changes to the plan.
05 / when to call

Do not wait for the next appointment

Use your oncology SOS instructionsCall oncology urgently for fever, shivers, sudden illness, uncontrolled vomiting, persistent diarrhoea, bleeding, inability to drink or marked weakness. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or severe chest pain: call 112/999.

If your team has given you different local thresholds or emergency instructions, follow those instructions.
