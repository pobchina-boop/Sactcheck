Understand your treatment

How your medicines work

CLV combines three chemotherapy medicines after craniospinal radiotherapy for selected adults with high-risk medulloblastoma or another eligible embryonal tumour. It is part of a specialist treatment plan; it is not a glioma regimen.

Oral chemotherapy capsule

Lomustine

CCNU
Damages deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)

Lomustine is an oral nitrosourea chemotherapy that can reach the brain. It damages DNA and interferes with cell division. Its effects on healthy blood-forming cells can appear several weeks after the dose and can build up over successive cycles. This explains the long gap between doses and the need for continuing blood tests.

One scheduled dose↓DNA damaged↓Delayed blood-count effects possible

What to watch for
This is one dose on Day 1, not a daily medicine. If an extra dose is taken, get urgent advice even if you feel well. Keep blood tests after the dose and report fever, bruising, bleeding, new cough or breathlessness.

Chemotherapy infusion into a vein

Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Forms DNA links that disrupt cell division

Cisplatin forms chemical links within DNA, making it harder for cells to copy and repair their genetic material. It can damage tumour cells but also affect kidney function, hearing, nerves and blood-forming cells. Fluids, electrolyte checks and anti-sickness medicines are part of the treatment plan to reduce avoidable harm.

Platinum enters cells↓DNA links form↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Report ringing or reduced hearing, numbness or weakness, reduced urine, persistent vomiting or difficulty drinking. Tell the team about fluid restrictions or heart and kidney conditions before hydration.

Chemotherapy given into a vein

Vincristine

Vincristine infusion
Disrupts the machinery used for cell division

Vincristine interferes with structures that cells use when dividing. It can also affect nerves controlling sensation, strength and bowel movement. This explains why tingling, weakness and constipation need early review even if blood tests are satisfactory.

Infusion into a vein↓Cell division disrupted↓Nerves and bowel may be affected

What to watch for
Report tingling, weakness, walking difficulty or constipation before the next dose. Tell staff immediately if the infusion site stings, burns or swells.

Complete timing and symptom notes

Day 1: Three medicines and hydration

Lomustine once by mouth, cisplatin and vincristine by infusion. Day 1 includes the individual fluid and anti-sickness plan. Confirm hearing, kidney, bowel and nerve symptoms before treatment.

Day 2–15: Vincristine visits on Days 8 and 15

There are no more scheduled lomustine or cisplatin doses in this cycle. Keep the bowel plan and report tingling or weakness. A held dose needs a new team decision; do not replace it yourself.

Day 16–42: Recovery and continuing tests

Counts can fall after infusion visits finish. Keep weekly lomustine blood-count checks and the team’s other appointments. Call for fever, bleeding, new respiratory symptoms or difficulty drinking.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Taking the treatment

Take lomustine only once on the authorised Day 1. The pharmacist must confirm the total dose, capsule strengths, how to take it and anti-sickness timing. Do not open or crush capsules or take another dose to replace a missed or vomited one without advice. Cisplatin and vincristine are given into a vein. Day 1 includes fluids before and after cisplatin; later visits on Days 8 and 15 are for vincristine. The total visit is longer than the infusion itself.

STEP 2

Missed, vomited or held medicine

If a capsule dose is missed or vomited, call the oncology team or pharmacist before taking more. Do not double up or use leftover capsules. An infusion or capsule held for toxicity needs a new team decision.

STEP 3

Medicine and supplement review

Bring all prescription, over-the-counter and herbal medicines, vitamins and supplements to review. Include steroids, seizure medicines, anticoagulants and any recent antibiotics or antifungals. Tell the team before starting or stopping a medicine.

STEP 4

Capsule supply and handling

Lomustine is supplied as an unlicensed medicine in this NCCP protocol. Confirm pharmacy supply in advance. Keep the labelled pack away from children and do not open or crush capsules. Return unused chemotherapy to the pharmacy; contact staff if swallowing or handling is difficult.

STEP 5

Pregnancy, breastfeeding and fertility

Discuss fertility preservation and contraception before starting. Treatment can harm a pregnancy. Do not breastfeed during treatment. Ask for the written medicine-specific precautions and the required periods after stopping; these may differ between products.

STEP 6

Appointments and transport

Before starting: Blood count, kidney/liver tests; hearing assessment and creatinine clearance when clinically indicated; NCCP lung testing for patients at high risk. Current lomustine SmPC additionally advises baseline lung-function assessment. Current cisplatin product information advises baseline audiometry. Confirm recovery after RT and the individual hydration/electrolyte plan. During treatment: Regular blood count, kidney/liver tests, with hearing and lung assessment when indicated. Current lomustine SmPC advises weekly blood counts for at least six weeks after a dose and continuing lung assessment. Current cisplatin product information additionally advises weekly kidney/liver tests, blood counts and electrolytes through treatment, with repeat hearing testing for symptoms. Review nerves and bowel function before vincristine. Brain/spine imaging and any other response tests follow the individual specialist plan; no universal scan or tumour-marker timetable is set here.

STEP 7

Help with the daily plan

Agree help with prescriptions, travel, calls and symptom notes if fatigue, memory, sight or weakness makes these difficult. Your preferences guide who is involved. Keep the printed plan and real urgent contacts together.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Anti-sickness plan checked for interactions

Cisplatin has high emetic potential; lomustine adds oral moderate-to-high risk. Use the team’s prevention and rescue plan, including delayed nausea after Day 1. Aprepitant and some antifungals interact with vincristine, so the oncology pharmacist must select and check the plan; do not add medicines yourself.

Hydration and electrolytes

Cisplatin needs fluids before and after treatment and an individual electrolyte plan. Tell the team about fluid limits, swelling, heart or kidney conditions, vomiting and reduced urine. Follow your own home-drinking advice rather than a universal volume.

Prevent constipation and monitor nerves

NCCP recommends preventive laxatives with vincristine. Report constipation, new tingling, weakness, balance or hand-function changes before each visit. Severe abdominal pain, vomiting or inability to pass stool or wind needs urgent assessment.

Medicines, infection prevention and recovery

Have all medicines checked, including seizure drugs, steroids, antibiotics, antifungals, non-prescription products and supplements. The team will confirm the local antifungal prevention plan and choose medicines that avoid vincristine interactions. Other infection prevention follows the individual specialist plan.

Your treatment and its purpose

Keep the exact diagnosis, regimen and treatment goal together.

Why this treatment?

CLV combines three chemotherapy medicines after craniospinal radiotherapy for selected adults with high-risk medulloblastoma or another eligible embryonal tumour. It is part of a specialist treatment plan; it is not a glioma regimen.

What each medicine does

Lomustine: Lomustine is an oral nitrosourea chemotherapy that can reach the brain. It damages DNA and interferes with cell division. Its effects on healthy blood-forming cells can appear several weeks after the dose and can build up over successive cycles. This explains the long gap between doses and the need for continuing blood tests. Cisplatin: Cisplatin forms chemical links within DNA, making it harder for cells to copy and repair their genetic material. Fluids, electrolyte checks and anti-sickness medicines are part of the treatment plan to reduce avoidable harm. Vincristine: Vincristine interferes with structures that cells use when dividing. It can also affect nerves controlling sensation, strength and bowel movement. This explains why tingling, weakness and constipation need early review even if blood tests are satisfactory.

Your schedule

Start four to six weeks after craniospinal radiotherapy, once cleared by your team. In each 42-day cycle, lomustine is taken once on Day 1, cisplatin is infused on Day 1, and vincristine is infused on Days 1, 8 and 15. Eight cycles are planned, with individual safety review.

Evidence and uncertainty

The treatment strategy has a role in specialist multimodal care, but paediatric outcomes cannot be used as an adult survival estimate or applied to every embryonal tumour. The cited childhood study does not establish a numerical benefit for this exact adult high-risk population. Medulloblastoma and other embryonal tumours have different molecular and clinical contexts. Glioma trials and the uploaded glioma guide do not establish CLV efficacy. The neuro-oncology team must explain the evidence, uncertainties, treatment burden and alternatives for the individual diagnosis.

Consent and decisions

Discuss the exact diagnosis, treatment goal, evidence limits, alternatives and burdens; medicine-specific risks, monitoring, fertility and pregnancy precautions; any unlicensed supply or off-label use; and practical support. This schedule follows craniospinal RT and needs its own consent discussion.

Treatment reference · 04

Understanding your particular brain tumour

CLV here is for selected adult high-risk medulloblastoma or another eligible embryonal tumour; it is not a glioma regimen.

The exact diagnosis matters

Medulloblastoma is an embryonal tumour that usually starts in the cerebellum. The NCCP regimen also names other primitive neuro-ectodermal tumours, including pinealoblastoma. Ask the specialist to record the current pathology name and explain any older wording. These diagnoses are not interchangeable with glioblastoma or other gliomas.

Risk group and spread

The team considers the exact tumour, what remains after surgery and whether it has spread within the brain, spine or cerebrospinal fluid. Ask what makes your tumour high risk and which results establish that. A general glioma grade explanation does not replace medulloblastoma risk assessment.

Molecular results

Medulloblastoma can be classified by molecular findings, including WNT, SHH and non-WNT/non-SHH groups. Ask which tests apply to your exact tumour and how they affect discussion. Molecular tests used for another tumour type are not automatically relevant to this diagnosis. If a result is pending or not applicable, record that clearly.

How the treatments fit together

Surgery obtains tissue and removes tumour where safely possible. Craniospinal radiotherapy treats planned areas of the brain and spine. This NCCP CLV schedule starts four to six weeks after craniospinal RT, after recovery and safety review. The team confirms the role of each part for your diagnosis.

A useful diagnosis record

Keep the pathology date and wording, risk group and its reasons, molecular results if relevant, surgery and RT dates, brain/spine scan findings and any cerebrospinal-fluid assessment. Ask who will explain pending results. Use this record to understand decisions, not to determine your own chemotherapy eligibility.

Treatment reference · 05

Tests, scans and recovery after treatment

Safety tests and tumour-response tests have different purposes.

Bloods, kidneys and hydration

Blood counts check marrow recovery; kidney and liver tests help assess whether treatment is safe. Cisplatin also needs an individual hydration and electrolyte plan. Tell staff about fluid restrictions, swelling, heart or kidney problems and difficulty drinking. Do not use a generic home fluid target in place of your plan. Current cisplatin product information advises weekly blood-count, kidney/liver and electrolyte checks through treatment.

Hearing, nerves and lung assessment

Cisplatin can affect hearing and nerves; vincristine can affect nerves and bowel function; lomustine can affect the lungs. Tell the team about changes before every visit. NCCP lists hearing and lung tests according to clinical need, while current lomustine product information advises baseline and continuing lung assessment. Ask which checks are booked for you. Current cisplatin product information advises a hearing test before treatment and repeat testing if hearing symptoms develop.

Brain and spine review

Medulloblastoma may involve the brain, spine or cerebrospinal fluid. Your specialist decides which imaging or other tests are needed at each stage. Keep the next scan date and result-review appointment in your passport. This guide does not set a universal scan, lumbar-puncture or blood tumour-marker timetable.

Delayed effects and rehabilitation

Symptoms may change after surgery, RT or chemotherapy. Hearing, balance, strength, memory, fatigue and daily function deserve follow-up as well as scans. Ask which longer-term checks and rehabilitation are relevant after your particular treatment. Report new changes promptly rather than waiting for a routine appointment.

Keep results with decisions

Record when a test is booked, completed and explained, and the next agreed step. A normal result from one test does not replace another medicine-safety check. The team authorises treatment after considering symptoms, current results and earlier side effects.

Treatment reference · 06

Brain symptoms, medicines and when to call

Symptoms may reflect the tumour, swelling, treatment, another medicine or a separate illness.

Know your usual symptoms

Before treatment, note your usual walking, strength, speech, sight, memory, sleep and seizure pattern. Record new changes and their timing. A trusted person can help if you want them involved.

Urgent neurological changes

Fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell needs urgent advice, including on treatment rest days. Call 112 or 999 for a new seizure, sudden weakness or speech difficulty, loss of consciousness, severe breathing difficulty or uncontrolled bleeding. Follow the emergency instructions in any established seizure rescue plan.

Steroids need a separate written plan

Steroids may reduce swelling around a brain tumour. If needed, ask for a written dose, review date and reduction plan; do not stop suddenly or adjust the dose yourself. Report infection symptoms, marked thirst, weakness or troublesome sleep or mood changes.

Seizure medicines and rescue instructions

Take prescribed seizure medicine on its own schedule. Ask what to do after a missed or vomited dose and how to manage a breakthrough seizure. Do not switch or stop it yourself. Anyone giving a rescue medicine needs the personal instructions and training.

Safety at home and outside

Discuss driving, work, bathing, swimming, heights and machinery with your team, taking account of symptoms and seizures. Individual medical and licensing advice is needed. Occupational therapy can help make daily routines safer.

Treatment reference · 07

Your overall treatment plan and choices

Treatment should account for the tumour and the life you want to lead.

Understand the sequence

Place biopsy or surgery, radiotherapy, medicines and follow-up on one plan. Concurrent means treatments overlap; adjuvant means treatment follows the main treatment. A new phase can require different doses, tests and support medicines.

Agree what treatment is trying to achieve

Ask what treatment can reasonably achieve for your tumour and which effects matter most to you. Discuss benefits, burdens, alternatives and the reasons for a pause or stop.

Put evidence in context

A study result applies to a particular group, diagnosis and schedule. Older studies may use different tumour names. Ask which evidence fits your plan; an average cannot forecast your response or lifespan.

Discuss options available to you

Ask about suitable trials, another specialist opinion and other treatment or symptom-care options. Your multidisciplinary team explains what is appropriate and available for you.

Make room for your priorities

Bring up thinking, independence, family time, work and the side effects you most want to avoid. Ask for time, written notes or a supporter if that helps you weigh the choices.

Treatment reference · 08

Living well, rehabilitation and support

Practical and symptom support can begin alongside anticancer treatment.

Thinking, communication and daily tasks

Tell the team about changes in memory, language, attention, sight or planning. Rehabilitation can involve physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy or psychology. Ask for practical help with routines and medicines.

Food, nausea, bowels and energy

Use your prescribed nausea and bowel plan and report problems early. Ask for dietitian help if intake or weight falls. Adapt movement to your balance and strength, and ask the team to assess fatigue that disrupts daily life.

Emotional support and carers

Tell the team about changes in mood, behaviour or coping. Patients and carers may each need psychological or practical support. Agree who can receive information and ask for help before caring becomes overwhelming.

Work, transport and finances

Tell the nurse or social worker about travel, employment, finances and caring responsibilities. Arrange practical help with medicines or appointments if symptoms make those tasks difficult.

Supportive care and future wishes

Supportive and palliative care can help with symptoms and quality of life while active treatment continues. You can discuss future care preferences and who you want involved in decisions at a time that suits you. Ask the team for advice from your team and documentation rather than using legal or hospice rules from a US booklet.

Information that works on paper

Keep the latest paper schedule, medicine list, results plan and emergency contacts together. The treatment card helps others identify your regimen. It supports, but does not replace, the prescription and emergency instructions.

CLV evidence: adult context and paediatric origins

The NCCP CLV regimen cites studies of cisplatin, lomustine and vincristine with radiotherapy in children with medulloblastoma.

The treatment strategy has a role in specialist multimodal care, but paediatric outcomes cannot be used as an adult survival estimate or applied to every embryonal tumour.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is my exact diagnosis and how does this regimen fit?

NCCP 00806: selected adults with high-risk medulloblastoma or another eligible primitive neuro-ectodermal tumour, including pinealoblastoma. Ask for the pathology, relevant molecular findings and the treatment goal in plain language.

Which medicines do I receive on which days?

Start four to six weeks after craniospinal radiotherapy, once cleared by your team. In each 42-day cycle, lomustine is taken once on Day 1, cisplatin is infused on Day 1, and vincristine is infused on Days 1, 8 and 15. Eight cycles are planned, with individual safety review.

Which tests must happen before the next treatment?

Before starting: Blood count, kidney/liver tests; hearing assessment and creatinine clearance when clinically indicated; NCCP lung testing for patients at high risk. Current lomustine SmPC additionally advises baseline lung-function assessment. Current cisplatin product information advises baseline audiometry. Confirm recovery after RT and the individual hydration/electrolyte plan. During treatment: Regular blood count, kidney/liver tests, with hearing and lung assessment when indicated. Current lomustine SmPC advises weekly blood counts for at least six weeks after a dose and continuing lung assessment. Current cisplatin product information additionally advises weekly kidney/liver tests, blood counts and electrolytes through treatment, with repeat hearing testing for symptoms. Review nerves and bowel function before vincristine. Brain/spine imaging and any other response tests follow the individual specialist plan; no universal scan or tumour-marker timetable is set here.

How do I handle a missed, vomited or held dose?

Contact the oncology team or pharmacist for medicine-specific instructions. Do not double a dose or replace treatment that was held for toxicity.

Who helps with steroids, seizures, hearing, memory or daily tasks?

Ask for written medicine plans and a named contact. Rehabilitation, practical support and a trusted helper may support the daily plan.

Which symptoms mean I should call now?

Fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell needs urgent advice, including on treatment rest days. Call 112 or 999 for a new seizure, sudden weakness or speech difficulty, loss of consciousness, severe breathing difficulty or uncontrolled bleeding. Follow the emergency instructions in any established seizure rescue plan. Call promptly for fever, bleeding, new cough or breathlessness, persistent vomiting or reduced urine. Report hearing, nerve or bowel changes before the next infusion.

When do we review whether treatment is helping?

Agree the next clinical and imaging review, what would count as benefit, how side effects will be weighed and what would lead to continuing, changing or stopping.

Try explaining the plan back: Can you show the day for each medicine, your next safety tests, the current support plan and whom to call for fever, new neurological symptoms or another urgent change?
