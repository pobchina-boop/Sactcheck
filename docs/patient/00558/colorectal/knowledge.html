When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology immediately for new breathlessness, persistent diarrhoea, severe tummy pain, yellow skin or eyes, much less urine, spreading or blistering rash, severe headache, marked weakness, confusion, unusual thirst or passing much more urine. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizure needs emergency care. Effects can begin after treatment ends.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Three medicines. Different jobs.

Pembrolizumab aims to control bowel cancer that has spread and has a particular DNA-repair feature, called MSI-H or dMMR. Your team discusses likely benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for you.

Immunotherapy

Pembrolizumab

Keytruda
Blocks an immune brake

Pembrolizumab is an antibody that blocks PD-1, a control switch on immune T cells. Cancer cells can use this switch to reduce immune attack. Blocking it can help the immune system recognise and attack cancer. The same increased immune activity can inflame healthy organs, which is why new symptoms need attention even after treatment ends.

Immune cell↓PD-1 blocked↓Cancer attacked

What to watch for
Report new breathing, bowel, skin, hormone, heart or nerve symptoms promptly; these may be immune inflammation.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Appointments

Pembrolizumab 400 mg is given over about 30 minutes on Day 1 every 6 weeks (42 days). The visit takes longer for assessment and preparation.

STEP 2

Course length

Continue while treatment helps and side effects remain acceptable. At 24 months, the team may consider stopping if there is a sustained response without progression; this is an individual review, not an automatic fixed final cycle.

STEP 3

Blood and thyroid appointments

Blood count, kidney/liver function, glucose and thyroid function at baseline. Blood count, kidney/liver and glucose before each 6-weekly cycle; thyroid every 3–6 weeks regardless of infusion interval; cortisol if indicated. Scans and markers follow the consultant plan.

STEP 4

Home medicines and records

There is no take-home chemotherapy pump. Keep your alert card, symptom record and medicine list. Discuss travel, work and transport needs.

STEP 5

After the final dose

Keep reporting new symptoms. Ask who follows persistent side effects and any hormone medicines.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Routine medicines

Routine premedication is not usually required. Pembrolizumab has minimal emetogenic potential; the team individualises anti-sickness treatment if needed.

Steroids and hormone replacement

If immune effects develop, the team may prescribe steroids or hormones. Follow the exact plan; do not stop steroids abruptly or restart pembrolizumab yourself. If treatment is paused because of an immune side effect, your team checks recovery and any steroid or hormone plan before deciding whether it can restart. Do not change steroids or restart treatment yourself.

Medicines and pregnancy

Bring prescription, non-prescription and herbal medicines. Discuss pregnancy prevention; effective contraception is required during pembrolizumab and for at least four months after the last dose. Do not breastfeed during treatment.

Treatment reference · 01

Your colorectal cancer and treatment aim

The tumour test and treatment setting matter.

MSI-H and dMMR

These tests identify a problem with the cancer’s DNA-repair system. They help identify colorectal cancers more likely to benefit from this immunotherapy. Ask whether genetics assessment is relevant to you.

Sources: nccp · smpc

First-line metastatic treatment

Pembrolizumab aims to control bowel cancer that has spread and has a particular DNA-repair feature, called MSI-H or dMMR. Your team discusses likely benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for you. First-line means the first systemic treatment for the metastatic setting.

Treatment reference · 02

How pembrolizumab works

It releases a brake on immune cells.

PD-1 blockade

Pembrolizumab is an antibody that blocks PD-1, a control switch on immune T cells. Cancer cells can use this switch to reduce immune attack. Blocking it can help the immune system recognise and attack cancer. The same increased immune activity can inflame healthy organs, which is why new symptoms need attention even after treatment ends.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Healthy tissues can be affected

The same immune activation can inflame the bowel, lungs, liver, kidneys, skin, hormone glands, heart, nerves or muscles. Some effects require hospital care, steroids or lasting hormone replacement.

Sources: nccp · smpc

A pause is a safety decision

Doses may be delayed or treatment stopped for immune effects. The standard approach is to hold or stop treatment, not simply to give a smaller dose.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Sources: nccp · smpc

Evidence for the schedule

KEYNOTE-177 tested 200 mg every three weeks. The 400 mg six-week schedule is supported by exposure bridging and product authorisation; it was not a separate randomised colorectal comparison.

Sources: keynote177 · pk-bridge · smpc

Treatment reference · 04

Tests and follow-up

Blood tests can identify a problem before symptoms appear.

Before starting

Blood count, kidney/liver function, glucose and thyroid function at baseline. Blood count, kidney/liver and glucose before each 6-weekly cycle; thyroid every 3–6 weeks regardless of infusion interval; cortisol if indicated. Scans and markers follow the consultant plan.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Scans and tumour markers

The consultant sets the disease-assessment plan. This regimen does not prescribe a universal CT or CEA interval. A scan schedule from a research study is not automatically your schedule.

Sources: nccp

During a hold and after stopping

Keep follow-up appointments and the agreed steroid or hormone plan. Continue reporting symptoms after the last dose; another clinician needs to know you have received immunotherapy.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Treatment reference · 05

Eating, energy and daily life

Report changes early.

Bowel changes

Report new or persistent diarrhoea, blood in stools or abdominal pain promptly. Do not treat ongoing diarrhoea as a minor stomach upset or rely on self-treatment without advice.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Energy and hormones

New marked fatigue, headache, dizziness, feeling unusually cold, unusual thirst or passing more urine may need hormone and glucose tests.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Medicines, pregnancy and feeding

Bring prescription, non-prescription and herbal medicines. Discuss pregnancy prevention; effective contraception is required during pembrolizumab and for at least four months after the last dose. Do not breastfeed during treatment.

Evidence in MSI-H/dMMR colorectal cancer

KEYNOTE-177 compared pembrolizumab 200 mg every three weeks with chemotherapy in previously untreated MSI-H/dMMR metastatic colorectal cancer.

The trial showed a longer time before cancer worsened with pembrolizumab. This is a group result, not a guarantee for one person; ask your team about benefits, risks and alternatives in your situation.

Sources: keynote177 · keynote177-final · pk-bridge
