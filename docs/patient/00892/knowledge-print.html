How this medicine supports your treatment

For eligible higher-risk HR-positive/HER2-negative early breast cancer, ribociclib is added to endocrine therapy to reduce recurrence risk.

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Ribociclib

Slows the cell-division checkpoint
CDK4/6 checkpoint↓Checkpoint inhibited↓Cell cycling slows

Ribociclib blocks CDK4 and CDK6, proteins that help cells move through the cycle of division. Endocrine therapy reduces the hormone signal that can drive HR-positive breast cancer.

Keep blood and electrocardiogram (ECG) appointments. Fever, jaundice, fainting, palpitations, severe rash or new breathlessness need prompt advice.

Endocrine partner

Aromatase inhibitor ± ovarian suppression

Reduces oestrogen signalling
Oestrogen pathway↓aromatase inhibitor (AI) or receptor blockade↓Growth signal reduced

The aromatase inhibitor reduces oestrogen production. Before menopause, ovarian suppression is also required; men receiving this AI combination need the appropriate LHRH-agonist plan under product information. The endocrine treatment is taken on a separate schedule and normally continues after ribociclib.

Discuss joint symptoms, hot flushes, sexual symptoms and bone health. Keep ovarian-suppression appointments if prescribed.

Your treatment aim

For eligible higher-risk HR-positive/HER2-negative early breast cancer, ribociclib is added to endocrine therapy to reduce recurrence risk.

Who this treatment is for

HR-positive/HER2-negative early breast cancer at high recurrence risk: stage IIA with qualifying nodal/high-risk factors, or stage IIB–III, as specified by NCCP. Current NCCP00892v2 includes selected node-negative eligibility and a conflicting node-negative exclusion; clinical reconciliation remains required.

NCCP 00892 v2 · official regimen

Schedule and duration

The usual adjuvant starting dose is ribociclib 400 mg once daily on Days 1–21, followed by Days 22–28 off, for up to three years. The aromatase inhibitor continues every day on its own plan; ovarian suppression is added when indicated. At completion of the planned three-year ribociclib course, continue the separately agreed endocrine and follow-up plan.

Checks before and during treatment

Baseline tests and ongoing tests have different purposes.

Before the first dose

full blood count (FBC), renal and liver profile; potassium, calcium, magnesium and phosphorus. ECG before initiation; QTcF must be below 450 ms. Correct electrolytes and review QT/CYP3A interactions. Confirm stage, recurrence-risk criteria, prior treatment, AI and ovarian-suppression plan; reconcile the NCCP node-negative eligibility/exclusion conflict before selection.

NCCP 00892 v2 · official regimen · EMA Kisqali current product information; checked 2026-10-25

During treatment

FBC, renal and liver profile every two weeks for cycles 1–2, then before cycles 3–6, then as clinically indicated. Electrolytes before each of the first six cycles, then as clinically indicated. Repeat ECG around cycle 1 Day 14, then as clinically indicated. Increase liver monitoring for grade 2 or worse abnormalities; review fever, jaundice, palpitations/fainting, breathlessness and severe skin symptoms. Keep early-breast surveillance and bone/endocrine follow-up distinct from treatment-toxicity checks and from metastatic response imaging.

Living with treatment

Supportive medicines and the endocrine partner have their own plan.

Nausea support

NCCP classifies ribociclib as oral moderate–high emetic risk. Agree preventive or rescue treatment under local policy after QT and CYP3A interaction review; no default QT-prolonging antiemetic is selected by this information card.

Heart rhythm and electrolytes

Attend the baseline and cycle 1 Day-14 ECG and scheduled electrolyte checks. Fainting, palpitations or severe dizziness need urgent assessment.

Blood and liver monitoring

Keep two-weekly blood tests during cycles 1–2 and pre-cycle tests during cycles 3–6. Report fever, jaundice, dark urine or new breathing/skin symptoms promptly.

AI and ovarian-suppression support

What the studies tell us

Ribociclib added to an aromatase inhibitor reduced the chance of invasive cancer returning in the study. Recent longer follow-up also reported a favorable survival finding, but that analysis was descriptive and needs that qualification. Your treatment team can explain how the study matches your recurrence risk.

How to interpret this

Study results describe groups with defined treatment and eligibility. Ask about your own likely benefits, important harms, alternatives and remaining uncertainty.

Questions to ask

How long does treatment last?

The usual adjuvant starting dose is ribociclib 400 mg once daily on Days 1–21, followed by Days 22–28 off, for up to three years. The aromatase inhibitor continues every day on its own plan; ovarian suppression is added when indicated. At completion of the planned three-year ribociclib course, continue the separately agreed endocrine and follow-up plan.

Do I need to enter dates?

No. This map shows general timing without personal information. Your hospital arranges actual appointments; ask who books each test and who reviews its result.

What if a dose is missed or treatment is held?

Take ribociclib once daily with or without food; swallow whole. Avoid grapefruit. If a dose is missed or vomited, do not take an extra dose; take the next scheduled dose. Confirm any clinical hold and restart with the team. The endocrine partner follows a separate prescription. A hold may affect one medicine rather than the whole combination; confirm each component.
