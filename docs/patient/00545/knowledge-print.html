Zoledronic acid · adjuvant breast treatment

How this medicine supports your treatment

Reduce recurrence risk as part of the agreed early-breast treatment plan.

Bone-modifying bisphosphonate

Zoledronic acid

Slows bone breakdown by osteoclasts
Binds to bone↓Osteoclast activity reduced↓Bone turnover slows

Zoledronic acid binds to bone and reduces the activity of cells that break bone down. In selected people with early breast cancer it forms part of the adjuvant plan. It does not replace surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy or endocrine treatment.

Report jaw pain or a wound that will not heal, reduced urine, muscle cramps, eye pain or new thigh/groin pain.

What each check is for

1Before treatmentBefore the first dose

Adjuvant breast cancer · postmenopausal (natural or treatment-induced). Before starting: renal profile, magnesium, calcium and phosphate. Check creatinine before every infusion; compare it with baseline as well as reviewing creatinine clearance. Correct low calcium and review vitamin D.

Ask / act: Confirm eligibility, test results and medicine list.

Sources: NCCP 00545

2Other checksAt the specified review points

Complete a dental assessment and preventive care before treatment where appropriate. Report dental symptoms; discuss invasive dental work with oncology and dental teams. Hydration is individualised, especially with heart failure.

Ask / act: Confirm who arranges and reviews each check.

Sources: NCCP 00545

3Support and practical planAt each treatment or phase change

Review renal function, calcium, vitamin D, dental health and hydration before each infusion.

Ask / act: Choose the matching support plan; confirm active medicines.

Sources: NCCP 00545

4Response and follow-upAgreed review points

After early-breast treatment, breast imaging and clinical review follow the agreed plan. Routine computed tomography (CT)/positron emission tomography (PET) scans or blood tumour markers are not used to search for distant recurrence in someone without symptoms or clinical concerns. Keep the agreed follow-up and report persistent or new symptoms between visits.

Ask / act: Review symptoms, results and patient priorities.

Practical support

Calcium and vitamin D

Follow the prescribed daily supplements; the team checks calcium and adjusts them if it is high or low.

Dental care

Keep routine dental care and tell the dentist about treatment. Report jaw pain or a wound that does not heal.

Flu-like symptoms

Aches or fever can occur after infusion. Use the agreed symptom plan; seek advice if severe or persistent.

Hydration

Follow the team’s fluid advice; heart or kidney problems may change it.

Discuss your own expected benefit, alternatives and uncertainty with the treating team.

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Report jaw pain, exposed bone or a non-healing mouth wound, reduced urine, tingling or muscle spasms, painful red eyes, or new thigh/groin pain.
