When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new breathlessness, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea, dehydration, jaundice, unusual bleeding or sudden illness promptly. Do not wait for the next planned test.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

Control the cancer and symptoms, with repeated review of benefit and tolerance.

Taxane chemotherapy

Docetaxel

Disrupts the cell’s division machinery

Docetaxel stabilises tiny structures called microtubules. Cells need these to separate their chromosomes during division; disrupting the process can stop cancer cells multiplying. Healthy dividing cells are also affected.

Microtubules stabilised↓Division disrupted↓Cell growth affected

What to watch for
Take prescribed steroid premedication. Report fever, lasting numbness, swelling, sore nails or infusion symptoms.

Sources: nccp

HER2-targeted antibody

Pertuzumab

Blocks HER2 pairing with other receptors

Pertuzumab binds a different area of HER2 from trastuzumab. It limits receptor pairing, so the two antibodies act together at different points on the same growth pathway.

HER2 pairing↓Pertuzumab blocks pairing↓Growth signals reduced

What to watch for
Report diarrhoea early, especially with poor fluid intake. Keep cardiac checks and report infusion symptoms immediately.

Sources: nccp

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

Targets HER2 growth signals

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

HER2 on cell surface↓Antibody binds↓Growth signalling reduced

What to watch for
Keep echo appointments throughout HER2 treatment. Report breathlessness, swelling, palpitations or a reaction; chemotherapy side effects are tracked separately.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

IV pertuzumab, IV trastuzumab and docetaxel every 21 days. Docetaxel duration is reviewed separately; the two HER2 antibodies may continue after chemotherapy stops while beneficial and tolerated.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm blood tests, heart assessment, active medicines, route and the consent discussion.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Staff check the line, give prescribed premedicines and treatment, and observe for reactions. Tell them immediately about burning, pain, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit time and transport

First visits can take longer because of loading doses, premedicines and observation. Ask the unit about expected time, transport and any escort needed after sedation or line insertion.

Phase-specific visit pattern

Infusion and observation times differ by drug and first versus later doses; the unit confirms the full visit duration, including blood review and preparation.

Treatment reference · 01

Why this treatment?

This combines docetaxel chemotherapy with two HER2-targeted antibodies for HER2-positive metastatic or unresectable locally recurrent breast cancer. The NCCP indication applies before chemotherapy or HER2 treatment for metastatic disease. The team considers earlier treatment, general health, heart function, symptoms and alternatives. Chemotherapy and later antibody-only maintenance are distinct phases.

Heart ultrasound and electrocardiogram (ECG)

Check heart pumping function before starting HER2 treatment. Repeat echo or MUGA every 12 weeks: around cycle 5, then cycle 9 if 21-day cycles stay on time. Earlier checks are needed for symptoms or abnormal findings. Count elapsed time from the first HER2 dose; changing phase or route does not restart the clock. Scans and tumour markers

Record baseline disease sites. Response imaging is generally reviewed every 2–4 months under international guidance, adapted to symptoms, disease pace and treatment. The team selects the scan; this is not an automatic CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order for every patient. Tumour markers may support assessment in selected cases, but do not alone decide progression or a treatment change.

Bone health and endocrine treatment

If the cancer is also hormone-receptor positive, endocrine treatment may form part of the wider plan. Questions worth asking

Who arranges my tests?

Ask the team who books each test, who checks the result and who to contact if an appointment is overdue. This map does not book appointments.

What if I become unwell between visits?

Use the urgent contact plan for sudden illness, fever, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea or poor fluid intake. Do not wait for a scheduled blood test or scan.

Why was this treatment chosen?

This combines docetaxel chemotherapy with two HER2-targeted antibodies for HER2-positive metastatic or unresectable locally recurrent breast cancer. When is my next heart scan?
