When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new cough or breathlessness, fever or sudden illness promptly. Tell staff immediately about an infusion reaction or pain at the infusion site. Painful palms / soles or mouth ulcers should be reported before the next dose.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

How this medicine works

The aim is to control the cancer and its symptoms. The team reviews benefit, side effects and your priorities before continuing.

Liposomal chemotherapy

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin

PLD · Caelyx
Damages cancer-cell deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)

Doxorubicin is carried inside tiny coated fat particles called liposomes. This changes how it circulates and its side-effect pattern. Heart injury remains possible; skin and mouth effects are especially important.

Liposomal carrier↓Doxorubicin reaches cells↓DNA damage disrupts division

What to watch for
Report painful palms / soles or mouth ulcers early. Keep heart-test appointments and report breathlessness, swelling or chest symptoms; tell staff immediately about an infusion reaction.

Practical treatment guide

Your infusion and access plan

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin on Day 1 every 28 days, typically for six cycles or until progression or unacceptable toxicity. The team confirms the intended duration and reviews benefit.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the indication, baseline bloods, required cardiac checks, medicines review and consent before the first infusion.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

The first infusion starts at 1 mg per minute. Later infusions may take 60 minutes if there was no reaction; a previous reaction requires a modified supervised schedule. Staff observe for reactions. Report burning, pain, swelling or leakage at the infusion site immediately.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit length

The first infusion starts at 1 mg per minute. Later infusions may take 60 minutes if there was no reaction; a previous reaction requires a modified supervised schedule. A port is optional access, not a requirement for a take-home pump. This regimen has no routine ambulatory pump.

Premedication

No routine premedicines are specified. Low-risk antiemetic support is separate. Infusion reactions may require a different supervised plan for future doses.

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines

Everyday care

Use skin care advised by your team; avoid heat, friction and tight shoes for 4–7 days after infusion. Report painful palms / soles or mouth ulcers early.

Tests and appointments

Ask who books each test, when it is due and who reviews the result. Tell the team if an expected appointment has not arrived.

Correct medicine and schedule

This is the pegylated liposomal formulation (for example Caelyx), not conventional doxorubicin or non-pegylated Myocet. These formulations and doses must not be interchanged.

Review the full medicine plan

Low emetic risk with an individual prevention/rescue plan; no routine hypersensitivity premedication is usually required. Reduce heat and friction affecting palms/soles; report painful redness, peeling or mouth ulcers before the next dose. Review cardiac symptoms and lifetime anthracycline exposure; a liposomal formulation still needs cardiac surveillance.

Treatment reference · 01

The exact treatment being discussed

The aim is to control the cancer and its symptoms. The team reviews benefit, side effects and your priorities before continuing.

Indication

Metastatic breast cancer; advanced ovarian cancer after failed first-line platinum chemotherapy; metastatic soft-tissue sarcoma; or recurrent / advanced endometrial cancer. Eligibility differs by cancer type.

Sources: nccp

Dose and formulation

50 mg/m². This is the pegylated liposomal formulation (for example Caelyx), not conventional doxorubicin or non-pegylated Myocet. These formulations and doses must not be interchanged.

Sources: nccp

Evidence limits

Comparable progression-free survival with less cardiotoxicity for PLD, while hand-foot syndrome and oral toxicity were more frequent. Overall survival was similar. Breast-trial findings do not establish efficacy in every NCCP 00205 cross-cancer indication. Normal cardiac function was required in this trial; lower cardiotoxicity does not mean cardiac safety in established heart disease or removal of monitoring. Do not interchange pegylated liposomal, non-pegylated liposomal and conventional doxorubicin.

Sources: trial

Treatment reference · 02

Checks throughout treatment

Ask who arranges each test and who reviews it.

Bloods and symptoms

FBC, renal and liver profile before starting and before each 28-day cycle. Check mouth soreness, palms / soles and recovery from earlier toxicity before each dose.

Sources: nccp

ECG and heart function

ECG is part of baseline and regular monitoring. The NCCP source advises frequent ECG monitoring; the team should specify when it is due rather than assuming an arbitrary cycle interval. Baseline echo / MUGA is required. Repeat during treatment as clinically indicated, and before each additional administration that exceeds a lifetime cumulative anthracycline exposure of 450 mg/m². Prior anthracyclines count; this threshold is not an automatic treatment stop.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Cancer response

For metastatic breast cancer, ESMO recommends response evaluation generally every 2–4 months, adjusted to disease, treatment and symptoms. Agree the scan type, due window and result-review appointment; symptoms can prompt earlier assessment. For the other cancer indications on this card, use the cancer-specific monitoring plan; the breast interval is not transferred automatically.

Sources: nccp · metastatic

Markers

Tumour markers, when useful for the cancer being treated, supplement clinical assessment and imaging. A marker result alone should not determine treatment failure or a treatment change.

Treatment reference · 04

How other care fits around chemotherapy

There is no automatic surgery, radiotherapy or maintenance phase in this card.

Surgery and radiation

These are advanced-disease regimens. Surgery or radiotherapy is used only for a defined local or symptom-control purpose. The oncology and radiation teams decide whether chemotherapy continues, pauses or precedes radiation; no routine overlap is implied.

Sources: metastatic

Bone health

dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (bone density) (DEXA) is not a routine test for this chemotherapy alone. Assess fracture risk and bone density when indicated by endocrine treatment, ovarian suppression or other clinical risks; repeat according to the relevant plan.

Sources: bone

Changing treatment

Discuss benefit, adverse effects, other treatment options and your preferences at each review. New symptoms can bring review forward.

Questions worth asking

Which treatment days and breaks should I expect?

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin on Day 1 every 28 days, typically for six cycles or until progression or unacceptable toxicity. The team confirms the intended duration and reviews benefit.

When do I need blood tests, an ECG or echo?

FBC, renal and liver profile before starting and before each 28-day cycle. Check mouth soreness, palms / soles and recovery from earlier toxicity before each dose. ECG is part of baseline and regular monitoring. The NCCP source advises frequent ECG monitoring; the team should specify when it is due rather than assuming an arbitrary cycle interval. Baseline echo / MUGA is required. Repeat during treatment as clinically indicated, and before each additional administration that exceeds a lifetime cumulative anthracycline exposure of 450 mg/m². Prior anthracyclines count; this threshold is not an automatic treatment stop.

When will we know whether treatment is helping?

For metastatic breast cancer, ESMO recommends response evaluation generally every 2–4 months, adjusted to disease, treatment and symptoms. Agree the scan type, due window and result-review appointment; symptoms can prompt earlier assessment. For the other cancer indications on this card, use the cancer-specific monitoring plan; the breast interval is not transferred automatically.

Where do surgery or radiotherapy fit?

These are advanced-disease regimens. Surgery or radiotherapy is used only for a defined local or symptom-control purpose. The oncology and radiation teams decide whether chemotherapy continues, pauses or precedes radiation; no routine overlap is implied.

Do tumour markers replace scans?

Tumour markers, when useful for the cancer being treated, supplement clinical assessment and imaging. A marker result alone should not determine treatment failure or a treatment change.

Does a port mean a pump at home?

No. A port is a route into a vein. Staff disconnect the infusion and remove the port needle after the visit when appropriate; removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure.
