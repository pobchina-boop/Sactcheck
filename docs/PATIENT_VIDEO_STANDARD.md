# SACTCheck patient video standard

Standing instruction from Paul O’Brien · 23November2026 · Version1

Every new or revised SACTCheck patient regimen film. Older films require an explicit gap review before being described as compliant.

Sections1–7 are mandatory in this exact order. Retain each named section even if a topic is not applicable, and explicitly explain non-applicability. Sections8–10 complete the patient safety and practical account. Never silently omit a section to meet a runtime target.

## 1. Medication names and treatment context

Say and display the trade name and full generic name of every anticancer medicine. State the exact regimen, indication, monotherapy or combination, and relevant treatment aim. Distinguish similarly named products and do not apply a film to another dose, indication or combination without an explicit source review.

## 2. Schedule and duration

Explain dose or dose basis where useful, treatment days, cycle length, breaks, repeats, planned duration or continuation/review criteria, and what a delay means. Separate induction, maintenance and oral partners. Use the actual authored regimen; do not infer a fixed course from blank calendar or passport spaces.

## 3. Checks, suitability and testing

Explain what is checked before the first dose, before each dose or cycle, at scheduled intervals, after a concern, and after treatment when applicable. Say why each check matters. Cover biomarkers, allergies, formal contraindications, regimen exclusions and relevant precautions without conflating them. Include medication review, pregnancy testing and reproductive advice when applicable. Preserve source-specific thresholds in the clinician material and explain their purpose in patient language.

## 4. Drug type and how it works

Name the drug class and explain the biological target, action and intended effect in plain language. Explain why healthy tissues can still be affected. An antibody-drug conjugate contains a cell-killing payload; do not describe it as checkpoint immunotherapy. For combinations explain each medicine and why they are combined.

## 5. What happens on the treatment day

Explain preparation, route and access, actual administration or infusion duration, how later visits may differ, and extra time for review or observation. Describe infusion-reaction symptoms and the instruction to tell the nurse immediately. Explain relevant cannula, port, pump, injection or oral handling details without implying an optional device is compulsory.

## 6. Support medicines and emetogenicity

Build this chapter from the exact generated, clinically reconciled regimen/phase supportive script. Name every prescribed scheduled and rescue medicine and explain its role, timing, duration and key practical precautions. Explain emetogenicity and the reason for prevention and delayed/breakthrough plans. Explain steroids, antiemetics and any other support, including a proton-pump inhibitor when present. Preserve local prescription additions. Separate routine, rescue and conditional medicines; do not add an unprescribed medicine or turn an optional medicine into an automatic order. Record source row and script hash. Explain that the patient follows their issued prescription, including any individual changes.

## 7. Common side effects and how to recognise them

Explain the relevant common effects with recognisable symptoms, practical self-care and a clear action threshold. Cover effects of each anticancer component and relevant support medicines. Distinguish common effects from less common serious effects. Use source-specific frequencies only with the population, dose and timepoint; do not mix pooled, trial or combination rates.

## 8. Urgent warning signs and contact plan

Give the regimen-specific serious symptoms, urgency and action. State when to contact the oncology team now and when to call112/999 in Ireland. Explain backup action when the team cannot be reached. Do not wait for the next appointment, scan or symptom diary. Show where the verified local daytime/out-of-hours numbers are written; never display example phone numbers as usable contacts. Include any official medicine alert card.

## 9. Practical care at home

Cover hydration, nutrition, fatigue/activity, mouth or skin care as relevant, infection precautions, new medicines and supplements, and treatment-specific travel/line/oral handling issues. Include contraception, pregnancy, breastfeeding and fertility advice when applicable, with accurate post-treatment durations. Do not invent interactions or universal restrictions.

## 10. Follow-up, patient tools and teach-back

Explain how response and toxicity follow-up differ, whom to contact after treatment stops, and delayed risks when relevant. Link the exact regimen portal, handbook, passport and official alert card. End with a brief recap: name and schedule, support medicines, warning signs, contact route and next checks. Invite questions and explain that the clinical team confirms the individual plan.

## Original Irish voice

Patrick — Engaging and Warm. Use the original approved narrator. No substitute narrator, synthetic clone of Paul, purchase or plan upgrade without authorization. Check the actual account licence and preserve any required attribution. If narration is unavailable, mark the film incomplete and retain the reviewed production package.

## Production requirements

- Keep clinical spelling on screen and in captions; pronunciation cues belong in the speech preparation only.
- Check trade and generic drug names, acronyms and trial names aloud before completing narration.
- Use a natural, calm pace. Extend or chapter the film rather than cut mandatory content.
- Captions must cover all speech, match the final narration and remain readable on a phone.
- Use restrained diagrams that explain mechanism and schedule; no unsupported promises or fabricated product screens.
- Export one clean narration track without clipped speech, repeated words, unexpected gaps, overlapping audio or loudness jumps. Listen to the final file across every chapter boundary.
- Verify the actual MP4, captions, transcript, poster and correct regimen portal. Do not publish a draft or a film with missing audio.

## Evidence and sign-off

- Freeze the regimen and generated supportive-script source before writing. Retain source URLs, versions, dates, page/table references and hashes.
- Map each section to the applicable sources and retain a complete clinical-to-script coverage checklist.
- Keep NCCP regimen rules, EMA/HPRA product information and local policy distinguishable. Broader licensed uses do not automatically extend an Irish regimen film.
- Automated completeness checks support, but do not replace, clinical, oncology-pharmacy and patient/PPI review. Record what is completed and what remains pending.
- Before release, verify all required sections, matching source content, pronunciation, audio, captions, visual/mobile readability, download/playback and regimen-specific placement.
