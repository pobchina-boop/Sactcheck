Act on symptoms, not the calendar

When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Palpitations, fainting, severe blistering or peeling rash needs urgent assessment. Report new numbness, weakness or difficulty walking before another dose.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Eribulin aims to control liposarcoma and may help symptoms. Your consultant considers the precise subtype, prior treatment, fitness and what matters to you.

Chemotherapy

Eribulin

Stops cell-division structures growing

Eribulin blocks the growth of microtubules, the small structures a cell needs to divide. It can slow liposarcoma growth but can also affect blood production and nerves.

Microtubules support division↓Microtubule growth is blocked↓Blood counts and nerves need review

What to watch for
Report fever or sudden illness immediately. Report numbness, weakness or difficulty walking before another dose; palpitations, fainting or a blistering rash need urgent assessment.

The drawings are simplified explanations of drug action, not anatomical pictures or a prediction of response.

Your treatment

The schedule

Eribulin 1.23 mg/m², expressed as eribulin base, is given on days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle. The team reviews safety before each dose. Treatment continues while benefit and tolerability justify it.

How it is given

National Cancer Control Programme specifies an intravenous infusion over 5 minutes in sodium chloride solution. Glucose is not used for dilution. Publications may describe the equivalent 1.4 mg/m² mesilate-salt dose; it must not be entered as 1.4 mg/m² eribulin base.

Other treatment and follow-up

A specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team reviews the exact pathology, disease extent, prior treatment and your priorities. Surgery or radiotherapy can have a separate role in selected situations. Symptom control, rehabilitation and palliative care can accompany systemic treatment; they are not restricted to the end of life.

Eribulin versus dacarbazine: prespecified liposarcoma subgroup

In the prespecified liposarcoma subgroup of a randomised trial, eribulin improved overall survival compared with dacarbazine. Participants had advanced dedifferentiated, myxoid/round-cell or pleomorphic liposarcoma and at least two previous systemic regimens.

Trial eligibility was narrower than the licensed indication. The result is not an individual prognosis and must not be generalised to leiomyosarcoma, all sarcomas or breast cancer. The trial salt dose is equivalent to the European base dose, not a higher prescribed base dose.

Fertility and pregnancy precautions

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Avoid pregnancy and obtain written contraception and breastfeeding instructions for every medicine, including how long precautions continue after the last dose. Do not assume all drugs have the same waiting period. Contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible. The readable current manufacturer information specifies highly effective contraception for women during treatment and for 7 months afterwards, and effective contraception for men with partners who could become pregnant during treatment and for 4 months afterwards. Confirm the supplied product and personal plan.

Driving and daily tasks

Do not drive or operate machinery when dizzy, unusually tired, weak or affected by nerve symptoms. Discuss adaptations and help with daily tasks.

Your treatment decision

Read the general chemotherapy risks and the individual medicine risks together.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Use this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk below.

Your priorities and alternatives

Discuss realistic benefit, uncertainty, alternatives including observation or symptom-focused care when appropriate, serious and lasting risks, personal priorities and the option to decline or reconsider treatment. Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Eribulin aims to control liposarcoma and may help symptoms. Your consultant considers the precise subtype, prior treatment, fitness and what matters to you. A specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team reviews the exact pathology, disease extent, prior treatment and your priorities. Surgery or radiotherapy can have a separate role in selected situations. Symptom control, rehabilitation and palliative care can accompany systemic treatment; they are not restricted to the end of life.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Can I restart after a delay or safety hold?

Only after the treating team gives an explicit plan. A normal result does not erase a previous severe reaction or permanent-stop decision.

Are safety tests the same as checking benefit?

No. Safety tests look for treatment effects on the body. Scans, symptoms and function help the specialist assess tumour control.
