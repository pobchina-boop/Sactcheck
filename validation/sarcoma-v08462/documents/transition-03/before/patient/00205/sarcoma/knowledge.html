When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Chest or throat tightness during infusion needs immediate staff attention. New breathlessness, fever, painful blistered hands or feet, or mouth ulcers preventing drinking needs urgent advice.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Treatment aims to control metastatic sarcoma and help symptoms. A specialist considers tumour subtype, previous treatment, heart risk and your priorities; benefit is uncertain for an individual.

Liposomal anthracycline chemotherapy

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin

Delivers doxorubicin in coated fat particles

Doxorubicin damages the genetic material, deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA). In this formulation it is carried in tiny coated fat particles called liposomes, changing its distribution and side-effect pattern. It remains capable of causing heart injury and cannot be substituted for other doxorubicin formulations.

What to watch for

Individual prescribed course

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin 50 mg/m² is given on day 1 every 28 days. National Cancer Control Programme describes typically 6 cycles or continuation until progression or unacceptable toxicity; the team confirms duration and review points.

This is the 50 mg/m², 28-day metastatic-sarcoma context. Current Caelyx European indications do not list general soft tissue sarcoma, so the off-label rationale and consent require documentation under local policy. Breast and gynaecological efficacy claims are not transferred here. Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin is not interchangeable with conventional doxorubicin or non-pegylated liposomal Myocet.

Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle. The 20 mg/m² and 50 mg/m² regimens have different source count decisions; additional checks follow symptoms and prior results.

Check infection, anaemia and bleeding risks.

Kidney and liver tests

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle; repeat sooner when indicated by symptoms or abnormal results.

Identify organ problems and review safe drug handling.

Heart function and cumulative exposure

WhenElectrocardiogram (heart tracing) and heart pumping assessment at baseline; regular heart tracings during treatment and further pumping tests as indicated. Measure left ventricular ejection fraction, the heart pumping measurement, before each further dose taking lifetime anthracycline exposure above 450 mg/m². This threshold does not itself mean automatic treatment stopping.

Assess cardiac risk despite the liposomal formulation.

Mouth, palms and soles

WhenAssess before each cycle and report symptoms between visits. Tell the team the worst symptoms and when they occurred; the team records their severity and any treatment delay.

Identify painful ulcers or hand–foot inflammation before they become disabling.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the exact pegylated liposomal product and all previous anthracycline doses. Report heart symptoms, mouth ulcers and painful hands or feet before the visit. Review interacting medicines, pregnancy precautions and any prior infusion reaction.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

The pharmacy prepares only the pegylated liposomal formulation in glucose 5%; trained staff administer it without an in-line filter. National Cancer Control Programme specifies a first infusion rate of 1 mg/min and later 60-minute infusions if tolerated; a previous reaction requires a supervised modified sequence. The total visit includes blood tests, review, observation and line care.

STEP 3

Intravenous access and line care

The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements.

STEP 4

Home support

National Cancer Control Programme classifies this as low nausea/vomiting risk and lists no usual premedication. Follow individual anti-sickness and mouth-care advice. For 4–7 days after treatment, avoid excessive heat, friction and tight pressure on hands or feet; cooling measures and any skin treatment follow the local plan. Do not start routine vitamin or steroid hand–foot prophylaxis yourself.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Fertility and pregnancy precautions

Your treatment decision

Read the general chemotherapy risks and the individual medicine risks together.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Use this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk below.

European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer randomised phase II study: liposomal versus conventional doxorubicin in advanced sarcoma

A small historical randomised phase II study compared pegylated liposomal doxorubicin 50 mg/m² every four weeks with conventional doxorubicin 75 mg/m² every three weeks in advanced sarcoma. Observed response rates were similar, with different toxicity patterns: less severe neutropenia and hair loss but more hand–foot toxicity with the liposomal treatment.

This was a small phase II trial with an older mixed-sarcoma population, including gastrointestinal stromal tumours. It does not prove modern non-inferiority or a survival advantage, and does not support this chemotherapy as a present-day gastrointestinal stromal tumour recommendation. Breast/ovarian outcomes are separate.

Sources: trial · nccp

When do I attend?

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin 50 mg/m² is given on day 1 every 28 days. National Cancer Control Programme describes typically 6 cycles or continuation until progression or unacceptable toxicity; the team confirms duration and review points.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Can I restart after a delay or safety hold?

Only after the treating team gives an explicit plan. A normal result does not erase a previous severe reaction or permanent-stop decision.

Are safety tests the same as checking benefit?

No. Safety tests look for treatment effects on the body. Scans, symptoms and function help the specialist assess tumour control.
