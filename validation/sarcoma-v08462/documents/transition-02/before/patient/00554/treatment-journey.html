Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle as specified by National Cancer Control Programme. The team also reviews current blood counts before each planned dose, including days 8 and 15, and confirms when samples are needed; count recovery is required before further vinblastine.

Detect low infection-fighting cells, anaemia and bleeding risk.

Kidney function and hydration

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle; sooner for dehydration, reduced urine, vomiting, diarrhoea or new fluid collections.

Identify reduced methotrexate clearance and prevent accumulation.

Liver tests

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle; repeat sooner for abnormal results or symptoms. Drug-specific source and product thresholds must be reconciled.

Assess safe dosing and identify liver injury.

Chest and breathing assessment

WhenChest X-ray before treatment. Investigate a new dry cough or breathlessness promptly; routine repeated X-rays are not specified.

Record baseline lung status and detect infection or drug-related inflammation.

Mouth and bowel review

WhenReview before further dosing and report new symptoms between visits. Severe pain, vomiting, abdominal swelling or inability to drink needs urgent advice.

Detect methotrexate-related ulcers and vinblastine-related constipation.

Nerves, hearing and balance

WhenAsk about numbness, weakness, hearing changes or balance problems before further treatment and whenever symptoms change.

Detect effects that may require vinblastine review.

The whole treatment journey

Vinblastine and methotrexate for aggressive fibromatosis: treatment and checks

Advanced aggressive fibromatosis (desmoid tumour)

Vinblastine 6 mg/m² and methotrexate 30 mg/m² are given on days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle. Day 22 is a planned break. National Cancer Control Programme describes 12 cycles unless progression or unacceptable toxicity requires a change.

What each check is for

1Treatment reviewVinblastine and methotrexate on days 1, 8 and 15; repeat every 28 days

Desmoid tumours do not spread to distant organs but can grow locally, cause pain and impair function. This treatment aims to control troublesome or progressive local disease and preserve daily function; it is not a metastatic-cancer survival treatment.

Ask / act: Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. New dry cough or breathlessness, severe mouth ulcers or diarrhoea, reduced urine, jaundice, or painful constipation with vomiting or abdominal swelling requires urgent advice.
