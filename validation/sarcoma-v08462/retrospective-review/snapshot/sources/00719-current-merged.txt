L129@P2: ADVERSE EFFECTS / REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS
L130@P2: The adverse effects listed are not exhaustive. Please refer to the relevant Summary of Product Characteristics for full
L131@P2: details.
L132@P2:  Cardiac Toxicity: Cardiovascular events, including heart failure, cardiomyopathy, and myocardial
L133@P2: ischaemia and myocardial infarction, some of which were fatal, have been reported in patients
L134@P2: treated with SUNitinib. These data suggest that SUNitinib increases the risk of cardiomyopathy. No
L135@P2: specific additional risk factors for SUNitinib-induced cardiomyopathy apart from the drug-specific
L136@P2: effect have been identified in the treated patients. Use SUNitinib with caution in patients who are at
L137@P2: risk for, or who have a history of, these events.
L138@P2: Baseline and periodic evaluations of LVEF should also be considered while the patient is receiving
L139@P2: SUNitinib. In the presence of clinical manifestations of CHF, discontinuation of SUNitinib is
L140@P2: recommended. The administration of SUNitinib should be interrupted and/or the dose reduced in
L141@P2: patients without clinical evidence of CHF but with an ejection fraction <50% and >20% below
L142@P2: baseline.
L143@P2:  QT prolongation: QT interval prolongation may lead to an increased risk of ventricular arrhythmias
L144@P2: including Torsade de pointes. Torsade de pointes has been observed in <0.1% of SUNitinib-exposed
L145@P2: patients. SUNitinib should be used with caution in patients with a known history of QT interval
L146@P2-3: prolongation, patients who are taking antiarrhythmics, or medicinal products that can prolong QT NCCP National SACT Regimen

L160@P3:  Hypertension: Patients should be screened for hypertension and controlled as appropriate.
L161@P3: Temporary suspension is recommended in patients with severe hypertension that is not controlled
L162@P3: with medical management. Treatment may be resumed once hypertension is appropriately
L163@P3: controlled.
L164@P3:  Aneurysms and artery dissections: The use of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) pathway
L165@P3: inhibitors in patients with or without hypertension may promote the formation of aneurysms
L166@P3: and/or artery dissections. Before initiating SUNitinib, this risk should be carefully considered in
L167@P3: patients with risk factors such as hypertension or history of aneurysm.
L168@P3:  Hypothyroidism: Baseline laboratory measurement of thyroid function is recommended in all
L169@P3: patients. Patients with pre-existing hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism should be treated as per
L170@P3: standard medical practice prior to the start of SUNitinib treatment. During SUNitinib treatment,
L171@P3: routine monitoring of thyroid function should be performed. Patients who develop thyroid
L172@P3: dysfunction should be treated as per standard medical practice. Hypothyroidism has been observed
L173@P3: to occur early as well as late during treatment with SUNitinib.
L174@P3:  Skin and tissue disorders:
L175@P3: o Skin discolouration, possibly due to the active substance colour (yellow), is a very common
L176@P3: adverse reaction occurring in approximately 30% of patients. Patients should be advised that
L177@P3: depigmentation of the hair or skin may also occur during treatment with SUNitinib. Other
L178@P3: possible dermatologic effects may include dryness, thickness or cracking of the skin, blisters, or
L179@P3: occasional rash on the palms of the hands and soles of the feet. The above reactions were not
L180@P3: cumulative, were typically reversible and generally did not result in treatment discontinuation.
L181@P3: o Cases of pyoderma gangrenosum, generally reversible after drug discontinuation, have been
L182@P3: reported. Severe cutaneous reactions have been reported, including cases of erythema
L183@P3: multiforme (EM) and cases suggestive of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal
L184@P3: necrolysis (TEN), some of which were fatal. If signs or symptoms of SJS, TEN, or EM
L185@P3: (e.g.progressive skin rash often with blisters or mucosal lesions) are present, SUNitinib treatment
L186@P3: should be discontinued. If the diagnosis of SJS or TEN is confirmed, treatment must not be restarted. In some cases of suspected EM, patients tolerated the reintroduction of SUNitinib
L187@P3: therapy at a lower dose after resolution of the reaction; some of these patients also received
L188@P3: concomitant treatment with corticosteroids or antihistamines.
L189@P3:  Wound healing: This treatment may impair wound healing and temporary interruption of treatment
L190@P3: is recommended in patients undergoing major surgical procedures.
L191@P3:  Haemorrhage and tumour bleeding: Haemorrhagic events, some of which were fatal, reported
L192@P3: through post-marketing experience have included gastro-intestinal, respiratory, urinary tract and
L193@P3: brain haemorrhages. Patients receiving concomitant treatment with anticoagulants) may be
L194@P3: periodically monitored by complete blood counts (platelets), coagulation factors (PT/INR) and
L195@P3: physical examination.
L196@P3: DRUG INTERACTIONS:
L197@P3:  Co-administration with potent CYP3A4 inducers should be avoided because it may decrease
L198@P3: SUNitinib plasma concentration.
L199@P3:  Limited clinical data are available on the interaction between SUNitinib and BCRP inhibitors and the
L200@P3: possibility of an interaction between SUNitinib and other BCRP inhibitors cannot be excluded.
L201@P3:  Co-administration with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors should be avoided because it may increase the
L202@P3: plasma concentration of SUNitinib.
