L21@P0: TREATMENT:
L22@P0: The starting dose of the drugs detailed below may be adjusted downward by the prescribing clinician, using their
L23@P0: independent medical judgement, to consider each patients individual clinical circumstances.
L24@P0: Dacarbazine is administered on day 1 of a 21 day cycle for 4 cycles or until disease progression or
L25@P0: unacceptable toxicity develops.
L26@P0: Day Drug Dose Route Diluent & Rate Cycle
L27@P0: 1 Dacarbazine 1.2 g/m2IV infusion 1000mls NaCl 0.9% over 1 hour Every 21 days
L28@P0: Dacarbazine is sensitive to light exposure. All reconstituted solutions should be suitably protected from light also during administration
L29@P0: (light-resistant infusion set)

L85@P1: ADVERSE EFFECTS / REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS
L86@P1: The adverse effects listed are not exhaustive. Please refer to the relevant Summary of Product Characteristics for full
L87@P1: details.
L88@P1:  Neutropenia: Fever or other evidence of infection must be assessed promptly and treated appropriately.
L89@P1:  Vein Irritation: Dacarbazine often causes pain during administration that usually responds to slowing the
L90@P1: infusion rate.
L91@P1:  Hepatotoxic drugs and alcohol should be avoided during chemotherapy.
L92@P1: DRUG INTERACTIONS:
L93@P1:  Dacarbazine is metabolised by cytochrome P450 (CYP1A1, CYP1A2, and CYP2E1). This has to be taken into
L94@P1: account if other medicinal products are co-administered which are metabolised by the same hepatic
L95@P1: enzymes.
L96@P1:  Concomitant use of phenytoin and dacarbazine should be avoided since there is a risk of exacerbation
L97@P1: of convulsions resulting from the decrease of phenytoin digestive absorption.
L98@P1-2:  Current drug interaction databases should be consulted for more information.NCCP Chemotherapy Regimen
