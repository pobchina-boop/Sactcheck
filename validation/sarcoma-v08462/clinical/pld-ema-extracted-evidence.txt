L3@P1: 1. NAME OF THE MEDICINAL PRODUCT
L4@P1: Caelyx pegylated liposomal 2 mg/ml concentrate for solution for infusion
L5@P1: 2. QUALITATIVE AND QUANTITATIVE COMPOSITION
L6@P1: One ml of Caelyx pegylated liposomal contains 2 mg doxorubicin hydrochloride in a pegylated
L7@P1: liposomal formulation.
L8@P1: Caelyx pegylated liposomal is doxorubicin hydrochloride encapsulated in liposomes with
L9@P1: surface-bound methoxypolyethylene glycol (MPEG). This process is known as pegylation and protects
L10@P1: liposomes from detection by the mononuclear phagocyte system (MPS), which increases blood
L11@P1: circulation time.
L12@P1: Excipients with known effect
L13@P1: Contains fully hydrogenated soy phosphatidylcholine (from soyabean) – see section 4.3.
L14@P1: For the full list of excipients, see section 6.1.
L15@P1: 3. PHARMACEUTICAL FORM
L16@P1: Concentrate for solution for infusion (sterile concentrate)
L17@P1: The dispersion is sterile, translucent and red.
L18@P1: 4. CLINICAL PARTICULARS
L19@P1: 4.1 Therapeutic indications
L20@P1: Caelyx pegylated liposomal is indicated:
L21@P1: - As monotherapy for patients with metastatic breast cancer, where there is an increased cardiac
L22@P1: risk.
L23@P1: - For treatment of advanced ovarian cancer in women who have failed a first-line platinum-based
L24@P1: chemotherapy regimen.
L25@P1: - In combination with bortezomib for the treatment of progressive multiple myeloma in patients
L26@P1: who have received at least one prior therapy and who have already undergone or are unsuitable
L27@P1: for bone marrow transplant.
L28@P1: - For treatment of AIDS-related Kaposi’s sarcoma (KS) in patients with low CD4 counts
L29@P1: (< 200 CD4 lymphocytes/mm3
L30@P1: ) and extensive mucocutaneous or visceral disease.
L31@P1: Caelyx pegylated liposomal may be used as first-line systemic chemotherapy, or as second line
L32@P1: chemotherapy in AIDS-KS patients with disease that has progressed with, or in patients intolerant to,
L33@P1: prior combination systemic chemotherapy comprising at least two of the following agents: a vinca
L34@P1: alkaloid, bleomycin and standard doxorubicin (or other anthracycline).
L35@P1: 4.2 Posology and method of administration
L36@P1: Caelyx pegylated liposomal should only be administered under the supervision of a qualified
L37@P1: oncologist specialised in the administration of cytotoxic agents.
L38@P1: Caelyx pegylated liposomal exhibits unique pharmacokinetic properties and must not be used
L39@P1-2: interchangeably with other formulations of doxorubicin hydrochloride.3
L40@P2: Posology
L41@P2: Breast cancer/Ovarian cancer
L42@P2: Caelyx pegylated liposomal is administered intravenously at a dose of 50 mg/m2 once every 4 weeks
L43@P2: for as long as the disease does not progress and the patient continues to tolerate treatment.
L44@P2: Multiple myeloma
L45@P2: Caelyx pegylated liposomal is administered at 30 mg/m² on day 4 of the bortezomib 3 week regimen
L46@P2: as a 1 hour infusion administered immediately after the bortezomib infusion. The bortezomib regimen
L47@P2: consists of 1.3 mg/m² on days 1, 4, 8, and 11 every 3 weeks. The dose should be repeated as long as
L48@P2: patients respond satisfactorily and tolerate treatment. Day 4 dosing of both medicinal products may be
L49@P2: delayed up to 48 hours as medically necessary. Doses of bortezomib should be at least 72 hours apart.
L50@P2: AIDS-related KS
L51@P2: Caelyx pegylated liposomal is administered intravenously at 20 mg/m2 every two-to-three weeks.
L52@P2: Avoid intervals shorter than 10 days as medicinal product accumulation and increased toxicity cannot
L53@P2: be ruled out. Treatment of patients for two-to-three months is recommended to achieve a therapeutic
L54@P2: response. Continue treatment as needed to maintain a therapeutic response.
L55@P2: For all patients
L56@P2: If the patient experiences early symptoms or signs of infusion reaction (see sections 4.4 and 4.8),
L57@P2: immediately discontinue the infusion, give appropriate premedications (antihistamine and/or short
L58@P2: acting corticosteroid) and restart at a slower rate.

L257@P4: Hepatic Impairment
L258@P4: Caelyx pegylated liposomal pharmacokinetics determined in a small number of patients with elevated
L259@P4: total bilirubin levels do not differ from patients with normal total bilirubin; however, until further
L260@P4: experience is gained, the Caelyx pegylated liposomal dosage in patients with impaired hepatic
L261@P4: function should be reduced based on the experience from the breast and ovarian clinical trial programs
L262@P4: as follows: at initiation of therapy, if the bilirubin is between 1.2-3.0 mg/dl, the first dose is reduced by
L263@P4: 25%. If the bilirubin is > 3.0 mg/dl, the first dose is reduced by 50%. If the patient tolerates the first
L264@P4: dose without an increase in serum bilirubin or liver enzymes, the dose for cycle 2 can be increased to
L265@P4: the next dose level, i.e., if reduced by 25% for the first dose, increase to full dose for cycle 2; if
L266@P4: reduced by 50% for the first dose, increase to 75% of full dose for cycle 2. The dosage can be
L267@P4: increased to full dose for subsequent cycles if tolerated. Caelyx pegylated liposomal can be
L268@P4: administered to patients with liver metastases with concurrent elevation of bilirubin and liver enzymes
L269@P4: up to 4 x the upper limit of the normal range. Prior to Caelyx pegylated liposomal administration,
L270@P4: evaluate hepatic function using conventional clinical laboratory tests such as ALT/AST, alkaline
L271@P4: phosphatase, and bilirubin.
L272@P4: Renal Impairment
L273@P4: As doxorubicin is metabolised by the liver and excreted in the bile, dose modification should not be
L274@P4: required. Population pharmacokinetic data (in the range of creatinine clearance tested of
L275@P4-5: 30-156 ml/min) demonstrate that Caelyx pegylated liposomal clearance is not influenced by renal 6
L276@P5: function. No pharmacokinetic data are available in patients with creatinine clearance of less than
L277@P5: 30 ml/min.
L278@P5: AIDS-related KS patients with splenectomy
L279@P5: As there is no experience with Caelyx pegylated liposomal in patients who have had splenectomy,
L280@P5: treatment with Caelyx pegylated liposomal is not recommended.
L281@P5: Paediatric population
L282@P5: The experience in children is limited. Caelyx pegylated liposomal is not recommended in patients
L283@P5: below 18 years of age.
L284@P5: Elderly
L285@P5: Population based analysis demonstrates that age across the range tested (21–75 years) does not
L286@P5: significantly alter the pharmacokinetics of Caelyx pegylated liposomal.
L287@P5: Method of administration
L288@P5: Caelyx pegylated liposomal is administered as an intravenous infusion. For further instructions on
L289@P5: preparation and special precautions for handling (see section 6.6).
L290@P5: Do not administer Caelyx pegylated liposomal as a bolus injection or undiluted dispersion. It is
L291@P5: recommended that the Caelyx pegylated liposomal infusion line be connected through the side port of
L292@P5: an intravenous infusion of 5% (50 mg/ml) glucose to achieve further dilution and minimise the risk of
L293@P5: thrombosis and extravasation. The infusion may be given through a peripheral vein. Do not use with
L294@P5: in-line filters. Caelyx pegylated liposomal must not be given by the intramuscular or subcutaneous
L295@P5: route (see section 6.6).
L296@P5: For doses < 90 mg: dilute Caelyx pegylated liposomal in 250 ml 5% (50 mg/ml) glucose solution for
L297@P5: infusion.
L298@P5: For doses ≥ 90 mg: dilute Caelyx pegylated liposomal in 500 ml 5% (50 mg/ml) glucose solution for
L299@P5: infusion.
L300@P5: Breast cancer/Ovarian cancer/Multiple myeloma
L301@P5: To minimise the risk of infusion reactions, the initial dose is administered at a rate no greater than
L302@P5: 1 mg/minute. If no infusion reaction is observed, subsequent Caelyx pegylated liposomal infusions
L303@P5: may be administered over a 60-minute period.
L304@P5: In those patients who experience an infusion reaction, the method of infusion should be modified as
L305@P5: follows:
L306@P5: 5% of the total dose should be infused slowly over the first 15 minutes. If tolerated without reaction,
L307@P5: the infusion rate may then be doubled for the next 15 minutes. If tolerated, the infusion may then be
L308@P5: completed over the next hour for a total infusion time of 90 minutes.
L309@P5: AIDS-related KS
L310@P5: The dose of Caelyx pegylated liposomal is diluted in 250 ml 5% (50 mg/ml) glucose solution for
L311@P5: infusion and administered by intravenous infusion over 30 minutes.
L312@P5: 4.3 Contraindications
L313@P5: Hypersensitivity to the active substance, peanut or soya, or to any of the excipients listed in section
L314@P5: 6.1.
L315@P5: Caelyx pegylated liposomal must not be used to treat AIDS-KS that may be treated effectively with
L316@P5-6: local therapy or systemic alfa-interferon.7
L317@P6: 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use
L318@P6: Given the difference in pharmacokinetic profiles and dosing schedules, Caelyx pegylated liposomal
L319@P6: should not be used interchangeably with other formulations of doxorubicin hydrochloride.
L320@P6: Cardiac toxicity
L321@P6: It is recommended that all patients receiving Caelyx pegylated liposomal routinely undergo frequent
L322@P6: ECG monitoring. Transient ECG changes such as T-wave flattening, S-T segment depression and
L323@P6: benign arrhythmias are not considered mandatory indications for the suspension of Caelyx pegylated
L324@P6: liposomal therapy. However, reduction of the QRS complex is considered more indicative of cardiac
L325@P6: toxicity. If this change occurs, the most definitive test for anthracycline myocardial injury, i.e.,
L326@P6: endomyocardial biopsy, must be considered.
L327@P6: More specific methods for the evaluation and monitoring of cardiac functions as compared to ECG are
L328@P6: a measurement of left ventricular ejection fraction by echocardiography or preferably by Multigated
L329@P6: Angiography (MUGA). These methods must be applied routinely before the initiation of Caelyx
L330@P6: pegylated liposomal therapy and repeated periodically during treatment. The evaluation of left
L331@P6: ventricular function is considered to be mandatory before each additional administration of Caelyx
L332@P6: pegylated liposomal that exceeds a lifetime cumulative anthracycline dose of 450 mg/m2
L333@P6: .
L334@P6: The evaluation tests and methods mentioned above concerning the monitoring of cardiac performance
L335@P6: during anthracycline therapy are to be employed in the following order: ECG monitoring,
L336@P6: measurement of left ventricular ejection fraction, endomyocardial biopsy. If a test result indicates
L337@P6: possible cardiac injury associated with Caelyx pegylated liposomal therapy, the benefit of continued
L338@P6: therapy must be carefully weighed against the risk of myocardial injury.
L339@P6: In patients with cardiac disease requiring treatment, administer Caelyx pegylated liposomal only when
L340@P6: the benefit outweighs the risk to the patient.
L341@P6: Exercise caution in patients with impaired cardiac function who receive Caelyx pegylated liposomal.
L342@P6: Whenever cardiomyopathy is suspected, i.e., the left ventricular ejection fraction has substantially
L343@P6: decreased relative to pre-treatment values and/or left ventricular ejection fraction is lower than a
L344@P6: prognostically relevant value (e.g., < 45%), endomyocardial biopsy may be considered and the benefit
L345@P6: of continued therapy must be carefully evaluated against the risk of developing irreversible cardiac
L346@P6: damage.
L347@P6: Congestive heart failure due to cardiomyopathy may occur suddenly, without prior ECG changes and
L348@P6: may also be encountered several weeks after discontinuation of therapy.
L349@P6: Caution must be observed in patients who have received other anthracyclines. The total dose of
L350@P6: doxorubicin hydrochloride must also take into account any previous (or concomitant) therapy with
L351@P6: cardiotoxic compounds such as other anthracyclines/anthraquinones or e.g., 5-fluorouracil. Cardiac
L352@P6: toxicity also may occur at cumulative anthracycline doses lower than 450 mg/m2 in patients with prior
L353@P6: mediastinal irradiation or in those receiving concurrent cyclophosphamide therapy.
L354@P6: The cardiac safety profile for the dosing schedule recommended for both breast and ovarian cancer
L355@P6: (50 mg/m2
L356@P6: ) is similar to the 20 mg/m2 profile in patients with AIDS-KS (see section 4.8).
L357@P6: Myelosuppression
L358@P6: Many patients treated with Caelyx pegylated liposomal have baseline myelosuppression due to such
L359@P6: factors as their pre-existing HIV disease or numerous concomitant or previous medications, or
L360@P6: tumours involving bone marrow. In the pivotal trial in patients with ovarian cancer treated at a dose of
L361@P6: 50 mg/m2
L362@P6: , myelosuppression was generally mild to moderate, reversible, and was not associated with
L363@P6: episodes of neutropaenic infection or sepsis. Moreover, in a controlled clinical trial of Caelyx
L364@P6: pegylated liposomal vs. topotecan, the incidence of treatment related sepsis was substantially less in
L365@P6-7: the Caelyx pegylated liposomal-treated ovarian cancer patients as compared to the topotecan treatment 8
L366@P7: group. A similar low incidence of myelosuppression was seen in patients with metastatic breast cancer
L367@P7: receiving Caelyx pegylated liposomal in a first-line clinical trial. In contrast to the experience in
L368@P7: patients with breast cancer or ovarian cancer, myelosuppression appears to be the dose-limiting
L369@P7: adverse event in patients with AIDS-KS (see section 4.8). Because of the potential for bone marrow
L370@P7: suppression, periodic blood counts must be performed frequently during the course of Caelyx
L371@P7: pegylated liposomal therapy, and at a minimum, prior to each dose of Caelyx pegylated liposomal.
L372@P7: Persistent severe myelosuppression, may result in superinfection or haemorrhage.
L373@P7: In controlled clinical studies in patients with AIDS-KS against a bleomycin/vincristine regimen,
L374@P7: opportunistic infections were apparently more frequent during treatment with Caelyx pegylated
L375@P7: liposomal. Patients and doctors must be aware of this higher incidence and take action as appropriate.
L376@P7: Secondary haematological malignancies
L377@P7: As with other DNA-damaging antineoplastic agents, secondary acute myeloid leukemias and
L378@P7: myelodysplasias have been reported in patients having received combined treatment with doxorubicin.
L379@P7: Therefore, any patient treated with doxorubicin should be kept under haematological supervision.
L380@P7: Secondary oral neoplasms
L381@P7: Very rare cases of secondary oral cancer have been reported in patients with long-term (more than one
L382@P7: year) exposure to Caelyx pegylated liposomal or those receiving a cumulative Caelyx pegylated
L383@P7: liposomal dose greater than 720 mg/m2
L384@P7: . Cases of secondary oral cancer were diagnosed both, during
L385@P7: treatment with Caelyx pegylated liposomal, and up to 6 years after the last dose. Patients should be
L386@P7: examined at regular intervals for the presence of oral ulceration or any oral discomfort that may be
L387@P7: indicative of secondary oral cancer.
L388@P7: Infusion-associated reactions
L389@P7: Serious and sometimes life-threatening infusion reactions, which are characterised by allergic-like or
L390@P7: anaphylactoid-like reactions, with symptoms including asthma, flushing, urticarial rash, chest pain,
L391@P7: fever, hypertension, tachycardia, pruritus, sweating, shortness of breath, facial oedema, chills, back
L392@P7: pain, tightness in the chest and throat and/or hypotension may occur within minutes of starting the
L393@P7: infusion of Caelyx pegylated liposomal. Very rarely, convulsions also have been observed in relation
L394@P7: to infusion reactions. Temporarily stopping the infusion usually resolves these symptoms without
L395@P7: further therapy. However, medications to treat these symptoms (e.g., antihistamines, corticosteroids,
L396@P7: adrenaline, and anticonvulsants), as well as emergency equipment should be available for immediate
L397@P7: use. In most patients treatment can be resumed after all symptoms have resolved, without recurrence.
L398@P7: Infusion reactions rarely recur after the first treatment cycle. To minimise the risk of infusion
L399@P7: reactions, the initial dose should be administered at a rate no greater than 1 mg/minute (see section
L400@P7: 4.2).
L401@P7: Palmar plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome (PPE)
L402@P7: PPE is characterised by painful, macular reddening skin eruptions. In patients experiencing this event,
L403@P7: it is generally seen after two or three cycles of treatment. Improvement usually occurs in 1-2 weeks,
L404@P7: and in some cases, may take up to 4 weeks or longer for complete resolution. Pyridoxine at a dose of
L405@P7: 50-150 mg per day and corticosteroids have been used for the prophylaxis and treatment of PPE,
L406@P7: however, these therapies have not been evaluated in phase III trials. Other strategies to prevent and
L407@P7: treat PPE include keeping hands and feet cool, by exposing them to cool water (soaks, baths, or
L408@P7: swimming), avoiding excessive heat/hot water and keeping them unrestricted (no socks, gloves, or
L409@P7: shoes that are tight fitting). PPE appears to be primarily related to the dose schedule and can be
L410@P7: reduced by extending the dose interval 1- 2 weeks (see section 4.2). However, this reaction can be
L411@P7: severe and debilitating in some patients and may require discontinuation of treatment (see section 4.8).
L412@P7: Interstitial lung disease (ILD)
L413@P7: Interstitial lung disease (ILD), which may have an acute onset, has been observed in patients receiving
