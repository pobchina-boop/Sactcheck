# Patient treatment summary and portal: focused evidence review

Source access date: 2026-11-02. This is a focused design evidence review, not a systematic review. Public primary studies and authoritative guidance were searched and read through web retrieval. No patient testing of SACTCheck was performed. Clinical, pharmacy and patient/PPI approval remain pending.

## Conclusion

The evidence supports a readable treatment summary, clear schedules, optional access to detail, and clinician-led explanation with teach-back. It does not establish that a wallet card, QR code, anatomy graphic or additional multimedia alone improves understanding. The proposed summary-first, progressively disclosed portal is a design inference to be tested with patients.

## Guidance and practical implications

- [HSE Writing in plain language](https://healthservice.hse.ie/staff/procedures-guidelines/plain-language-guidelines/writing-in-plain-language/): important information first, simple details, one topic per paragraph, explained abbreviations and links to further detail. Aim for 12-point printed body text. This argues against fitting a fixed one-page target by reducing readability.
- [AHRQ Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit, third edition: Teach-back](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html): explain in small segments, invite the patient to explain in their own words, clarify and recheck. The patient may consult the handout. This checks understanding rather than unaided memory.
- [ASCO/ONS 2024 standards: official ONS entry](https://www.ons.org/ascoons-chemotherapy-administration-safety-standards) and [official article](https://www.ons.org/publications-research/journal-article/antineoplastic-therapy-administration-safety-standards-adult): consensus framework for safe treatment delivery. Detailed education clauses were accessible in the [Guideline Central reproduction](https://www.guidelinecentral.com/guideline/3709993/pocket-guide/3709994/). They cover treatment goals, medicines/schedule, duration, supportive medicines, adverse effects, urgent actions, follow-up and contacts. This review does not claim that all detailed clauses were checked against a downloaded official PDF.
- [CDC Clear Communication Index: main message](https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/tool/page-1.html): a clear primary message and explicit action support communication assessment. This is guidance, not clinical evidence for the exact SACTCheck layout.

## Proposed architecture: design inference

The core document should remain useful when printed without access to the portal. Prefer two readable A4 sides over one overcrowded page. A detachable SACT card can be included or printed separately. Essential safety information must not depend on scanning a QR code.

Front:

1. Treatment names, modality and clinician-confirmed treatment aim.
2. Plain-language schedule, such as every 3 weeks (21 days), treatment/rest days, planned cycles or review point, and separate chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgery or maintenance phases when applicable.
3. Essential preparation, blood tests and individually prescribed supportive medicines.
4. Prominent urgent action and verified daytime/out-of-hours contacts with availability. No invented telephone numbers.

Reverse:

5. Regimen-specific anatomical side-effect visual with adjacent plain-language labels. Distinguish manageable symptoms from urgent warnings using words as well as colour.
6. Broad monitoring and review plan without invented appointments.
7. Portable SACT card: treatment names, care team, emergency contacts and relevant treatment alert. Patient-specific identity fields are completed by the treating team, not inferred.
8. One QR and short printed URL for further information about the same regimen.

The portal landing page should mirror this summary. Suggested entry points are My treatment at a glance, Help with symptoms, and Learn more about my treatment. Further information can appear under questions about treatment days, medicines at home, tests/scans, daily life and consent. Trial evidence remains optional depth. Urgent information must remain directly reachable, with no forced sequential tutorial. Preserve existing deep links and downloadable documents.

## PPI and usability test plan

Use realistic tasks with participants allowed to refer to the material:

- Name the treatment and explain its intended purpose.
- Identify attendance days and explain every three weeks.
- Distinguish chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgery and maintenance where relevant.
- Find whom to call when unwell at 9 pm.
- Identify a symptom requiring urgent contact and the next action.
- Find more detailed information without assistance.
- Demonstrate what remains usable without a smartphone.

Record task completion, time to find the urgent number, wrong turns, assistance needed, meaning attributed to icons, perceived overload and preferences for depth. Reassess at the next treatment cycle. Include carers, older adults and people with limited digital confidence, vision/literacy difficulties or different language needs. Compare paper and phone use. A small iterative PPI sample can reveal usability failures; it cannot demonstrate fewer admissions, safer treatment or improved clinical outcomes.

## Claims to avoid

Do not state that the exact SACTCheck design is validated, that pictograms always outperform clear text, that a card improves recall, that a QR code improves outcomes, or that a shorter page substitutes for informed consent. Do not infer anatomy-graphic efficacy from a study of treatment-sequence timelines. Treat reduced navigation burden and improved information access as testable design hypotheses.
