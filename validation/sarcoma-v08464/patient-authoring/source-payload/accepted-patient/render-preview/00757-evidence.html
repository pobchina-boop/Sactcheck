Why this regimen?

Vincristine interferes with microtubules, structures needed for cells to divide and for nerves to work normally. This helps explain numbness, weakness and bowel slowing. It is given only into a vein by trained staff; administration into spinal fluid can be fatal. Irinotecan blocks topoisomerase I, an enzyme involved in handling deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material. Cancer-cell division is disrupted. Blood and bowel cells are also affected. Early infusion-related bowel symptoms and later diarrhoea need different treatment plans. The body converts temozolomide into an active substance that damages deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material, interrupting cell division. Although taken as capsules, it can cause serious blood, liver and infection problems and requires the prescribed checks. The specialist sarcoma team reviews prior treatments, disease sites, symptoms, available further systemic options and suitable clinical trials. Selected surgery or radiotherapy may help local control or symptoms; it is not automatically part of this capsule/infusion calendar. Symptom-focused treatment, rehabilitation and supportive or palliative care remain options alongside or instead of further chemotherapy.

Primary study report

A randomised study found a higher observed response and longer overall survival with the three-drug combination than with vincristine/irinotecan alone, but the primary response comparison did not meet conventional statistical significance and severe adverse events were more frequent. Most participants were children; adult-specific benefit cannot be assumed.

Toxicity context. Grade 3 or greater adverse events: 98% versus 78%. The higher observed severe-toxicity burden is part of the consent discussion, not an individual forecast.

Endpoint key and interpretation
◇ Locoregional events · Locoregional events
Recurrence or progression at the primary site or regional nodes, using the trial definition and stated follow-up. This is not overall survival.
◷ OS · Overall survival
Time to death from any cause, from the study-defined starting point.
◫ PFS · Progression-free survival
Time to progression or death; preserve the study assessment criteria.
◫ TTP · Time to progression
Time to the trial-defined progression event. Death handling varies by trial; TTP is not interchangeable with PFS.
◇ EFS · Event-free survival
Time to a study-defined event. Check the exact events included; this is not interchangeable with DFS.
◇ RFS · Relapse-free survival
Time to relapse or death as defined by the trial. Check its event definition and analysis; it is not overall survival.
◇ DFS · Disease-free survival
Time to disease recurrence or other events specified by the study, including death where defined.
◇ iDFS · Invasive disease-free survival
Time to the invasive-disease events and deaths defined by the study; review the full composite definition.
◷ DOR · Duration of response
Among responders, time from the first documented objective response to progression or death under the study definition. It is not an outcome for every enrolled patient.
◎ ORR · Objective response rate
Proportion meeting objective response criteria, generally complete plus partial responses. Not an estimate of cure.
⌕ pCR · Pathological complete response
Complete pathological response under the study definition. Specify the examined sites and residual-disease criteria; not proof of cure.
♧ QoL · Quality of life
Specify the instrument, domain, time point, score direction and clinically meaningful threshold where established.

Colour identifies the endpoint family. Effect size, statistical interpretation and maturity are separate. Median differences are not an individual's expected added lifespan. Do not rank regimens using results from separate trials.
