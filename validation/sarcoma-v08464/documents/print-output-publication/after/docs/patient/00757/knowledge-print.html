Act on symptoms, not the calendar

When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Irinotecan can cause two different bowel problems. Cramping, sweating and diarrhoea during or shortly after infusion can be an acute cholinergic reaction: tell the nurse immediately; prescribed atropine may be used. Delayed diarrhoea begins more than 24 hours later and can cause dangerous dehydration or infection. Contact oncology at the first loose stool and follow the written anti-diarrhoeal and rehydration prescription. Fever, severe pain, blood, faintness, very little urine or inability to drink needs immediate assessment; never wait for a time threshold when seriously unwell. Report a new cough, breathlessness, jaundice, severe rash or marked weakness urgently.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Aim to shrink or control cancer and help symptoms after previous treatment. Your specialist explains the realistic benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your particular disease.

Vinca alkaloid chemotherapy

Vincristine

Disrupts the structures needed for cell division

Vincristine interferes with microtubules, structures needed for cells to divide and for nerves to work normally. This helps explain numbness, weakness and bowel slowing. It is given only into a vein by trained staff; administration into spinal fluid can be fatal.

Microtubules are disrupted↓Cell division and nerves are affected↓Bowel and nerve symptoms need early review

What to watch for
Report new numbness, weakness, difficulty walking, jaw pain or hearing/vision changes before another dose. Constipation with pain, vomiting or abdominal swelling, difficulty passing urine, confusion or seizures needs urgent advice. Report infusion-site pain immediately.

Chemotherapy

Irinotecan

Interferes with repair of genetic-material breaks

Irinotecan blocks topoisomerase I, an enzyme involved in handling deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material. Cancer-cell division is disrupted. Blood and bowel cells are also affected. Early infusion-related bowel symptoms and later diarrhoea need different treatment plans.

Genetic-material repair is disrupted↓Blood and bowel cells can be affected↓Early and delayed diarrhoea need different actions

What to watch for
Tell staff about cramping, sweating, runny eyes/nose or diarrhoea during or soon after infusion. Later watery stools require the prescribed diarrhoea plan and immediate oncology contact. Fever, dizziness, reduced urine, severe abdominal pain or inability to drink needs urgent assessment.

Oral alkylating chemotherapy

Temozolomide

Adds chemical damage to genetic material

The body converts temozolomide into an active substance that damages deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material, interrupting cell division. Although taken as capsules, it can cause serious blood, liver and infection problems and requires the prescribed checks.

Capsules deliver active anticancer treatment↓Genetic material is damaged↓Blood, liver and infection checks continue

What to watch for
Report fever, new cough or breathlessness, unusual bleeding, jaundice, severe rash or persistent vomiting urgently. Take capsules exactly as prescribed; do not replace a dose after vomiting. Follow the infection-prevention prescription and report any missed doses to the team.

The drawings are simplified explanations of drug action, not anatomical pictures or a prediction of response.

Your treatment

The schedule

Irinotecan 50 mg/m² is given intravenously on days 1–5 of each 21-day cycle. Temozolomide is taken by mouth on days 1–5: 125 mg/m² in cycle 1. Only the treating team may select 150 mg/m² from cycle 2 after toxicity review. Vincristine 1.5 mg/m², maximum 2 mg, is given on days 1 and 8. The national plan specifies 12 cycles unless progression, toxicity or the specialist plan changes treatment. Take temozolomide fasting, swallowed whole with water, at least one hour before irinotecan. If you vomit after temozolomide, do not take an extra dose.

How it is given

Irinotecan is infused over 60 minutes after the oral temozolomide dose, with prescribed anti-sickness and bowel protection. Vincristine is administered only into a vein using a minibag over 15 minutes. Confirm which doses are taken at home or in the unit and bring the exact capsule prescription; capsule strengths can differ.

Other treatment and follow-up

The specialist sarcoma team reviews prior treatments, disease sites, symptoms, available further systemic options and suitable clinical trials. Selected surgery or radiotherapy may help local control or symptoms; it is not automatically part of this capsule/infusion calendar. Symptom-focused treatment, rehabilitation and supportive or palliative care remain options alongside or instead of further chemotherapy.

Randomised vincristine/irinotecan with or without temozolomide in relapsed rhabdomyosarcoma

A randomised study found a higher observed response and longer overall survival with the three-drug combination than with vincristine/irinotecan alone, but the primary response comparison did not meet conventional statistical significance and severe adverse events were more frequent. Most participants were children; adult-specific benefit cannot be assumed.

The primary response endpoint had P=0.09; progression-free survival also did not reach conventional significance. The overall-survival result is a separate endpoint. The trial treated until progression or unacceptable toxicity, whereas the national schedule specifies 12 cycles. Mixed ages do not establish a separate adult estimate.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Anti-sickness medicines

Follow one written prevention, home and rescue plan. The team reconciles all steroids and interacting medicines; no generic reference is an additional prescription.

Infection prevention

Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia prevention is required with this temozolomide regimen. The prescribed medicine helps prevent an opportunistic lung infection; follow its full course and report new cough or breathlessness. Hepatitis B results may require a separate expert prevention and follow-up plan.

Acute irinotecan reactions

Sweating, cramps and early diarrhoea during or soon after infusion can be a cholinergic reaction. Tell the nurse immediately. The national regimen says atropine should not be used in patients with glaucoma. Tell the treatment team if you have glaucoma. This source specifies 250 micrograms under the skin before irinotecan; the team confirms the appropriate prescription.

Capsules and missed or vomited doses

If a temozolomide dose is missed, contact the treatment team promptly so timing with irinotecan can be confirmed. The national source allows making up a missed dose unless the next dose is due within 12 hours; never double a dose or restart a held course yourself. If vomiting occurs after temozolomide, do not take an extra dose. Capsules must be swallowed whole, fasting, at least one hour before irinotecan.

Constipation and antifungal review

Constipation prevention is recommended because vincristine slows the bowel. Antifungal prophylaxis may be considered, but interacting azoles can increase nerve and bowel toxicity and require pharmacy review.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment; these combinations can permanently affect fertility. Obtain a written pregnancy-prevention, contraception and breastfeeding plan covering every medicine and the longest relevant precaution period. Current product instructions differ from the older general six-month regimen paragraph. Do not assume one interval applies to everyone, and contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines, doses and people. Read this general section together with the risks of every medicine actually included in the agreed treatment phase.

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Aim to shrink or control cancer and help symptoms after previous treatment. Your specialist explains the realistic benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your particular disease. The specialist sarcoma team reviews prior treatments, disease sites, symptoms, available further systemic options and suitable clinical trials. Selected surgery or radiotherapy may help local control or symptoms; it is not automatically part of this capsule/infusion calendar. Symptom-focused treatment, rehabilitation and supportive or palliative care remain options alongside or instead of further chemotherapy.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Can I restart after symptoms improve?

Only after the treating team confirms the new prescription. A normal test or recovered symptom does not erase previous severe toxicity or a permanent-stop decision.
