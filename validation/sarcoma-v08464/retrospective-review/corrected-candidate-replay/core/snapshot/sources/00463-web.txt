L450@P9: REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS
L451@P9:  Neutropenia: Fever or other evidence of infection must be assessed promptly and treated
L452@P9: appropriately. Consider use of G-CSF on further cycles as per Table 4.
L453@P9:  High dose methotrexate: Monitoring of methotrexate levels is essential as delayed methotrexate
L454@P9: excretion is potentially an emergency situation. Renal function must be evaluated prior to treatment
L455@P9: and patients with creatinine clearance less than 70mL/min/1.73m2should not receive high dose
L456@P9: methotrexate. Methotrexate exits slowly from third space compartments (e.g. pleural effusions or
L457@P9: ascites), resulting in a prolonged terminal plasma half-life and unexpected toxicity. In patients with
L458@P9: significant third space accumulations, it is advisable to evacuate the fluid before treatment and to
L459@P9: monitor plasma methotrexate levels.
L460@P9:  Avoid concurrent use of CISplatin with nephrotoxic drugs (e.g. aminoglycosides, furosemide, NSAIDS)
L461@P9: due to additive nephrotoxicity. If necessary monitor renal function closely.

L512@P10: iCardiotoxicity is a risk associated with anthracycline therapy that may be manifested by early (acute) or late
L513@P10: (delayed) effects.
L514@P10: Risk factors for developing anthracycline-induced cardiotoxicity include:
L515@P10: • high cumulative dose, previous therapy with other anthracyclines or anthracenediones
L516@P10: • prior or concomitant radiotherapy to the mediastinal/pericardial area
L517@P10: • pre-existing heart disease
L518@P10: • concomitant use of other potentially cardiotoxic drugs
L519@P10: In establishing the maximal cumulative dose of an anthracycline, consideration should be given to the risk
L520@P10: factors above and to the age of the patient
