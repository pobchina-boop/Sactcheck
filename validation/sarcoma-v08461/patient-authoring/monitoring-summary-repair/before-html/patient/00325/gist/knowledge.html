When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Chest pain, new breathlessness, fainting, severe headache, confusion, significant bleeding, severe abdominal pain or blistering skin requires urgent assessment.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Sunitinib aims to slow gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) growth and help symptoms after imatinib has stopped working or cannot be tolerated. The team reviews previous treatment, tumour biology, your health and priorities before choosing the schedule.

Oral targeted therapy: tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Sunitinib

Sutent
Blocks growth and blood-vessel signalling

Sunitinib blocks several kinases, including KIT and blood-vessel growth receptors. After imatinib resistance or intolerance it may slow gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) growth. Its effects on normal blood vessels and other tissues explain the need for blood-pressure, heart, thyroid and organ-function monitoring.

KIT and vessel signals↓Several kinases blocked↓Tumour growth support reduced

What to watch for
Report painful hands or feet, worsening diarrhoea, bleeding, chest pain, breathlessness, faintness, severe headache or a blistering rash promptly. Do not restart after a serious reaction without oncology review.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Confirm the exact calendar and dose strength before each cycle. Review all prescription, non-prescription and herbal products, especially the liver enzyme cytochrome P450 3A4 interactions; avoid grapefruit and St John’s wort. Keep a blood-pressure and symptom record if your team asks.

STEP 2

Taking the medicine

Swallow the prescribed capsules with water, with or without food. Take at a consistent time each treatment day. Use the written calendar to distinguish treatment days from the planned break.

STEP 3

Missed or vomited doses

Skip a missed sunitinib dose and take the next scheduled dose at the usual time. Do not take an extra or double dose. Ask the team about vomiting or repeated missed doses.

STEP 4

Supply and handling

Keep tablets or capsules in their supplied packaging, away from children, and follow the pharmacy storage instructions. Do not share medicines or alter the dose yourself. Ask the pharmacy before crushing, opening or dispersing a medicine; formulation-specific instructions differ. Return unused treatment to the pharmacy.

STEP 5

Monitoring and next review

Blood count and kidney/liver tests before each cycle, thyroid tests every 12 weeks and blood pressure at each attendance. Cardiac assessment and further tests depend on risk, symptoms and previous abnormalities.

Your wider treatment plan

gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) is a distinct tumour type. A specialist team reviews pathology, KIT/platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha and other relevant molecular findings, prior treatment, symptoms and scans. Randomised sunitinib trial after imatinib failure in gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST)

A placebo-controlled trial showed that sunitinib delayed gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) progression after imatinib resistance or intolerance. The studied schedule was 50 mg daily for four weeks followed by two weeks off.

Trial findings apply to previously treated gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) and the studied schedule. They do not predict an individual response or establish benefit in renal cancer, another treatment line or the alternative 21-day calendar.

Sources: trial · nccp

What is the treatment trying to do?

When do I attend?

National Cancer Control Programme 00325: sunitinib 50 mg by mouth once daily on days 1–28, followed by 14 days without sunitinib. Repeat the 42-day cycle only as confirmed by your team. Dose reductions or additional holds may be needed; symptoms and monitoring continue during the planned break.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

What should I do about a missed or vomited dose?

Skip a missed sunitinib dose and take the next scheduled dose at the usual time. Do not take an extra or double dose. Ask the team about vomiting or repeated missed doses.

Can I take other medicines or supplements?

Why do I need checks if I feel well?

High blood pressure, thyroid changes, liver injury or low blood counts may be detected before you notice symptoms. The required checks differ by medicine; keep your individual schedule.
