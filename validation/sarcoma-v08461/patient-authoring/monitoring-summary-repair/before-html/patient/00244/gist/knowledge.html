Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Regorafenib aims to control gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) after progression on, or intolerance to, both imatinib and sunitinib. Your team explains the likely benefit, uncertainty, alternatives and the monitoring needed for your circumstances.

Oral targeted therapy: tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Regorafenib

Stivarga
Blocks several growth and blood-vessel signals

Regorafenib inhibits several kinases that help tumour cells grow and blood vessels support them. In gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) it is used after imatinib and sunitinib. Effects on healthy tissues make skin care, blood-pressure checks and early liver monitoring particularly important.

Growth and blood-vessel signals↓Several kinases blocked↓Tumour-supporting signals reduced

What to watch for
Report sore hands or feet early. Seek urgent help for yellow eyes, dark urine, severe abdominal pain, significant bleeding, chest pain, breathlessness, severe headache or confusion.

Individual prescribed course

National Cancer Control Programme 00244 uses regorafenib 160 mg by mouth once daily on days 1–21 of a 28-day cycle, followed by 7 days off. The team may reduce the dose in 40 mg steps or interrupt treatment; do not change the dose or restart independently.

This treatment plan is for gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) after both imatinib and sunitinib. Prior serious reactions remain relevant even when a current test or symptom has improved; an explicit oncology restart decision is required after a safety hold.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Confirm that both prior imatinib and sunitinib treatments have been reviewed. Reconcile all prescription, non-prescription and herbal products, including warfarin and the liver enzyme cytochrome P450 3A4 interactions; avoid grapefruit and St John’s wort. Agree liver-test and blood-pressure appointments before starting.

STEP 2

Taking the medicine

Swallow tablets whole with water after a light, low-fat meal, at a consistent time. Your pharmacy supplies the written food and dose instructions for the product you receive.

STEP 3

Missed or vomited doses

Take a missed dose when remembered on the same day. Do not take two doses on one day. Do not take extra tablets after vomiting; contact the team if vomiting continues.

STEP 4

Supply and handling

Keep tablets or capsules in their supplied packaging, away from children, and follow the pharmacy storage instructions. Do not share medicines or alter the dose yourself. Ask the pharmacy before crushing, opening or dispersing a medicine; formulation-specific instructions differ. Return unused treatment to the pharmacy.

STEP 5

Monitoring and next review

Liver tests at least every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then monthly or more often as indicated. Blood pressure weekly for the first 6 weeks, then before each cycle and as needed. Blood count/kidney tests regularly; thyroid/pancreatic tests and cardiac review when indicated. Monitor international normalised ratio (warfarin clotting test) closely with warfarin.

Your wider treatment plan

gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) is a distinct tumour type. A specialist team reviews pathology, KIT/platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha and other relevant molecular findings, prior treatment, symptoms and scans. Surgery may be appropriate for resectable disease or selected situations. Radiotherapy has a selective role, rather than being a routine partner to these tablets. Nutrition, symptom control and supportive or palliative care can be used alongside active cancer treatment.

GRID: regorafenib after imatinib and sunitinib in advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST)

GRID compared regorafenib plus best supportive care with placebo plus best supportive care in advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) after imatinib and sunitinib. Regorafenib improved progression-free survival. This explains its later-line disease-control role.

Progression-free survival is not the same as a proven overall-survival gain or an individual prognosis. Crossover complicates overall-survival interpretation. Do not use colorectal CORRECT or liver-cancer RESORCE results as gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) estimates.

Sources: trial · nccp

When do I attend?

National Cancer Control Programme 00244 uses regorafenib 160 mg by mouth once daily on days 1–21 of a 28-day cycle, followed by 7 days off. The team may reduce the dose in 40 mg steps or interrupt treatment; do not change the dose or restart independently.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Why do I need checks if I feel well?
