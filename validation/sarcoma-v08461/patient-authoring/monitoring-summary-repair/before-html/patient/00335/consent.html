1. Treatment and purpose

Imatinib for gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) · NCCP 00335 v4

The usual National Cancer Control Programme starting dose is 400 mg by mouth every day with food. For progression in advanced disease, a specialist may consider 800 mg daily as 400 mg twice daily. Adjuvant treatment after resection is described as up to 3 years in this National Cancer Control Programme document; your specialist confirms duration using current risk and evidence. The 28-day graphic is a reference view, not a treatment break or fixed course.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Imatinib · Oral targeted therapy: tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Report rapid weight gain, swelling, new breathlessness, fever, unusual bleeding, severe rash or yellow eyes promptly. Keep blood tests even if you feel well.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss the intended benefit, uncertainty, alternatives including supportive care, generic targeted-therapy risks and the separate agent risks, lasting effects, fertility and the patient’s priorities. This is National Cancer Control Programme 00335 for gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST), not an imatinib schedule for leukaemia or another tumour. Advanced and adjuvant aims/durations differ. Low-risk adjuvant GIST is outside this source indication; molecular resistance can also alter suitability.
