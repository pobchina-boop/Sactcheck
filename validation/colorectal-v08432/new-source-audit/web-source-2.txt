L27@P0: The starting dose of the drugs detailed below may be adjusted downward by the prescribing clinician, using their
L28@P0: independent medical judgement, to consider each patients individual clinical circumstances.

L30@P0: treatment cycle), followed by a 2-week rest period.
L31@P0:  5-Fluorouracil is administered as an intravenous bolus 1 hour after the folinic acid infusion has begun,
L32@P0: and is administered weekly for 6 weeks (on days 1, 8, 15, 22, 29, and 36 of the treatment cycle), followed
L33@P0: by a 2-week rest period.
L34@P0:  Oxaliplatin is administered as a 2-hour infusion before folinic acid and 5-Fluorouracil on days 1, 15, and
L35@P0: 29 of the treatment cycle.

L37@P0: months) unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity develops, whichever occurs first.

L111@P2: TESTS:
L112@P2: Baseline tests:
L113@P2:  FBC, liver and renal profile
L114@P2:  ECG (if patient has compromised cardiac function)
L115@P2:  DPD testing prior to first treatment with 5-Fluorouracil using phenotype and/or genotype testing
L116@P2: unless patient has been previously tested
L117@P2: o In patients with moderate or severe renal impairment, blood uracil levels used for
L118@P2: dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) phenotyping should be interpreted with caution,
L119@P2: as impaired kidney function can lead to increased uracil blood levels. Consequently, there
L120@P2: is an increased risk for incorrect diagnosis of DPD deficiency, which may result in under
L121@P2: dosing of 5-Fluorouracil or other fluoropyrimidines, leading to reduced treatment
L122@P2: efficacy. Genotype testing for DPD deficiency should be considered for patients with renal
L123@P2: impairment.

L286@P5: REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS
L287@P5: Oxaliplatin
L288@P5:  Laryngopharyngeal dysaesthesia: An acute syndrome of laryngopharyngeal dysaesthesia occurs in 1%
L289@P5: - 2% of patients and is characterised by subjective sensations of dysphagia or dyspnoea/feeling of
L290@P5: suffocation, without any objective evidence of respiratory distress (no cyanosis or hypoxia) or of
L291@P5: laryngospasm or bronchospasm. Symptoms are often precipitated by exposure to cold. Although
L292@P5: antihistamines and bronchodilators have been administered in such cases, the symptoms are rapidly
L293@P5: reversible even in the absence of treatment. Prolongation of the infusion helps to reduce the incidence
L294@P5: of this syndrome.
L295@P5: 5-Fluorouracil:
L296@P5: Myocardial ischaemia and angina: Cardiotoxicity is a serious complication during treatment with 5-
L297@P5: Fluorouracil. Patients, especially those with a prior history of cardiac disease or other risk factors,
L298@P5: treated with 5-Fluorouracil, should be carefully monitored during therapy.
L299@P5:  DPD deficiency: DPD is an enzyme encoded by the DPYD gene which is responsible for the breakdown
L300@P5: of fluoropyrimidines. Patients with DPD deficiency are therefore at increased risk of fluoropyrimidinerelated toxicity, including for example stomatitis, diarrhoea, mucosal inflammation, neutropenia and
L301@P5: neurotoxicity. Treatment with 5-Fluorouracil, capecitabine or tegafur-containing medicinal products is
L302@P5: contraindicated in patients with known complete DPD deficiency. Consider a reduced starting dose in
L303@P5: patients with identified partial DPD deficiency. Initial dose reduction may impact the efficacy of
L304@P5: treatment. In the absence of serious toxicity, subsequent doses may be increased with careful
L305@P5: monitoring. Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) of 5-Fluorouracil may improve clinical outcomes in
L306@P5: patients receiving continuous 5-Fluorouracil infusions.
L307@P5:  Hand-foot syndrome (HFS), also known as palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia (PPE) has been reported
L308@P5: as an unusual complication of high dose bolus or protracted continuous therapy for 5-Fluorouracil (see
L309@P5: table 3 for dose modifications).
