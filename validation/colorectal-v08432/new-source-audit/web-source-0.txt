L84@P1:  Blood, renal and liver profile
L85@P1:  ECG (if patient has compromised cardiac function)
L86@P1:  DPD testing prior to first treatment with 5-Fluorouracil using phenotype and/or genotype
L87@P1: testing unless patient has been previously tested
L88@P1: o In patients with moderate or severe renal impairment, blood uracil levels used for
L89@P1: dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) phenotyping should be interpreted with caution,
L90@P1: as impaired kidney function can lead to increased uracil blood levels. Consequently, there

L93@P1: efficacy. Genotype testing for DPD deficiency should be considered for patients with renal
L94@P1: impairment.

L290@P4: ADVERSE EFFECTS / REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS:
L291@P4: The adverse effects listed are not exhaustive. Please refer to the relevant Summary of Product Characteristics for full
L292@P4: details.
L293@P4:  Neutropenia: Fever or other evidence of infection must be assessed promptly and treated
L294@P4: aggressively.
L295@P4:  Gastrointestinal toxicity: Patients treated with 5-Fluorouracil should be closely monitored for
L296@P4: diarrhoea and managed appropriately.
L297@P4:  Hand-foot syndrome (HFS), also known as palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia (PPE), has been
L298@P4: reported as an unusual complication of high dose bolus or protracted continuous therapy for 5-
L299@P4: Fluorouracil.
L300@P4:  Myocardial ischaemia and angina: Cardiotoxicity is a serious complication during treatment with 5-
L301@P4: Fluorouracil. Patients, especially those with a prior history of cardiac disease or other risk factors,
L302@P4: treated with 5-Fluorouracil, should be carefully monitored during therapy.
L303@P4:  DPD deficiency: DPD is an enzyme encoded by the DPYD gene which is responsible for the
L304@P4: breakdown of fluoropyrimidines. Patients with DPD deficiency are therefore at increased risk of
L305@P4: fluoropyrimidine-related toxicity, including for example stomatitis, diarrhoea, mucosal
L306@P4: inflammation, neutropenia and neurotoxicity. Treatment with 5-Fluorouracil, capecitabine or
L307@P4: tegafur-containing medicinal products is contraindicated in patients with known complete DPD
L308@P4: deficiency. Consider a reduced starting dose in patients with identified partial DPD deficiency. Initial
L309@P4: dose reduction may impact the efficacy of treatment. In the absence of serious toxicity, subsequent
L310@P4: doses may be increased with careful monitoring. Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) of 5-
L311@P4: Fluorouracil may improve clinical outcomes in patients receiving continuous 5-Fluorouracil infusions.
L312@P4:  Stomatitis: Sucking ice chips may be considered for patients experiencing stomatitis. Remove
L313@P4: dentures and place ice chips in mouth five minutes before chemotherapy. Continuously swish in
L314@P4: mouth for 30 minutes, replenishing as ice melts. This may cause numbness or headaches, which
L315@P4-5: subside quickly. NCCP National SACT Regimen

L329@P5: DRUG INTERACTIONS:
L330@P5:  Marked elevations of prothrombin time and INR have been reported in patients stabilized on
L331@P5: warfarin therapy following initiation of 5-Fluorouracil regimens.
L332@P5:  Concurrent administration of 5-Fluorouracil and phenytoin may result in increased serum levels of
L333@P5: phenytoin.
L334@P5:  5-Fluorouracil is contraindicated in combination with brivudin, sorivudin and analogues as these are
L335@P5: potent inhibitors of the 5-Fluorouracilmetabolising enzyme DPD.
L336@P5:  Caution should be taken when using 5-Fluorouracil in conjunction with medications which may affect
L337@P5: DPD activity.
L338@P5:  Current drug interaction databases should be consulted for more information.
