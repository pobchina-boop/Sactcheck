L75@P1: TESTS:
L76@P1: Baseline tests:
L77@P1: • FBC, liver and renal profile.
L78@P1: • DPD testing prior to first treatment with 5-Fluorouracil using phenotype and/or genotype testing
L79@P1: unless patient has been previously tested.
L80@P1: o In patients with moderate or severe renal impairment, blood uracil levels used for
L81@P1: dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) phenotyping should be interpreted with caution, as
L82@P1: impaired kidney function can lead to increased uracil blood levels. Consequently, there is an
L83@P1: increased risk for incorrect diagnosis of DPD deficiency, which may result in under dosing of
L84@P1: 5-Fluorouracil or other fluoropyrimidines, leading to reduced treatment efficacy. Genotype
L85@P1: testing for DPD deficiency should be considered for patients with renal impairment.

L211@P4: ADVERSE EFFECTS / REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS:
L212@P4: The adverse effects listed are not exhaustive. Please refer to the relevant Summary of Product Characteristics for full
L213@P4: details.
L214@P4: • Neutropenia: Fever or other evidence of infection must be assessed promptly and treated aggressively.
L215@P4: • Gastrointestinal toxicity: Patients treated with 5-Fluorouracilshould be closely monitored for diarrhoea
L216@P4: and managed appropriately.
L217@P4: • Myocardial ischaemia and angina: Cardiotoxicity is a serious complication during treatment with 5-
L218@P4: Fluorouracil. Patients, especially those with a prior history of cardiac disease or other risk factors,
L219@P4: treated with 5-Fluorouracil, should be carefully monitored during therapy.
L220@P4: • DPD deficiency: DPD is an enzyme encoded by the DPYD gene which is responsible for the breakdown
L221@P4: of fluoropyrimidines. Patients with DPD deficiency are therefore at increased risk of fluoropyrimidinerelated toxicity, including for example stomatitis, diarrhoea, mucosal inflammation, neutropenia and
L222@P4: neurotoxicity. Treatment with 5-Fluorouracil, capecitabine or tegafur-containing medicinal products is
L223@P4: contraindicated in patients with known complete DPD deficiency. Consider a reduced starting dose in
L224@P4: patients with identified partial DPD deficiency. Initial dose reduction may impact the efficacy of
L225@P4: treatment. In the absence of serious toxicity, subsequent doses may be increased with careful
L226@P4: monitoring. Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) of 5-Fluorouracil may improve clinical outcomes in
L227@P4: patients receiving continuous 5-Fluorouracil infusions.
L228@P4: • Hand-foot syndrome, (HFS), also known as palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia (PPE) has been reported
L229@P4: as an unusual complication of high dose bolus or protracted continuous therapy for 5-Fluorouracil (see
L230@P4: Table 3 for dose modifications).
L231@P4: DRUG INTERACTIONS:
L232@P4: • Marked elevations of prothrombin time and INR have been reported in patients stabilised on warfarin
L233@P4: therapy following initiation of 5-Fluorouracil regimes.
L234@P4: • Concurrent administration of 5-Fluorouracil and phenytoin may result in increased serum levels of
L235@P4: phenytoin.
L236@P4: • 5-Fluorouracil is contraindicated in combination with brivudin, sorivudin and analogues as these are
L237@P4: potent inhibitors of the 5-Fluorouracil-metabolising enzyme DPD.
L238@P4: • Current drug interaction databases should be consulted for more information.
