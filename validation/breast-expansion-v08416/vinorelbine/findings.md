# NCCP 00526 and 00798 source audit — v0.84.16

Checked 21 October 2026. Candidate protocols are in `proposed/`; the live protocol files have not been changed by this audit. This is source and software verification, not independent clinical authorisation.

## Material findings

1. **Wrong blood-count action and percentage.** Both current combination sources specify delaying vinorelbine until recovery, then giving 80% of subsequent doses for ANC 0.5–0.99 or platelets 75–99. The existing implementation instead suggested immediate 75% dosing. ANC below 0.5 or platelets below 75 has no quantified restart row in these source tables; candidates withhold and refer for review without inventing a restart dose.
2. **A genuine source ambiguity remains.** The rendered grade ≥3 vinorelbine row on page 6 of both PDFs contains both recovery to grade 1 followed by 80% of original dose and an unqualified discontinuation statement in the same cell. Candidates withhold and explicitly require consultant/pharmacy clarification. They do not choose a permanent-stop or automatic-restart interpretation. The page-6 image is retained in the audit folder.
3. **The oral route was missing.** Both NCCP regimens offer IV or oral vinorelbine on Days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle. IV doses begin at 25 mg/m² and usually become 30 mg/m² from cycle 2; 35 mg/m² is discretionary. Oral begins at 60 mg/m², with discretionary escalation to 80 mg/m² from cycle 2 or 3 if tolerated. The 120 mg cap belongs to the 60 mg/m² tier; the 80 mg/m² BSA table reaches 160 mg. Never combine the routes or infer patient-led escalation.
4. **Different emetogenic plans are necessary.** NCCP V6 (2025) classifies IV vinorelbine as minimal (Table 7, page 12), but oral vinorelbine as moderate–high (Table 8, page 13). Table 5, page 8 supports a selected oral 5-HT3 antagonist: ondansetron 8–16 mg total daily or granisetron 1–2 mg total daily. The prescription must specify dosing around the actual Day 1/Day 8 schedule; do not infer continuous treatment for 21 days. No automatic NK1/steroid high-risk IV script is appropriate merely because the oral category includes “high”.
5. **The product-specific oral guidance is stricter than the generic NCCP table.** The Irish Navelbine capsule SmPC requires counts on the day of each administration and delaying capsules for ANC <1.5 or platelets <100. It also uses different hepatic recommendations (specified mild disorder: 60 mg/m²/week; moderate: 50 mg/m²/week; severe: not recommended). Candidates retain the NCCP combination data but explicitly withhold/refer oral cases for product/protocol reconciliation; they do not silently apply the generic no-adjustment or 66% recommendation to capsules. Oral absorption problems, recent severe infection, long-term oxygen, yellow-fever vaccine and concurrent liver-field radiotherapy need product-specific review.
6. **00798 had the wrong antibody reload.** It inherited pertuzumab 840 mg IV plus trastuzumab 8 mg/kg IV. Its actual ≥6-week reload is Phesgo 1200 mg/600 mg SC, then 600 mg/600 mg every 3 weeks. For a <6-week gap or switch from IV, use 600 mg/600 mg. The 00526 IV reload remains distinct. Both old indication selectors incorrectly named maintenance regimen 00726a.
7. **Baseline and ongoing cardiac assessment were conflated.** Current initiation LVEF <50% was not reliably caught while historical baseline <50% could stop ongoing treatment despite a recovered current result. Candidates distinguish stage and require clarification of missing context. Ongoing current LVEF <40%, or 40–45% with a ≥10-point fall, triggers a hold; missing fall at 40–45% requires review. 00798 explicitly repeats LVEF within 3 weeks; EMA also specifies a hold of at least 3 weeks. Routine echo remains every 12 weeks, approximately cycle 5, then 9, if uninterrupted.
8. **Unsupported inherited vinorelbine rules were removed.** The old grade-2 neuropathy 75%, grade-3 neuropathy permanent-stop, constipation 75%, three-week neutropenia mandatory-stop and previous FN automatic 25% reduction were not supported by these combination PDFs. Candidates require the named clinical review instead.
9. **Active component handling now matters.** HER2-only continuation omits vinorelbine-specific toxicity actions; vinorelbine-only treatment omits antibody reload/cardiac rules. An invalid phase cannot suppress independent safety findings. Single-entry partial assessment remains possible.
10. **Current reactions require an action.** Grades 1–3 now prompt pause/hold and management, with severity-based specialist reassessment. Grade 4 and bronchospasm/ARDS follow permanent-stop requirements from the relevant product information. No general “proceed” message should appear for an active significant reaction.

## Exact longitudinal distinction

The NCCP documents specify FBC/renal/liver before each cycle, baseline echo/MUGA and peripheral-neuropathy review, and echo/MUGA every 12 weeks (4–8 weeks may be more appropriate if cardiac impairment appears). The oral SmPC adds blood counts for each administration, including Day 8. The regimen PDFs do not mandate a universal baseline ECG, 3-monthly CT, tumour-marker schedule or DEXA. Any international ECG/response-imaging guidance should retain its separate attribution and individualisation. Switching route or entering maintenance does not reset the cardiac clock.

00526 IV antibody infusions and observation differ from Phesgo: first pertuzumab 60-minute infusion plus 1-hour observation; later 30–60-minute observation, with omission allowed by NCCP after 3 consecutive uneventful pertuzumab treatments. Trastuzumab observation is at least 6 hours from the start of the first infusion and 2 hours from the start of subsequent infusions, with local deviations documented. 00798 is a single thigh injection over 8 minutes with 30 minutes observation when loading, or 5 minutes with 15 minutes observation in maintenance, completed before chemotherapy. Phesgo observation should not inherit the IV pertuzumab omission rule.

## Efficacy evidence limits

VELVET cohort 1 was a single-arm phase II study (106 patients) of IV pertuzumab/trastuzumab and IV vinorelbine, without prior advanced-disease chemotherapy/biological therapy. Investigator-assessed ORR was 74.2% (95% CI 63.8–82.9) among 89 patients with measurable disease; median PFS was 14.3 months (95% CI 11.2–17.5) in the ITT group. These are descriptive outcomes, not incremental benefit versus docetaxel or another option, and do not establish an OS advantage. They do not directly test oral vinorelbine or Phesgo. FeDeriCa is formulation/PK bridging, not evidence of additional survival from the SC route. Numerical efficacy belongs in the clinician knowledge base only.
