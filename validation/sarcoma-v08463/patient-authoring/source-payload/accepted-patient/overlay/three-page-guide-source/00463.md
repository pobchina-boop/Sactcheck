## Page 1 source: purpose and treatment

The three medicines act through different mechanisms against osteosarcoma throughout the body. Surgery removes the visible tumour, and chemotherapy before and after surgery aims to reduce recurrence risk.

Surgery-linked reference course: weeks 1–10, surgery week 11, then weeks 12–29

**Before surgery: weeks 1–10** Doxorubicin and cisplatin on days 1–2 of weeks 1 and 6; methotrexate in weeks 4, 5, 9 and 10.

**Surgery: reference week 11** Actual timing depends on the operation and recovery. Additional preoperative methotrexate is only a separate consultant decision.

**After surgery: weeks 12–29** Doxorubicin in weeks 12, 17, 22 and 26; cisplatin in weeks 12 and 17; methotrexate in weeks 15, 16, 20, 21, 24, 25, 28 and 29.

This course is limited to the stated age, site and complete-resection context. Up to two additional preoperative methotrexate doses may be prescribed if surgery is delayed. These are not automatic. Poor response on surgical pathology does not automatically justify adding ifosfamide/etoposide.

## Page 2 source: body map and risks

**Brain and alertness** New confusion, unusual sleepiness, hallucinations, unsteadiness or seizures needs immediate assessment.

**Hearing** Report ringing ears or reduced hearing promptly. Hearing changes can persist.

**Mouth** Report ulcers, painful swallowing or difficulty eating and drinking early.

**Heart** New breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations, faintness or swelling needs urgent assessment.

**Liver** Blood tests monitor liver effects; report yellow eyes or dark urine promptly.

**Kidneys** Report reduced urine or inability to drink. Kidney damage and electrolyte losses may need extra tests.

**Blood cells and infection** Fever, shaking chills, sudden illness or unusual bleeding requires immediate oncology advice.

**Bowel and appetite** Use prescribed anti-sickness support; report persistent vomiting, severe diarrhoea or difficulty drinking.

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Read this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk.

Read the complete medicine-specific risk sections in the handbook and approved local consent discussion.

## Page 3 source: checks, support and urgent help

**Full blood count, kidney and liver tests** Before each treatment. Check creatinine daily during methotrexate treatment and clearance monitoring.

**Methotrexate concentrations** At 24, 48, 72, 96 and 120 hours as appropriate from infusion START; continue later checks when required for delayed clearance.

**Urine pH and fluid balance** Confirm urine pH at least 7 before methotrexate and check every 6 hours during treatment/rescue; record fluid balance every 4 hours.

**Folinic acid rescue** Starts 24 hours after methotrexate START and continues under the specialist clearance plan. Low counts or a delay to later chemotherapy must not automatically stop rescue.

**Organ and symptom review** Hearing/nerve review before each course. Heart tracing and baseline/serial imaging follow the reconciled anthracycline plan; symptoms may need extra checks.

**Surgery and postoperative review** Surgery at reference week 11; confirm actual dates, recovery and pathology before the postoperative plan.

Rescue and blood levels follow methotrexate START time. No fixed clearance or discharge date.

Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Reduced urine, severe mouth ulcers or diarrhoea, new neurological symptoms or delayed methotrexate clearance needs urgent specialist assessment.
