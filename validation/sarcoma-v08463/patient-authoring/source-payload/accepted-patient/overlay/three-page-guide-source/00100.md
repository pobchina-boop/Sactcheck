## Page 1 source: purpose and treatment

Mifamurtide stimulates innate immune cells to help act against remaining osteosarcoma cells after complete surgery. It is an additional postoperative treatment discussed with the patient and family, alongside chemotherapy.

48 infusions over 36 weeks: twice weekly for 12 weeks, then weekly for 24 weeks

**Weeks 1–12: two infusions each week** At least three days between the two weekly infusions: 24 infusions in this phase.

**Weeks 13–36: one infusion each week** Another 24 infusions, for 48 total over 36 weeks. The clock is independent of chemotherapy.

Only the completely resected, nonmetastatic postoperative context is covered. European product information describes evidence in patients aged 2–30 at initial diagnosis; the team reconciles this with the national age wording. Mifamurtide is an innate immune activator, not a checkpoint inhibitor, and should not automatically receive checkpoint-specific consent or steroid pathways.

## Page 2 source: body map and risks

**Headache and dizziness** Headache or dizziness may follow infusion. Severe headache, confusion, seizures or collapse needs urgent assessment.

**Heart** New breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations, faintness or swelling needs urgent assessment.

**Breathing** Report new wheeze, cough or breathlessness promptly; severe breathing difficulty is an emergency.

**Liver monitoring** Liver blood tests continue during combined treatment and after chemotherapy ends until all therapy is complete. Report yellow eyes, dark urine or new significant abdominal pain promptly.

**Kidney monitoring** Blood and urine checks monitor kidney function during combined treatment and after chemotherapy finishes until the whole course ends. Report blood or pain when passing urine, reduced urine or difficulty drinking promptly.

**Fever and infection** Fever may follow infusion but can also signal serious infection. Do not wait eight hours before seeking advice if unwell.

**Bowel and appetite** Use prescribed anti-sickness support; report persistent vomiting, severe diarrhoea or difficulty drinking.

**Muscles and joints** Aches may occur. New swollen joints or persistent worsening inflammatory symptoms need review.

Mifamurtide has its own innate immune-activation risks. Discuss fever/chills, infusion/allergic reactions, breathing problems, inflammatory and cardiovascular symptoms, gastrointestinal effects, dizziness and limited reproductive-safety data. The general chemotherapy section applies to the separately prescribed concurrent chemotherapy: Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Read this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk.

Read the complete medicine-specific risk sections in the handbook and approved local consent discussion.

## Page 3 source: checks, support and urgent help

**Full blood count** Before starting and at the source-defined treatment-cycle reviews; the team writes the actual twice-weekly/weekly-phase test dates.

**Kidney and liver tests** Before starting and at scheduled reviews; continue after chemotherapy ends until all mifamurtide therapy is complete.

**Clotting tests** After the first dose and again after several doses under product information; confirm exact appointments.

**Heart ultrasound** Annually or according to local practice in the national source; concurrent anthracyclines may require extra checks.

**Fever, breathing, allergy and cardiovascular review** Observe during administration; fever or sudden illness needs immediate advice between visits. Do not wait eight hours when unwell.

48 infusions on a separate 36-week clock. Fever or sudden illness needs immediate advice.

Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Do not wait eight hours to seek advice for fever or sudden illness. Severe wheeze, breathing difficulty, chest pain or faintness needs urgent assessment.
