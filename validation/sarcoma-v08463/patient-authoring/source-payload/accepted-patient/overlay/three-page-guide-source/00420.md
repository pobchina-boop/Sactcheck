## Page 1 source: purpose and treatment

Chemotherapy treats osteosarcoma cells throughout the body, including cells too small to see on scans, alongside surgery intended to remove the visible cancer.

Doxorubicin days 1–3 and cisplatin day 1; six 21-day cycles

**Treatment** Doxorubicin 25 mg/m² is given on days 1, 2 and 3; cisplatin 100 mg/m² on day 1. The reference course contains six 21-day cycles, usually three before surgery and three afterwards, with pathology review. Treatment dates and doses depend on recovery and the specialist plan.

This is the six-cycle doxorubicin/cisplatin course. It is distinct from the surgery-linked high-dose methotrexate combination. The national source gives no numeric age cutoff. Lifetime anthracycline exposure and individual heart risk must be reconciled.

## Page 2 source: body map and risks

**Hearing** Report ringing ears or reduced hearing promptly. Hearing changes can persist.

**Mouth** Report ulcers, painful swallowing or difficulty eating and drinking early.

**Heart** New breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations, faintness or swelling needs urgent assessment.

**Kidneys** Report reduced urine or inability to drink. Kidney damage and electrolyte losses may need extra tests.

**Blood cells and infection** Fever, shaking chills, sudden illness or unusual bleeding requires immediate oncology advice.

**Bowel and appetite** Use prescribed anti-sickness support; report persistent vomiting, severe diarrhoea or difficulty drinking.

**Skin, hair and infusion line** Hair and skin changes may occur. Report line-site pain, burning, swelling or a severe rash immediately.

**Nerves and function** Report tingling, numbness or weakness before another dose; these effects can affect walking or hand tasks.

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Read this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk.

Read the complete medicine-specific risk sections in the handbook and approved local consent discussion.

## Page 3 source: checks, support and urgent help

**Full blood count, kidney, liver and electrolytes** Before starting and before each 21-day cycle; repeat sooner for illness or abnormal results.

**Heart tracing and pumping-function imaging** Heart tracing before starting. Doxorubicin product information recommends baseline imaging and repeats after approximately each accumulated 100 mg/m²; the team confirms the reconciled schedule.

**Hearing and nerve review** Review symptoms before more cisplatin; formal hearing tests at baseline or later when clinically indicated. Report changes immediately.

**Surgery and pathology review** Usually after three preoperative cycles, with postoperative review before the remaining treatment; scans follow the specialist plan.

Report hearing changes, reduced urine, chest symptoms or infusion-site pain promptly.

Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Report new hearing loss, reduced urine, persistent vomiting, chest symptoms or painful infusion sites urgently.

Six planned 21-day cycles, usually three before and three after surgery. Recording spaces do not define duration.

Keep verified daytime and out-of-hours oncology contact numbers. The information website is not an emergency contact service.
