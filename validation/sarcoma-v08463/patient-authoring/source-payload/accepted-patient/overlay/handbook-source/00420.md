# Doxorubicin and cisplatin for osteosarcoma

External educational source draft · 00420 · checked 2026-11-18. Independent clinical, pharmacy and patient review pending.


## Treatment purpose

Chemotherapy treats osteosarcoma cells throughout the body, including cells too small to see on scans, alongside surgery intended to remove the visible cancer.

## Setting and schedule

Chemotherapy before and after surgery for osteosarcoma

Doxorubicin 25 mg/m² is given on days 1, 2 and 3; cisplatin 100 mg/m² on day 1. The reference course contains six 21-day cycles, usually three before surgery and three afterwards, with pathology review. Treatment dates and doses depend on recovery and the specialist plan.

Doxorubicin is given through supervised intravenous access. Cisplatin is infused over 2 hours, with prescribed fluids and electrolytes before and afterwards. The full visit is longer than the drug infusion. A hospital fluid prescription is not a home drinking target.

## Doxorubicin

Disrupts genetic material and the enzyme that repairs it

Doxorubicin enters deoxyribonucleic acid, the genetic material called DNA, and interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme needed when cells copy and repair their genes. This damages tumour cells. Normal marrow, mouth and heart cells can also be injured.

Report fever, mouth ulcers, breathlessness, ankle swelling, palpitations or pain at the drip immediately.

- Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
- Nausea, vomiting, mouth ulcers, appetite changes and diarrhoea
- Hair loss, fatigue and skin or nail changes
- Heart muscle damage or abnormal rhythm; damage can appear later and relates to lifetime anthracycline exposure
- Severe local tissue damage if the drug leaks outside the vein; allergic reactions
- Fertility and pregnancy harm; uncommon later blood cancers

## Cisplatin

Crosslinks genetic material

Cisplatin forms links within and between strands of DNA, the genetic material. These links stop cells copying their instructions normally. Kidney cells, the inner ear and nerves may also be injured, so prescribed fluids and organ checks are essential.

Report reduced urine, persistent vomiting, ringing ears, hearing changes, numbness or weakness before further treatment.

- Severe nausea and vomiting; dehydration can worsen kidney injury
- Kidney damage and low magnesium, potassium or other electrolytes
- Hearing loss or ringing in the ears, sometimes permanent
- Numbness, tingling or weakness from nerve damage, sometimes lasting
- Low blood counts, serious infection, anaemia and bleeding
- Allergic reactions, infusion-site injury, fertility and pregnancy harm

## Individual tests and timings

### Full blood count

Purpose: Check infection-fighting cells, haemoglobin and platelets.

Timing: Before starting and before each 21-day cycle; repeat sooner for fever, bleeding or abnormal results.

### Kidney function and electrolytes

Purpose: Assess cisplatin kidney safety and identify magnesium or other electrolyte losses.

Timing: Before starting and before each cycle; additional checks accompany the prescribed hydration plan and reduced urine, dehydration or abnormal results.

### Liver tests

Purpose: Assess doxorubicin handling and detect liver injury.

Timing: Before starting and before each cycle; repeat when results or symptoms warrant.

### Heart tracing and heart imaging

Purpose: Assess cardiac risk alongside lifetime anthracycline exposure.

Timing: Heart tracing before starting. Heart ultrasound or multigated acquisition scan if age above 65 or clinically indicated in the source; repeat imaging when indicated by symptoms or risk. The linked doxorubicin product information additionally recommends baseline pumping-function imaging and repeats after approximately each accumulated 100 mg/m². The specialist team reconciles this with the national source and writes the cardiac monitoring schedule.

### Hearing assessment

Purpose: Identify cisplatin-related hearing injury.

Timing: Review symptoms before further cisplatin; formal audiology at baseline or later when clinically indicated. Report changes immediately.

### Nerve and symptom review

Purpose: Detect neuropathy and other treatment effects before more dosing.

Timing: At each treatment review and whenever numbness, weakness or other symptoms change.

### Surgery, pathology and response review

Purpose: Coordinate cancer control and postoperative treatment.

Timing: Review at the planned transition after three preoperative cycles and after surgery; scans and further reviews follow the consultant-set plan.

### Other treatment and follow-up

A specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team plans chemotherapy together with complete removal of the tumour and any other resectable disease sites. The operation, reconstruction, rehabilitation and pathology review are central parts of the plan. Radiotherapy can be considered when surgery is not feasible or margins are inadequate; it is not automatically added to every osteosarcoma course.

## Tests and reviews

Use the individual test cards for purposes and timings.

### Confirm the next tests

Before leaving, confirm which tests are due, where they will be done and who reviews each result.

### Safety and treatment benefit

Normal blood tests do not show that the tumour is responding. The specialist plans separate imaging, symptom and function reviews.

## Support and daily life

Follow the written plan agreed with your team.

### Support medicines

Cisplatin has high nausea/vomiting risk. Agree prevention and rescue anti-sickness medicines, prescribed hydration and electrolyte replacement. A separate white-cell growth-factor plan may be chosen for clinical need. Do not add diuretics or change fluid intake yourself.

### Intravenous access and care

The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements. Never change pump settings or disconnect an infusion yourself.

### Fertility and pregnancy

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Obtain written contraception, pregnancy and breastfeeding instructions for every medicine, including how long precautions continue after the final dose. Different medicines have different requirements. Contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible. Children and young people need age-appropriate information, assent and the applicable parent or guardian consent process.

## Symptoms and help

Report new or worsening symptoms promptly.

### Call now

Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Report new hearing loss, reduced urine, persistent vomiting, chest symptoms or painful infusion sites urgently.

### During a drip

Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, rash, chills, breathlessness, faintness or pain/swelling at the infusion site.

### Your contacts

Keep verified daytime and out-of-hours oncology numbers with your treatment card. The quick response code website is information, not an emergency service.

## Your treatment decision

Read the general treatment discussion alongside the individual medicine risks.

### General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Read this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk.

### Priorities, alternatives and uncertainty

Discuss the individual aim, realistic benefit and uncertainty, treatment burden, alternatives, serious and lasting risks, fertility, personal priorities and the option to decline or reconsider. This is the six-cycle doxorubicin/cisplatin course. It is distinct from the surgery-linked high-dose methotrexate combination. The national source gives no numeric age cutoff. Lifetime anthracycline exposure and individual heart risk must be reconciled.

### Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Obtain written contraception, pregnancy and breastfeeding instructions for every medicine, including how long precautions continue after the final dose. Different medicines have different requirements. Contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible. ## Understanding the evidence

A historical randomised study of operable, nonmetastatic osteosarcoma found similar observed five-year survival and progression outcomes with six courses of doxorubicin/cisplatin and a much longer multidrug course. More patients completed the shorter course.

The comparator was a historical 44-week regimen. This is not a modern direct comparison against methotrexate/doxorubicin/cisplatin and does not prove equivalence to all current regimens or predict an individual outcome.

### Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

### Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

### Can treatment restart when a blood test improves?

Only when the treating team confirms a new plan. A previous serious reaction, stopped medicine or required protective treatment still matters.

### How do I prepare for each visit?

Bring the full medicine list, report infection or hearing/nerve changes, and confirm the anti-sickness and hydration prescription. Discuss heart history, all prior anthracyclines, fertility and the surgery plan.
