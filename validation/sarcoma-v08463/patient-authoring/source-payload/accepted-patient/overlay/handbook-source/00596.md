# Ifosfamide and etoposide for Ewing sarcoma or osteosarcoma

External educational source draft · 00596 · checked 2026-11-18. Independent clinical, pharmacy and patient review pending.


## Treatment purpose

The two medicines damage tumour genetic material through different mechanisms to help control sarcoma. The intended role, chance of benefit and how this fits with surgery, radiotherapy or trials depend on the diagnosis and previous treatment.

## Setting and schedule

Relapsed Ewing sarcoma, or treatment of osteosarcoma under the national source indication

Etoposide 100 mg/m² is infused over 60 minutes, followed by ifosfamide 1,800 mg/m² over 60 minutes, on each of days 1–5. Courses repeat every 21 days for up to seven cycles while benefit and tolerability justify treatment. Mesna is given before each ifosfamide infusion and at 4 and 8 hours measured from its START.

The daily sequence includes etoposide, protective mesna, then ifosfamide, with further mesna at the prescribed times. Hydration starts before the first ifosfamide dose and continues for 24 hours after the last dose ends. Monitoring and supportive treatment make attendance longer than the drug infusion times.

## Ifosfamide

A liver-activated medicine damages genetic material

The liver converts ifosfamide into active compounds that crosslink DNA, the genetic material, preventing normal cell division. Other breakdown products can damage the bladder, kidneys or brain. Mesna reduces bladder injury; it does not prevent kidney injury or brain toxicity.

New confusion, unusual sleepiness, hallucinations, unsteadiness, seizures, blood in urine or reduced urine needs immediate assessment.

- Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
- Nausea, vomiting, mouth soreness, hair loss and fatigue
- Brain toxicity, called encephalopathy: confusion, drowsiness, hallucinations or seizures; may be severe
- Kidney tubular injury, electrolyte loss and reduced kidney function, which may persist
- Bladder inflammation and bleeding despite protective mesna; report blood or pain when passing urine
- Possible heart or lung injury, allergic reactions, fertility and pregnancy harm, and later cancers

## Etoposide

Prevents repair of breaks in genetic material

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme that controls breaks in DNA, the genetic material. Damaged tumour cells cannot divide normally. The bone marrow and other healthy tissues are also affected.

Tell the nurse immediately about dizziness, flushing, rash, chest tightness, breathlessness or feeling faint during infusion.

- Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
- Nausea, vomiting, appetite loss, mouth soreness, diarrhoea or constipation
- Hair loss and fatigue
- Allergic or severe infusion reactions and low blood pressure, especially if given too quickly
- Local inflammation or tissue damage around the infusion site; liver effects and rare severe skin reactions
- Fertility and pregnancy harm and uncommon later blood cancers

## Individual tests and timings

### Full blood count

Purpose: Assess infection risk, anaemia and bleeding risk.

Timing: Before starting and before each 21-day cycle; extra testing follows fever, bleeding or abnormal results. Ifosfamide product information additionally specifies counts before each administration and appropriate intervals afterwards; the team reconciles the daily course monitoring.

### Kidney and liver tests

Purpose: Assess drug handling and detect organ injury.

Timing: Before starting and before each cycle. Kidney filtration and tubular function also need assessment during and after ifosfamide, with timing intensified for abnormal results or symptoms.

### Neurological assessment

Purpose: Detect early ifosfamide brain toxicity.

Timing: Before EVERY ifosfamide dose and urgently for new confusion, sleepiness, hallucinations, unsteadiness or seizures.

### Urine checks for blood

Purpose: Detect bladder injury despite mesna.

Timing: Before each ifosfamide dose and every 8 hours on treatment days; report blood or urinary pain immediately.

### Fluid balance and weight

Purpose: Detect low urine output, fluid retention and dehydration.

Timing: Monitor fluid balance during treatment and record weight daily. Prescribed hydration begins before ifosfamide and continues for 24 hours after the final ifosfamide infusion ends.

### Tumour, symptom and function review

Purpose: Assess benefit separately from treatment safety.

Timing: Before continuation at the specialist-set review points; scan timing depends on the individual Ewing sarcoma or osteosarcoma treatment plan.

### Sodium, potassium and phosphate

Purpose: Detect electrolyte losses including ifosfamide tubular injury.

Timing: At baseline and as regular tests with treatment-cycle reviews; repeat during treatment or recovery when results, fluid balance or symptoms require.

### Blood pressure and etoposide reaction monitoring

Purpose: Detect infusion-related hypotension or allergy.

Timing: Monitor during infusion, especially the first 15 minutes. Tell staff immediately about dizziness, flushing, breathlessness or feeling faint.

## Support and daily life

Follow the written plan agreed with your team.

### Support medicines

This combination has high overall nausea/vomiting risk and requires white-cell growth-factor support for every cycle, timed by local prescription. Mesna reduces bladder injury but does not protect the kidneys or brain. Pharmacy must review anti-sickness interactions, particularly aprepitant, and specify all delayed and rescue treatment.

### Intravenous access and care

The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements. Never change pump settings or disconnect an infusion yourself.

### Fertility and pregnancy

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Obtain written contraception, pregnancy and breastfeeding instructions for every medicine, including how long precautions continue after the final dose. Different medicines have different requirements. Contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible. Children and young people need age-appropriate information, assent and the applicable parent or guardian consent process.

## Symptoms and help

Report new or worsening symptoms promptly.

### Call now

Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Any new confusion or unusual sleepiness, blood in urine, low urine output or infusion-related faintness needs immediate assessment.

### During a drip

Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, rash, chills, breathlessness, faintness or pain/swelling at the infusion site.

### Your contacts

Keep verified daytime and out-of-hours oncology numbers with your treatment card. The quick response code website is information, not an emergency service.

## Your treatment decision

Read the general treatment discussion alongside the individual medicine risks.

### General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Read this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk.

### Priorities, alternatives and uncertainty

Discuss the individual aim, realistic benefit and uncertainty, treatment burden, alternatives, serious and lasting risks, fertility, personal priorities and the option to decline or reconsider. Relapse is explicit for Ewing sarcoma; the national osteosarcoma indication does not add a relapse qualifier. The source gives no numeric age cutoff. Etoposide use for this indication is unlicensed in Ireland and requires the documented local discussion and governance process.

### Unlicensed indication: etoposide

The national source states that this use of etoposide is an unlicensed indication in Ireland. Your clinician must explain the reason, evidence, uncertainties and alternatives, obtain consent under hospital policy, and confirm acknowledgement by the local Drugs and Therapeutics Committee or equivalent.

## Understanding the evidence

A historical single-arm study showed tumour shrinkage in some previously treated patients, including small groups with Ewing sarcoma and osteosarcoma. The lack of a comparison group and very small disease-specific groups limit the estimate of benefit for today’s individual patient.

Of 124 enrolled patients, 77 were evaluable for response. Mixed tumour types, small subgroups and short follow-up limit interpretation. The trial’s longer course must not replace the current national maximum of seven cycles. Response does not establish improved survival.

### Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

### Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

### Can treatment restart when a blood test improves?

Only when the treating team confirms a new plan. A previous serious reaction, stopped medicine or required protective treatment still matters.
