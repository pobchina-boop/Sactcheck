# Methotrexate, doxorubicin and cisplatin for osteosarcoma

External educational source draft · 00463 · checked 2026-11-18. Independent clinical, pharmacy and patient review pending.


## Treatment purpose

The three medicines act through different mechanisms against osteosarcoma throughout the body. Surgery removes the visible tumour, and chemotherapy before and after surgery aims to reduce recurrence risk.

## Setting and schedule

High-grade osteosarcoma of an extremity or axial skeleton, excluding craniofacial sites, with all disease sites amenable to complete surgical resection; national eligibility age 40 years or younger

The reference plan is not a repeating 21-day cycle. Before surgery, doxorubicin 37.5 mg/m² and cisplatin 60 mg/m² are each given on days 1 and 2 of weeks 1 and 6; methotrexate 12 g/m² is given on day 1 of weeks 4, 5, 9 and 10. Surgery is planned for reference week 11. Afterwards, doxorubicin is given on days 1 and 2 of weeks 12, 17, 22 and 26; cisplatin on days 1 and 2 of weeks 12 and 17; methotrexate on day 1 of weeks 15, 16, 20, 21, 24, 25, 28 and 29. Actual dates depend on surgery, recovery, drug clearance and safety review.

Methotrexate is infused over 4 hours after at least 6 hours of prescribed hydration and urine alkalinisation. Folinic acid rescue starts 24 hours after the methotrexate infusion starts, usually every 6 hours initially, and is adjusted to timed levels and kidney function. Cisplatin runs over 4 hours with its own fluid prescription. Doxorubicin may be given by intravenous push or a separately prescribed 48-hour central-line infusion.

## High-dose methotrexate

Blocks folate-dependent production of genetic material

Methotrexate blocks processes that use folate to make DNA, the genetic material. At this high dose it requires carefully timed folinic acid rescue to protect healthy cells. The kidneys remove methotrexate: slow removal can increase exposure and severe toxicity. Hydration, urine alkalinisation, blood levels and rescue are inseparable parts of treatment.

Report mouth ulcers, severe diarrhoea, reduced urine, dry cough, breathlessness or new neurological symptoms urgently. Do not stop rescue medicine or start other medicines yourself.

- Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
- Severe mouth or bowel inflammation and ulceration, nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea
- Kidney injury and delayed clearance; toxicity may require prolonged admission and intensified rescue
- Liver injury and liver-test changes; lung inflammation
- Uncommon serious brain or nerve effects and severe skin reactions
- Fertility and pregnancy harm, rare later cancers and serious interactions with other medicines

## Doxorubicin

Disrupts genetic material and the enzyme that repairs it

Doxorubicin enters deoxyribonucleic acid, the genetic material called DNA, and interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme needed when cells copy and repair their genes. This damages tumour cells. Normal marrow, mouth and heart cells can also be injured.

Report fever, mouth ulcers, breathlessness, ankle swelling, palpitations or pain at the drip immediately.

- Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
- Nausea, vomiting, mouth ulcers, appetite changes and diarrhoea
- Hair loss, fatigue and skin or nail changes
- Heart muscle damage or abnormal rhythm; damage can appear later and relates to lifetime anthracycline exposure
- Severe local tissue damage if the drug leaks outside the vein; allergic reactions
- Fertility and pregnancy harm; uncommon later blood cancers

## Cisplatin

Crosslinks genetic material

Cisplatin forms links within and between strands of DNA, the genetic material. These links stop cells copying their instructions normally. Kidney cells, the inner ear and nerves may also be injured, so prescribed fluids and organ checks are essential.

Report reduced urine, persistent vomiting, ringing ears, hearing changes, numbness or weakness before further treatment.

- Severe nausea and vomiting; dehydration can worsen kidney injury
- Kidney damage and low magnesium, potassium or other electrolytes
- Hearing loss or ringing in the ears, sometimes permanent
- Numbness, tingling or weakness from nerve damage, sometimes lasting
- Low blood counts, serious infection, anaemia and bleeding
- Allergic reactions, infusion-site injury, fertility and pregnancy harm

## Individual tests and timings

### Full blood count

Purpose: Check infection, anaemia and bleeding risk for the selected drug phase.

Timing: Before starting and before each treatment, with repeat checks as indicated. Methotrexate and doxorubicin/cisplatin use different source treatment thresholds.

### Kidney function and daily creatinine

Purpose: Assess high-dose methotrexate clearance and cisplatin injury.

Timing: Before each treatment; creatinine daily during methotrexate administration and clearance monitoring. Further testing follows a rise in creatinine or delayed clearance.

### Methotrexate blood concentration

Purpose: Guide safe continuation and adjustment of folinic acid rescue.

Timing: At 24, 48, 72, 96 and 120 hours as appropriate from the actual infusion START; continue later measurements if the specialist clearance plan requires them.

### Urine acidity

Purpose: Reduce methotrexate precipitation in the kidneys.

Timing: Confirm urine pH at least 7 before methotrexate, then check every 6 hours during methotrexate and rescue while prescribed alkalinisation continues.

### Fluid balance and hydration

Purpose: Maintain clearance while identifying fluid retention or reduced output.

Timing: Begin prescribed prehydration at least 6 hours before methotrexate; record fluid balance every 4 hours during treatment. Cisplatin has a separate pre/post-hydration prescription.

### Liver tests

Purpose: Detect hepatic effects and interpret persistent abnormalities.

Timing: Before each treatment; repeat after abnormal results or symptoms. The team distinguishes expected transient methotrexate-related changes from persistent injury.

### Coagulation and urine screening

Purpose: Identify baseline bleeding and urinary abnormalities.

Timing: Coagulation tests and urine dipstick for blood, protein and glucose before starting; repeats are directed by findings and the clinical plan.

### Heart function

Purpose: Detect anthracycline injury.

Timing: Heart tracing before starting; imaging when clinically indicated. Symptoms or abnormal pumping measurements trigger a specific reassessment before further doxorubicin. The linked doxorubicin product information additionally recommends baseline pumping-function imaging and repeats after approximately each accumulated 100 mg/m². The specialist team reconciles this with the national source and writes the cardiac monitoring schedule.

### Hearing and nerve assessment

Purpose: Identify cisplatin injury that may change future doses.

Timing: Assess hearing symptoms and sensory nerve damage before each course; audiology when clinically indicated.

### Surgery, pathology and tumour review

Purpose: Connect local treatment, response and postoperative planning.

Timing: Plan surgery at reference week 11 after induction, review resection/pathology afterwards, and individualise imaging and follow-up.

A specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team plans chemotherapy together with complete removal of the tumour and any other resectable disease sites. ## Support and daily life

Follow the written plan agreed with your team.

### Support medicines

Cisplatin has high nausea/vomiting risk; doxorubicin and high-dose methotrexate have moderate risk. Each visit needs its own reconciled anti-sickness prescription. Methotrexate hydration, alkalinisation, level monitoring and folinic acid rescue must continue until the specialist team confirms safe clearance. Delayed clearance may need intensified rescue or glucarpidase. No fixed discharge day is promised. Low blood counts or a delay to the next chemotherapy dose must not automatically withhold or stop folinic acid rescue for methotrexate already given.

### Intravenous access and care

The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements. Never change pump settings or disconnect an infusion yourself.

### Fertility and pregnancy

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Obtain written contraception, pregnancy and breastfeeding instructions for every medicine, including how long precautions continue after the final dose. Different medicines have different requirements. Contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible. Children and young people need age-appropriate information, assent and the applicable parent or guardian consent process.

## Symptoms and help

Report new or worsening symptoms promptly.

### Call now

Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Reduced urine, severe mouth ulcers or diarrhoea, new neurological symptoms or delayed methotrexate clearance needs urgent specialist assessment.

### During a drip

Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, rash, chills, breathlessness, faintness or pain/swelling at the infusion site.

### Your contacts

Keep verified daytime and out-of-hours oncology numbers with your treatment card. The quick response code website is information, not an emergency service.

## Your treatment decision

Read the general treatment discussion alongside the individual medicine risks.

### General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Read this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk.

### Priorities, alternatives and uncertainty

Discuss the individual aim, realistic benefit and uncertainty, treatment burden, alternatives, serious and lasting risks, fertility, personal priorities and the option to decline or reconsider. This course is limited to the stated age, site and complete-resection context. Up to two additional preoperative methotrexate doses may be prescribed if surgery is delayed. These are not automatic. Poor response on surgical pathology does not automatically justify adding ifosfamide/etoposide.

### Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Obtain written contraception, pregnancy and breastfeeding instructions for every medicine, including how long precautions continue after the final dose. Different medicines have different requirements. Contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible. Children and young people need age-appropriate information, assent and the applicable parent or guardian consent process.

## Understanding the evidence

The international study established a large practical treatment platform. In the separate randomised comparison after a poor pathological response, adding ifosfamide and etoposide did not improve event-free survival and increased toxicity. The specialist team interprets pathology together with the whole treatment plan.

The pre-randomisation report is not comparative survival proof. The poor-response randomisation studied postoperative intensification, not treatment of relapse. Its result must not be used to withhold all future salvage options.

### Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

### Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

### Can treatment restart when a blood test improves?

Only when the treating team confirms a new plan. A previous serious reaction, stopped medicine or required protective treatment still matters.

### How do the operation and other treatments fit?

A specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team plans chemotherapy together with complete removal of the tumour and any other resectable disease sites. The operation, reconstruction, rehabilitation and pathology review are central parts of the plan. Radiotherapy can be considered when surgery is not feasible or margins are inadequate; it is not automatically added to every osteosarcoma course.

### How do I prepare for each visit?

Confirm the entire methotrexate admission, rescue, blood-level and interaction plan. Ask oncology/pharmacy before any new prescription, over-the-counter painkiller, antibiotic or supplement. Agree the operation and recovery plan, fertility preservation and line arrangements.
