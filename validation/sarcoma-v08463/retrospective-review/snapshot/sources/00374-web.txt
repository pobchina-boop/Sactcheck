L185@P3: REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS
L186@P3: • Please refer to the relevant Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for details
L187@P3: • Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS): Including serious cases resulting in death, has been observed
L188@P3: uncommonly with this treatment. Monitor for symptoms including peripheral oedema,
L189@P3: weight gain, abdominal distension, a sense of fullness or bloating, hypotension,
L190@P3: hypoalbuminaemia and/or fatigue. A rapid decline in serum albumin may be indicative of
L191@P3: capillary leak syndrome
L192@P3: • Immunisation with live vaccines: Combination of trabectedin with phenytoin or live
L193@P3: attenuated vaccines is not recommended and with yellow fever vaccine is specifically
L194@P3: contraindicated
L195@P3: • Cardiac Dysfunction: Patients should be monitored for cardiac-related adverse events or
L196@P3: myocardial dysfunction. A thorough cardiac assessment including determination of left
L197@P3: ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) by echocardiogram or multigated acquisition scan (MUGA)
L198@P3: should be conducted before initiation of trabectedin and at 2 to 3-month intervals thereafter
L199@P3: until trabectedin is discontinued. Patients with LVEF less than the lower limit of normal (LVEF
L200@P3: < LLN), prior cumulative anthracycline dose of >300mg/m2, aged > 65 years, or a history of
L201@P3: cardiovascular disease (especially in those with cardiac medication) may be at increased risk
L202@P3-4: of cardiac dysfunction at treatment with trabectedin as monotherapy or in combination with NCCP National SACT Regimen

L217@P4: • Rhabdomyolosis and severe CPK elevations (> 5 x ULN): Trabectedin must not be used in
L218@P4: patients with CPK > 2.5 x ULN. Rhabdomyolysis has been uncommonly reported, usually in
L219@P4: association with myelotoxicity, severe liver function test abnormalities and/or renal or
L220@P4: multiorgan failure. Therefore, CPK should be closely monitored whenever a patient may be
L221@P4: experiencing any of these toxicities or muscle weakness or muscle pain. If rhabdomyolysis
L222@P4: occurs, supportive measures such as parenteral hydration, urine alkalinisation and dialysis
L223@P4: should be promptly established, as indicated. Treatment with trabectedin should be
L224@P4: discontinued until the patient fully recovers. Caution should be taken if medicinal products
L225@P4: associated with rhabdomyolysis (e.g. statins), are administered concomitantly with
L226@P4: trabectedin, since the risk of rhabdomyolysis may be increased
L227@P4: • Injection site reactions: The use of central venous access is strongly recommended. Patients
L228@P4: may develop a potentially severe injection site reaction when trabectedin is administered
L229@P4: through a peripheral venous line. Trabectedin extravasation may cause tissue necrosis
L230@P4: requiring debridement
L231@P4: • Allergic Reactions: During postmarketing experience, hypersensitivity reactions with very rare
L232@P4: occurrence of fatal outcome, have been reported in association with trabectedin
L233@P4: administration either alone or in combination with pegylated DOXOrubicin
L234@P4: • Acute kidney injury: Patient may experience kidney problems including passing of smaller
L235@P4: amount of urine than usual, swelling in legs or feet, nausea, and confusion (acute kidney
L236@P4: injury)
