Supplied local prescription reference

CUH Department of Medical Oncology: Adjuvant medication prescription for chemotherapy High Risk for nausea and vomiting, Version 2 (05/05/2021)

Complete historical CUH reference for reconciliation, not an issued prescription. Preserve one individual treatment-day, delayed and rescue plan. Do not automatically repeat a start-day course, combine competing courses, or transfer fixed adult reference doses to a child. The treating team confirms each item, actual days, interactions and duration. The source classifies the overall ifosfamide regimen as high risk. Individual multiday prevention, delayed support and rescue need specialist reconciliation; the source template day labels must not restart overlapping courses. The local CUH source is retained as a reference list, not as the national antiemetic standard. Nystatin/chlorhexidine are displayed as local mouth-care adjuncts, not universal antiemetic or mucositis prophylaxis. Verify current local oral-care policy. Inherited reference safety notes: Caution: metoclopramide with olanzapine may increase extrapyramidal adverse effects. Dexamethasone may be modified or omitted where the systemic anticancer therapy regimen already includes a steroid. Metoclopramide: adult maximum 10 mg up to three times daily (30 mg/day) and maximum treatment duration 5 days under current European/Irish product information; avoid prolonged repeated use without review.

Additional phase-specific support

  • This combination has high overall nausea/vomiting risk and requires white-cell growth-factor support for every cycle, timed by local prescription. Mesna reduces bladder injury but does not protect the kidneys or brain. Pharmacy must review anti-sickness interactions, particularly aprepitant, and specify all delayed and rescue treatment.
  • Confirm mesna, hydration, white-cell growth-factor support and the complete five-day anti-sickness plan. Review interactions including aprepitant with ifosfamide. The source identifies this etoposide indication as unlicensed in Ireland: the patient must be informed and consented under hospital policy, with acknowledgement by the local Drugs and Therapeutics Committee or equivalent.
  • The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements. Never change pump settings or disconnect an infusion yourself.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
