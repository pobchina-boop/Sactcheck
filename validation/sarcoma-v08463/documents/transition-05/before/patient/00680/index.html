High-dose ifosfamide for recurrent Ewing sarcoma or relapsed osteosarcoma

High-dose ifosfamide aims to control previously treated sarcoma and can sometimes make further local treatment possible. The specialist team weighs potential tumour shrinkage against substantial bone-marrow, brain, kidney and bladder risks.

Recurrent or primary refractory Ewing sarcoma, or relapsed osteosarcoma · NCCP 00680 v2 · Document revised 18 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

The maximum is four cycles. The detailed national eligibility paragraph describes Ewing sarcoma; its timing definitions must not be copied automatically into the separately listed relapsed osteosarcoma indication. No numeric age or performance-status cutoff is invented.

My overall treatment

Five consecutive 24-hour ifosfamide infusions; every 21 days, maximum 4 cycles

21day view

Treatment timing in this window

Ifosfamide: five 24-hour infusionsDay 1–6
Mesna before and during infusionDay 1–6
Hydration and fluid monitoringDay 1–7
  1. TreatmentIfosfamide 3,000 mg/m² is infused over 24 hours on each of days 1–5, giving five consecutive 24-hour infusions. Repeat every 21 days for a maximum of four cycles unless progression or unacceptable toxicity stops treatment sooner. Mesna starts before the first infusion, continues throughout the five-day treatment and includes a final dose on day 6 after the last ifosfamide infusion.

Between treatments

  • Side effects can occur on any day. Use the verified oncology contact plan promptly if unwell.
  • This high-dose regimen has high nausea/vomiting risk and requires white-cell growth-factor support. Mesna and prescribed hydration are essential but do not remove kidney or brain toxicity. The anti-sickness plan needs specific interaction review and coverage for treatment and delayed symptoms.

Bring the medicine list and treatment/contact record. Report new symptoms before treatment.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
