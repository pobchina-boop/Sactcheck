Act on symptoms, not the calendar

When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Reduced urine, severe mouth ulcers or diarrhoea, new neurological symptoms or delayed methotrexate clearance needs urgent specialist assessment.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

The three medicines act through different mechanisms against osteosarcoma throughout the body. Surgery removes the visible tumour, and chemotherapy before and after surgery aims to reduce recurrence risk.

Chemotherapy

High-dose methotrexate

Blocks folate-dependent production of genetic material

Methotrexate blocks processes that use folate to make deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA), the genetic material. At this high dose it requires carefully timed folinic acid rescue to protect healthy cells. The kidneys remove methotrexate: slow removal can increase exposure and severe toxicity. Hydration, urine alkalinisation, blood levels and rescue are inseparable parts of treatment.

Folate-dependent synthesis is blocked↓Tumour and normal cells are affected↓Timed folinic acid protects healthy cells

What to watch for
Report mouth ulcers, severe diarrhoea, reduced urine, dry cough, breathlessness or new neurological symptoms urgently. Do not stop rescue medicine or start other medicines yourself.

Chemotherapy

Doxorubicin

Disrupts genetic material and the enzyme that repairs it

Doxorubicin enters deoxyribonucleic acid, the genetic material called DNA, and interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme needed when cells copy and repair their genes. This damages tumour cells. Normal marrow, mouth and heart cells can also be injured.

Tumour genetic material is damaged↓Cell division is disrupted↓Heart and blood-count surveillance

What to watch for
Report fever, mouth ulcers, breathlessness, ankle swelling, palpitations or pain at the drip immediately.

Chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Crosslinks genetic material

Cisplatin forms links within and between strands of DNA, the genetic material. These links stop cells copying their instructions normally. Kidney cells, the inner ear and nerves may also be injured, so prescribed fluids and organ checks are essential.

Genetic strands become crosslinked↓Tumour replication is interrupted↓Kidney, hearing and nerve checks

What to watch for
Report reduced urine, persistent vomiting, ringing ears, hearing changes, numbness or weakness before further treatment.

Your treatment

The schedule

The reference plan is not a repeating 21-day cycle. Before surgery, doxorubicin 37.5 mg/m² and cisplatin 60 mg/m² are each given on days 1 and 2 of weeks 1 and 6; methotrexate 12 g/m² is given on day 1 of weeks 4, 5, 9 and 10. Surgery is planned for reference week 11. Afterwards, doxorubicin is given on days 1 and 2 of weeks 12, 17, 22 and 26; cisplatin on days 1 and 2 of weeks 12 and 17; methotrexate on day 1 of weeks 15, 16, 20, 21, 24, 25, 28 and 29. Actual dates depend on surgery, recovery, drug clearance and safety review.

How it is given

Methotrexate is infused over 4 hours after at least 6 hours of prescribed hydration and urine alkalinisation. Folinic acid rescue starts 24 hours after the methotrexate infusion starts, usually every 6 hours initially, and is adjusted to timed levels and kidney function. Cisplatin runs over 4 hours with its own fluid prescription. Doxorubicin may be given by intravenous push or a separately prescribed 48-hour central-line infusion.

Other treatment and follow-up

A specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team plans chemotherapy together with complete removal of the tumour and any other resectable disease sites. The operation, reconstruction, rehabilitation and pathology review are central parts of the plan. Radiotherapy can be considered when surgery is not feasible or margins are inadequate; it is not automatically added to every osteosarcoma course.

EURAMOS-1: preoperative platform and randomised methotrexate, doxorubicin and cisplatin plus ifosfamide and etoposide versus methotrexate, doxorubicin and cisplatin in poor responders

The international study established a large practical treatment platform. In the separate randomised comparison after a poor pathological response, adding ifosfamide and etoposide did not improve event-free survival and increased toxicity. The specialist team interprets pathology together with the whole treatment plan.

The pre-randomisation report is not comparative survival proof. The poor-response randomisation studied postoperative intensification, not treatment of relapse. Its result must not be used to withhold all future salvage options.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the entire methotrexate admission, rescue, blood-level and interaction plan. Ask oncology/pharmacy before any new prescription, over-the-counter painkiller, antibiotic or supplement. Agree the operation and recovery plan, fertility preservation and line arrangements.

STEP 3

Intravenous access and care

The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements. Never change pump settings or disconnect an infusion yourself.

STEP 4

Home support

Cisplatin has high nausea/vomiting risk; doxorubicin and high-dose methotrexate have moderate risk. Each visit needs its own reconciled anti-sickness prescription. Methotrexate hydration, alkalinisation, level monitoring and folinic acid rescue must continue until the specialist team confirms safe clearance. Delayed clearance may need intensified rescue or glucarpidase. No fixed discharge day is promised. Low blood counts or a delay to the next chemotherapy dose must not automatically withhold or stop folinic acid rescue for methotrexate already given.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Read this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

The three medicines act through different mechanisms against osteosarcoma throughout the body. Surgery removes the visible tumour, and chemotherapy before and after surgery aims to reduce recurrence risk. A specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team plans chemotherapy together with complete removal of the tumour and any other resectable disease sites. The operation, reconstruction, rehabilitation and pathology review are central parts of the plan. Radiotherapy can be considered when surgery is not feasible or margins are inadequate; it is not automatically added to every osteosarcoma course.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Can treatment restart when a blood test improves?

Only when the treating team confirms a new plan. A previous serious reaction, stopped medicine or required protective treatment still matters.
