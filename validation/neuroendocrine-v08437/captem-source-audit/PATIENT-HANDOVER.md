# CAPTEM patient/monitoring handover

Source: [NCCP 00505 v5, 11 November 2025](https://healthservice.hse.ie/documents/6609/505_v5_Capecitabine_and_temozolomide__zFX2DSB.pdf), freshly checked 5 November 2026. Seven pages; review date 11 November 2030. SHA-256 `b990c71f88fc2762210f47aeaf0709deca48e8559d263eaf110048d5cb3ce827`.

## Schedule and oral treatment (page 1)

- Days 1–9: capecitabine twice daily only.
- Days 10–14: continue capecitabine twice daily and add temozolomide once daily.
- Days 15–28: rest from both anticancer medicines. Supportive medicines continue only as individually prescribed.
- The NCCP plan is six 28-day cycles unless progression or unacceptable toxicity; any individual change or extension needs the treating team's plan. Six complete cycle blocks equal approximately 24 weeks, including rest days; this is not a guarantee of treatment duration or response.
- Capecitabine: with food or within 30 minutes after eating, swallow tablets whole with water; no crushing/cutting.
- Temozolomide: fasting, swallow capsules whole with water; no opening/chewing. If vomiting occurs after the dose, do not take another dose that day.
- Because food instructions differ, the pharmacy needs to provide a practical written daily timing plan for overlap Days 10–14. Do not present the tablets as being swallowed together.
- NCCP specifically says omitted capecitabine doses for toxicity are not replaced (page 2). This is not a complete missed-dose algorithm for either drug; a patient should contact the treatment team/pharmacist and not improvise replacement doses.

## Tests (pages 2 and 6)

Baseline: full blood count, renal and liver profile; hepatitis B surface antigen AND core antibody; DPD testing before first capecitabine unless already tested. INR if taking warfarin, as clinically indicated. Kidney impairment can complicate uracil-based DPD interpretation; specialist interpretation may use genotype testing.

Before each cycle: full blood count, renal and liver profile; INR if clinically indicated for warfarin. In addition, page 6 expressly requires liver tests AFTER each cycle because of temozolomide, with benefit/risk assessment for significant abnormalities. Serious liver injury can emerge several weeks or more after the last dose. Clarify local scheduling rather than implying the pre-cycle test alone satisfies every post-cycle need.

Scans and other disease tests, including any tumour markers, follow the consultant's treatment plan; the source gives no universal fixed imaging or biomarker interval. Do not transplant glioma temozolomide Day-22 or weekly 42-day-radiotherapy schedules into CAPTEM.

## Support and contact advice (pages 2, 5–6)

Capecitabine is oral minimal–low emetogenic risk; temozolomide is oral moderate–high. Have the prescribed anti-sickness plan ready before temozolomide starts on Day 10 and follow the local plan on Days 10–14. “Premedication not usually needed” is not a statement that antiemetics are unnecessary.

PJP prophylaxis is assessed under local policy. Watch for respiratory infection/PJP with temozolomide, particularly with steroids; universal 42-day chemoradiotherapy prophylaxis is a different setting. A positive HBV screen requires the local antiviral/hepatology plan. Hand-foot prevention and diarrhoea treatment are also part of the support plan; loperamide is a source example, but patient prescriptions/escalation should be individualised.

Explain that capecitabine should be interrupted immediately and the team contacted for moderate/severe toxicity, rather than waiting until the next visit. Warn about substantial diarrhoea, painful mouth or hand-foot symptoms, dehydration/poor intake, infection and new breathlessness. Chest pain needs urgent assessment because capecitabine can cause cardiac toxicity; jaundice/dark urine or concerning liver symptoms also require prompt contact. Use the established local urgent-care instructions and verified contact placeholders, without inventing a regimen-specific fever threshold or a universal emergency number.

The page 7 footnote identifies this pancreatic NET indication as off-label in Ireland; preserve the consent boundary and local policy explanation. Patient guides should not turn this into a promise that the treatment suits every NET subtype.
