When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Help control advanced urothelial cancer with a susceptible FGFR3 alteration after previous PD1/PDL1 treatment.

Targeted FGFR inhibitor

Erdafitinib

Erdafitinib
Blocks abnormal FGFR signalling

Some urothelial cancers use an altered fibroblast growth factor receptor, FGFR3, to drive growth. Erdafitinib blocks this signal. FGFR also helps regulate phosphate handling, explaining why blood phosphate may rise.

Individual prescribed course

The usual starting dose is8 mg once daily. The team may increase it to9 mg after reviewing phosphate and toxicity on days 14–21. Never change the dose yourself. Treatment is continuous until progression or unacceptable toxicity; the28-day view is only a reference.

The molecular result must be a susceptible FGFR3 alteration. A general FGFR result or a phosphate rise alone does not prove tumour benefit.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the tablet strength, daily dose, early phosphate appointment and baseline eye examination before starting.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Swallow tablets whole, with or without food, at about the same time each day. Avoid grapefruit and Seville oranges. If a dose is missed, take it as soon as possible and resume the usual schedule next day; never take extra tablets. If you vomit after a dose, take the next dose the following day.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

This is oral treatment; it does not require routine infusion access or a pump. Store and handle tablets as advised and keep a dose record.

STEP 4

Home support

High phosphate may need a dietitian-guided phosphate restriction and prescribed phosphate binder. Do not add vitaminD supplements without oncology review. Ask about mouth care, skin/nail care, diarrhoea treatment and dry-eye support. Bring all medicines and supplements for interaction checking.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

THOR randomised phase III study

After previous PD1/PDL1 therapy, median overall survival was 12.1 months with erdafitinib versus 7.8 months with chemotherapy (hazard ratio0.64;95% confidence interval0.47–0.88). Median progression-free survival was 5.6 versus 2.7 months.

These are trial group estimates, not a personal prognosis. Trial molecular eligibility included FGFR3/2 alterations; the current Irish indication requires susceptible FGFR3 alterations.

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Help control advanced urothelial cancer with a susceptible FGFR3 alteration after previous PD1/PDL1 treatment. FGFR testing selects people whose tumour may respond to this mechanism. Treatment aims to control cancer; scans and symptoms assess benefit separately from phosphate and other safety tests. Other systemic treatment or symptom-directed radiotherapy depends on your individual situation.

When do I attend?

The usual starting dose is8 mg once daily. The team may increase it to9 mg after reviewing phosphate and toxicity on days 14–21. Never change the dose yourself. Treatment is continuous until progression or unacceptable toxicity; the28-day view is only a reference.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
