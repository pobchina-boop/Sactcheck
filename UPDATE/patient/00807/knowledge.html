When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact your urology or oncology treatment team immediately for fever, chills, sudden illness, severe bladder pain, persistent bleeding or inability to pass urine. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or chest pain: call112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Reduce the risk of bladder cancer returning or worsening, and treat suitable carcinoma in situ, using local immune treatment.

Intravesical live bacterial immunotherapy

BCG

BCG
Activates immune cells in the bladder lining

BCG contains weakened live bacteria. Placing it inside the bladder stimulates a local immune response against abnormal cells. It is not a checkpoint-inhibitor medicine.

Treatment target↓Cancer-cell effect↓Healthy-tissue effects

What to watch for
Call urgently for fever, shaking chills, marked illness, breathlessness or inability to pass urine. Always tell other clinicians about prior BCG.

Individual prescribed course

BCG is placed in the bladder once weekly for six weeks. Maintenance uses three weekly instillations at months 3,6 and12 for intermediate-risk disease; high/very-high-risk plans may add months 18,24,30 and36. Your urologist confirms the course according to risk, response, tolerance and supply.

Live BCG placed inside the bladder. It is not intravenous chemotherapy or PD1/PDL1 checkpoint immunotherapy.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Tell the unit about fever, urinary infection, visible blood, recent bladder surgery/biopsy, antibiotics or immune-suppressing medicines before attending.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

A urinary catheter is used to place one prescribed vial of BCG into the bladder, usually in up to50 mL saline. NCCP gives a dwell time of one to two hours; BCG-medac instructions specify two hours. Staff confirm your product and how and when to empty your bladder.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

Treatment uses a temporary urinary catheter, not an intravenous chemotherapy pump. Catheter trauma or bleeding may mean postponement. Do not stop prescribed medicines or diuretics without the team’s instructions.

STEP 4

Home support

Routine anti-sickness or steroid premedication is not usually needed. The source asks for no fluids for four hours before and until two hours after instillation; confirm this is safe with your team, especially with fluid-sensitive conditions. Obtain written instructions for restarting fluids, passing urine, toilet hygiene, spill management and sexual precautions for your exact product.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Your wider treatment plan

Cystoscopy surveillance remains essential after BCG. Persistent high-grade disease or progression needs a fresh specialist discussion, which may include bladder removal or other treatment. Maintenance is not proof that the cancer has been cured.

BCG precautions after treatment

For BCG-medac, sit to pass urine after the agreed dwell time and wash hands and genital skin carefully. Follow the unit’s written toilet-disinfection instructions. Use a condom for one week after each treatment. Carry the product alert card; tell clinicians about BCG even years later if unexplained fever or illness occurs.

Treatment reference · 04

Symptoms and help

Report new or worsening symptoms promptly.

Call now

Call your treatment team immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Sources: nccp

During catheter treatment

Tell the nurse immediately about severe pain, bleeding, catheter difficulty or an allergic reaction. The team may postpone instillation.

NCCP 00807 and BCG-medac product information

BCG stimulates immune activity in the bladder. Maintenance can reduce recurrence and may reduce progression, but its benefit must be weighed against urinary symptoms, serious infection risk and the individual cancer risk.

The NCCP schedule allows different BCG products. Product-specific exclusions and precautions must be checked; the BCG-medac information is not permission to substitute brands without review.

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Reduce the risk of bladder cancer returning or worsening, and treat suitable carcinoma in situ, using local immune treatment. Cystoscopy surveillance remains essential after BCG. Persistent high-grade disease or progression needs a fresh specialist discussion, which may include bladder removal or other treatment. Maintenance is not proof that the cancer has been cured.

When do I attend?

BCG is placed in the bladder once weekly for six weeks. Maintenance uses three weekly instillations at months 3,6 and12 for intermediate-risk disease; high/very-high-risk plans may add months 18,24,30 and36. Your urologist confirms the course according to risk, response, tolerance and supply.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
