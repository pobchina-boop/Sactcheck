  1. TreatmentBoth medicines are given at each weekly induction visit for six weeks. Selected patients then receive treatment monthly for 18 months, beginning six weeks after the final induction dose. Maintenance depends on response and tolerability; it is not automatic.

Between treatments

  • Side effects may occur on any day, including during a break. Report problems promptly.
  • The source includes prescribed sodium bicarbonate1200 mg the night before and1200 mg one hour before treatment to reduce bladder irritation. Anti-sickness medicine may be considered if nausea followed a previous instillation. Pain or spasm medicines are individual prescriptions; ask before taking ibuprofen or other medicines.

Confirm the bicarbonate prescription and the fluid/diuretic plan. The source advises avoiding fluids for four hours before and until two hours after instillation, and avoiding alcohol/coffee around treatment. The team must adapt instructions for your medical conditions; do not change a prescribed diuretic yourself.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call your treatment team immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
