1. Treatment and purpose

Enfortumab vedotin + pembrolizumab · NCCP 00945 v2

Option 1: enfortumab on days 1 and8 plus pembrolizumab200 mg on day 1, repeating every 21 days. Option 2: enfortumab on days 1,8,22 and29 plus pembrolizumab400 mg on day 1, repeating every 42 days. Your team selects one option. The six-week reference shows two enfortumab blocks; pembrolizumab day 22 applies only to option 1.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Enfortumab vedotin · Antibody-drug conjugate (targeted chemotherapy)

Report any rash early, especially blistering, fever or mouth/eye/genital sores; also report thirst, frequent urination, numbness or new breathing symptoms.

Pembrolizumab · Immunotherapy

The immune system can also inflame healthy organs. New cough, diarrhoea, unusual weakness, severe headache, thirst or a spreading rash need prompt advice. Effects can start during treatment or after it finishes.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss the intended benefit, alternatives, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and individual priorities. Record the patient’s questions and the agreed plan. This is the NCCP 00945 advanced-disease combination. It is distinct from28-day enfortumab monotherapy and from perioperative bladder treatment.
