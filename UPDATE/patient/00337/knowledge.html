When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Treat possible microscopic spread before definitive local treatment for muscle-invasive bladder cancer.

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

Methotrexate

Intravenous chemotherapy
Blocks folate-dependent deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) production

Methotrexate interferes with folate-dependent reactions that cells need to make DNA. This can slow cancer-cell division, while also affecting blood-cell production and the lining of the mouth and bowel.

Folate pathway inhibited↓DNA building blocks reduced↓Cell division slowed

What to watch for
Tell the pharmacist about all painkillers, antibiotics, supplements and other medicines because interactions can increase methotrexate toxicity. Report new cough or breathlessness, mouth ulcers, unusual bleeding, jaundice, reduced urine or severe rash promptly.

Sources: nccp

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

Vinblastine

Vinca alkaloid
Disrupts the cell-division machinery

Vinblastine prevents cells from separating normally when they divide. It can also affect blood cells, nerves and bowel movement.

Cell division blocked↓Cancer growth slowed↓Nerves and marrow monitored

What to watch for
Report constipation, abdominal swelling, numbness or pain around the drip.

Sources: nccp

Platinum chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Damages DNA through platinum links

Cisplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing, but healthy tissues may also be affected. Kidney protection with fluids and monitoring is central to cisplatin treatment.

Platinum reaches cells↓DNA links form↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report ringing in the ears, hearing changes, numbness or loss of function. Tell the team if you cannot drink, are passing less urine or become swollen or breathless during fluids.

Sources: nccp

Bladder cancer · neoadjuvant CMV

Days 1, 2 and 8 · every 21 days · up to three cycles

Individual prescribed course

Methotrexate and vinblastine are given on days 1 and 8; cisplatin on day 2. Up to three 21-day cycles are planned before definitive local treatment. Folinic acid 15 mg is prescribed every six hours for four doses, starting 24 hours after each methotrexate dose (days 2 and 9). Confirm the written prescription.

The NCCP body specifies 21-day CMV; a 14-day footer label conflicts with it. The prescribing team must reconcile that source error. This schedule has no doxorubicin and requires folinic-acid rescue.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm each treatment day, blood-test appointments, support medicines and any growth-factor or folinic-acid prescription.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Treatment is given intravenously. Methotrexate and vinblastine are short administrations; where used, doxorubicin is given through carefully checked access. Cisplatin requires prescribed hydration and a longer visit. The team confirms exact administration and observation times.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

Suitable intravenous access is essential. Vinblastine and doxorubicin can damage tissue if they leak; report burning, pain or swelling immediately. No routine take-home infusion pump is part of these schedules.

STEP 4

Home support

Use one written anti-sickness plan, including medicines before treatment, tablets for home and rescue treatment. Your team reconciles steroids, interactions and any stomach-protection medicine from your local prescription. Cisplatin needs prescribed intravenous fluids and electrolyte monitoring/replacement. Follow your own fluid advice; tell the team about heart failure or fluid restriction. Review kidney function, hearing, nerves and other medicines before treatment. Methotrexate interacts with several medicines, including some antibiotics, anti-inflammatory painkillers and proton-pump inhibitors. Do not start, stop or combine medicines without oncology/pharmacy advice. The aprepitant–vinblastine interaction also requires pharmacy reconciliation. Folinic acid is a required methotrexate rescue medicine, not optional vitamin supplementation. Contact the unit if doses are missed or vomited; never double without advice.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

BA06 30894 · long-term report

The BA06 trial compared three cycles of CMV before local treatment with local treatment alone. At ten years, overall survival was 36% versus 30% (hazard ratio 0.84, 95% confidence interval 0.72–0.99).

The benefit was measured across trial groups receiving definitive local treatment. It does not mean that chemotherapy alone is curative or predict an individual outcome.

What is the treatment trying to do?

Treat possible microscopic spread before definitive local treatment for muscle-invasive bladder cancer. The multidisciplinary team plans the surgery or other definitive local treatment and recovery interval. Chemotherapy does not replace that local treatment. Follow-up remains necessary after completion.

When do I attend?

Methotrexate and vinblastine are given on days 1 and 8; cisplatin on day 2. Up to three 21-day cycles are planned before definitive local treatment. Folinic acid 15 mg is prescribed every six hours for four doses, starting 24 hours after each methotrexate dose (days 2 and 9). Confirm the written prescription.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
