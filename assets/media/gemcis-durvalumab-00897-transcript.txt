Cisplatin, gemcitabine and durvalumab for advanced biliary tract cancer

00:00:00   Treating biliary tract cancer
Narration
The aim is to control the cancer and help people live longer when surgery to remove it is not an option.
Durvalumab, also called Imfinzi, is immunotherapy.
Cancer cells can use a signal called PD-L1 to slow down immune cells.
Durvalumab blocks that signal, helping immune cells attack cancer.

On screen
This combination is a first treatment for adults with advanced cancer of the bile ducts or gallbladder. Gemcitabine and cisplatin damage DNA or interrupt its copying as cells divide.

00:00:18   The TOPAZ-1 trial
Narration
The study behind this treatment is called TOPAZ-1.
Patients were assigned to treatment groups by chance.
These are group results.
Your team can explain what they mean for you.

On screen
TOPAZ-1 enrolled 685 people. Both groups received gemcitabine and cisplatin. The later analysis estimated four-year survival at 11.8% with durvalumab and 4.3% with placebo.

00:00:28   Your 21-day cycle
Narration
Your treatment card shows which schedule is yours.
Day 1. All three drugs.
Day 8. Gemcitabine and cisplatin.
The plan repeats every three weeks.
A visit may move if you are unwell or need more tests, so use the date your day ward gives you.

On screen
The combination phase lasts up to eight 21-day cycles. All three medicines are given on day 1. On day 8, gemcitabine and cisplatin are given. Days 15 to 21 are a recovery week with no planned anti-cancer infusion.

00:00:43   Your infusion visits
Narration
Allow extra time at the hospital for blood results, assessment, preparation and any further observation.
At each visit, mention any new symptom, even if it has settled.
It helps the team decide whether it is safe to give the next dose.

On screen
On day 1, durvalumab runs over about 60 minutes, then gemcitabine over 30 minutes and cisplatin over 60 minutes. On day 8, gemcitabine and cisplatin run for about 30 and 60 minutes respectively. IV fluids before cisplatin on both days protect the kidneys and can add one to two hours. Anti-sickness medicines and checks add time.

00:00:56   From cycle 9
Narration
This time you receive just durvalumab, through a drip lasting about an hour.
The plan repeats every four weeks while the treatment is helping and side effects are manageable.

On screen
After up to eight combination cycles, cycle 9 starts durvalumab alone every 28 days if treatment continues.

00:01:06   Your blood checks
Narration
Liver inflammation can first show up on a blood test.
Repeat blood tests help spot changes early. Your team may also check cortisol if a hormone problem is suspected.

On screen
During combination treatment, day 1 tests check blood counts, kidneys and liver. Day 8 tests check blood counts and kidneys. Blood sugar and thyroid function are also monitored. Hearing tests may be arranged.

00:01:16   Tell the nurse straight away
Narration
A reaction can happen during a drip or shortly afterwards.
Tell the nurse immediately if you feel hot, shivery, itchy or dizzy, or if your throat feels tight or breathing becomes difficult.
Staff can pause the infusion, check you and treat the reaction.

On screen
Tell the nurse immediately if you feel unwell during a drip, especially for flushing, shivers, itching, dizziness, throat tightness or breathing difficulty.

00:01:31   Sickness and tiredness
Narration
At home, you may notice tiredness, itchy skin, a rash, loose stools, or aching muscles and joints.
Ring your oncology team promptly about new symptoms.
Reporting symptoms early gives your team time to find the cause.

On screen
Chemotherapy can cause tiredness, sickness and a sore mouth. Use your anti-sickness medicines and follow your personal fluid plan for the first 24 hours after cisplatin, especially if you have a fluid restriction. Ring if vomiting continues or you cannot drink.

00:01:45   Infection can be urgent
Narration
If you develop a fever or suddenly feel unwell, ring the 24-hour number straight away so the team can also check for infection.
Ring your oncology team urgently, even between visits.

On screen
Chemotherapy can reduce infection-fighting white blood cells. Ring immediately at 37.5°C or above, or 36°C or below. Shaking chills or sudden illness need urgent advice even with a normal temperature. Report unusual bruising or bleeding, which can happen if platelet counts fall.

00:01:55   Bile duct infection
Narration
Ring urgently if the whites of your eyes or your skin turn yellow, your urine becomes dark, or your belly becomes more swollen.

On screen
Fever, shaking chills, new yellow skin or eyes, or new upper tummy pain can signal bile duct infection. Ring oncology urgently even if only one sign is present. Tell them if you have a bile duct stent.

00:02:03   Kidneys, hearing and nerves
Narration
Passing much less urine than usual or developing new swelling is another reason to ring.
Your team can arrange the right tests.

On screen
Cisplatin can affect the kidneys, hearing and nerves. Tell your team promptly about much less urine, new swelling, ringing ears or changes to your hearing, or tingling or numb hands and feet.

00:02:10   Immune side effects
Narration
Sometimes the immune system inflames healthy organs too.
A new cough or shortness of breath may be a sign of lung inflammation.
For the bowel, ring about new diarrhoea, even if mild.
Tell the team how often you are going and whether there is pain, blood or mucus.
Blisters, peeling skin or painful mouth sores need urgent assessment.
Side effects can first appear months after your last infusion.

On screen
Durvalumab can cause inflammation that may first appear months later. Urgently report a new cough or breathlessness, any new diarrhoea, tummy pain, blood or mucus in your stool, or blistering or peeling skin.

00:02:34   Your oncology contacts
Narration
Before you leave the day ward, save your hospital’s 24-hour oncology number.
Carry your immunotherapy alert card and show it if you see a GP or an emergency team.
If you feel unwell and cannot reach oncology, seek urgent medical assessment.
For severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain or collapse, call 112 or 999.

On screen
Keep your oncology and out-of-hours contact numbers. If you feel unwell and cannot reach your team, seek urgent assessment. Call 112 or 999 for an ambulance for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain or collapse.
