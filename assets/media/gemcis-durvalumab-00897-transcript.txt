Cisplatin, gemcitabine and durvalumab for advanced biliary tract cancer
SACTCheck patient guide

00:00:00   Treating biliary tract cancer
This is a first treatment for advanced cancer of the bile ducts or gallbladder. It combines chemotherapy and immunotherapy.
Gemcitabine and cisplatin.
These two chemotherapy medicines damage DNA or interrupt its copying as cells divide.
Durvalumab, also called Imfinzi, is immunotherapy.
Cancer cells can use a signal called PD-L1 to slow down immune cells.
Durvalumab blocks that signal, helping immune cells attack cancer.

00:00:30   The TOPAZ-1 trial
TOPAZ-1.
This trial tested whether adding durvalumab to chemotherapy helped people live longer.
In a later analysis, about 12 in 100 people were alive at four years with durvalumab. About 4 in 100 were alive with placebo.
These are group results.
Your team can explain what they mean for you.

00:00:53   Your 21-day cycle
Three weeks.
Day 1. All three drugs.
Day 8. Gemcitabine and cisplatin.
Days 15 to 21 are a recovery week, without a scheduled anti-cancer drip. The next cycle starts three weeks after day 1, if your team says it is safe.
A visit may move if you are unwell or need more tests, so use the date your day ward gives you.

00:01:18   Three separate drips
Durvalumab runs for about an hour, gemcitabine for half an hour and cisplatin for an hour.
The drips total about two and a half hours. Fluids, anti-sickness medicines and checks make the visit longer.

00:01:33   Two drips and fluids
On day 8, gemcitabine is given first, followed by cisplatin. There is no durvalumab on this visit.
On both treatment days, you receive extra fluid before cisplatin to protect your kidneys. This can take another one to two hours.
Allow extra time at the hospital for blood results, assessment, preparation and any further observation.

00:01:57   From cycle 9
This time you receive just durvalumab, through a drip lasting about an hour.
The plan repeats every four weeks while the treatment is helping and side effects are manageable.

00:02:07   Your blood checks
Blood tests check your blood cells and kidneys on both treatment days, and your liver on day 1. Your team also monitors blood sugar and thyroid function.
At each visit, mention any new symptom, even if it has settled.
It helps the team decide whether it is safe to give the next dose.

00:02:26   Tell the nurse straight away
A reaction can happen during a drip or shortly afterwards.
Tell the nurse immediately if you feel hot, shivery, itchy or dizzy, or if your throat feels tight or breathing becomes difficult.
Staff can pause the infusion, check you and treat the reaction.

00:02:42   Sickness and tiredness
Nausea, vomiting, tiredness and a sore mouth can happen. Your team can help treat them.
Follow your team’s fluid advice for the first 24 hours after cisplatin, especially if you have a fluid restriction.
Ring urgently if you cannot keep fluids down or mouth ulcers stop you drinking.

00:03:04   Infection can be urgent
If you develop a fever or suddenly feel unwell, ring the 24-hour number straight away so the team can also check for infection.
Ring immediately if your temperature is 37.5°C or higher, or 36°C or lower. Ring if you feel suddenly unwell even with a normal reading.
Low platelets can cause easy bruising or bleeding. Tell your team about new bruises or bleeding.

00:03:29   Bile duct infection
Ring your oncology team urgently for new fever, shaking chills, yellow skin or eyes, or upper tummy pain.
Tell them if you have a bile duct stent. Do not wait for all these symptoms or for your next visit.

00:03:47   Kidneys, hearing and nerves
Passing much less urine than usual or developing new swelling is another reason to ring.
Cisplatin can affect hearing and nerves. Tell your team promptly about ringing in your ears, hearing loss, or new tingling or numbness in your fingers or toes.

00:04:03   Immune side effects
Sometimes the immune system inflames healthy organs too.
A new cough or shortness of breath may be a sign of lung inflammation.
Ring your oncology team urgently, even between visits.
For the bowel, ring about new diarrhoea, even if mild.
Tell the team how often you are going and whether there is pain, blood or mucus.
Blisters, peeling skin or painful mouth sores need urgent assessment.
Side effects can first appear months after your last infusion.

00:04:32   Your oncology contacts
Before you leave the day ward, save your hospital’s 24-hour oncology number.
Carry your immunotherapy alert card and show it if you see a GP or an emergency team.
If you feel unwell and cannot reach oncology, seek urgent medical assessment.
For severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain or collapse, call 112 or 999.
