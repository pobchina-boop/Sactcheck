SACTCheck | Durvalumab (Imfinzi) after chemoradiotherapy
Single-agent immunotherapy for stage III non-small cell lung cancer
Film transcript • Irish NCCP regimens 00576 and 00655

0:00   Durvalumab (Imfinzi)
Durvalumab, also called Imfinzi, is immunotherapy. This guide is for people receiving it on
its own for stage three non-small cell lung cancer after chemotherapy and radiotherapy.

0:11   How it helps immune cells
Cancer cells can use a signal called PD-L1 to slow down immune cells. Durvalumab blocks that
signal, helping immune cells attack cancer. Sometimes the immune system inflames healthy
organs too.

0:23   Every 2 weeks or every 4 weeks
You receive a drip into a vein over about an hour. Visits are every two weeks or every four
weeks, for up to a year. Your treatment card shows which schedule is yours.

0:33   What your team checks
Before starting, your team checks blood counts, kidney and liver function, blood sugar,
thyroid hormones and hepatitis B and C. A tumour test checks PD-L1. Repeat blood tests help
spot changes early. Your team may also check cortisol if a hormone problem is suspected.

0:50   Changes you may notice
Common effects include itching or rash, loose stools, cough, joint aches, fever and an
underactive thyroid. Report changes early. If you feel unwell during an infusion, tell your
nurse immediately.

1:02   Pneumonitis
Pneumonitis means lung inflammation. Call your oncology team now for a new cough or more
breathlessness, even if mild. The team may pause treatment, check for infection and give
steroids.

1:12   Colitis
Colitis means bowel inflammation. Call now for more frequent or watery stools, tummy pain,
or blood or mucus in your stool. You may need fluids, steroids and a treatment break.

1:23   Hepatitis and nephritis
Hepatitis affects the liver. Nephritis affects the kidneys. Blood tests may pick these up
before you feel unwell. Call now for yellow eyes, dark urine, passing much less urine or new
swelling.

1:34   Dermatitis
Dermatitis means skin inflammation. Tell your team about a new rash. A spreading rash,
blisters, peeling skin or mouth sores need urgent assessment.

1:43   Thyroiditis
Thyroiditis can make the thyroid overactive, then underactive. Tiredness and feeling cold
can be signs. An underactive thyroid is treated with hormone tablets called levothyroxine,
sometimes for life. Many people can continue immunotherapy once stable.

1:58   Adrenal insufficiency
Adrenal insufficiency means too little cortisol. It can follow inflammation of the adrenal
or pituitary glands. Call now for marked weakness, dizziness, vomiting or a persistent
headache. Treatment may pause. Some people need lifelong hydrocortisone to replace cortisol.

2:12   Other effects to recognise
Other serious effects include myocarditis in the heart, myositis in muscles, nerve or brain
inflammation, and type one diabetes. Call urgently for palpitations, new weakness,
confusion, severe thirst or frequent urination. Eye and pancreas inflammation can occur too.
Report changes even if they are not listed here.

2:30   Even months after your last dose
Problems can start months after the last dose. Some effects can cause permanent damage.
Severe reactions can be life-threatening. Your team may use steroids or other medicines to
calm inflammation and may stop immunotherapy permanently.

2:44   Who would you call tonight?
Carry your immunotherapy alert card and save your twenty-four-hour oncology number. Call
about new symptoms, even between visits. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent care. For
severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain or collapse, call 112 or 999.
