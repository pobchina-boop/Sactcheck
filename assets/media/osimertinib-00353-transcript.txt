Osimertinib (Tagrisso) monotherapy for non-small cell lung cancer
SACTCheck patient guide

00:00:00   Osimertinib
Osimertinib, also called Tagrisso, is a daily tablet for certain types of non-small cell lung cancer.
A change in a gene called EGFR can keep telling cancer cells to grow.
This medicine blocks that signal.
A test on your cancer helps your team decide if it is right for you.

00:00:17   How long will I take it?
After surgery, you may take it for up to three years to reduce the chance of cancer coming back.
For advanced cancer, treatment usually continues while it is helping and side effects are manageable.

00:00:27   Once a day
The usual dose is 80 mg once a day.
Take your prescribed dose at about the same time each day, with or without food.
There is no planned week off.

00:00:36   Swallow it whole
Swallow the tablet whole with water.
If swallowing is difficult, ask your pharmacist how to disperse it safely.

00:00:42   When is your next dose due?
If you miss a dose, take it if your next dose is more than 12 hours away.
If fewer than 12 hours remain, skip it.
At exactly 12 hours, ask your pharmacist.
Never double up.

00:00:54   Check new medicines
Check new medicines with your pharmacist.
Avoid St John’s wort.

00:00:58   Your check-ups
Your team will check your blood and check your heart rhythm.

00:01:03   Common side effects
Loose stools, rash, dry skin, mouth ulcers and sore nails are common.
Ring your team if diarrhoea persists or you struggle to drink.

00:01:14   Ring oncology straight away
Ring your 24-hour oncology line straight away for a new or worsening cough, breathlessness or fever.
These can be signs of lung inflammation.
Also call urgently for blistering skin or a painful red eye.

00:01:26   Other urgent changes
Tell your team urgently about a racing heartbeat, fainting or changes in your eyesight.

00:01:31   Getting help in Ireland
If you feel unwell and cannot reach oncology, seek urgent medical assessment.
For severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain or collapse, call 112 for an ambulance.

Also shown on screen
Your team will check your heart rhythm with an ECG before treatment and early on, at cycle 2. Extra ECGs may be needed.
In Ireland, call 112 or 999 for an ambulance in an emergency.
