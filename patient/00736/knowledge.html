When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new cough or breathlessness, fever or sudden illness promptly. Tell staff immediately about an infusion reaction or pain at the infusion site. Report numbness, tingling or loss of daily function before the next dose.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

How this medicine works

The aim is to control the cancer and its symptoms. The team reviews benefit, side effects and your priorities before continuing.

Chemotherapy

Nab-paclitaxel

Albumin-bound paclitaxel · Abraxane
Disrupts cell division by stabilising microtubules

This formulation carries paclitaxel with albumin, a protein. It interferes with the structures cells use to divide. Its dosing and allergy precautions differ from ordinary paclitaxel.

Practical treatment guide

Your infusion and access plan

Nab-paclitaxel on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle, with a planned week off on Day 22. Continue while beneficial and tolerated. This off-label schedule requires specific consent.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the indication, baseline bloods, required cardiac checks, medicines review and consent before the first infusion.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Each nab-paclitaxel dose is infused over 30 minutes. Your full visit also includes checks, preparation and any observation. Staff observe for reactions. Report burning, pain, swelling or leakage at the infusion site immediately.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit length

Each nab-paclitaxel dose is infused over 30 minutes. Your full visit also includes checks, preparation and any observation. A port is optional access, not a requirement for a take-home pump. This regimen has no routine ambulatory pump.

Premedication

Routine allergy premedicines are usually not required for nab-paclitaxel. Prior reactions require individual review; treatment is still given where a serious reaction can be managed.

Treatment reference · 01

The exact treatment being discussed

The aim is to control the cancer and its symptoms. The team reviews benefit, side effects and your priorities before continuing.

Indication

Metastatic breast cancer after anthracycline failure or when anthracyclines are unsuitable; this regimen is intended when hypersensitivity or intolerance prevents standard paclitaxel. It is an off-label schedule.

Sources: nccp

Dose and formulation

100 mg/m². This is a 100 mg/m² weekly schedule with a planned break. It is not a divided version of the 260 mg/m² three-weekly dose. Its dose-reduction levels are different.

Sources: nccp

Evidence limits

Supports activity and tolerability of the weekly 100 mg/m² schedule. Separate randomized phase II first-line evidence included 100 and 150 mg/m² weekly schedules (Gradishar 2009). Neither trial specifically proves outcomes in a population selected for standard-paclitaxel hypersensitivity. The significant PFS comparison reported for 150 mg/m² in Gradishar 2009 must not be assigned to NCCP 100 mg/m². The weekly schedule is outside the licensed Irish indication described in NCCP 00736 and needs the specified consent/local approval pathway. Do not copy the q21-day phase III efficacy numbers.

Treatment reference · 04

How other care fits around chemotherapy

There is no automatic surgery, radiotherapy or maintenance phase in this card.

Surgery and radiation

These are advanced-disease regimens. Surgery or radiotherapy is used only for a defined local or symptom-control purpose. The oncology and radiation teams decide whether chemotherapy continues, pauses or precedes radiation; no routine overlap is implied.

Sources: metastatic

Bone health

DEXA is not a routine test for this chemotherapy alone. Assess fracture risk and bone density when indicated by endocrine treatment, ovarian suppression or other clinical risks; repeat according to the relevant plan.

Sources: bone

Changing treatment

Discuss benefit, adverse effects, other treatment options and your preferences at each review. New symptoms can bring review forward.

Questions worth asking

Which treatment days and breaks should I expect?

Nab-paclitaxel on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle, with a planned week off on Day 22. Continue while beneficial and tolerated. This off-label schedule requires specific consent.

When do I need blood tests, an ECG or echo?

FBC, renal and liver profile at baseline and before each cycle; the NCCP source additionally specifies FBC on Day 8. The team decides if extra blood tests are needed, including before Day 15. Cardiac assessment when clinically indicated by symptoms, significant cardiac history or prior anthracycline therapy. ECG does not replace an echo. Baseline echo / MUGA if significant cardiac history or prior anthracycline exposure; repeat cardiac tests if clinically indicated. No fixed routine 3-month echo schedule is specified for this medicine alone.

When will we know whether treatment is helping?

For metastatic breast cancer, ESMO recommends response evaluation generally every 2–4 months, adjusted to disease, treatment and symptoms. Agree the scan type, due window and result-review appointment; symptoms can prompt earlier assessment.

Where do surgery or radiotherapy fit?

These are advanced-disease regimens. Surgery or radiotherapy is used only for a defined local or symptom-control purpose. The oncology and radiation teams decide whether chemotherapy continues, pauses or precedes radiation; no routine overlap is implied.

Do tumour markers replace scans?

Tumour markers, when useful for the cancer being treated, supplement clinical assessment and imaging. A marker result alone should not determine treatment failure or a treatment change.

Does a port mean a pump at home?

No. A port is a route into a vein. Staff disconnect the infusion and remove the port needle after the visit when appropriate; removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure.
