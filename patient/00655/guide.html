NCCP 00655 · version 3a

Durvalumab 1500 mg every 28 days

Durvalumab is infused on Day 1 of each 28-day cycle. The course lasts up to 12 months (13 cycles), and can finish earlier if the cancer progresses or side effects make continuation unsuitable. The first dose should start within 42 days after chemoradiotherapy finishes.

Important medicine-specific effects

Durvalumab

  • Inflammation of the lungs, bowel, liver, kidneys or skin
  • Thyroid, adrenal, pituitary or blood-sugar problems that may need long-term replacement treatment
  • Rare serious inflammation of the heart, muscles or nerves
  • Infusion reactions, fatigue and infections; effects can appear after treatment ends

Make the plan practical

Immune side effects and the alert card

Immune side effects may start during treatment or after the last dose. Keep the immunotherapy alert card and tell every treating clinician that you have received durvalumab, even during a treatment break or after finishing.

Lung symptoms after radiotherapy

Radiation inflammation, immune inflammation, infection and cancer can cause similar symptoms. Report new or worsening breathing symptoms promptly so the team can assess the cause. Do not assume a new cough is only an old radiotherapy effect.

Support medicines

Durvalumab alone has minimal sickness risk and no routine premedication is specified. Use anti-sickness or other medicines only as prescribed. Steroids for an immune reaction are a treatment plan supervised by the team, not routine premedication; do not start, reduce or stop them yourself.

Hormone replacement and blood tests

Thyroid, adrenal or diabetes problems may need hormone replacement or insulin, sometimes long term. Keep taking prescribed medicines and attend blood tests even when you feel well. Sudden severe weakness, vomiting, faintness, unusual thirst or drowsiness need urgent advice.

Pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding

Discuss pregnancy plans, fertility and breastfeeding before treatment. Effective contraception is advised during durvalumab and for at least three months after the last dose. Durvalumab is not recommended during pregnancy. The team must help decide whether breastfeeding or durvalumab should be stopped or avoided, weighing the benefits and risks.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
