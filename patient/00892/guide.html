Adjuvant ribociclib + endocrine therapy

Ribociclib 400 mg once daily on Days 1–21, followed by Days 22–28 off, for up to 3 years. The aromatase inhibitor continues daily on its own schedule; ovarian suppression is added when indicated.

Your treatment, at home.

Important medicine-specific effects

Ribociclib

  • Low white cells and infection
  • Liver injury / abnormal blood tests
  • QT prolongation / heart-rhythm problems
  • Nausea, fatigue and hair thinning
  • Rare serious lung or skin reactions

Non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor ± ovarian suppression

  • Hot flushes, joint pain and fatigue
  • Bone loss with AI / ovarian suppression
  • Injection-site discomfort with fulvestrant
  • Drug-specific liver and other effects

Make the plan practical

Support for this phase

Agree symptom medicines with the oncology pharmacist because many anti-sickness and other medicines can affect QT or CYP3A interactions. Do not automatically reuse a chemotherapy antiemetic prescription.

Bloods and cardiac checks

Review prior toxicity → FBC, renal, liver and electrolytes → FBC / renal / liver every 2 weeks. Repeat ECG around Cycle 1 Day 14.

Visits and practical support

Ask about travel, work, fertility, menopause, exercise and help at home. The team arranges and reviews clinical checks; the patient should not have to calculate the monitoring timetable.

Follow-up and bone health

If an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression is prescribed, assess fracture risk and baseline DEXA as indicated. Repeat timing depends on the result, risk and bone treatment; it is not an automatic scan for everyone.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
