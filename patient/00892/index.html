Adjuvant ribociclib + endocrine therapy

For eligible higher-risk HR-positive/HER2-negative early breast cancer, ribociclib is added to endocrine therapy to reduce recurrence risk.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

The usual adjuvant starting dose is ribociclib 400 mg once daily on Days 1–21, followed by Days 22–28 off, for up to three years. The aromatase inhibitor continues every day on its own plan; ovarian suppression is added when indicated.

Tests and checks

full blood count (FBC), renal and liver profile; potassium, calcium, magnesium and phosphorus. electrocardiogram (ECG) before initiation; QTcF must be below 450 ms. Correct electrolytes and review QT/CYP3A interactions. Confirm stage, recurrence-risk criteria, prior treatment, aromatase inhibitor (AI) and ovarian-suppression plan; reconcile the National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) node-negative eligibility/exclusion conflict before selection. FBC, renal and liver profile every two weeks for cycles 1–2, then before cycles 3–6, then as clinically indicated. Electrolytes before each of the first six cycles, then as clinically indicated. Repeat ECG around cycle 1 Day 14, then as clinically indicated. Increase liver monitoring for grade 2 or worse abnormalities; review fever, jaundice, palpitations/fainting, breathlessness and severe skin symptoms. Keep early-breast surveillance and bone/endocrine follow-up distinct from treatment-toxicity checks and from metastatic response imaging.

Between treatment visits

Planned treatment days are distinct from broad possible symptom patterns. Symptoms can occur outside these bands.

There are no guaranteed safe days. Fever, shivers, sudden illness, new breathing problems or severe symptoms need prompt advice on any day, including a treatment break.
