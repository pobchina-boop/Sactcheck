How your medicines work

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Ribociclib

Slows the cell-division checkpoint
CDK4/6 checkpoint→Checkpoint inhibited→Cell cycling slows

Ribociclib blocks CDK4 and CDK6, proteins that help cells move through the cycle of division. Endocrine therapy reduces the hormone signal that can drive HR-positive breast cancer.

Keep blood and ECG appointments. Fever, jaundice, fainting, palpitations, severe rash or new breathlessness need prompt advice.

Endocrine partner

Non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor ± ovarian suppression

Reduces oestrogen-driven growth
Oestrogen pathway→AI or receptor blockade→Growth signal reduced

An aromatase inhibitor lowers oestrogen production. Premenopausal patients need an agreed ovarian-suppression plan; men also require the appropriate hormonal partner plan. The endocrine schedule continues separately from the ribociclib breaks.

The endocrine medicine continues during the ribociclib week off. Report troublesome joint, menopausal or injection-site symptoms.

Support around treatment

Support for this phase

Agree symptom medicines with the oncology pharmacist because many anti-sickness and other medicines can affect QT or CYP3A interactions. Do not automatically reuse a chemotherapy antiemetic prescription.

Bloods and cardiac checks

Review prior toxicity → FBC, renal, liver and electrolytes → FBC / renal / liver every 2 weeks. Repeat ECG around Cycle 1 Day 14.

Visits and practical support

Ask about travel, work, fertility, menopause, exercise and help at home. The team arranges and reviews clinical checks; the patient should not have to calculate the monitoring timetable.

Follow-up and bone health

If an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression is prescribed, assess fracture risk and baseline DEXA as indicated. Repeat timing depends on the result, risk and bone treatment; it is not an automatic scan for everyone.

Do not wait for the next appointment

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
