Modified FOLFIRINOX for pancreatic cancer

Your treatment and how the medicines work

After surgery the aim is to reduce the chance of pancreatic cancer returning. If the cancer has spread, the aim is to control it. Ask which aim applies to you.

Oxaliplatin · Eloxatin

Platinum chemotherapy. Oxaliplatin makes links within DNA. These interfere with copying and repair, which can stop a cancer cell dividing. Healthy tissues can also be affected. Cold can trigger tingling or throat discomfort. Tell the team about persistent numbness, difficulty with buttons or walking, or infusion symptoms. New breathing difficulty always needs assessment.

Folinic acid · Calcium leucovorin

Chemotherapy modulator. Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself. The combined treatment can increase mouth soreness and diarrhoea. Report these promptly. Rare allergic reactions can occur.

Irinotecan · Campto

Topoisomerase I chemotherapy. The body converts irinotecan into SN-38. This traps an enzyme called topoisomerase I while DNA is being copied, causing damage that can stop cancer cells dividing. Normal bowel and blood-forming cells can also be affected. Tell the nurse immediately about cramps, sweating or diarrhoea during the infusion. Diarrhoea starting later needs the separate written treatment and contact plan.

Fluorouracil · 5-FU

Chemotherapy. Fluorouracil interferes with the production and use of DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. The infusion extends exposure over approximately two days. Diarrhoea, mouth sores and skin soreness may become serious. Chest pain during the pump infusion needs emergency assessment. Unexpected severe early toxicity can occur even after DPD testing.

What the research found

Following resection, modified FOLFIRINOX improved disease-free survival compared with gemcitabine. In metastatic disease the pivotal comparison tested standard FOLFIRINOX. It does not establish identical outcomes for the modified doses.

These are group results from the named setting. They cannot predict the benefit or side effects for an individual.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · FOLFIRINOX infusion

Oxaliplatin is followed by folinic acid and irinotecan. Fluorouracil runs by pump over 46 hours. This modified schedule omits the short fluorouracil injection.

Common effects and what to do

Eating and drinking

Tell the team about nausea or reduced appetite. Call if you cannot keep fluids down.

Fatigue and low counts

Rest between activities. Report fever, breathlessness or unusual bleeding promptly.

Nerve symptoms

Report numbness or loss of balance before the next visit. Cold can trigger tingling after oxaliplatin.

Serious effects: when to call

Infection

Fever, shivering or sudden illness: phone oncology immediately.

Urgent symptoms

Seek urgent help for chest pain, trouble breathing or collapse.

Bowel symptoms

Ring promptly for persistent diarrhoea, dehydration or severe abdominal pain.

During irinotecan

Tell the nurse about sweating, cramps or sudden diarrhoea. Later diarrhoea needs your written home plan.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Fever, shivering or sudden illness: phone oncology immediately. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Using your 46-hour pump

Connecting the pump

The day unit connects the 46-hour fluorouracil pump on day 1. Arrange disconnection by a trained team around day 3.

At home with the pump

Keep the line secure, dry and free of kinks. Follow your unit’s instructions for sleeping and washing. Leave disconnection and any change to the flow to your trained team.

Pump or line trouble

Phone the unit promptly for swelling, pain, a wet dressing, leakage or a pump that finishes much earlier or later than planned. Follow your unit’s spill instructions.
