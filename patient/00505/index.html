When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

More about my medicines

Capecitabine

Capecitabine is changed into fluorouracil, which interferes with the processes cells use to make and use their genetic instructions. This can damage cancer cells, but healthy bowel, mouth, skin and blood-forming cells can also be affected.

Temozolomide

Temozolomide produces deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) damage that can prevent cancer cells from continuing to grow. Blood-forming cells and other healthy tissues can also be affected, so nausea prevention, blood counts, infection awareness and liver tests are important.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1–9: Capecitabine only

Take the prescribed morning and evening doses with food or within 30 minutes afterwards.

What to do: Check your calendar and report bowel, mouth or hand-foot symptoms early.

Day 10–14: Both medicines; different food instructions

Continue capecitabine twice daily. Add the once-daily temozolomide dose fasting, with the prescribed anti-sickness plan.

What to do: Ask the pharmacist to write practical dose and meal times; do not assume both medicines are taken together.

Day 15–28: Planned rest from both anticancer medicines

Take neither CAPTEM medicine unless the team has changed your written plan. Symptoms and low blood counts may continue.

What to do: Complete liver tests after the cycle and blood tests before the next. Restart only when authorised.

Support at home

Separate nausea-prevention plan

Capecitabine is minimal-to-low emetogenic, while temozolomide is moderate-to-high emetogenic in this oral schedule. The team must prescribe an oral anti-sickness plan for Days 10–14 and any rescue treatment; this is not a single low-risk infusion plan.

Mouth, hands and feet

Use the unit’s mouth and skin care. Significant pain, ulcers or hand-foot symptoms may require capecitabine interruption; do not keep taking tablets just to finish the fortnight.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Ask for written diarrhoea instructions and the point at which to stop capecitabine and call. Persistent vomiting, severe diarrhoea, dizziness or inability to drink needs urgent advice.

Infection and PJP prevention

The team assesses whether PJP prevention is needed under local policy, especially with steroid exposure or other risk factors. CAPTEM is not the 42-day temozolomide-plus-radiotherapy schedule; do not borrow that schedule’s automatic prevention plan.
