Active medicines: Capecitabine + Temozolomide

Emetogenic risk: Capecitabine oral minimal-to-low; temozolomide oral moderate-to-high

No routine prophylactic antiemetic order is populated for this phase. Agree an individual symptom-treatment plan where needed.

Additional phase-specific support

  • Capecitabine is minimal-to-low emetogenic, while temozolomide is moderate-to-high emetogenic in this oral schedule. The team must prescribe an oral anti-sickness plan for Days 10–14 and any rescue treatment; this is not a single low-risk infusion plan.
  • Use the unit’s mouth and skin care. Significant pain, ulcers or hand-foot symptoms may require capecitabine interruption; do not keep taking tablets just to finish the fortnight.
  • Ask for written diarrhoea instructions and the point at which to stop capecitabine and call. Persistent vomiting, severe diarrhoea, dizziness or inability to drink needs urgent advice.
  • The team assesses whether PJP prevention is needed under local policy, especially with steroid exposure or other risk factors. CAPTEM is not the 42-day temozolomide-plus-radiotherapy schedule; do not borrow that schedule’s automatic prevention plan.
  • A separate prescribed oral antiemetic plan is required for temozolomide Days 10–14. The source does not specify one universal drug/dose; do not substitute a parenteral low-risk bundle.
  • Day-specific administration: capecitabine with food/within 30 minutes; temozolomide fasting. Omitted toxicity doses are not replaced.
  • PJP prevention is a local risk-based decision for this five-day temozolomide exposure, not automatic adoption of the 42-day brain radiotherapy regimen.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
