When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Stop capecitabine and contact oncology for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth or hand-foot symptoms affecting activity, vomiting that prevents drinking, fever or sudden illness. Call urgently for new cough/breathlessness, bleeding or yellow skin/eyes. Chest pain or severe breathlessness needs emergency help. Do not restart held treatment without instructions.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the two medicines work

CAPTEM combines two oral chemotherapy medicines to help control an advanced pancreatic NET. The team discusses the intended benefit, side effects and alternatives. NCCP identifies this pancreatic NET use as outside the medicines’ licences, requiring the local consent process.

Chemotherapy tablet

Capecitabine

Xeloda / generic
Converted to fluorouracil inside the body

Capecitabine is changed into fluorouracil, which interferes with the processes cells use to make and use their genetic instructions. This can damage cancer cells, but healthy bowel, mouth, skin and blood-forming cells can also be affected.

Tablet taken↓Converted to fluorouracil↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Stop and call for significant diarrhoea, mouth or hand-foot symptoms; chest pain needs emergency assessment.

Chemotherapy capsule

Temozolomide

Temodal / generic
Damages deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) in cells

Temozolomide produces DNA damage that can prevent cancer cells from continuing to grow. Blood-forming cells and other healthy tissues can also be affected, so nausea prevention, blood counts, infection awareness and liver tests are important.

Capsule taken↓DNA damaged↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Take the prescribed anti-sickness plan on temozolomide days; report fever, bleeding, new breathing symptoms or liver symptoms.

Complete timing and symptom notes

Day 1–9: Capecitabine only

Take the prescribed morning and evening doses with food or within 30 minutes afterwards. Check your calendar and report bowel, mouth or hand-foot symptoms early.

Day 10–14: Both medicines; different food instructions

Continue capecitabine twice daily. Add the once-daily temozolomide dose fasting, with the prescribed anti-sickness plan. Ask the pharmacist to write practical dose and meal times; do not assume both medicines are taken together.

Day 15–28: Planned rest from both anticancer medicines

Take neither CAPTEM medicine unless the team has changed your written plan. Symptoms and low blood counts may continue. Complete liver tests after the cycle and blood tests before the next. Restart only when authorised.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Dose and meal timing

The source starting schedule is capecitabine 750 mg/m² twice daily on Days 1–14 and temozolomide 200 mg/m² once daily on Days 10–14 of a 28-day cycle. Your own label gives the actual tablet/capsule numbers. Take capecitabine with food or within 30 minutes afterwards; take temozolomide fasting according to the pharmacy instructions. Swallow both whole; do not crush, cut or open them.

STEP 2

Missed or vomited doses

Do not double capecitabine or take a replacement dose after vomiting. If you miss temozolomide, follow the pharmacy’s written instructions or call before taking it late. If you vomit after temozolomide, do not take a second dose that day. Never replace doses omitted because of toxicity.

STEP 3

Support medicines

Have the Days 10–14 anti-sickness prescription ready before Day 10. Ask how it fits around fasting temozolomide and meals for capecitabine. Follow the local rescue and constipation/diarrhoea plan.

STEP 4

Medicine interactions

Bring a complete medicine list. Warfarin may need extra INR checks. Tell the team about seizure medicines and recent brivudine or related antiviral treatment; dangerous interactions require specialist review.

STEP 5

Storage and handling

Keep each medicine in its own labelled original packaging and away from children. Do not cut or crush capecitabine or open temozolomide capsules. If swallowing is difficult, contact pharmacy before treatment. Return unused medicine as advised.

STEP 6

Pregnancy, breastfeeding and fertility

These medicines can harm a pregnancy and may affect fertility. Discuss contraception for patients and partners, the required duration after the last dose, fertility preservation and breastfeeding before starting; follow the medicine-specific written plan.

STEP 7

Review appointments

Before starting: blood count, kidney/liver tests, hepatitis B screening and DPD testing unless already tested. Blood count, kidney and liver tests before every cycle; liver tests also after each cycle. INR if indicated with warfarin. Liver injury can appear weeks after the last dose. Scans and hormone markers follow the consultant plan.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Separate nausea-prevention plan

Capecitabine is minimal-to-low emetogenic, while temozolomide is moderate-to-high emetogenic in this oral schedule. The team must prescribe an oral anti-sickness plan for Days 10–14 and any rescue treatment; this is not a single low-risk infusion plan.

Mouth, hands and feet

Use the unit’s mouth and skin care. Significant pain, ulcers or hand-foot symptoms may require capecitabine interruption; do not keep taking tablets just to finish the fortnight.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Ask for written diarrhoea instructions and the point at which to stop capecitabine and call. Persistent vomiting, severe diarrhoea, dizziness or inability to drink needs urgent advice.

Infection and PJP prevention

The team assesses whether PJP prevention is needed under local policy, especially with steroid exposure or other risk factors. CAPTEM is not the 42-day temozolomide-plus-radiotherapy schedule; do not borrow that schedule’s automatic prevention plan.

Treatment reference · 01

Your CAPTEM treatment

Two chemotherapy medicines with carefully separated instructions.

Why CAPTEM?

CAPTEM combines two oral chemotherapy medicines to help control an advanced pancreatic NET. The team discusses the intended benefit, side effects and alternatives. NCCP identifies this pancreatic NET use as outside the medicines’ licences, requiring the local consent process.

What the medicines do

Capecitabine is changed into fluorouracil, which interferes with the processes cells use to make and use their genetic instructions. This can damage cancer cells, but healthy bowel, mouth, skin and blood-forming cells can also be affected. Temozolomide produces DNA damage that can prevent cancer cells from continuing to grow. Blood-forming cells and other healthy tissues can also be affected, so nausea prevention, blood counts, infection awareness and liver tests are important.

Schedule and planned duration

Each cycle lasts 28 days: capecitabine twice daily on Days 1–14; add temozolomide once daily on Days 10–14; take neither anticancer medicine on Days 15–28. NCCP plans six cycles unless progression or unacceptable toxicity changes the plan.

Evidence and uncertainty

Adding capecitabine improved the time before disease progression in the trial. The final overall-survival comparison was not statistically significant. Progression-free survival is not a personal survival forecast. The study population and trial treatment duration do not replace the Irish six-cycle regimen, consent process or individual decision. Routine O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) testing is not mandated by this source.

Tests, urgent symptoms and follow-up

Some effects appear after treatment days have ended.

Before, during and after cycles

Before starting: blood count, kidney/liver tests, hepatitis B screening and DPD testing unless already tested. Blood count, kidney and liver tests before every cycle; liver tests also after each cycle. INR if indicated with warfarin. Liver injury can appear weeks after the last dose. Scans and hormone markers follow the consultant plan.

When to stop and call

Stop capecitabine and contact oncology for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth or hand-foot symptoms affecting activity, vomiting that prevents drinking, fever or sudden illness. Call urgently for new cough/breathlessness, bleeding or yellow skin/eyes. Chest pain or severe breathlessness needs emergency help. Do not restart held treatment without instructions.

Unusually severe early effects

DPD helps break down capecitabine’s active drug. DPD testing reduces risk but does not remove it. Severe early diarrhoea, mouth soreness, infection or neurological symptoms needs immediate team assessment; do not continue waiting for the next cycle.

Treatment reference · 04

Understanding your particular NET

Several different features help the team choose treatment.

Where it started and where it is now

A neuroendocrine tumour begins in cells with hormone-making features. Its starting organ and the places it has spread are separate parts of the diagnosis. A pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour differs from pancreatic adenocarcinoma; a deposit in the liver may have come from another organ.

Differentiation, grade and Ki-67

The pathology report describes how organised the cells look (differentiation) and their growth activity (grade). Ki-67 measures cells showing a growth-related protein in the sample. Its percentage describes that sample, not the amount of your body affected or your chance of survival.

Grade is different from stage

Stage describes extent; grade describes tumour biology. Either can vary independently. A well-differentiated grade 3 neuroendocrine tumour and a poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma are distinct diagnoses and may need different treatments.

Functioning and nonfunctioning

A functioning tumour produces a hormone syndrome. A nonfunctioning tumour can still cause problems through growth or pressure. Somatostatin receptor uptake on a scan shows a treatment target; it does not, on its own, identify a hormone syndrome.

Your diagnosis record

Ask for one diagnosis summary: starting organ, grade, differentiation, Ki-67 result if reported, sites involved, hormone syndrome and receptor-imaging result. Keep pending results clearly marked.

Treatment reference · 05

Making sense of tests and results

Safety tests, tumour tests and hormone tests answer different questions.

Blood tests for treatment safety

Blood counts assess oxygen-carrying cells, infection-fighting cells and platelets. Kidney and liver results help assess treatment safety. These checks are needed even when you feel well; a reassuring scan answers a different question.

computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and receptor imaging

CT and MRI show anatomy and tumour size. Somatostatin receptor imaging tests whether a particular receptor target is present. An fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (FDG-PET) scan measures uptake of a glucose-like tracer and is used selectively. Your team chooses the scans that can guide your care.

Hormones and tumour markers are individual

Selected markers may help answer a hormone or tumour question. Examples include 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA) for serotonin-related symptoms, chromogranin A, or a specific pancreatic hormone. They are not a routine panel for every neuroendocrine tumour. Interpret changes with symptoms and imaging.

Preparing for a test

Use the laboratory instructions for fasting, food restrictions and any timed urine collection. Some medicines affect marker results, including acid-suppressing medicines and chromogranin A. Ask the team what to do; do not stop a prescribed medicine yourself.

Heart and inherited-risk checks

Some hormone syndromes can affect heart valves, so an ultrasound of the heart may be requested. Inherited-risk assessment depends on the tumour and family history; multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 is one example. An inherited-risk test and a test of tumour tissue answer different questions.

Who does what, and when?

For each test, write its name, purpose, booking date and result-review contact. Ask the team to set your next scan or marker date. A schedule for someone after surgery may not apply during your treatment.

Treatment reference · 06

Hormone symptoms and day-to-day changes

A new symptom may come from the NET, treatment or another illness.

Build a picture of what is normal for you

Keep a brief record of your usual bowel pattern, flushing, appetite, weight, pain and energy. Note new changes and when they started. This helps the team assess whether treatment, hormones or another problem needs attention.

Hormone-related symptoms

Hormone symptoms depend on the tumour: examples include flushing and watery diarrhoea, acid-related symptoms, or changes in blood sugar. An insulin-producing tumour can lower blood sugar. Ask which symptoms apply to you and whether you need a written rescue plan.

Hormone treatment is a separate part of the plan

Hormone-control medicines have their own schedule. A pause in anticancer tablets does not automatically mean pausing those medicines. Before peptide receptor radionuclide therapy, the specialist team coordinates any interruption and restart of somatostatin analogue injections.

When a change is urgent

Follow your oncology emergency plan for sudden or severe symptoms. Collapse, severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, confusion or uncontrolled bleeding needs emergency assessment. Severe diarrhoea, persistent vomiting, dehydration, fever or sudden illness needs urgent oncology advice. Do not wait for a scan, an online answer or the next clinic visit.

Procedures and hormone syndromes

Tell teams arranging an operation, anaesthetic or other procedure about a hormone-producing tumour or previous hormone crisis. Your specialist decides whether extra preparation is needed. Carry the agreed emergency instructions and current medicine list.

Treatment reference · 07

Your overall plan and choices

Your priorities belong in the treatment plan.

What are we trying to achieve?

Agree your treatment aim: slowing growth, reducing tumour size, controlling hormone symptoms or preserving daily function. A scan showing no growth may be a useful result even when the tumour has not shrunk.

How treatments fit together

Your plan may use different treatments at different times, including surgery, medicines, receptor-targeted radiation or treatment directed at the liver. Ask the team to mark your own sequence and who coordinates it.

Choosing between options

Ask why the proposed option fits the tumour site, grade, receptor findings, symptoms and your health. Discuss reasonable alternatives, observation when appropriate and suitable trials. Your team explains which options are available to you.

Evidence is about groups

Studies measure different outcomes, such as shrinkage, time before worsening and survival. Ask which outcome matters for your plan and how closely the study group matches your diagnosis. No study average can predict one person’s course.

Review points and shared decisions

Agree when to review the plan and what could lead to continuing, pausing or changing treatment. Include the effects on work, caring duties, travel and the activities that matter to you.

Treatment reference · 08

Eating, energy, practical help and support

Supportive care can be used alongside treatment from the start.

Food, weight and digestion

Report weight loss, persistent diarrhoea, greasy stools or difficulty eating. Different causes need different treatment. Ask for a dietitian and whether reduced digestive-enzyme function needs assessment.

Fluids and safe activity

Use your personal fluid advice, especially with diarrhoea or heart or kidney disease. Adjust activity and rest to your energy. Report dizziness, breathlessness or fatigue that limits daily tasks so treatable causes can be checked.

Emotions, relationships and quality of life

Ask for help with mood, sleep, relationships, intimacy or caring responsibilities when you need it. Symptom and palliative-care teams can support comfort and quality of life while anticancer treatment continues.

Practical help

Ask your specialist nurse or social worker about transport, employment, finances and help at home. Tell them about caring responsibilities and ask which support organisations fit your needs.

A record that works without a phone

Keep a paper schedule, current medicine list and real contact numbers together. The handbook can be read without internet access. Use the written emergency plan whenever you need urgent help.

Questions to take to the next visit

For the next visit, choose the questions that matter most: my exact diagnosis; the aim of treatment; which tests I need and why; who explains results; when to call; and what help is available.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the aim of CAPTEM, and how was its off-label use explained?

Ask the team to explain the expected benefit, alternatives and burdens for your pancreatic NET. NCCP identifies this use as off-label and requires the local consent process.

Has my DPD result been reviewed?

DPD testing is required before first capecitabine unless already completed. Ask the team to confirm the result and any special plan; testing does not remove every risk of severe toxicity.

Can you write exact dose and meal times for Days 10–14?

Ask the pharmacist to write a practical calendar. Capecitabine is taken with food or within 30 minutes afterwards; temozolomide is taken fasting. Do not assume the two medicines should be swallowed together.

What anti-sickness medicines should I take on temozolomide days?

Temozolomide Days 10–14 has moderate-to-high oral emetic potential. Your team must prescribe the local preventive and rescue plan and explain its timing; this guide does not select a universal drug or dose.

When are liver tests after each cycle and after the last dose?

Liver tests are needed before and after each cycle. Injury may occur weeks after the final dose, so ask for the follow-up dates and whom to call for yellow skin/eyes or dark urine.

Which symptoms mean stop capecitabine and call?

Significant diarrhoea, mouth pain or hand-foot symptoms, vomiting that prevents drinking, fever or sudden illness needs prompt advice and capecitabine interruption as instructed. Chest pain or severe breathlessness needs emergency assessment.

When will we review the six-cycle plan and response?

The NCCP plan is six 28-day cycles unless progression or unacceptable toxicity changes it. Your consultant decides response-scan timing and whether the course should continue or change.

Try explaining the plan back: Can you point out the capecitabine days, the five temozolomide days, the different food instructions and your anti-sickness plan? What symptoms mean stop tablets and call?
