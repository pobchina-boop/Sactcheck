Tests in your plan

Baseline bloods and tests

WhenFull blood count, kidney and liver tests, coagulation, urine protein, thyroid-stimulating hormone, ECG and blood pressure. Urine protein must be below 1 g/24 hours at initiation; blood pressure must be controlled.

Checks organ safety and suitability before treatment.

Treatment safety monitoring

WhenEvery 28 days: blood count, kidney tests, coagulation, urine protein and thyroid-stimulating hormone. Liver tests every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then monthly; calcium monthly. Blood pressure after week 1, every 2 weeks in the first 2 months, then monthly. ECG when clinically indicated.

Finds changes that may need supportive care, a dose hold or adjustment.

Specialist and cancer tests

If part of your plan
WhenThyroid-stimulating hormone checks the hormone-suppression plan. Urine protein and blood calcium are safety checks. Thyroglobulin and response imaging follow the individual thyroid cancer plan.

Distinguishes treatment selection, hormone monitoring and cancer response.

The whole treatment journey

Lenvatinib: differentiated thyroid cancer: treatment and checks

Progressive, locally advanced or metastatic differentiated thyroid cancer that is refractory to radioactive iodine.

Lenvatinib 24 mg once daily, continuously, until progression or unacceptable side effects. The clinician may prescribe a lower starting dose or later reductions.

What each check is for

1Treatment and reviewOnce daily, continuously

This treatment aims to slow cancer growth and help control symptoms. Your team explains the expected benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your situation.

Ask / act: Every 28 days: blood count, kidney tests, coagulation, urine protein and thyroid-stimulating hormone. Liver tests every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then monthly; calcium monthly. Blood pressure after week 1, every 2 weeks in the first 2 months, then monthly. ECG when clinically indicated.

Sources: NCCP
