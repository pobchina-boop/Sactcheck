Adult treatment scope · NCCP 00702a / 00702b This adult suite covers entrectinib alone for ROS1-positive advanced NSCLC before any ROS1 inhibitor (00702a), or a separate NTRK gene-fusion solid-tumour indication with no previous NTRK inhibitor and no satisfactory treatment options (00702b). Molecular tests and Irish access criteria must be confirmed. The adult 600 mg schedule does not apply to children.

Understand the treatment. Keep the plan manageable.

Entrectinib aims to control a cancer with a matching gene fusion. It may help shrink or slow cancer, including some cancer in the brain. Your team explains the treatment aim, uncertainty and alternatives for your exact setting.

ROS1 / TRK targeted therapy

Entrectinib

Blocks abnormal ROS1 or TRK growth signals
Matching gene fusion identified↓Abnormal growth signalling blocked↓Cancer growth may slow

Entrectinib inhibits proteins made by selected ROS1 or NTRK gene fusions. Blocking these signals can slow cancer growth. The relevant fusion, tumour setting and previous medicines determine eligibility; ROS1 and NTRK are different indications.

Support throughout treatment

Symptom medicines

NCCP rates entrectinib minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Agree individual rescue treatment and check heart-rhythm interactions; no routine infusion premedication is specified.

Daily life and medicines

Keep the medicine list current. Avoid driving or machinery while dizzy, faint, visually affected or cognitively affected. Review falls risk and report bone pain.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Use the agreed contraception plan during and after treatment. Do not breastfeed during treatment; ask about fertility and family plans before starting.

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Call the oncology team urgently for new or worsening breathlessness, swelling, palpitations or fainting; marked confusion or memory change; fever, jaundice, severe diarrhoea or inability to drink. Report new visual disturbance and focal bone pain promptly. Follow your unit’s temperature/contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain or collapse needs emergency help.
