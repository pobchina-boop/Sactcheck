When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. New chest pain, breathing difficulty, severe diarrhoea, inability to drink, reduced urine, uncontrolled bleeding or a rapidly worsening symptom needs prompt assessment.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Your medicines and what they do

CAPOX, also called XELOX, combines capecitabine tablets with oxaliplatin infusion. This NCCP protocol covers adjuvant stage III colon cancer, advanced colorectal cancer and adjuvant gastric cancer after D2 surgery. Confirm the treatment aim and duration for your indication.

Oral chemotherapy

Capecitabine

Xeloda
Becomes fluorouracil in the body

The body converts capecitabine into fluorouracil, which disrupts DNA and RNA processes needed for cell growth. Tablets are active chemotherapy; taking them at home still requires a clear monitoring and interruption plan.

Swallowed tablet↓Converted to fluorouracil↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Stop tablets and contact the team for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth ulcers, hand-foot soreness affecting activities, fever or dehydration. Chest pain needs urgent assessment. Do not restart until instructed.

Sources: nccp · patient

Platinum chemotherapy

Oxaliplatin

Eloxatin
Links DNA strands

Oxaliplatin makes links within DNA. These interfere with copying and repair, which can stop a cancer cell dividing. Healthy tissues can also be affected.

Platinum enters cell↓DNA links form↓Copying is disrupted

What to watch for
Cold can trigger tingling or throat discomfort. Tell the team about persistent numbness, difficulty with buttons or walking, or infusion symptoms. New breathing difficulty always needs assessment.

Sources: nccp · patient

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Infusion and tablet plan

Your team checks symptoms, blood results and the oral prescription. Make sure you know the tablet strengths, number per dose and exact start and stop instructions. Use the written prescription. Take capecitabine with water within 30 minutes after food; never guess the tablet count.

Day 2–7: Tablets at home

Continue twice-daily capecitabine according to the prescription. A missed or vomited dose is not replaced. Record doses missed and contact the team if you are unsure. Do not double the next dose. Keep tablets in the original packaging, away from children; do not crush or split unless the oncology pharmacist has arranged it.

Day 8–14: Watch for accumulating symptoms

Diarrhoea, mouth ulcers or painful hands and feet can develop or worsen. Poor intake and dehydration can worsen kidney function and increase risk from capecitabine. Stop tablets and contact oncology for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth or hand-foot symptoms affecting activity, fever, dehydration or chest symptoms. Do not restart without advice.

Day 15–21: The prescribed tablet-free week

No capecitabine is planned in this interval, but side effects and infection can still occur. Keep recording symptoms and confirm blood-test and review arrangements. Do not use leftover tablets to make up missed doses or start the next cycle early. The next cycle requires team confirmation.

Sources: nccp · patient

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

A written tablet calendar

The dose may require different tablet strengths. Ask the pharmacist to mark the number of each strength per dose. The guide does not calculate your dose. Bring the calendar and remaining tablets to review as instructed.

DPD testing and kidney function

DPD deficiency can cause severe fluoropyrimidine toxicity. Kidney impairment can also increase exposure to capecitabine metabolites. Testing and blood monitoring support safety but do not make serious symptoms safe to ignore.

Interaction check

Tell the team about blood thinners, especially warfarin, and all new medicines. Some combinations require closer monitoring or a different plan. Ask the oncology pharmacist before starting supplements or over-the-counter medicines.

Hands, feet and cold exposure

Use gentle skin care and reduce pressure, friction and heat on sore hands and feet. Oxaliplatin can also cause cold-triggered nerve symptoms. These effects differ; report pain, numbness or functional change.

Treatment reference · 01

Treatment aim and duration

CAPOX is used in several distinct settings.

Adjuvant colon cancer

Following resection of stage III colon cancer, CAPOX aims to reduce recurrence risk. Duration is a discussion about cancer risk and toxicity; three and six months are not interchangeable for everyone.

Sources: nccp

Advanced colorectal cancer

The aim may be cancer control, symptom relief and longer survival. Molecular findings, previous therapy and fitness affect the overall plan. Additional targeted medicines require their own regimen and counselling.

Sources: nccp

After gastric surgery

The adjuvant gastric evidence concerns a specific postoperative setting after D2 gastrectomy. Nutrition, postoperative recovery and oral tolerance need particular attention.

Sources: nccp

Alternatives and review points

Ask why this oral/infusion combination was chosen, whether an infusional alternative is suitable, and what would prompt a pause or change. Taking tablets at home does not mean the treatment is mild.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 02

Taking capecitabine safely

Your prescription is the authority for dose and dates.

Food, doses and tablet handling

Take doses about 12 hours apart and within 30 minutes after food, as instructed. Swallow whole with water. If swallowing is difficult, ask the oncology pharmacist for a safe plan. Do not crush tablets or improvise a liquid.

Sources: patient

Missed or vomited doses

Do not replace a missed or vomited dose and do not take an extra dose. Record it and resume the next scheduled dose only if otherwise safe under your written instructions. Repeated vomiting or uncertainty needs a call.

Sources: patient

When to stop and call

Do not continue through significant diarrhoea, painful mouth sores, painful hand-foot symptoms, fever, dehydration or chest pain. Stop capecitabine and obtain prompt advice. A clinician decides whether and how to restart.

Sources: patient

The rest week is part of treatment

Days without tablets are planned. Do not fill them with leftovers. Return unused medicine through the hospital/pharmacy process and keep it out of household waste and away from other people.

Sources: patient

Treatment reference · 03

What the checks are for

Symptoms, blood results and the treatment aim answer different questions.

Blood counts

Neutrophils protect against infection, haemoglobin carries oxygen and platelets help clotting. A normal previous result or feeling well does not prove the current counts are safe. Fever or sudden illness needs immediate advice.

Sources: hse

Kidney and liver function

Organ function can change drug exposure. Vomiting or diarrhoea can cause dehydration and worsen kidney function. Report reduced urine, poor intake, jaundice and new medicines. The clinical assessment combines results with symptoms.

Sources: nccp

Describe symptoms in practical terms

Tell the team about the worst symptoms since the last visit, even if improved today. Examples include extra stools above your usual pattern, whether mouth pain affects drinking, and whether numbness affects buttons or balance.

Sources: patient

How benefit is assessed

After surgery, treatment aims to reduce the chance of recurrence; there may be no visible cancer to measure. In advanced disease, scans, symptoms and function help assess control. Ask when review is planned and what would lead to a change.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 04

Separate the symptoms from the cause

You do not need to diagnose an adverse effect before calling.

Hand-foot syndrome versus neuropathy

Capecitabine can cause tender, red or peeling palms and soles. Oxaliplatin more often causes cold-triggered tingling and cumulative numbness. Both can affect walking or hand use. Tell the team about both the sensation and what you can no longer do.

Sources: patient

Diarrhoea and dehydration

Record stools above your own usual pattern, night-time symptoms and stoma output if relevant. Reduced urine, dizziness, inability to drink or worsening diarrhoea needs prompt advice. A medicine to stop diarrhoea does not replace the interruption plan.

Sources: patient

Mouth, appetite and fatigue

Mouth soreness can make food and fluid difficult. Start gentle mouth care and report painful swallowing or reduced intake. Ask for symptom and nutritional support. New major weakness or breathlessness needs assessment.

Sources: hse

Heart or infusion symptoms

Fluoropyrimidines can cause serious cardiac effects; oxaliplatin can cause allergic reactions. New chest symptoms, fainting or breathing difficulty need urgent assessment. Do not assume these are anxiety or ordinary cold sensitivity.

Sources: patient

Treatment reference · 05

Living with treatment

Plan around the activities and people that matter to you.

Food, drink and practical help

Use foods and drinks you can manage. Tell the team about weight loss, poor intake or difficulty shopping and cooking; ask for dietitian or practical support. A fixed daily fluid target is not appropriate for everyone, particularly with heart or kidney problems.

Sources: patient

Activity, rest and driving

Balance rest with manageable activity. Record what fatigue stops you doing. Do not drive when dizzy, unwell or affected by sedating medicines. Ask about work adjustments and transport for treatment days.

Sources: patient

Medicines, vaccines and supplements

Bring a complete medicine list, including over-the-counter products and supplements. Ask the oncology pharmacist before changes. Discuss vaccination timing. Do not stop prescribed long-term medicines on your own.

Sources: hse

Fertility, contraception and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment when relevant. Ask for a written contraception and breastfeeding plan covering each medicine and the period after treatment. Tell the team promptly about a possible pregnancy.

Sources: hse

Support for you and your family

Ask about psycho-oncology, social work, counselling and symptom support. Take information in manageable amounts. A family member or trusted person can help record questions if you wish. Discuss how treatment fits your priorities.

Sources: patient

Treatment reference · 06

The names on your treatment plan

Useful terms for clinic and pharmacy.

CAPOX, XELOX and fluoropyrimidine

CAPOX and XELOX name the same capecitabine–oxaliplatin combination. Capecitabine is a fluoropyrimidine, a drug family that includes fluorouracil. It is not interchangeable dose-for-dose with another tablet or infusion.

Sources: nccp

Adherence and interruption

Adherence means following the agreed plan, including stopping for toxicity when instructed. Interrupting tablets and calling about a serious symptom is part of safe treatment, not a failure to comply.

Sources: nccp

CAPOXBolus fluorouracil + folinic acid

Absolute differences were 6 and 7 percentage points respectively. This trial does not establish that three months is sufficient for everyone, nor does it predict outcomes in metastatic colorectal or postoperative gastric cancer.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Does having more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably show whether the cancer is responding. Report them so they can be treated. Your team assesses benefit using the tests and review plan appropriate to your cancer.

Why might a dose be delayed or reduced?

Your team may allow recovery, investigate a symptom or reduce the chance of lasting harm. Ask which drug changes, why, and when review is planned. A delay does not itself mean treatment has failed.

What should I say when calling?

Give the regimen name, last treatment date, symptoms and when they started. Explain whether you can eat, drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Give your temperature if available, but do not delay calling to collect details.

What belongs in the consent conversation?

The individual aim and likely benefit, uncertainty, reasonable alternatives including not proceeding, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and practical concerns. You can ask for an interpreter, more time or another explanation and can reconsider your decision.

How many cycles should I use this passport for?

The passport provides reusable recording pages, not a prescribed course length. Your oncology team sets the duration and can provide extra pages.

Can I use this page to decide if blood results are safe?

No. Treatment decisions require the clinical protocol, symptoms, previous toxicity, organ function and the prescriber’s review. The patient page explains the checks and does not authorise treatment.
