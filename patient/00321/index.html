CAPOX / XELOX

CAPOX, also called XELOX, combines capecitabine tablets with oxaliplatin infusion. This NCCP protocol covers adjuvant stage III colon cancer, advanced colorectal cancer and adjuvant gastric cancer after D2 surgery. Confirm the treatment aim and duration for your indication.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

More about my medicines

Capecitabine

The body converts capecitabine into fluorouracil, which disrupts DNA and RNA processes needed for cell growth. Tablets are active chemotherapy; taking them at home still requires a clear monitoring and interruption plan.

Oxaliplatin

Oxaliplatin makes links within DNA. These interfere with copying and repair, which can stop a cancer cell dividing. Healthy tissues can also be affected.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Infusion and tablet plan

Your team checks symptoms, blood results and the oral prescription. Make sure you know the tablet strengths, number per dose and exact start and stop instructions.

What to do: Use the written prescription. Take capecitabine with water within 30 minutes after food; never guess the tablet count.

Day 2–7: Tablets at home

Continue twice-daily capecitabine according to the prescription. A missed or vomited dose is not replaced. Record doses missed and contact the team if you are unsure.

What to do: Do not double the next dose. Keep tablets in the original packaging, away from children; do not crush or split unless the oncology pharmacist has arranged it.

Day 8–14: Watch for accumulating symptoms

Diarrhoea, mouth ulcers or painful hands and feet can develop or worsen. Poor intake and dehydration can worsen kidney function and increase risk from capecitabine.

What to do: Stop tablets and contact oncology for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth or hand-foot symptoms affecting activity, fever, dehydration or chest symptoms. Do not restart without advice.

Day 15–21: The prescribed tablet-free week

No capecitabine is planned in this interval, but side effects and infection can still occur. Keep recording symptoms and confirm blood-test and review arrangements.

What to do: Do not use leftover tablets to make up missed doses or start the next cycle early. The next cycle requires team confirmation.

Support at home

Nausea prevention and rescue

Take the prescribed prevention medicines at the planned times and keep rescue treatment available. Tell the team if nausea or constipation is not controlled; do not duplicate steroid or anti-sickness medicines.

Bowel symptoms

Report diarrhoea promptly and follow the written rescue plan. Severe symptoms, fever, dehydration or inability to drink need urgent advice.

Counts and recovery

White-cell growth-factor injections are prescribed according to individual risk and previous complications. They are not automatically required for every patient or every cycle.

Cold and nerve symptoms

Avoid cold exposure as advised after oxaliplatin. Report persistent numbness or difficulty with buttons, walking or everyday tasks before the next dose.

Skin and mouth care

Use gentle skin and mouth care. Painful hands and feet or mouth sores can require a tablet hold and team review; do not continue simply to finish the pack.

Warfarin monitoring

If taking warfarin: baseline and regular INR monitoring as clinically indicated.
