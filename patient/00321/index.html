Gastrointestinal cancers · NCCP 00321 v9

CAPOX / XELOX

CAPOX, also called XELOX, combines capecitabine tablets with oxaliplatin infusion. This NCCP protocol covers adjuvant stage III colon cancer, advanced colorectal cancer and adjuvant gastric cancer after D2 surgery. Confirm the treatment aim and duration for your indication.

How your medicines work

Oral chemotherapy

Capecitabine

Becomes fluorouracil in the body
Swallowed tablet→Converted to fluorouracil→Cell growth disrupted

The body converts capecitabine into fluorouracil, which disrupts DNA and RNA processes needed for cell growth. Tablets are active chemotherapy; taking them at home still requires a clear monitoring and interruption plan.

Stop tablets and contact the team for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth ulcers, hand-foot soreness affecting activities, fever or dehydration. Chest pain needs urgent assessment. Do not restart until instructed.

Platinum chemotherapy

Oxaliplatin

Links DNA strands
Platinum enters cell→DNA links form→Copying is disrupted

Oxaliplatin makes links within DNA. These interfere with copying and repair, which can stop a cancer cell dividing. Healthy tissues can also be affected.

Cold can trigger tingling or throat discomfort. Tell the team about persistent numbness, difficulty with buttons or walking, or infusion symptoms. New breathing difficulty always needs assessment.

Capecitabine

  • Diarrhoea / dehydration
  • Hand-foot syndrome
  • Mouth ulcers / reduced intake
  • Low blood counts and infection
  • Cardiotoxicity including chest pain / ischaemia
  • Severe early toxicity including DPD-related toxicity
  • Renal impairment and medicine interactions

Oxaliplatin

  • Cold-triggered tingling / throat discomfort
  • Persistent sensory neuropathy, sometimes lasting after treatment
  • Allergic / infusion reaction
  • Low blood counts and infection
  • Nausea, diarrhoea and fatigue
  • Rare serious lung or blood complications

Support around treatment

A written tablet calendar

The dose may require different tablet strengths. Ask the pharmacist to mark the number of each strength per dose. The guide does not calculate your dose. Bring the calendar and remaining tablets to review as instructed.

DPD testing and kidney function

DPD deficiency can cause severe fluoropyrimidine toxicity. Kidney impairment can also increase exposure to capecitabine metabolites. Testing and blood monitoring support safety but do not make serious symptoms safe to ignore.

Interaction check

Tell the team about blood thinners, especially warfarin, and all new medicines. Some combinations require closer monitoring or a different plan. Ask the oncology pharmacist before starting supplements or over-the-counter medicines.

Hands, feet and cold exposure

Use gentle skin care and reduce pressure, friction and heat on sore hands and feet. Oxaliplatin can also cause cold-triggered nerve symptoms. These effects differ; report pain, numbness or functional change.

Do not wait for the next appointment

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
