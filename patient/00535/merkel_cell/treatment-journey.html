A reference cycle is not a personalised calendar. Tests listed as symptom-triggered or individual are not presented as mandatory tests at every visit. No universal imaging or tumour-marker interval is invented.

Keep the pathology diagnosis and the agreed metastatic-disease imaging plan.Blood count, kidney/liver tests, glucose and thyroid tests before every cycle. The nurse watches for infusion reactions and the team checks for bowel, breathing, skin, endocrine and other immune symptoms. Additional tests depend on symptoms, not just the routine blood panel.Immunotherapy can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening diarrhoea, blood or mucus in stools, abdominal pain, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell every treating clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy. During the infusion, tell the nurse immediately about chills, flushing, itching, chest or back pain, dizziness or breathing difficulty.Keep reporting possible immune effects after treatment stops. Follow any steroid-taper or hormone-replacement instructions and the individual scan and follow-up plan. The whole treatment journey

Avelumab — metastatic Merkel-cell carcinoma: treatment and checks

Metastatic Merkel-cell carcinoma after chemotherapy

Avelumab by IV infusion every 14 days, continuing while beneficial and tolerable. The first four infusions have a specific premedication plan; four is not the course limit.

Read from left to right. Columns show stages, not equal lengths of time. This is a general plan: no dates or personal information are needed. Your team confirms which steps apply and arranges appointments.
