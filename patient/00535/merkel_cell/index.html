More about my medicines

Avelumab

Avelumab blocks PD-L1, a signal that can reduce the activity of immune cells. It may help the immune system attack Merkel-cell cancer, but can also inflame healthy organs. It is not cytotoxic chemotherapy, even when it follows chemotherapy.

Getting ready for treatment visits

Consent discussion aid

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Treatment day

Avelumab 800 mg is infused over 60 minutes. An antihistamine and paracetamol are given before the first four infusions; NCCP specifies at least 30 minutes beforehand. After that, premedication is at consultant discretion if no reaction occurred. Allow additional time for checks, preparation and observation.

What to do: Report symptoms and previous reactions before treatment.

Day 2–14: Between treatment visits

Immunotherapy can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening diarrhoea, blood or mucus in stools, abdominal pain, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell every treating clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy. During the infusion, tell the nurse immediately about chills, flushing, itching, chest or back pain, dizziness or breathing difficulty.

What to do: Do not wait for the next appointment to report urgent symptoms.

Support at home

Supportive medicines and symptom plan

Avelumab has minimal emetogenic risk in current NCCP. Premedication for the first four infusions prevents or reduces infusion reactions and is distinct from anti-sickness treatment. Confirm the medicine names and timing on the appointment plan. Other symptom treatments depend on the individual; immune-related symptoms need assessment rather than self-treatment alone.

Skin and daily care

Report rash and itching early and obtain urgent assessment for blistering, peeling or painful widespread rash. Check your skin and report a new or growing lump or persistent lesion; do not assume every new skin change is a treatment rash.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least one month after the last avelumab dose. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding with the team before treatment; do not substitute the different interval used for another immunotherapy.
