When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Immunotherapy can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening diarrhoea, blood or mucus in stools, abdominal pain, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell every treating clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

To help immune cells control metastatic Merkel-cell cancer after chemotherapy; benefit, side effects and suitability for continued treatment are reviewed regularly.

PD-L1 checkpoint immunotherapy

Avelumab

Bavencio
Blocks a PD-L1 immune brake

Avelumab blocks PD-L1, a signal that can reduce the activity of immune cells. It may help the immune system attack Merkel-cell cancer, but can also inflame healthy organs. It is not cytotoxic chemotherapy, even when it follows chemotherapy.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Treatment day

Avelumab 800 mg is infused over 60 minutes. An antihistamine and paracetamol are given before the first four infusions; NCCP specifies at least 30 minutes beforehand. After that, premedication is at consultant discretion if no reaction occurred. Allow additional time for checks, preparation and observation. Report symptoms and previous reactions before treatment.

Day 2–14: Between treatment visits

Immunotherapy can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening diarrhoea, blood or mucus in stools, abdominal pain, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell every treating clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy. During the infusion, tell the nurse immediately about chills, flushing, itching, chest or back pain, dizziness or breathing difficulty. Do not wait for the next appointment to report urgent symptoms.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Taking or receiving treatment

Avelumab 800 mg is infused over 60 minutes. An antihistamine and paracetamol are given before the first four infusions; NCCP specifies at least 30 minutes beforehand. After that, premedication is at consultant discretion if no reaction occurred. Allow additional time for checks, preparation and observation.

STEP 2

Missed, vomited or held treatment

Contact the unit if a visit is missed or treatment is held. A new appointment and any changed premedication or infusion plan must be confirmed by the team. A severe infusion or immune reaction can change whether treatment is restarted at all.

STEP 3

Appointments and supply

Before each visit, check four things: the blood-test booking, how you will get there, your medicine supply and any help you need. Ask who will review the results and tell you whether the next dose can go ahead.

STEP 4

Paper copies and contacts

Keep the handbook, short guide and treatment card in one place. Ask your team to fill in the real daytime and emergency numbers and print a replacement when the plan changes. A phone is optional.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines and symptom plan

Avelumab has minimal emetogenic risk in current NCCP. Premedication for the first four infusions prevents or reduces infusion reactions and is distinct from anti-sickness treatment. Confirm the medicine names and timing on the appointment plan. Other symptom treatments depend on the individual; immune-related symptoms need assessment rather than self-treatment alone.

Skin and daily care

Report rash and itching early and obtain urgent assessment for blistering, peeling or painful widespread rash. Check your skin and report a new or growing lump or persistent lesion; do not assume every new skin change is a treatment rash.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least one month after the last avelumab dose. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding with the team before treatment; do not substitute the different interval used for another immunotherapy.

Treatment reference · 01

Understanding your diagnosis and treatment goal

Use your own pathology, stage and agreed plan alongside this guide.

Confirm the cancer type

Merkel-cell carcinoma is a distinct skin cancer. Keep the pathology report and the sites of metastatic disease; this guide is not for melanoma or urothelial cancer. Ask the team to explain unfamiliar terms in the report.

Where this treatment fits

The current NCCP Merkel-cell indication is metastatic Merkel-cell carcinoma after at least one line of chemotherapy.

Scans and local care

Ask which scans will measure response and whether surgery or radiotherapy has a separate role for symptoms or a particular cancer site. Record their timing in the overall plan.

Your previous treatment

Keep the names, dates, benefits and significant side effects of prior chemotherapy. Bring that history to the immunotherapy discussion rather than assuming the old treatment schedule or toxicity plan still applies.

Treatment reference · 02

Your medicines and overall plan

To help immune cells control metastatic Merkel-cell cancer after chemotherapy; benefit, side effects and suitability for continued treatment are reviewed regularly.

The schedule

Avelumab by IV infusion every 14 days, continuing while beneficial and tolerable. The first four infusions have a specific premedication plan; four is not the course limit.

How treatment works

Avelumab blocks PD-L1, a signal that can reduce the activity of immune cells. It may help the immune system attack Merkel-cell cancer, but can also inflame healthy organs. It is not cytotoxic chemotherapy, even when it follows chemotherapy.

Important boundaries

This is the Merkel-cell cancer context after chemotherapy under current NCCP. It is not avelumab maintenance for urothelial cancer. Evidence from untreated first-line Merkel-cell cohorts must not be presented as the evidence for this specific post-chemotherapy indication.

Evidence and choices

28 of 88 patients responded (31.8%): eight complete and twenty partial responses; 23 of 28 responses were ongoing at the analysis. These are group results from that study, not a prediction for you. No randomised comparator or demonstrated comparative survival advantage. Trial dose was weight-based; current NCCP uses 800 mg. First-line Part B is a different population and must not replace Part A for this source indication.

Treatment reference · 03

Taking or receiving treatment safely

Keep the written prescription and actual treatment record together.

Administration

Avelumab 800 mg is infused over 60 minutes. An antihistamine and paracetamol are given before the first four infusions; NCCP specifies at least 30 minutes beforehand. After that, premedication is at consultant discretion if no reaction occurred. Allow additional time for checks, preparation and observation.

Missed or held treatment

Contact the unit if a visit is missed or treatment is held. A new appointment and any changed premedication or infusion plan must be confirmed by the team. A severe infusion or immune reaction can change whether treatment is restarted at all.

Other medicines

Give the team a complete list of prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines, supplements and allergies. Ask before adding a new medicine; treatment interactions and symptom-masking can matter.

Practical arrangements

Write down where blood samples are taken, who receives the results and how you obtain the next appointment or medicine supply. Request a printed plan if that is easier to use.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests, results and treatment decisions

Safety tests and scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Blood count, kidney and liver function, glucose and thyroid tests; review prior chemotherapy, immune conditions, previous reactions, transplantation, infection and current medicines. Keep the pathology diagnosis and the agreed metastatic-disease imaging plan.

During treatment

Blood count, kidney/liver tests, glucose and thyroid tests before every cycle. The nurse watches for infusion reactions and the team checks for bowel, breathing, skin, endocrine and other immune symptoms. Additional tests depend on symptoms, not just the routine blood panel.

What a result means

Ask which result is abnormal, whether the finding needs a repeat test and whether treatment is held, changed or stopped. Do not use a reference range or one normal result to decide independently that a dose is safe.

Response and follow-up

Keep reporting possible immune effects after treatment stops. Follow any steroid-taper or hormone-replacement instructions and the individual scan and follow-up plan. There is no automatic four-infusion stop or universal tumour-marker schedule.

What to expect between visits

A quiet treatment day does not rule out later problems.

Act on new symptoms

Immunotherapy can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening diarrhoea, blood or mucus in stools, abdominal pain, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell every treating clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy. During the infusion, tell the nurse immediately about chills, flushing, itching, chest or back pain, dizziness or breathing difficulty.

Supportive medicines

Avelumab has minimal emetogenic risk in current NCCP. Premedication for the first four infusions prevents or reduces infusion reactions and is distinct from anti-sickness treatment. Confirm the medicine names and timing on the appointment plan. Other symptom treatments depend on the individual; immune-related symptoms need assessment rather than self-treatment alone.

A useful daily record

Record symptoms, temperature when unwell, bowel changes, fluid intake when reduced, missed doses and any medicines used for relief. Note how symptoms affect eating, walking, sleep and normal activities; this helps the team judge severity.

When life is affected

Bring up difficulties with eating, sleep, pain, energy, work or caring for someone. The team can arrange symptom support while cancer treatment continues.

Treatment reference · 06

Skin, body changes and daily care

Use the detailed body map to locate symptoms, not to diagnose them.

Skin and sun

Report rash and itching early and obtain urgent assessment for blistering, peeling or painful widespread rash. Check your skin and report a new or growing lump or persistent lesion; do not assume every new skin change is a treatment rash.

The body map

The organ anchors show possible locations and warning symptoms. They do not show how common each effect is, and a symptom may have another cause. New symptoms should be assessed in their clinical context.

Food, fluids and activity

Choose food, fluids and movement that you can manage, within any personal restrictions. Ask for help early if you are losing weight, drinking less or finding everyday activity harder.

Keep checking the plan

A new medicine, fever, hospital admission, procedure or radiotherapy may change treatment timing. Tell the oncology team and take the wallet card or medicine list to other appointments.

Treatment reference · 07

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss these issues before the first dose whenever possible.

Contraception and pregnancy

Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least one month after the last avelumab dose. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding with the team before treatment; do not substitute the different interval used for another immunotherapy.

Fertility preservation

Ask whether treatment or the cancer may affect fertility and whether fertility preservation is appropriate before treatment. A change in periods or sexual function does not reliably establish infertility.

Written dates and methods

Record the agreed method, last treatment date and how long precautions continue. Different medicines have different intervals, so ask for clarification after a switch or combination change.

Sexual health

You can request a private discussion about intimacy, sexual symptoms or relationships. Ask for help if the agreed pregnancy precautions are difficult to follow.

Treatment reference · 08

Decisions, support and useful records

Your priorities belong in the treatment discussion.

Benefits and alternatives

Ask what the treatment aims to achieve, what alternatives are suitable, how likely important benefits are in your situation and how treatment burden or uncertainty affects that choice. A trial average cannot promise an individual outcome.

Support alongside treatment

Supportive care addresses symptoms and daily life during cancer treatment. Palliative care can be part of that support; accepting it does not mean you must stop anticancer treatment.

A person you trust

Choose whether to involve a relative or supporter and what may be shared with them. Ask for an interpreter or a different information format. Check that you can explain the next dose, tests and urgent-contact plan in your own words.

Your document set

Keep the current treatment summary, medicine list, test plan and contact card together. Use the printed instructions when you need them; the QR code is an optional link to further information.

Treatment reference · 09

After a treatment hold, change or completion

The next step should be written down, not assumed.

Follow-up and late effects

Keep reporting possible immune effects after treatment stops. Follow any steroid-taper or hormone-replacement instructions and the individual scan and follow-up plan. There is no automatic four-infusion stop or universal tumour-marker schedule.

Restarting or changing treatment

Contact the unit if a visit is missed or treatment is held. A new appointment and any changed premedication or infusion plan must be confirmed by the team. A severe infusion or immune reaction can change whether treatment is restarted at all.

A treatment summary

Ask for a treatment summary showing what you received, important side effects, admissions, the last dose and the follow-up team. Share the current version with your GP and other clinicians.

Your next review

Before leaving, confirm the next test and review, how you will receive results and whom to call sooner. Keep those numbers available after treatment ends.

What the treatment evidence can and cannot tell you

Evidence needs to match the medicine, dose combination, disease stage and previous treatment.
