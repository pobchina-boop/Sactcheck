When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Treat relapsed or resistant testicular germ-cell cancer, with the possibility of lasting remission in selected patients.

Taxane chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Taxol (conventional paclitaxel)
Locks the cell division machinery

Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues.

Microtubules form↓Structures remain locked↓Division is interrupted

What to watch for
Report tingling or numbness before the next dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Sources: nccp

Chemotherapy

Ifosfamide

Ifosfamide
Damages deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) needed for cell division

Ifosfamide damages cancer-cell DNA. Its breakdown products can also affect the bladder, kidneys and brain. Mesna protects the bladder but does not prevent all kidney or brain effects.

Treatment target↓Cancer-cell effect↓Healthy-tissue effects

What to watch for
Report confusion, sleepiness, blood in urine or reduced urine immediately.

Sources: nccp

Platinum chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Damages DNA through platinum links

Cisplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing, but healthy tissues may also be affected. Kidney protection with fluids and monitoring is central to cisplatin treatment.

Platinum reaches cells↓DNA links form↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report ringing in the ears, hearing changes, numbness or loss of function. Tell the team if you cannot drink, are passing less urine or become swollen or breathless during fluids.

Sources: nccp

Individual prescribed course

Paclitaxel is given on day 1. Cisplatin and ifosfamide are given on days 1–5, with bladder-protecting mesna each day. Growth-factor injections start on day 6 until neutrophil recovery under local policy. Four 21-day cycles are planned, subject to consultant review.

This is NCCP 00602: paclitaxel175 mg/m² day 1, ifosfamide1200 mg/m² and cisplatin20 mg/m² days 1–5. Other published TIP schedules differ.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the five-day attendance/admission plan, mesna timings, hydration, allergy prevention and growth-factor prescription.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Paclitaxel runs over three hours on day 1, cisplatin over one hour and ifosfamide over two hours on days 1–5. Mesna is given immediately before ifosfamide and again four and eight hours after the ifosfamide infusion finishes. The unit coordinates the complete schedule; do not calculate home timings yourself.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

Suitable intravenous access is needed for repeated infusions. Report drip-site pain or swelling immediately. Admission and line choice are agreed locally.

STEP 4

Home support

Cisplatin needs a high-risk anti-sickness plan and prescribed hydration/electrolytes. Ifosfamide needs mesna and adequate fluids. Paclitaxel needs prescribed allergy premedication; reconcile steroid doses with antiemetics. Aprepitant may increase ifosfamide brain toxicity, so pharmacy must review the combination. Growth factor is included from day 6. Retain prescribed mouth care, pain relief and stomach protection.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

MRC phase II TIP study · Mead2005

Among 43 patients eligible for response assessment, the favourable response rate was 60% (95% confidence interval44–75%). This combined complete responses with partial responses in which tumour markers became negative.

This small, non-randomised study used ifosfamide1000 mg/m²/day, compared with 1200 mg/m²/day in NCCP 00602. A favourable response is not the same as cure, and these results cannot predict an individual outcome.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Treat relapsed or resistant testicular germ-cell cancer, with the possibility of lasting remission in selected patients. The germ-cell specialist team chooses salvage chemotherapy using previous response, relapse timing, tumour markers and disease extent. Surgery for remaining masses or a high-dose stem-cell pathway may be considered separately; neither is automatically part of this calendar.

When do I attend?

Paclitaxel is given on day 1. Cisplatin and ifosfamide are given on days 1–5, with bladder-protecting mesna each day. Growth-factor injections start on day 6 until neutrophil recovery under local policy. Four 21-day cycles are planned, subject to consultant review.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
