Nab-paclitaxel and gemcitabine for metastatic pancreatic cancer

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

This first treatment for pancreatic cancer that has spread aims to slow its growth and relieve symptoms. First-line metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma

Treatment schedule. Two infusions on days 1, 8 and 15. Week four is a break. Repeat every 28 days while helping and tolerable.

Regimen-specific side effects

Nab-paclitaxel

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Nerve injuryReport tingling, numbness or weakness before the next dose, especially if walking or fine movements are affected.
Infection, hair loss and infusion reactionFever or shivering needs an urgent call. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, wheeze or faintness during the infusion. Hair loss is common.

Gemcitabine

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Low blood countsCall promptly for fever, shivering, unexplained bruises or bleeding.
Lungs, kidneys and skinContact oncology urgently for new breathlessness, reduced urine or a painful blistering rash.

Treatment discussion and decision

Nab-paclitaxel and gemcitabine for metastatic pancreatic cancer

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

In metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma, adding nab-paclitaxel to gemcitabine improved median overall survival (8.5 versus 6.7 months). These are group results from the named setting. They cannot predict the benefit or side effects for an individual.

This first treatment for pancreatic cancer that has spread aims to slow its growth and relieve symptoms. Discuss alternatives, fertility, individual risks and supportive care with the treating team.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Fever, shivering or sudden illness: phone oncology immediately. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
