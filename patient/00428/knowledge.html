When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology immediately for fever, shivering, sudden illness, severe diarrhoea, inability to drink, very little urine or unusual bleeding. New severe chest pain, collapse or major breathing difficulty needs emergency help.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Your medicines and what they do

Weekly fluorouracil with folinic acid can reduce recurrence risk after surgery or help control metastatic colorectal cancer. Your team confirms the intended benefit.

Chemotherapy modulator

Folinic acid

Calcium leucovorin
Strengthens fluorouracil action

Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself.

Folinic acid↓Stronger enzyme binding↓Fluorouracil effect increases

What to watch for
The combined treatment can increase mouth soreness and diarrhoea. Report these promptly. Rare allergic reactions can occur.

Sources: nccp

Chemotherapy

Fluorouracil

5-FU
Disrupts cell building blocks

Fluorouracil interferes with DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. The same drug has different schedules; follow the prescription for this regimen.

Drug enters cell↓DNA / RNA disrupted↓Growth slows

What to watch for
Report diarrhoea, mouth sores, painful hands or feet and unexpected severe early symptoms. Chest pain needs urgent assessment even after a normal DPD test.

Sources: nccp

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Weekly treatment visit

Blood tests and symptom review before the injections. Side effects can occur on any day; the calendar does not predict when you will feel well. Call the oncology team if unwell. Report symptoms before the next dose; do not wait for your next appointment if urgent.

Day 2–7: Between weekly visits

Report bowel, mouth, skin or infection symptoms promptly. Side effects can occur on any day; the calendar does not predict when you will feel well. Call the oncology team if unwell. Report symptoms before the next dose; do not wait for your next appointment if urgent.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines

Use the prescribed anti-sickness and diarrhoea plan; check the medicine, timing and rescue duration. Do not duplicate steroids or anti-sickness medicines.

Mouth, bowel and skin care

Report soreness and diarrhoea early. Use gentle mouth and skin care and ask for help with food or fluids.

Medicine checks

Bring all prescription medicines, supplements and non-prescription products. Warfarin and phenytoin may need closer monitoring; brivudine/sorivudine interactions require pharmacy review.

Understanding the evidence

Ask which evidence matches your treatment aim.

The QUASAR trial compared adjuvant fluorouracil/folinic acid with observation after colorectal cancer resection, mainly in stage II disease. Its group results do not predict an individual benefit or establish the metastatic indication.

Sources: nccp · trial

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Which treatment plan applies to me?

Folinic acid and fluorouracil injections are given once each week. The adjuvant course is 30 weeks; metastatic treatment continues only while appropriate. This weekly schedule has no planned two-week rest block. Your team confirms any modification.

Do more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably measure response. Report symptoms and follow the agreed review plan.

Who do I contact overnight?

Ask the team to replace example numbers with working daytime, out-of-hours and backup contact routes before leaving.

Try explaining the plan back: Can you explain which medicines you are receiving, the schedule, the contact plan and the symptoms that need prompt help?
