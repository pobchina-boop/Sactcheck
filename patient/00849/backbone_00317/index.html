Nivolumab, pemetrexed and cisplatin before surgery

This treatment is given before planned surgery for resectable non-small-cell lung cancer at high risk of returning. It aims to treat the cancer before the operation and reduce the chance of recurrence. Surgery and benefit are not guaranteed.

How your medicines work

Immunotherapy

Nivolumab

Blocks the PD-1 immune checkpoint
T cell→PD-1 blocked→Immune response

Nivolumab binds PD-1 on T cells. It prevents signals that can reduce the immune attack on cancer. This can also let immune cells inflame healthy tissues. It is infused every 21 days in this plan.

New breathing, bowel, skin, hormone or other symptoms need prompt advice during or after treatment.

Antifolate chemotherapy

Pemetrexed

Interrupts deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) building-block production
Folate-dependent enzymes blocked→DNA building blocks reduced→Cell growth disrupted

Pemetrexed blocks several enzymes that use folate to make the building blocks of DNA and RNA. This can slow or stop cancer-cell growth. Healthy cells can also be affected. Prescribed folic acid and vitamin B12 reduce important toxicities and are part of treatment.

Take the prescribed folic acid and attend for B12. Report mouth sores, diarrhoea, a rash, breathlessness or reduced urine; check before taking ibuprofen or other non-prescription medicines.

Platinum chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Damages DNA through platinum links
Platinum reaches cells→DNA links form→Cell division disrupted

Cisplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing, but healthy tissues may also be affected. Kidney protection with fluids and monitoring is central to cisplatin treatment.

Report ringing in the ears, hearing changes, numbness or loss of function. Tell the team if you cannot drink, are passing less urine or become swollen or breathless during fluids.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm blood tests, transport, medicine instructions and the oncology contact number.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Nivolumab is infused over 30 minutes before chemotherapy. Pemetrexed is infused over 10 minutes. Cisplatin is infused over one hour, with prescribed hydration before and afterwards. Blood-result review, preparation, support medicines and observation add to the visit.

STEP 3

Access

Treatment uses suitable intravenous (IV) access. No routine take-home infusion pump is used.

STEP 4

Home support

Take the prescribed folic acid, vitamin B12 and rash-prevention steroid plan; continue folic acid for 21 days after the final pemetrexed dose. Cisplatin needs hydration, electrolyte checks and monitoring of kidneys, hearing and nerves.

STEP 5

Surgical review

Keep the oncology and surgical teams informed about new symptoms and other appointments.

Support around treatment

Your written prevention plan

Take the prescribed folic acid, vitamin B12 and rash-prevention steroid plan; continue folic acid for 21 days after the final pemetrexed dose. Cisplatin needs hydration, electrolyte checks and monitoring of kidneys, hearing and nerves.

Immune alert card

Nivolumab can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Report new symptoms promptly and carry your immune alert card for at least five months after the last dose.

Prepare for surgery

The oncology and surgical teams agree scans, recovery checks and timing. Report any illness or new symptom before the planned operation.

Do not wait for the next appointment
