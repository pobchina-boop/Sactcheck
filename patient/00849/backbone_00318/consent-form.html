Treatment context
This treatment is given before planned surgery for resectable non-small-cell lung cancer at high risk of returning. It aims to treat the cancer before the operation and reduce the chance of recurrence. Surgery and benefit are not guaranteed.
Schedule
Up to three 21-day cycles. Day 1: nivolumab, pemetrexed and carboplatin. Then surgical MDT reassessment. The five listed backbones are alternatives; this guide covers only your selected combination.

2. Regimen / agent-specific material risks

Nivolumab

Immunotherapy
Immune inflammation in multiple organs
Infusion reactions
Delayed effects after treatment stops

Pemetrexed

Antifolate chemotherapy
Low blood counts, infection and bleeding
Nausea, vomiting and fatigue
Mouth soreness and diarrhoea
Kidney problems
Allergic or infusion reactions
Skin rash; rarely serious skin reactions
Rare serious lung inflammation

Carboplatin

Chemotherapy
Low blood counts and infection
Nausea and vomiting
Kidney, hearing and nerve effects
Allergic reactions

Take the prescribed folic acid, vitamin B12 and rash-prevention steroid plan; continue folic acid for 21 days after the final pemetrexed dose. Carboplatin dosing uses a verified kidney-function measurement; report dehydration or a change in health. Nivolumab can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Report new symptoms promptly and carry your immune alert card for at least five months after the last dose. Discuss severe or fatal immune inflammation and chemotherapy effects, including lasting hormone replacement, organ injury and effects on surgery timing.
