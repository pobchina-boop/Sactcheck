SACTCheck · National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) 00455 · My treatment at a glance

Pembrolizumab every 3 weeks for head and neck cancer

Pembrolizumab aims to control head and neck cancer that has returned or spread, relieve symptoms and help some people live longer. Benefit and side effects are reviewed for you.

First-line treatment of metastatic or unresectable recurrent head and neck squamous cell carcinoma with a tumour PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) of at least 1. This indication excludes nasopharyngeal cancer and cancer progressing within six months after curatively intended systemic treatment for locoregionally advanced disease. · NCCP 00455 v16 · Document revised 9 November 2026

My overall treatment

Pembrolizumab alone every 3 weeks; duration individually reviewed.

21day view

Treatment timing in this window

Pembrolizumab treatmentDay 1
  1. Pembrolizumab monotherapyPembrolizumab 200 mg is given through a vein over about 30 minutes on Day 1 every 3 weeks. The whole visit includes safety checks, preparation and observation. Treatment continues until progression or unacceptable toxicity. After 24 months of sustained response without progression, the team may consider stopping; stopping at 24 months is not automatic.

My tests and checks

WhenWhat is checkedTiming / reason
BeforeBaseline safety and preparationNCCPFull blood count, kidney and liver tests, glucose and thyroid tests; review autoimmune disease, transplants, previous immune side effects and all medicines. Confirm the tumour PD-L1 combined positive score; this is a tumour test, not a routine blood test.
DuringBloods, symptoms and supportNCCPFull blood count, kidney and liver tests and glucose before every 3-week cycle. Thyroid tests every 3–6 weeks; cortisol when clinically indicated.
After / responseFollow-up and testsNCCP / individual planKeep reporting new symptoms after treatment ends. Steroid tapers, hormone replacement or specialist review may continue longer. Scans and examinations follow your individual response and follow-up plan.

Between treatments

  • Immune side effects may start between visits or after the final dose. Report changes promptly.
  • A longer gap between treatments does not remove scheduled tests or the need to report symptoms.

Bring your medicine list, test dates, treatment calendar and actual urgent contacts.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
