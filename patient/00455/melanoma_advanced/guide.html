Pembrolizumab · every 3 weeks · advanced melanoma

Pembrolizumab helps immune cells act against melanoma that cannot be completely removed or has spread. The aim is disease control; benefit and side effects are reviewed for you.

My overall treatment

Every 3 weeks; duration reviewed with your team.

  1. Ongoing treatment and reviewPembrolizumab is given as 200 mg by IV infusion over 30 minutes, on Day 1 every 3 weeks. Allow extra time for assessment, preparation and observation. Treatment continues until progression or unacceptable toxicity. For sustained response without progression, stopping after 24 months may be considered by the treating team; this is not an automatic stop.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Immunotherapy can cause inflammation in healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new diarrhoea, blood in stools, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell any clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
