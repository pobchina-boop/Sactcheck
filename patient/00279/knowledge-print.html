When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, sudden illness, new breathlessness, uncontrolled vomiting, inability to swallow fluids, much less urine or unusual bleeding. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or heavy bleeding needs emergency care. Tell staff immediately about infusion pain, swelling or allergy symptoms.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

My chemotherapy medicines

Chemotherapy and radiotherapy work together to treat limited-stage small-cell lung cancer. Your team explains the aim and why this combined treatment is suitable for you.

Platinum chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Damages DNA

Cisplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with cell copying. Fluids and kidney checks help reduce harm to healthy tissue.

Treatment reaches cells↓Damages DNA↓Cancer growth may slow

What to watch for
Report poor fluid intake, reduced urine, ringing in the ears or numbness.

Chemotherapy

Etoposide

Chemotherapy
Disrupts DNA repair during cell division

Etoposide blocks an enzyme cells need while copying DNA. The route and prescribed amount differ between infusion and capsules; they are not interchangeable milligram for milligram.

Treatment reaches cells↓Disrupts DNA repair during cell division↓Cancer growth may slow

What to watch for
Report fever, bleeding, severe mouth soreness or vomiting.

Reference plan: four 21-day cycles. Day 1: cisplatin and etoposide. Days 2–3: etoposide. Radiotherapy usually starts with cycle 1; its appointments are planned separately. Capsules, split cisplatin, a 28-day interval or a different platinum are used only if explicitly prescribed.

Radiotherapy alongside chemotherapy

Team-agreed

Usually begins with cycle 1; later start may be appropriate. The radiation team confirms dose, number of visits and timing. A chemotherapy delay does not automatically mean radiation stops.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Day 1: reference combination

Blood results, symptoms, anti-sickness medicines and hydration are checked before both drugs. Tell the nurse about any hearing, nerve or fluid-intake problems.

Day 2–3: Days 2–3: etoposide

IV treatment each day or prescribed capsules twice daily. The split-cisplatin alternative changes these visits. Use your written prescription; do not add IV and oral etoposide together.

Day 4–7: Support and recovery

No routine chemotherapy in the reference plan. Follow ongoing anti-sickness medicines and separate RT appointments. Call early for poor intake, vomiting, swallowing pain or fever.

Day 8–21: Between chemotherapy cycles

Fatigue, mouth symptoms and infection risk can persist. RT may continue according to its own plan. Report symptoms when they occur; do not wait for the next blood test.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Confirm your plan

Reference IV plan: etoposide first on Days 1–3, cisplatin after etoposide on Day 1, each infusion about one hour plus checks and fluids. If prescribed, Days 2–3 etoposide may instead be capsules twice daily. Confirm route, platinum, interval and hydration before treatment; never combine alternatives yourself.

STEP 2

Keep the contact card

The team supplies the daytime and out-of-hours numbers. Keep the card with you and show it when seeking medical care.

STEP 3

Two linked treatment teams

Confirm which team to contact for radiation or chemotherapy changes. Report swallowing problems early and ask about transport, nutrition and fatigue support.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Fluids and sickness prevention

Cisplatin needs a planned fluid and anti-sickness course. Follow the labelled medicines for each treatment day and the days afterwards. Call if vomiting prevents drinking or taking medicines.

If capsules are prescribed

Etoposide capsules on Days 2–3 replace the IV doses on those days. Take the prescribed amount twice daily on an empty stomach. Follow pharmacy handling instructions and contact the team if a dose is missed or vomited; do not take an extra dose without advice.

Radiotherapy support

Ask about swallowing, eating, skin care and fatigue during radiation. Painful swallowing or poor intake needs early help; inability to drink needs urgent assessment.

Sources: nccp · ncis

Why chemotherapy and radiotherapy are combined

Studies support combining systemic treatment with radiation in the relevant lung-cancer setting.

The evidence comes from specific treatment schedules and patient groups. Your team explains how it applies to your situation; study averages cannot predict an individual outcome.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Which drugs and routes are prescribed for me?

Confirm the exact drug names, routes and interval against your written prescription. The team must explain any change from this reference plan.

How do my radiation appointments fit around chemotherapy?

Radiation has its own calendar. Ask for the combined plan and whom to contact if a chemotherapy or radiation visit changes.

Which blood tests are required before the next dose?

Use the checks table as a prompt. Your team confirms which tests are needed, where they are done and who reviews the results before treatment.

Who do I call during the day and overnight?

Ask the team to replace the example numbers on your card with the correct daytime, overnight and backup contact routes before you leave.

When will recovery and response be reviewed?

Agree the follow-up appointments and any scans with your team. This regimen does not set a universal scan or tumour-marker timetable.

Try explaining the plan back: Explain back your chemotherapy days, how radiation is scheduled separately, the tests before treatment and whom you will call if unwell.
