Cisplatin + etoposide with radiotherapy

Chemotherapy and radiation work together to treat limited-stage small-cell lung cancer. Your team explains your treatment aim and choices.

For limited-stage lung cancer: IV cisplatin/etoposide every 21 days. Oral, split-dose, carboplatin or 28-day plans need personalised information.

My overall treatment

Reference IV plan: four 21-day cycles. Day 1: cisplatin + etoposide. Days 2–3: etoposide. Radiation follows its own appointments. Capsules, split cisplatin, carboplatin substitution or 28-day cycles require a different personalised prescription.

  1. ChemotherapyThree treatment days per reference cycle, then recovery. Your team confirms each cycle after safety checks.
  2. RadiotherapyUsually starts with cycle 1. Radiation oncology sets the dose and visits; chemotherapy delays need separate coordination.
  3. After treatmentYour team reviews recovery, persistent symptoms and response. Scan or marker timing is individual.

Between treatments

  • Follow the prescribed anti-sickness course and fluid advice; call early if eating or drinking becomes difficult.
  • Fever, new breathlessness, uncontrolled vomiting or inability to drink needs urgent advice, including after treatment ends.

Confirm your prescription, radiation appointments and blood tests. Bring your medicine list and daytime / overnight contact numbers.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
