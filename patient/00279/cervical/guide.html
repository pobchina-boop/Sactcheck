Cisplatin and etoposide with radiotherapy for small-cell cervical cancer

The team uses chemotherapy to treat the cancer throughout the body and radiotherapy to treat planned areas. Ask your specialist whether the aim in your case is cure or disease control.

Small-cell cervical cancer · NCCP 00279 v8 · Source checked 2026-11-27

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

My overall treatment

Usually four 21-day cycles. Cisplatin is normally given on day 1 and etoposide on days 1–3. The consultant can select a 28-day interval. Radiation usually starts with cycle 1 but can start later for clinical reasons.

My treatment calendar

Day 1 · platinum and etoposideTwo drugs on day 1. Ask about the length of your hydration visit and your signed schedule for days 2 and 3.
Days 2–3 · IV etoposide aloneReturn for etoposide by drip if this is your prescribed route. Continue the dated anti-sickness course started on day 1.
Days 2–3 · oral etoposide aloneTake the capsule strength and number on your label twice daily on the two prescribed days, on an empty stomach. If a dose is missed or vomited, phone the unit for advice before taking more.
Days 2–3 · fractionated cisplatin with etoposideA consultant may divide cisplatin across days 1–3. Return for fluids and cisplatin. The prescription separately states the route of etoposide.

Your team confirms the active phase and dates. Symptoms may occur during or after treatment; there are no guaranteed safe days.

Between treatments

  • Hair loss, tiredness, reduced appetite, nausea and a sore mouth may occur. Pelvic radiation can add diarrhoea or bladder irritation. Tell the team if drinking becomes difficult.
  • Call the 24-hour oncology number for fever, chills, unexplained bleeding, very little urine, uncontrolled vomiting or diarrhoea. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, breathlessness or faintness during an infusion.

Tell the team about ringing ears, hearing aids, tingling or previous platinum treatment.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
