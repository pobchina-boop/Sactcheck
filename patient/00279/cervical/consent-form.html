Cisplatin and etoposide with radiotherapy for small-cell cervical cancer

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

The team uses chemotherapy to treat the cancer throughout the body and radiotherapy to treat planned areas. Ask your specialist whether the aim in your case is cure or disease control. This pathway covers the cervical small-cell neuroendocrine cancer indication. The specialist team confirms the pathology, stage and local radiation plan.

Treatment schedule. Usually four 21-day cycles. Cisplatin is normally given on day 1 and etoposide on days 1–3. The consultant can select a 28-day interval. Radiation usually starts with cycle 1 but can start later for clinical reasons.

Treatment discussion and decision

Cisplatin and etoposide with radiotherapy for small-cell cervical cancer

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

Specialist teams use platinum and etoposide for this rare cancer. A cervical cancer study looked back at earlier treatment records. It cannot tell exactly how much an individual will benefit. Small retrospective subgroups, differing treatments and selection bias. The study used five or more cycles in one group. NCCP 00279 specifies four cycles. Lung cancer survival estimates and thoracic radiation schedules do not apply to this cervical pathway.

In Ireland, using cisplatin with etoposide for small-cell cervical cancer is an off-label use. Your consultant will explain the evidence, risks and alternatives and ask for your consent under hospital policy. Discuss treatment intent and uncertainty in this rare cancer, alternative etoposide routes, kidney and hearing injury, marrow suppression, fertility preservation before pelvic therapy and longer-term pelvic effects.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call the 24-hour oncology number for fever, chills, unexplained bleeding, very little urine, uncontrolled vomiting or diarrhoea. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, breathlessness or faintness during an infusion. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
