Your complete treatment reference

Cisplatin and etoposide with radiotherapy for small-cell cervical cancer

Your treatment and how the medicines work

The team uses chemotherapy to treat the cancer throughout the body and radiotherapy to treat planned areas. Ask your specialist whether the aim in your case is cure or disease control.

Etoposide · Etoposide

Chemotherapy. Blocks an enzyme that cells need to repair DNA while dividing. Rapidly dividing blood and hair cells can be affected too. Call for fever, chills, bleeding or a sore mouth that prevents drinking. Report flushing or dizziness during an infusion.

Cisplatin · Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. Attaches to DNA so cells struggle to copy and repair it. During radiotherapy this can increase damage to the cancer cells. Call if you pass much less urine, cannot keep fluids down, or develop ringing ears, hearing changes or persistent tingling.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · platinum and etoposide

Two drugs on day 1. Ask about the length of your hydration visit and your signed schedule for days 2 and 3.

Days 2–3 · IV etoposide alone

Return for etoposide by drip if this is your prescribed route. Continue the dated anti-sickness course started on day 1.

Days 2–3 · oral etoposide alone

Take the capsule strength and number on your label twice daily on the two prescribed days, on an empty stomach. If a dose is missed or vomited, phone the unit for advice before taking more.

Days 2–3 · fractionated cisplatin with etoposide

A consultant may divide cisplatin across days 1–3. Return for fluids and cisplatin. The prescription separately states the route of etoposide.

Common effects and what to do

What you may notice

Hair loss, tiredness, reduced appetite, nausea and a sore mouth may occur. Pelvic radiation can add diarrhoea or bladder irritation. Tell the team if drinking becomes difficult.

Planning your visits

Appointments

Usually four 21-day cycles. Cisplatin is normally given on day 1 and etoposide on days 1–3. The consultant can select a 28-day interval. Radiation usually starts with cycle 1 but can start later for clinical reasons.

On the day

On the usual day 1, etoposide runs into a vein over about an hour and is followed by cisplatin over about an hour, with protective fluids. On days 2–3, etoposide is either infused or prescribed as capsules twice daily. The written plan states which route and whether cisplatin is split across three days.

Between visits

Radiation appointments are booked separately. Keep both calendars together. Tell the chemotherapy and radiation teams about any dose or appointment change.

What the research found

Specialist teams use platinum and etoposide for this rare cancer. A cervical cancer study looked back at earlier treatment records. It cannot tell exactly how much an individual will benefit.

Small retrospective subgroups, differing treatments and selection bias. The study used five or more cycles in one group. NCCP 00279 specifies four cycles. Lung cancer survival estimates and thoracic radiation schedules do not apply to this cervical pathway.
