Cisplatin + etoposide + chest radiotherapy

Chemotherapy and chest radiotherapy work together to treat lung cancer. Your team will explain the aim, possible benefit and alternatives for you.

Stage III non-small-cell lung cancer · NCCP 00456 v4 · Document revised 4 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

This guide is for Stage III non-small-cell lung cancer. Your radiation team supplies the exact radiation calendar; later treatment is a separate decision.

My overall treatment

Cisplatin on Days 1 and 8; etoposide on Days 1–5. Each cycle lasts 28 days. Two cycles first; two more may be offered after review. Radiotherapy usually starts within 24 hours of the first chemotherapy dose; follow your separate radiation appointment plan.

A typical 28-day cycle

Day 1Both chemotherapy medicines
Day 2–5Etoposide each day
Day 6–7No planned chemotherapy
Day 8Cisplatin only
Day 9–28No further chemotherapy this cycle

Timing may change after review. Side effects can occur on any day; there are no guaranteed safe days.

Between treatments

  • Tiredness, sickness or swallowing discomfort can occur; report difficulty eating or drinking early.
  • Report fever or new breathing changes at any time, including after treatment.

Bring your medicine list and both treatment calendars. Confirm tests, support medicines and urgent contacts before leaving.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
