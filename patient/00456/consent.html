1. Treatment and purpose

Cisplatin + etoposide + chest radiotherapy · NCCP 00456 v4

Cisplatin on Days 1 and 8; etoposide on Days 1–5. Radiation appointments run alongside chemotherapy as planned by radiation oncology. If the cancer has not progressed and treatment remains appropriate, the consultant may prescribe two additional cycles with an agreed radiation plan. These are not automatic. Your team reviews recovery and response, then agrees the next plan. Further drug treatment or surgery is a separate decision.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Cisplatin · Chemotherapy

Report fever, unusual bleeding, reduced urine, hearing changes or tingling.

Etoposide · Chemotherapy

Report fever, bleeding, a sore mouth or feeling faint during an infusion.

Chest radiotherapy · Radiotherapy

Report painful swallowing, poor intake, skin changes, new cough or breathlessness.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss the aim and alternatives, blood-count suppression and serious infection, kidney/hearing/nerve injury, nausea, hair loss, infusion reactions, fertility, and chest radiation effects including swallowing and breathing problems. Etoposide for non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is outside its licensed indication; explain the rationale and follow local off-label consent policy.
