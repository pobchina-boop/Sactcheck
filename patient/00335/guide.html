Imatinib for gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST)

For unresectable or metastatic gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST), imatinib aims to control disease and symptoms. After removal of higher-risk GIST, adjuvant treatment aims to reduce the chance of recurrence. These are different treatment goals and must be clearly recorded.

This is National Cancer Control Programme 00335 for gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST), not an imatinib schedule for leukaemia or another tumour. Advanced and adjuvant aims/durations differ. Low-risk adjuvant GIST is outside this source indication; molecular resistance can also alter suitability.

Between treatments

  • Oral treatment can cause serious effects between visits. Report problems promptly.
  • Use the agreed calendar and never double or restart doses without instructions.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each completed review. Record any agreed medicine change, missed dose or treatment pause. Delays or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A recorded review does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
