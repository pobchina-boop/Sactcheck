Understand your treatment

Your medicines and what they do

Control advanced cancer and symptoms while balancing side effects and treatment burden.

Anti-VEGF targeted antibody

Bevacizumab

Avastin or a prescribed biosimilar
Blocks a blood-vessel growth signal

Bevacizumab binds VEGF, a signal that helps blood vessels grow. Blocking it can restrict the blood supply supporting a tumour. It also affects normal blood vessels and healing, which is why blood pressure, urine and planned procedures matter.

VEGF signal↓Bevacizumab binds VEGF↓New-vessel signalling is reduced

What to watch for
Report bleeding, a wound that is not healing, severe abdominal pain, new swelling, chest symptoms or a severe headache. Tell the team before surgery, dental procedures or port insertion.

Sources: nccp

Taxane chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Taxol (conventional paclitaxel)
Locks the cell division machinery

Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues.

Microtubules form↓Structures remain locked↓Division is interrupted

What to watch for
Report tingling or numbness before the next weekly dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Practical treatment guide

Your treatment and access plan

Bevacizumab 10 mg/kg is given on Days 1 and 15, with paclitaxel 80 mg/m² on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle. Continue according to benefit, toxicity and the treating team’s plan.

STEP 1

Before treatment

Baseline FBC, kidney/liver tests and urine protein. Review blood counts, renal/liver function and urine protein before cycles; Day 8 FBC is explicitly required. Check current counts for each paclitaxel decision.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. A port is not automatically required; insertion and removal are separate procedures with individual instructions.

STEP 3

On the treatment day

Confirm the medicines due that day, any premedicines and the reviewed blood results. Tell staff immediately about pain, burning, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

After the visit

No routine take-home infusion pump is required for this regimen. Keep line-care, urgent-contact and next-review instructions.

Visit length

Bevacizumab: 90 minutes initially, then 60 and 30 minutes if tolerated; paclitaxel: about one hour. Full visit time is longer.

Line care

A PICC or port has its own care plan. Report fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or line damage; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

Treatment changes

A delayed, omitted or changed dose needs an updated plan from the treating team. Do not move doses or restart on your own.

Support for this visit

Separate the first paclitaxel premedication from later weekly doses; review blood pressure, urine protein, wounds and surgery timing for bevacizumab.
