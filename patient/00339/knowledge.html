When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team immediately for fever or sudden illness, shaking chills, breathing difficulty, chest pain, uncontrolled vomiting, very little urine, severe abdominal pain or constipation with vomiting. Tell the nurse immediately about burning or pain at the infusion site. Do not wait until the next visit.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Three medicines. Different jobs.

After surgery, treatment aims to lower the chance of cancer returning. For advanced or recurrent lung cancer, it aims to control disease and symptoms. Your team should mark which goal applies to you; benefit is not guaranteed.

Vinca alkaloid chemotherapy

Vinorelbine

intravenous (IV) infusion
Disrupts microtubules needed for cell division

Vinorelbine disrupts the cell structures that help divide chromosomes. This can slow cancer growth and also affect blood cells, nerves and the bowel.

Binds cell-division structures↓Microtubules cannot function normally↓Cell division is disrupted

What to watch for
Report fever, constipation, numbness or pain at the infusion site promptly.

Sources: nccp

Platinum chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Damages deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) through platinum links

Cisplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing, but healthy tissues may also be affected. Kidney protection with fluids and monitoring is central to cisplatin treatment.

Platinum reaches cells↓DNA links form↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report ringing in the ears, hearing changes, numbness or loss of function. Tell the team if you cannot drink, are passing less urine or become swollen or breathless during fluids.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm weekly blood tests, transport, medicines and your daytime/out-of-hours oncology numbers.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Vinorelbine is infused over15 minutes, followed by a saline flush. Cisplatin is infused over about one hour on its treatment days, with fluids before and afterwards. Checks, preparation and support medicines make the appointment longer than the infusion itself.

STEP 3

IV access

Suitable IV access is required. No routine take-home pump. Tell the nurse promptly about pain or burning.

STEP 4

Home support

Follow your prescribed anti-sickness and constipation-prevention plan. Cisplatin days need kidney-protecting fluids and electrolyte checks. Do not repeat the full cisplatin anti-sickness course on a vinorelbine-only day unless prescribed.

STEP 5

After the course

Agree follow-up, scans and how to report persistent problems.

Treatment reference · 01

Your treatment and its aim

After surgery, treatment aims to lower the chance of cancer returning. For advanced or recurrent lung cancer, it aims to control disease and symptoms. Your team should mark which goal applies to you; benefit is not guaranteed.

Why two medicines?

The medicines interfere with cancer-cell division in different ways. They can also affect healthy cells.

Sources: nccp · jbr10

Choosing treatment

Discuss likely benefit, side effects, alternatives and what matters most to you.

Sources: nccp · jbr10

Evidence and uncertainty

Trials support cisplatin-based chemotherapy after surgery for selected lung cancers. The benefit depends on the stage and treatment goal; evidence after surgery cannot be used as a prognosis for advanced cancer. JBR.10 studied resected stage IB/II disease. Its schedule was not this 21-day cisplatin 80/vinorelbine 30 schedule. No individual survival estimate or metastatic benefit percentage is provided here.

Sources: nccp · jbr10

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Why am I having treatment?

After surgery, treatment aims to lower the chance of cancer returning. For advanced or recurrent lung cancer, it aims to control disease and symptoms. Your team should mark which goal applies to you; benefit is not guaranteed.

What if a weekly dose is missed?

The team decides whether to omit, delay or change a dose. Do not add a missed dose yourself; the two vinorelbine/cisplatin schedules have different rules.

Will I also have radiotherapy?

It is not built into this chemotherapy schedule. Ask your team to explain any separately planned radiotherapy or other treatment.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are you having now? What changes after induction? What would make you call today? Ask your team to fill any gaps.
