CMF · IV · 28-day schedule

All three medicines are given intravenously on Days 1 and 8 of each 28-day cycle. Six cycles for adjuvant treatment; metastatic treatment continues while beneficial and tolerated.

NCCP 00378 · practical support

Your treatment, at home.

Important medicine-specific effects

Cyclophosphamide

  • Low counts and infection
  • Nausea, vomiting and hair loss
  • Bladder irritation / bleeding
  • Fertility effects and menopause
  • Rare later blood cancers

Methotrexate

  • Low blood counts and serious infection
  • Mouth ulcers and diarrhoea
  • Liver injury and prolonged exposure with kidney impairment
  • Uncommon serious lung inflammation

Fluorouracil (5-FU)

  • Low blood counts and infection
  • Diarrhoea and mouth ulcers
  • Rare serious heart symptoms
  • Severe toxicity with DPD deficiency

Make the plan practical

Anti-sickness plan

Follow the prevention and rescue instructions prescribed for this schedule. Call if vomiting prevents drinking.

Infection and bleeding

Know the local urgent-contact number. Fever, shivering, sudden illness or unusual bleeding needs prompt advice even between visits.

Medicine review

Show the oncology pharmacist all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines. Ask before starting anything new.

Hydration and symptoms

Agree fluid intake with the team, especially with heart or kidney fluid restrictions. Report reduced urine, swelling, mouth ulcers or diarrhoea.

DPD result and methotrexate clearance

Confirm DPD testing before the first fluorouracil dose. Kidney impairment can affect interpretation of uracil-based testing; ask the oncology team to review the result and testing method. Tell the team about fluid around the lungs or in the abdomen, which can prolong methotrexate exposure.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
