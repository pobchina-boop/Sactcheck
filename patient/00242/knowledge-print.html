When to get help

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicine works

This treatment aims to slow cancer growth and help control symptoms. Your team explains the expected benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your situation.

Kinase inhibitor

Vandetanib

Oral targeted treatment
Blocks RET and other growth signals

Vandetanib blocks RET and other enzymes involved in cancer growth and blood-vessel development. It can also change the heart’s electrical recovery, making electrocardiogram (ECG) and blood-salt monitoring essential.

RET signals blocked↓Growth pathways interrupted↓Cancer growth slowed

What to watch for
Carry the manufacturer’s patient alert card and receive one with every prescription. Report fainting or palpitations urgently. Check every new medicine for heart-rhythm interactions. Diarrhoea can disturb blood salts and increase rhythm risk. Protect skin from sunlight.

The drawings are simplified explanations of drug action, not anatomical pictures or a prediction of response.

What the evidence can tell you

Research supports a treatment discussion; it cannot predict one person’s outcome.

In the randomised placebo-controlled ZETA study, progression-free survival favoured vandetanib (hazard ratio 0.46; 95% CI 0.31–0.69). The vandetanib median had not been reached: 30.5 months was a statistical prediction, compared with an observed placebo median of 19.3 months. These values do not predict individual survival. Current RET-positive eligibility must be checked separately from the original study population.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Validated RET mutation test; full blood count, kidney and liver tests, potassium, calcium, magnesium, thyroid-stimulating hormone, ECG and blood pressure. Baseline calcitonin is additionally advised.

STEP 2

Taking or receiving treatment

Take at the same time daily, with or without food. If a dose is missed, take it when remembered unless less than 12 hours remain before the next dose; then skip it. Never double a dose. If swallowing is difficult, disperse the whole tablet without crushing in half a glass of still drinking water, stir for about 10 minutes and drink immediately; rinse the glass with another half-glass and drink. Use no other liquid. Pharmacy must teach feeding-tube administration if needed.

STEP 3

Tests and reviews

Blood count, kidney and liver tests at weeks 1, 3, 6 and 12. ECG, potassium, calcium, magnesium and thyroid-stimulating hormone at weeks 1, 3, 6 and 12, then every 3 months for at least a year. Repeat this schedule after a QT-related reduction or interruption longer than 2 weeks. Continue checks after stopping when clinically indicated; monitor blood pressure.

STEP 5

Leaving with a clear plan

Confirm the next appointment, dose, support medicines and verified daytime and out-of-hours phone numbers.
