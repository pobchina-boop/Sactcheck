Your treatment journey

Dactinomycin for low-risk GTN

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · dactinomycin

The nurse gives dactinomycin into a vein once every two weeks. Tell the nurse at once if the line stings. After hCG normalises the plan includes three more cycles.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat is checkedWhat you can do
Before treatmentCheck blood count, kidney and liver function and serum hCG using a validated assay.Bring your medicines list and your GTN centre contact details.
Before each cycleRepeat blood count, kidney and liver tests. Check serum hCG on day 1, more often if indicated.Ask how your hCG trend affects the next treatment.
After treatmentContinue follow-up through your GTN service.After remission your specialist arranges hCG every two weeks for six months, then monthly for six months, then every two months for two years.

Scan dates and tumour-marker testing are individualised by your oncology team.

At review visits

  • What happens at my next visit? One injection into a vein on day 1 of a 14-day cycle.
  • Which symptoms should I phone about? Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. A burning or painful IV site must be checked immediately, including redness or blistering after going home.
  • What should I plan before starting? Bring your medicines list and your GTN centre contact details.
  • How should I plan follow-up? After remission your specialist arranges hCG every two weeks for six months, then monthly for six months, then every two months for two years.
  • Why do I still have appointments after my hormone level returns to normal? The first normal blood result is a milestone. Three more cycles help clear any remaining disease. Ask your specialist nurse to write down your last treatment date, your next blood test and who will give you the result.
