Your treatment reference

Dactinomycin for low-risk GTN

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds.

Dactinomycin · Dactinomycin

Cytotoxic antibiotic. Interferes with the way tumour cells use DNA to make RNA. Tell the nurse immediately if the injection site burns or stings.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · dactinomycin

The nurse gives dactinomycin into a vein once every two weeks. Tell the nurse at once if the line stings. After hCG normalises the plan includes three more cycles.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat is checkedWhat you can do
Before treatmentCheck blood count, kidney and liver function and serum hCG using a validated assay.Bring your medicines list and your GTN centre contact details.

After treatmentContinue follow-up through your GTN service.After remission your specialist arranges hCG every two weeks for six months, then monthly for six months, then every two months for two years.

Scan dates and tumour-marker testing are individualised by your oncology team.

Common effects and what to do

How you feel between visits

Tiredness, nausea or appetite changes can make everyday activities harder. Ask your team for help if symptoms interfere with eating or drinking.

Serious effects: when to call

IV site injury · Dactinomycin

Report pain, swelling or blistering at the injection site immediately.

Sore mouth · Dactinomycin

Phone if mouth ulcers prevent you drinking.

Infection or bleeding · Dactinomycin

Contact the unit urgently for fever, chills or unusual bleeding.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. A burning or painful IV site must be checked immediately, including redness or blistering after going home. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.
