How this medicine supports your treatment

Bevacizumab blocks a signal that helps cancers develop blood vessels. With paclitaxel, it can help delay cancer worsening. The aim in this setting is disease control, with treatment reviewed against benefit and side effects.

Targeted anti-VEGF antibody

Bevacizumab

Blocks the VEGF blood-vessel growth signal
VEGF promotes new blood vessels↓Bevacizumab binds VEGF↓Less VEGF signalling supports tumour blood vessels

Binds VEGF, a signal that promotes blood-vessel growth. Blocking VEGF can reduce the blood supply that supports tumour growth; it also affects normal blood vessels and wound healing.

Report bleeding, severe pain, neurological symptoms or wound problems promptly, and attend blood-pressure and urine checks.

Treatment and its aim

Bevacizumab blocks a signal that helps cancers develop blood vessels. With paclitaxel, it can help delay cancer worsening. The aim in this setting is disease control, with treatment reviewed against benefit and side effects.

Who this guide covers

First-line HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer with paclitaxel. This suite explains the bevacizumab component; paclitaxel and any other treatment need their own plan and assessment.

NCCP 00212 v7 · official regimen

Infusion schedule

The usual bevacizumab reference dose is 10 mg/kg intravenously every 14 days. The first infusion is over 90 minutes; if tolerated, the second can be over 60 minutes and later infusions over 30 minutes. The team checks the actual prescription, blood pressure, urine and other tests before treatment.

NCCP 00212 v7 · official regimen

Evidence and personal choice

The supporting study showed a delay in cancer worsening; a longer overall survival was not established. The team weighs this against side effects and treatment burden. The supporting paclitaxel trial used bevacizumab every two weeks. That matches this bevacizumab schedule. Discuss the expected benefit and alternatives for your own situation.

Tests and review

Before starting: blood count, kidney and liver tests, urine protein, blood pressure and a heart history/examination. Repeat blood count, kidney/liver tests and urine testing before each cycle; check blood pressure before treatment and after it. Extra checks depend on symptoms and results.

Urine results

Protein in the urine can require a 24-hour collection and additional checks. Your team explains how and when to collect it, and reviews whether treatment can continue.

NCCP 00212 v7 · official regimen

Heart and blood-thinning medicines

An ultrasound of the heart may be considered if you have had certain earlier chemotherapy or chest-wall radiotherapy. If you take warfarin, INR tests are usually weekly until stable and then before each cycle; follow your individual plan.

NCCP 00212 v7 · official regimen

Cancer response

Scans and cancer-response checks follow the individual consultant plan. This guide does not promise a fixed scan or repeat heart-ultrasound schedule.

NCCP 00212 v7 · official regimen

Support and urgent symptoms

Seek urgent help for sudden severe abdominal pain, significant bleeding or coughing blood, chest pain, breathlessness, a swollen painful leg, a severe headache, confusion, seizures, a change in vision, or a rapidly painful/red spreading wound. Follow the chemotherapy emergency plan for fever or feeling acutely unwell.

Blood pressure and urine

Take prescribed blood-pressure medicines as agreed and attend urine checks. If a 24-hour collection is requested, confirm how to collect and return it before the next treatment.

NCCP 00212 v7 · official regimen

Surgery, wounds and teeth

Tell the team about planned operations, dental procedures and any wound that is not healing. Bevacizumab can require a treatment break around procedures; the team sets the timing.

Separate chemotherapy support

Bevacizumab itself usually needs no routine anti-sickness premedication. Paclitaxel has separate allergy-prevention, anti-sickness and symptom plans; keep both plans and the emergency contact details.

NCCP 00212 v7 · official regimen

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility and effective contraception before treatment and continue contraception during treatment and for at least six months after the last bevacizumab dose. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for at least six months after the final dose. The partner treatment may require additional precautions.

Why this treatment may be chosen

Adding bevacizumab to paclitaxel can delay worsening of metastatic breast cancer for some people.

The supporting paclitaxel trial used bevacizumab every two weeks. That matches this bevacizumab schedule. Discuss the expected benefit and alternatives for your own situation.

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.

Questions to ask

Is this a stand-alone breast treatment?

This breast regimen uses bevacizumab with paclitaxel. Any change to the chemotherapy partner or a continuation plan must be agreed with your oncologist.

How long will I have treatment?

There is no fixed number of breast-treatment cycles. Your team reviews cancer control, side effects and your preferences and agrees when to continue, pause or change.

What happens if urine protein or blood pressure rises?

The team may repeat tests, treat blood pressure or pause bevacizumab. Severe problems may mean stopping it. Do not restart after a pause without an agreed plan.

What if I need an operation or dental work?

Tell the oncology team before the procedure. Bevacizumab can slow healing, and the team must coordinate any treatment break and check healing before restarting.

Explain the plan back: Which days have bevacizumab and paclitaxel, when are blood-pressure and urine checks, which symptoms need urgent help, and who must know about surgery or a wound?
