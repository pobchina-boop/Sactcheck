My treatment passport

Bevacizumab every two weeks with chemotherapy for recurrent ovarian cancer

My treatment record

Bevacizumab is given with chemotherapy to help control ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer that has returned and is resistant to platinum. Your team chooses one partner medicine.

PhaseWhenWhat changes
With weekly paclitaxelWith weekly paclitaxelBevacizumab and paclitaxel are both due on days 1 and 15 of the 28-day partner schedule. Paclitaxel also has separate weekly visits.
With weekly topotecanWith weekly topotecanBevacizumab is due every two weeks. Use the weekly topotecan calendar for additional appointments.
With liposomal doxorubicinWith liposomal doxorubicinBoth medicines are given on day 1 of the partner cycle. Bevacizumab is given alone on day 15.
Bevacizumab alone · PLD day 15Bevacizumab alone · PLD day 15Day 15 of the liposomal doxorubicin cycle is a bevacizumab-only visit.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call now for new severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding, severe headache or a change in vision. Call 112 or 999 for sudden chest pain, severe breathlessness, seizure or stroke symptoms. With chemotherapy, fever, chills or suddenly feeling unwell also need urgent assessment. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

My questions and next steps

  • What do I need to check before my next visit? Tell the team before dental surgery or an operation. Starting or restarting bevacizumab after major surgery requires adequate healing and at least 28 days.
  • When is my next treatment due? Bevacizumab every 14 days alongside weekly paclitaxel, weekly topotecan or pegylated liposomal doxorubicin. Your other medicine has its own appointment dates.
