Temozolomide + short RT Adults older than 65 with newly diagnosed glioblastoma who are not suitable for the standard radiotherapy regimen with temozolomide, under NCCP 00461. Use the plan matched to your diagnosis and phase; do not borrow another glioma schedule.

Additional phase-specific support

  • Temozolomide has moderate-to-high oral emetic potential. Take the prescribed anti-sickness plan before chemotherapy and use rescue medicine as instructed. Ask what to do if nausea prevents taking medicines or fluids.
  • Continue or review PJP prevention according to lymphocyte recovery, steroid exposure and the specialist plan. Starting five-day adjuvant cycles does not itself authorise stopping prevention prescribed during radiotherapy.
  • Anti-sickness medicines and other medicines can worsen constipation. Ask for your bowel plan, and call for persistent vomiting, severe constipation or difficulty drinking. Do not double a dose after vomiting.
  • Keep steroids and seizure medicines on their own written schedules if prescribed. They are not routine medicines for every patient, and chemotherapy rest days do not automatically mean stopping them. Ask the pharmacist to check interactions.
  • These are prescription choices for clinician/pharmacist completion, not an issued prescription. General NCCP multiday guidance includes cover after the last SACT day; record the individual duration. Review pre-existing steroid and seizure treatment rather than duplicating it.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
