How your medicines work

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Ribociclib

Slows the cell-division checkpoint
CDK4/6 checkpoint→Checkpoint inhibited→Cell cycling slows

Ribociclib blocks CDK4 and CDK6, proteins that help cells move through the cycle of division. Endocrine therapy reduces the hormone signal that can drive HR-positive breast cancer.

Keep blood and ECG appointments. Fever, jaundice, fainting, palpitations, severe rash or new breathlessness need prompt advice.

Endocrine partner

AI or fulvestrant

Reduces oestrogen-driven growth
Oestrogen pathway→AI or receptor blockade→Growth signal reduced

An aromatase inhibitor (AI) lowers oestrogen production; fulvestrant blocks and reduces oestrogen receptors. These are alternative endocrine partners, selected by the team. Ovarian suppression is needed in pre/perimenopausal patients.

The endocrine medicine continues during the ribociclib week off. Report troublesome joint, menopausal or injection-site symptoms.

What each check is for

1ECG: an electrical heart tracingBefore starting and around Day 14 of cycle 1; then as indicated

Ribociclib can alter the QT interval, which affects heart rhythm.

Ask / act: Attend the early ECG and report fainting, palpitations or new dizziness promptly.

Sources: NCCP 00525 · RIBO

2Bloods and electrolytesBloods every 2 weeks for cycles 1–2; before cycles 3–6; then as indicated

Checks blood counts, kidney/liver tests and minerals relevant to heart rhythm.

Ask / act: Ask who checks the results before the next pack starts.

Sources: NCCP 00525

3Cancer-response imagingUsually every 2–4 months, adapted to the clinical picture

A baseline scan records where the cancer is. Response is usually reviewed every 2–4 months, adjusted for the treatment, disease pace, symptoms and previous results. CT chest/abdomen/pelvis is common when appropriate; the team chooses the imaging needed for the known sites.

Ask / act: Ask how scan results, symptoms and your priorities will guide the next decision.

Sources: METASTATIC

4Markers, if selectedAlongside the individual response plan

CA 15-3 or CEA may be helpful in selected metastatic cases, especially if previously raised. They support the assessment with symptoms and imaging; a marker alone does not decide whether treatment is working or should change.

Ask / act: Ask whether markers add useful information in your case.

Sources: MARKERS

5Bone health with endocrine therapyAt AI/OFS initiation and then by risk

Assess fracture risk and whether a baseline DEXA bone-density scan is needed when starting an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression/treatment-induced menopause. It may already have been done before this regimen.

Ask / act: Ask about DEXA, weight-bearing activity and any indicated bone treatment.

Sources: BONE · NOGG

6Week off ribociclibDays 22–28 of each cycle

The planned break applies to ribociclib, not automatically to the endocrine partner.

Ask / act: Follow the written prescription for each medicine; do not make up missed or vomited doses.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Ribociclib is taken once daily on Days 1–21, followed by seven days off, in a 28-day cycle. The endocrine partner continues according to its own prescription. Treatment continues while it is helping and tolerable.

STEP 1

Before starting

Confirm the exact tablet strength, endocrine partner, blood-test and ECG plan where relevant.

STEP 2

At home

Take tablets whole at the prescribed times. Abemaciclib is continuous twice daily; ribociclib has a planned 7-day break. Follow the schedule for this card only.

STEP 3

If a dose is missed or vomited

Do not double up or take a replacement dose. Follow the medicine instructions and ask the oncology pharmacist if uncertain.

STEP 4

Before the next supply

Confirm symptoms and required test results have been reviewed. Ask whether the prescription changes; do not restart a held medicine yourself.

Interactions and storage

Check all new medicines and supplements with oncology/pharmacy, avoid grapefruit, store as labelled and keep away from children. Return unwanted tablets to the pharmacy.

Endocrine and ovarian-suppression visits

An aromatase inhibitor is generally a daily tablet. Fulvestrant, when selected for metastatic treatment, has loading injections on Days 1, 15 and 29 then monthly. Ovarian-suppression injections have their own prescription and visit schedule.

No infusion pump

The CDK4/6 medicine is taken orally. An implanted port or infusion pump is not required for these tablets; other treatment may have separate access needs.

Support around treatment

Three weeks on, one off

The ribociclib break is Days 22–28. Your endocrine partner usually continues.

Bloods and ECG

Early blood tests and a Day-14 ECG are important even if you feel well.

Medicines check

Avoid grapefruit; check new prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines and supplements for interactions.

Missed or vomited tablets

Do not take an extra dose. Take the next prescribed dose at the usual time and contact the team if uncertain.

Do not wait for the next appointment
