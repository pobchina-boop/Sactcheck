How this medicine supports your treatment

Ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant aims to control advanced HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer. The exact endocrine partner depends on previous treatment and menopausal status.

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Ribociclib

Slows the cell-division checkpoint
CDK4/6 checkpoint↓Checkpoint inhibited↓Cell cycling slows

Ribociclib blocks CDK4 and CDK6, proteins that help cells move through the cycle of division. Endocrine therapy reduces the hormone signal that can drive HR-positive breast cancer.

Keep blood and ECG appointments. Fever, jaundice, fainting, palpitations, severe rash or new breathlessness need prompt advice.

Endocrine partner

AI or fulvestrant

Reduces oestrogen-driven growth
Oestrogen pathway↓AI or receptor blockade↓Growth signal reduced

An aromatase inhibitor (AI) lowers oestrogen production; fulvestrant blocks and reduces oestrogen receptors. These are alternative endocrine partners, selected by the team. Ovarian suppression is needed in pre/perimenopausal patients.

The endocrine medicine continues during the ribociclib week off. Report troublesome joint, menopausal or injection-site symptoms.

Your treatment aim

Ribociclib with an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant aims to control advanced HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer. The exact endocrine partner depends on previous treatment and menopausal status.

Who this treatment is for

HR-positive/HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic disease with an AI or fulvestrant in the exact NCCP indication. NCCP page 1 and page 2 do not fully align on menopause and previous advanced-disease treatment; preserve consultant reconciliation.

Schedule and duration

The usual metastatic starting dose is ribociclib 600 mg once daily on Days 1–21 followed by Days 22–28 off. Continue the prescribed AI daily or fulvestrant on its own injection schedule; add ovarian suppression when indicated. Continue while beneficial and tolerated. There is no fixed three-year stop in this metastatic regimen; the team reviews response, side effects and the next treatment decision.

Checks before and during treatment

Baseline tests and ongoing tests have different purposes.

Before the first dose

FBC, renal and liver profile, potassium, calcium, magnesium and phosphorus. ECG before initiation; QTcF must be below 450 ms. Correct electrolyte abnormalities and review QT/CYP3A interactions. Confirm endocrine partner, menopause/ovarian suppression, prior treatment and the NCCP indication/eligibility discrepancy.

NCCP 00525 v6 · official regimen · EMA Kisqali current product information; checked 2026-10-25

During treatment

FBC, renal and liver profile every two weeks for cycles 1–2, then before cycles 3–6, then as clinically indicated. Electrolytes before each of the first six cycles, then as clinically indicated; correct abnormalities. Repeat ECG around Day 14 of cycle 1, then as clinically indicated. Increase liver monitoring for grade 2 or worse abnormalities. Review fever, jaundice, palpitations/fainting, pulmonary and severe skin symptoms; response imaging follows the advanced-disease plan.

Living with treatment

Nausea support

NCCP classifies ribociclib as oral moderate–high emetic risk. The team chooses preventive or rescue antiemetics using local policy, symptoms, QT risk and drug interactions; do not reuse another regimen’s prescription.

What the studies tell us

Studies of ribociclib with hormone therapy in defined advanced-breast-cancer groups showed a survival benefit. The studies used different hormone partners and included different menopausal and prior-treatment groups. Ask which study matches your plan; the results do not set an individual time limit.

How to interpret this

Study results describe groups with defined treatment and eligibility. Ask about your own likely benefits, important harms, alternatives and remaining uncertainty.

How long does treatment last?

Do I need to enter dates?

No. This map shows general timing without personal information. Your hospital arranges actual appointments; ask who books each test and who reviews its result.

What if a dose is missed or treatment is held?

Take ribociclib once daily with or without food; swallow whole. Avoid grapefruit. If a dose is missed or vomited, do not take an extra dose; take the next scheduled dose. Confirm any clinical hold and restart with the team. The endocrine partner follows a separate prescription. A hold may affect one medicine rather than the whole combination; confirm each component.

What happens after treatment changes or ends?

There is no fixed three-year stop in this metastatic regimen; the team reviews response, side effects and the next treatment decision. Keep the separately agreed disease follow-up, endocrine and bone-health plan.
