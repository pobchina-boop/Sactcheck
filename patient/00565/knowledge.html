How this medicine supports your treatment

Dacomitinib blocks abnormal EGFR-family growth signals in selected lung cancers. Treatment aims to control advanced cancer and delay progression.

EGFR-family targeted therapy

Dacomitinib

Blocks abnormal growth signals
Activating EGFR mutation identified↓Growth signalling blocked↓Cancer growth slowed

Dacomitinib is an irreversible EGFR-family tyrosine kinase inhibitor. It interrupts signals involved in cancer-cell growth; the tumour mutation test and treatment setting determine whether it is suitable.

Report diarrhoea, rash, breathing changes and eye symptoms promptly.

Your treatment aim

Dacomitinib blocks abnormal EGFR-family growth signals in selected lung cancers. Treatment aims to control advanced cancer and delay progression.

Which setting?

NCCP 00565a covers first-line treatment of adults with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with an activating EGFR mutation. This is daily dacomitinib alone; it is not an adjuvant regimen or a chemotherapy combination.

Checks before and during treatment

Tests and symptoms guide safe treatment decisions.

Before starting

Confirm the activating EGFR test, first-line treatment setting and prescription. Check blood count, kidneys, liver and bone profile, and arrange baseline staging scans. Review lung symptoms, bowel and skin health, all medicines and pregnancy precautions.

NCCP 00565 v3 · official regimen · EMA Vizimpro product information (updated 17 December 2024)

During treatment

NCCP requires regular blood count, kidney, liver and bone tests but does not specify a fixed interval; your team sets appointments. Diarrhoea often starts early, so keep the symptom plan ready from the first dose. Scans follow your Consultant’s plan.

NCCP 00565 v3 · official regimen · EMA Vizimpro product information (updated 17 December 2024)

Taking dacomitinib

Follow your individual prescription.

Daily tablets

Take at approximately the same time each day with or without food and swallow with water. If you miss a dose or vomit, do not take a replacement or extra dose. Take your next prescribed dose at the usual time the following day.

NCCP 00565 v3 · official regimen · EMA Vizimpro product information (updated 17 December 2024)

Dose changes

The defined daily dose levels are 45 mg, 30 mg and 15 mg. After side effects, the team decides whether and when to restart and which dose applies. Do not select a dose or add a rest week yourself.

NCCP 00565 v3 · official regimen · EMA Vizimpro product information (updated 17 December 2024)

Living with treatment

Report symptoms early and keep contact details available.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Keep prescribed anti-diarrhoea medicine available from the first dose. Start your agreed plan at the first loose stool, keep drinking if able and report symptoms early. Your team may advise continuing symptom medicine until loose stools have stopped for 12 hours. Severe diarrhoea, weakness or reduced urine needs urgent advice.

NCCP 00565 v3 · official regimen · EMA Vizimpro product information (updated 17 December 2024)

Skin, nails and mouth

Use the agreed EGFR-inhibitor skin-care and mouth-care plan. Emollients and sun protection may help; report rash, painful nails or mouth sores early. The team decides whether creams, antibiotics or other treatment are needed.

NCCP 00565 v3 · official regimen · EMA Vizimpro product information (updated 17 December 2024)

Nausea medicines

Dacomitinib has minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Routine infusion premedication is not required. Agree any as-needed anti-sickness medicine with the team, after checking interactions and your medical history.

NCCP 00565 v3 · official regimen · NCCP SACT antiemetic guidance V6 (2025), oral tables · NCCP NCIS antiemetic medicines V8 (2025); parenteral scope

Medicine interactions

Acid-suppressing medicines can reduce dacomitinib exposure. Proton pump inhibitors should be avoided if possible; ask pharmacy about alternatives. NCCP and current product information give different timing before H2 blockers, so obtain a written individual schedule. CYP2D6 medicines also need pharmacy review. Do not change medicines yourself.

Pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding

Avoid pregnancy. Women who could become pregnant need adequate contraception during treatment and for at least 17 days after the final dose. Do not breastfeed during treatment. Discuss fertility and future pregnancy plans before starting.

When to get urgent help

Do not wait for the next appointment.

Call urgently

Call urgently for new or worsening breathlessness, cough or fever; severe diarrhoea, inability to drink or much less urine; severe or peeling skin reactions; or yellow skin/eyes. Eye pain, light sensitivity or blurred vision needs prompt assessment. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or swelling of the tongue/throat needs emergency help. Use your unit’s temperature and 24-hour contact instructions.

When you call

Give the medicine name, last dose and symptoms. Tell the team if you can drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Do not delay calling while gathering information.

What studies tell us

In a study of previously untreated advanced lung cancer with common EGFR mutations, dacomitinib delayed progression and improved overall survival compared with gefitinib. The study excluded people with a history of brain or other central nervous system metastases. It did not compare dacomitinib with every treatment available today. Group results cannot predict your own outcome.

Your own decision

Ask how the study population matches your situation and discuss alternatives, harms and remaining uncertainty.

Questions to ask

Is there a planned week off?

No. These tablets are continuous daily treatment. A hold for side effects is a clinical decision.

What if I miss a dose or vomit?

Take at approximately the same time each day with or without food and swallow with water. If you miss a dose or vomit, do not take a replacement or extra dose. Take your next prescribed dose at the usual time the following day.

Can I adjust the dose myself?

No. Keep the prescription and contact plan. Your team authorises every hold, restart or dose change.

Do I need all the tests every day?

No. Your team schedules tests and reviews. Contact them between appointments if symptoms occur.

Explain the plan back: Can you explain how to take your tablets, what to do after a missed or vomited dose, which tests are next and which symptoms mean you should call today?
