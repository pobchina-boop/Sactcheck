My treatment at a glance · NCCP 00271

Carboplatin + Etoposide

These two chemotherapy medicines aim to control the cancer. Your team will explain the expected benefit for you.

Check your treatment: This summary is for chemotherapy for extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer. Treatment that also includes immunotherapy or radiotherapy needs its own complete plan.

Your treatment plan

Carboplatin is given on Day 1. Etoposide is given on Days 1–3. A cycle lasts 21 days (3 weeks); the team checks you before the next cycle.

  1. Before startingBlood tests and a review of your health. Confirm your three treatment days and whether etoposide is by drip or capsules.
  2. Treatment and time at homeBoth medicines on Day 1; etoposide continues on Days 2–3. Follow your medicines plan at home.
  3. Review and next stepsUsually 4–6 cycles. Your team may delay, reduce or stop treatment after reviewing your health and response.

Before and between visits

  • Confirm whether Days 2–3 etoposide will be drips or prescribed capsules.
  • Ask for a written anti-sickness plan covering every treatment day and the following days.
  • Bring your medicines list and report hearing problems, numbness or signs of infection.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately for temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions.

Cannot reach the team? Seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Call urgently for fever or shaking chills, sudden illness, new or worsening breathlessness, chest pain, uncontrolled bleeding, severe diarrhoea, vomiting despite medicines, inability to drink, very little urine, or a spreading/blistering rash. Tell staff immediately about wheeze, swelling, dizziness or faintness during an infusion. Report hearing changes, ringing in the ears, numbness, weakness or difficulty walking promptly.
