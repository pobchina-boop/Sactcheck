When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call urgently for fever or shaking chills, sudden illness, new or worsening breathlessness, chest pain, uncontrolled bleeding, severe diarrhoea, vomiting despite medicines, inability to drink, very little urine, or a spreading/blistering rash. Tell staff immediately about wheeze, swelling, dizziness or faintness during an infusion. Report hearing changes, ringing in the ears, numbness, weakness or difficulty walking promptly.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Your two chemotherapy medicines

Carboplatin + Etoposide is chemotherapy used for extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer. It aims to control the cancer and its symptoms. Ask your oncology team what benefit is realistic for you and how this option compares with other suitable treatments.

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Forms links that damage DNA

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing; normal tissues can also be affected. Carboplatin dosing is tailored using a verified kidney-function result; low blood counts can be an important limiting effect.

Platinum enters cells↓DNA links form↓DNA copying disrupted

What to watch for
Report changes in urine, hearing, ringing in the ears, numbness or daily function. Tell the team about infection, unusual bruising or bleeding and any symptoms during the infusion.

Sources: nccp · platinum

Topoisomerase II chemotherapy

Etoposide

Disrupts DNA repair during cell division

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme that helps manage DNA during copying. DNA breaks can remain unrepaired and cancer cells may stop dividing or die. Healthy cells are also affected; the infusion or prescribed capsules deliver the same medicine by different routes.

Topoisomerase II disrupted↓DNA breaks persist↓Cell division interrupted

What to watch for
Report fever, bleeding, mouth sores or diarrhoea. Tell the nurse about faintness, rash or breathing difficulty during an infusion. If capsules are prescribed, use the exact pharmacy instructions and contact the team about a missed or vomited dose.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Day 1 in the unit

Carboplatin is infused over about 30 minutes, followed by etoposide over about one hour on Day 1. Etoposide alone is given on Days 2 and 3 unless the prescribed capsule alternative is selected. The whole visit also includes review, preparation, prevention medicines and observation. Tell staff immediately about rash, swelling, wheeze, faintness or pain near the drip.

Day 2–3: Etoposide continues

Return for etoposide infusions unless the team prescribed the oral alternative. If you have capsules, follow the twice-daily pharmacy label and empty-stomach instructions. Confirm today’s anti-sickness medicines. Contact the team about a missed or vomited capsule dose.

Day 4–5: After the last treatment day

Follow the written prevention and rescue plan. Multi-day antiemetic cover extends to two days after the final treatment day; the exact medicines are chosen by your team. Call for sickness despite medicines, inability to drink or reduced urine.

Day 6–20: Recovery and symptom monitoring

Fatigue, low counts, infection, mouth or bowel symptoms can occur between visits. Hearing and nerve symptoms may build across cycles. Use the urgent contact plan at any time; do not wait for the next appointment.

Day 21: Review before the next cycle

The team reviews blood counts, kidney/liver function and symptoms before deciding to continue, delay or change the plan. Bring your medicine list and symptom notes; confirm the remaining course and next review.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before each visit

Confirm transport for Days 1–3, blood-test arrangements, prescriptions and the 24-hour contact details. Tell the team if you are ill before travelling.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Carboplatin is infused over about 30 minutes, followed by etoposide over about one hour on Day 1. Etoposide alone is given on Days 2 and 3 unless the prescribed capsule alternative is selected. The whole visit also includes review, preparation, prevention medicines and observation.

STEP 3

Infusion access

Treatment may use a cannula or an existing suitable central line. Ask for line-care instructions if you have a line. There is no routine take-home infusion pump.

STEP 4

Capsules and home medicines

If prescribed, confirm your etoposide capsule doses, empty-stomach timing, safe handling and what to do if a dose is missed or vomited. Keep prevention and rescue medicines clearly labelled.

STEP 5

Support between visits

Arrange practical help if needed and keep symptom notes. Bring questions and a current medication list to review.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Preventing sickness across all treatment days

Take the written anti-sickness plan even if you feel well. The plan must cover the risk of the medicines on each of Days 1–3 and the two days after the last treatment. Ask the pharmacist to show which medicines are scheduled and which are rescue medicines.

One clear steroid plan

The anti-sickness plan may include dexamethasone. Follow the prescribed day-by-day instructions; do not add an extra steroid course or extend it yourself. Tell the team about diabetes, poor sleep or mood changes.

Fluids and kidney protection

Ask for a personal drinking plan. Kidney results help calculate the carboplatin dose; illness or dehydration may require repeat tests. Call if you cannot drink or pass much urine.

If etoposide capsules are prescribed

The source alternative uses capsules on an empty stomach twice daily on Days 2 and 3. Your pharmacy label gives your individual capsule dose. Do not substitute capsules for an infusion yourself, crush/open capsules, double a missed dose or repeat a vomited dose without advice. Obtain safe handling and storage instructions.

Medicines, mouth care and infection advice

Check prescriptions, painkillers, supplements and vaccines with the oncology team. Some medicines affect kidney/hearing toxicity or etoposide exposure. Follow mouth-care advice and keep the unit’s urgent contact instructions available.

Treatment reference · 01

Your treatment and its aim

Confirm the full plan before starting.

The treatment covered here

This guide covers Carboplatin + Etoposide chemotherapy for extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer. Immunotherapy combinations and limited-stage treatment with radiotherapy use separate regimens. If an additional medicine or radiotherapy is prescribed, obtain its own plan and consent information.

Sources: nccp

Why two medicines?

The platinum drug damages DNA and etoposide interferes with DNA repair. Using them together attacks cell growth in different ways. Healthy cells can also be affected.

Sources: nccp · smpc · platinum

Evidence and choices

Platinum and etoposide are established chemotherapy components for extensive-stage SCLC. A pooled comparison found no clear survival difference between carboplatin-based and cisplatin-based treatment, but their side effects differed. The elderly/poor-risk JCOG9702 trial used etoposide 80 mg/m² and split cisplatin, so its survival figures cannot be assigned directly to this 100 mg/m² etoposide schedule. These older chemotherapy comparisons do not rank current immunotherapy combinations or predict an individual outcome.

Sources: cocis · jcog9702

Treatment reference · 04

Effects to discuss and monitor

Report problems early; they are not a measure of benefit.

Blood counts and infection

Both medicines may lower blood counts. Infection can become serious quickly; follow the urgent advice plan for fever, chills or sudden illness.

Sources: nccp · hse

Hearing, nerves and kidneys

Platinum medicines may affect hearing, nerves and kidneys, and some changes can last. Tell the team promptly about tinnitus, hearing change, numbness or reduced urine.

Sources: nccp

Mouth, bowel, hair and fatigue

Mouth soreness, nausea, bowel changes, hair loss and tiredness can affect daily life. Ask for help with symptoms; call urgently if you cannot drink or symptoms are severe.

Sources: smpc · hse

Later effects

Etoposide can be associated with a later blood cancer. Discuss serious and lasting risks together with likely benefit and alternatives.

Sources: smpc

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Why are there three treatment days?

Etoposide is planned on three consecutive days. Usually you attend for infusions on all three. The alternative capsule plan is only used when prescribed and clearly labelled by your team.

Are etoposide capsules the same dose as the drip?

No. The source oral alternative has a different dose and twice-daily schedule. Your prescription and pharmacy label specify your individual dose and capsule strengths. Do not convert it yourself.

Will I have radiotherapy or immunotherapy too?

This guide covers Carboplatin + Etoposide chemotherapy for extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer. Immunotherapy combinations and limited-stage treatment with radiotherapy use separate regimens. If an additional medicine or radiotherapy is prescribed, obtain its own plan and consent information.

How long does the course last?

The source describes 4–6 cycles every 21 days, or stopping for cancer progression or unacceptable side effects. Your team confirms the intended course and may change it earlier. The passport pages are reusable records, not a prescription for a fixed number of visits.

Do more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably show whether the cancer is responding. Report them so they can be assessed and treated; your team reviews benefit with the agreed clinical and scan plan.

Why might treatment be delayed or changed?

Blood counts, kidney/liver function or symptoms may need time to recover. A dose may change to reduce lasting harm. Ask which medicine changes, why, and when it will be reviewed.

What should I say when I call?

Give the regimen name, last treatment day, symptoms and when they started. Explain whether you can drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Give your temperature if available, but do not delay calling to collect information.

Can this guide decide whether treatment is safe?

No. Treatment decisions need your clinical results, symptoms, previous toxicity and prescriber/pharmacy review. This guide helps you understand and discuss the plan.

Try explaining the plan back: Can you describe which days you have treatment, whether you have etoposide capsules, how you take prevention medicines and which number to call at any hour?
