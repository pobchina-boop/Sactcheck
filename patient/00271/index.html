When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

My treatment

My overall treatment

Carboplatin on Day 1 and etoposide on Days 1–3, every 21 days. The intended course is usually four to six cycles and may change after review.

  1. Treatment daysBoth medicines on Day 1; etoposide continues on Days2–3 by the prescribed route.
  2. Between cyclesFollow your support plan, arrange blood tests and report symptoms.

Your 21-day treatment cycle

Day 1Carboplatin and etoposide.
Days 2–3Etoposide by your confirmed route.

Your team confirms the active phase and dates. Symptoms may occur during or after treatment; there are no guaranteed safe days.

More about my medicines

Carboplatin

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing; normal tissues can also be affected. Carboplatin dosing is tailored using a verified kidney-function result; low blood counts can be an important limiting effect.

Etoposide

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme that helps manage DNA during copying. DNA breaks can remain unrepaired and cancer cells may stop dividing or die. Healthy cells are also affected; the infusion or prescribed capsules deliver the same medicine by different routes.

Getting ready for treatment visits

Consent discussion aid

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Day 1 in the unit

Carboplatin is infused over about 30 minutes, followed by etoposide over about one hour on Day 1. Etoposide alone is given on Days 2 and 3 unless the prescribed capsule alternative is selected. The whole visit also includes review, preparation, prevention medicines and observation.

What to do: Tell staff immediately about rash, swelling, wheeze, faintness or pain near the drip.

Day 2–3: Etoposide continues

Return for etoposide infusions unless the team prescribed the oral alternative. If you have capsules, follow the twice-daily pharmacy label and empty-stomach instructions.

What to do: Confirm today’s anti-sickness medicines. Contact the team about a missed or vomited capsule dose.

Day 4–5: After the last treatment day

Follow the written prevention and rescue plan. Multi-day antiemetic cover extends to two days after the final treatment day; the exact medicines are chosen by your team.

What to do: Call for sickness despite medicines, inability to drink or reduced urine.

Day 6–20: Recovery and symptom monitoring

Fatigue, low counts, infection, mouth or bowel symptoms can occur between visits. Hearing and nerve symptoms may build across cycles.

What to do: Use the urgent contact plan at any time; do not wait for the next appointment.

Day 21: Review before the next cycle

The team reviews blood counts, kidney/liver function and symptoms before deciding to continue, delay or change the plan.

What to do: Bring your medicine list and symptom notes; confirm the remaining course and next review.

Support at home

Preventing sickness across all treatment days

Take the written anti-sickness plan even if you feel well. The plan must cover the risk of the medicines on each of Days 1–3 and the two days after the last treatment. Ask the pharmacist to show which medicines are scheduled and which are rescue medicines.

One clear steroid plan

The anti-sickness plan may include dexamethasone. Follow the prescribed day-by-day instructions; do not add an extra steroid course or extend it yourself. Tell the team about diabetes, poor sleep or mood changes.

Fluids and kidney protection

Ask for a personal drinking plan. Kidney results help calculate the carboplatin dose; illness or dehydration may require repeat tests. Call if you cannot drink or pass much urine.

If etoposide capsules are prescribed

The source alternative uses capsules on an empty stomach twice daily on Days 2 and 3. Your pharmacy label gives your individual capsule dose. Do not substitute capsules for an infusion yourself, crush/open capsules, double a missed dose or repeat a vomited dose without advice. Obtain safe handling and storage instructions.

Medicines, mouth care and infection advice

Check prescriptions, painkillers, supplements and vaccines with the oncology team. Some medicines affect kidney/hearing toxicity or etoposide exposure. Follow mouth-care advice and keep the unit’s urgent contact instructions available.
