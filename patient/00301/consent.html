Five-day EP for good-risk metastatic germ-cell tumours

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

The aim is to cure a germ-cell tumour that has spread. Each course includes five days of chemotherapy followed by time for recovery. NCCP 00301a covers good-prognosis metastatic seminoma and non-seminoma by International Germ Cell Cancer Collaborative Group criteria. The published indication carries ICD-10 C62 (testis).

Treatment schedule. Both medicines on days 1–5 of a 21-day cycle. Four cycles are planned if treatment remains suitable. Days 6–21 have no scheduled EP infusions. Your blood count can still fall. The next cycle starts only after review.

Regimen-specific side effects

Infusion reactions and late effectsTell the nurse immediately about flushing, dizziness or breathlessness. There is a small long-term risk of treatment-related leukaemia.

Treatment discussion and decision

Five-day EP for good-risk metastatic germ-cell tumours

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

At one specialist centre, about 94 of every 100 people treated with four cycles were alive five years later without the cancer getting worse. These figures describe a group of people with good-risk disease. Most people in this study had testicular germ-cell tumours. Your own outlook depends on the type of tumour and how it responds to treatment.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills, unexpected bleeding or sudden illness, including during the treatment break. Sudden chest pain, collapse or severe breathlessness needs emergency help. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
