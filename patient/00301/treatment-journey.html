Your treatment journey

Five-day EP for good-risk metastatic germ-cell tumours

The aim is to cure a germ-cell tumour that has spread. Each course includes five days of chemotherapy followed by time for recovery.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · begin five-day EP

Bring your medicines list. The team checks your treatment plan and dates one anti-sickness course for the whole block.

Days 2–5 · continue EP

On each of these four days both infusions and kidney-protecting fluid are repeated. Show the team your dated anti-sickness plan each day.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat is checked and whyWhat you can do
Before the first cycleBlood count, kidney and liver function are checked. The team considers a hearing test and discusses fertility preservation, including sperm banking where appropriate.Raise fertility questions before the first infusion, even if you are unsure about future plans.
During each cycleA blood count is checked weekly. Kidney and liver tests are repeated before each cycle. A change in hearing may prompt audiology.Keep your weekly blood appointment during the break. Report numbness, ringing in the ears, or a change in how much urine you pass.
At response reviewYour specialist plans scans and tumour-marker tests according to your disease. AFP, beta-hCG and LDH may be relevant depending on tumour type and earlier results.Ask which markers matter for you and how any residual mass will be assessed.

Your team records the dates and arranges extra tests if your symptoms or results change.

At review visits

  • Can I skip the last infusion if I feel unwell? Call the team straight away. The prescribing consultant weighs any delay against the aim of cure and tells you what to do about the next visit.
  • What happens after this part of treatment? Days 6–21 have no scheduled EP infusions. Your blood count can still fall. The next cycle starts only after review.
  • Which symptoms need urgent help? Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills, unexpected bleeding or sudden illness, including during the treatment break. Sudden chest pain, collapse or severe breathlessness needs emergency help.
  • What are tumour markers? Some germ-cell tumours release substances into the blood. Your team may follow alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), a hormone called beta-hCG, and an enzyme called LDH. They interpret changes alongside your scans.
