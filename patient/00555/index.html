FOLFOXIRI

FOLFOXIRI combines three chemotherapy medicines with folinic acid to treat colorectal cancer that has spread. The aim is cancer control, symptom relief and, for selected people, a response that allows further local treatment after specialist review.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

More about my medicines

Irinotecan

The body converts irinotecan into SN-38. This traps an enzyme called topoisomerase I while DNA is being copied, causing damage that can stop cancer cells dividing. Normal bowel and blood-forming cells can also be affected.

Oxaliplatin

Oxaliplatin makes links within DNA. These interfere with copying and repair, which can stop a cancer cell dividing. Healthy tissues can also be affected.

Folinic acid

Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself.

Fluorouracil

Fluorouracil interferes with the production and use of DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. The infusion extends exposure over approximately two days.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Infusion and pump connection

The team checks symptoms, blood results and the active treatment plan. Day-ward infusions are followed by connection of the fluorouracil pump.

What to do: Before leaving, confirm the pump-care instructions, disconnection appointment and contact number.

Day 2–3: The pump is delivering treatment

The pump runs for approximately 48 hours from connection. Keep the device and line secure and dry as taught. The team confirms the actual disconnection appointment.

What to do: Call for a leaking, disconnected or unexpectedly stopped pump. Do not reconnect, alter settings or disconnect it yourself unless specifically trained and instructed.

Day 4–7: After disconnection

Delayed diarrhoea can begin after the first 24 hours and may continue after pump removal. Use your written plan and contact the team early. Cold-triggered tingling may occur and nerve symptoms can persist. Nausea and tiredness can overlap; symptoms are not restricted to these days.

What to do: Report poor intake, persistent diarrhoea or mouth pain early. Chest pain or major breathing difficulty requires emergency help.

Day 8–14: Between visits and the next review

Blood counts can fall between visits. Tell the team about bowel symptoms, mouth pain and how you are managing daily activities. Treatment proceeds only after review.

What to do: Bring the symptom passport and check the next appointment. Day 1 usually repeats 14 days after this cycle started, only if confirmed.

Support at home

Nausea prevention and rescue

This chemotherapy combination has a high risk of sickness without prevention. Follow the prescribed prevention and rescue medicines. Call if vomiting prevents drinking; do not duplicate steroids or anti-sickness doses.

Blood counts and recovery

Growth-factor injections depend on your risk, blood counts and previous complications. If prescribed, confirm their timing in relation to chemotherapy and pump removal; do not assume everyone receives them.

Early and delayed diarrhoea plans

Atropine is normally prescribed before irinotecan for early cramps, sweating or diarrhoea. It is avoided with glaucoma; tell the team about asthma as well. At home use the separately prescribed delayed-diarrhoea instructions. This can differ from standard over-the-counter dosing. Keep the medicine and urgent-contact plan available.

Cold and mouth care

Avoid cold drinks, cold air and cold objects if they trigger symptoms. Do not use ice chips during oxaliplatin treatment. Ask for non-cold mouth-care advice. New breathing difficulty must still be assessed rather than assumed to be a cold reaction.
