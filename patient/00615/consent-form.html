Treatment context
This treatment aims to slow cancer growth and help control symptoms. Your team explains the expected benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your situation.
Schedule
Doxorubicin 50 mg/m², cisplatin 50 mg/m² and cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m² on Day 1, repeated every 21 days for up to 6 cycles, stopping earlier for progression or unacceptable side effects.

2. Regimen / agent-specific material risks

Cyclophosphamide

Cytotoxic chemotherapy
Low blood counts, infection, bleeding and anaemia
Sickness, mouth ulcers, diarrhoea, tiredness and hair loss
Cyclophosphamide-related bladder irritation and blood in urine
Fertility effects, pregnancy risk and rare later second cancers

Doxorubicin

Cytotoxic chemotherapy
Low blood counts, infection, bleeding and anaemia
Sickness, mouth ulcers, diarrhoea, tiredness and hair loss
Doxorubicin-related heart damage and tissue injury if it leaks outside a vein
Fertility effects, pregnancy risk and rare later second cancers

Cisplatin

Cytotoxic chemotherapy
Low blood counts, infection, bleeding and anaemia
Sickness, mouth ulcers, diarrhoea, tiredness and hair loss
Cisplatin-related kidney damage, low blood salts, hearing loss and nerve damage
Fertility effects, pregnancy risk and rare later second cancers

Discuss treatment aim, expected benefit and uncertainty, alternatives and the option of declining; each medicine’s risks, potentially lasting effects, required tests, urgent symptoms, fertility, contraception, pregnancy, breastfeeding and the individual support plan. Follow the unit’s cisplatin hydration plan and individual fluid target. The source advises 2–3 litres on Day 1 to reduce bladder irritation from cyclophosphamide; the team must adapt this if heart or kidney problems restrict fluids. Report blood in urine or painful urination promptly.
