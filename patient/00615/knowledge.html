When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology urgently for fever, sudden illness, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, persistent vomiting or inability to drink. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

This treatment aims to slow cancer growth and help control symptoms. Your team explains the expected benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your situation.

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

Cyclophosphamide

Intravenous chemotherapy
Alkylates and damages deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)

Cyclophosphamide is activated in the body into compounds that damage DNA. Bladder irritation is one reason for a clear hydration plan.

Activated in the body↓DNA damaged↓Division reduced

What to watch for
Follow the unit’s cisplatin hydration plan and individual fluid target. The source advises 2–3 litres on Day 1 to reduce bladder irritation from cyclophosphamide; the team must adapt this if heart or kidney problems restrict fluids. Report blood in urine or painful urination promptly.

Sources: nccp

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

Doxorubicin

Intravenous chemotherapy
Damages DNA and interferes with repair

Doxorubicin interacts with DNA and topoisomerase II, disrupting copying and repair. Its heart and tissue effects require particular care.

DNA interaction↓Repair disrupted↓Division reduced

What to watch for
Follow the unit’s cisplatin hydration plan and individual fluid target. The source advises 2–3 litres on Day 1 to reduce bladder irritation from cyclophosphamide; the team must adapt this if heart or kidney problems restrict fluids. Report blood in urine or painful urination promptly.

Sources: nccp

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Intravenous chemotherapy
Cross-links cancer-cell DNA

Cisplatin forms links within DNA that impede copying. The kidneys, hearing and nerves can also be affected.

DNA cross-links↓Copying blocked↓Division reduced

What to watch for
Follow the unit’s cisplatin hydration plan and individual fluid target. The source advises 2–3 litres on Day 1 to reduce bladder irritation from cyclophosphamide; the team must adapt this if heart or kidney problems restrict fluids. Report blood in urine or painful urination promptly.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests and ECG. Heart-pumping assessment with an echo or MUGA scan if over 65 or clinically indicated; doxorubicin requires LVEF above 50%. Hearing assessment and measured kidney clearance when indicated.

STEP 2

Taking or receiving treatment

Doxorubicin is given slowly into a vein over about 15 minutes, cisplatin over 2 hours, then cyclophosphamide over 30 minutes. Cisplatin requires prescribed intravenous fluids before and after treatment, so the visit takes longer than the drug infusion times. Tell the nurse immediately about burning, pain, swelling or leakage around the line.

STEP 3

Tests and reviews

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests during treatment. Review hydration, blood salts, hearing, tingling or numbness, heart symptoms, mouth care and prior toxicities before further doses. Record lifetime anthracycline exposure; the source doxorubicin limit is 450 mg/m².

STEP 4

Specific practical instructions

Follow the unit’s cisplatin hydration plan and individual fluid target. The source advises 2–3 litres on Day 1 to reduce bladder irritation from cyclophosphamide; the team must adapt this if heart or kidney problems restrict fluids. Report blood in urine or painful urination promptly.

STEP 5

Leaving with a clear plan

Confirm the next appointment, dose, support medicines and verified daytime and out-of-hours phone numbers.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Preventing and treating sickness

CAP is high sickness risk: take the full prescribed prevention and rescue plan, including any locally prescribed stomach protection.

Medicine-specific support

Follow the unit’s cisplatin hydration plan and individual fluid target. The source advises 2–3 litres on Day 1 to reduce bladder irritation from cyclophosphamide; the team must adapt this if heart or kidney problems restrict fluids. Report blood in urine or painful urination promptly.

Mouth care, nutrition and daily life

Follow the unit’s mouth-care plan. Report pain, poor intake or weight loss early; ask for dietetic support. Pace activity, rest and work around symptoms.

Urgent symptoms

Call oncology urgently for fever, sudden illness, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, persistent vomiting or inability to drink. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure: call 112/999.

Medicine and interaction review

Bring all prescription, non-prescription and herbal medicines. Check with the oncology pharmacist before starting or stopping anything.

What the evidence can tell you

Research supports a treatment discussion; it cannot predict one person’s outcome.

The NCCP CAP regimen draws on small salivary gland cancer studies. Salivary gland cancers include different histologies, and this evidence does not provide a reliable personalised survival gain. Discuss the aim of symptom control, chance of tumour response, burdens of treatment and histology-specific alternatives.
