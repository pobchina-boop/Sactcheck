How your medicines work

EGFR-targeted antibody

Panitumumab

Blocks growth messages at the cell surface
EGFR receives a growth signal→Panitumumab blocks the receptor→Some growth signals are reduced

Panitumumab attaches to EGFR, a receptor on the outside of cells. This can interrupt signals that help some bowel cancers grow. A tumour RAS test helps identify whether this approach is suitable. EGFR also supports healthy skin, which explains why skin and nail changes need attention.

Report rash, painful cracks or nail changes early. Eye pain or blurred vision, new breathing symptoms, severe diarrhoea, or blistering skin need prompt assessment. Blood tests check minerals such as magnesium.

Practical treatment guide

Visits, access and care between treatments

Panitumumab on Day 1 every 14 days while beneficial and tolerable. There is no home chemotherapy pump.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the exact regimen, tests, medicines, emergency number and treatment goal. Ask whether a peripheral cannula or an existing line is appropriate; a port is not automatically required.

STEP 2

At the treatment unit

The first panitumumab infusion usually takes about one hour; higher doses or reactions may need longer. Later tolerated infusions may be shorter. Blood review, preparation, premedicines and observation add time; infusion time is not the total appointment length.

STEP 3

After the infusion

There is no fluorouracil home pump and no disconnection visit in this regimen.

STEP 4

Between doses

Use the written symptom and support plan; do not wait for the next appointment if urgent symptoms appear.

STEP 5

The next decision

Confirm the next visit after any delay or dose change. Treatment continues only while the oncology team judges benefit and tolerability acceptable. Follow-up mineral checks continue after the final EGFR-antibody dose where applicable.

Transport and daily life

Plan transport if antihistamines, symptoms or other medicines affect driving. Ask for practical, work, nutrition or emotional support.

If treatment changes

Ask which component has been held and which visits remain. A treatment delay does not automatically cancel blood tests, line care or symptom follow-up.

Support around treatment

Start skin care early

Ask for your skin-care plan before the first infusion. Use the recommended moisturiser and sun protection; your team may prescribe a cream or an antibiotic. Do not use ordinary acne treatments without advice.

Minerals and blood tests

Panitumumab can lower magnesium and other minerals even when you feel well. Blood tests continue during treatment and for about eight weeks after it finishes. Take replacement treatment only as prescribed.

Feeling sick or loose stools

Panitumumab has low nausea risk, but symptoms can still occur. Follow the rescue medicine and hydration plan from your team. Severe or persistent diarrhoea, reduced urine or inability to drink needs urgent advice.

Travel and daily life

Allow time for blood tests, review, preparation, infusion and observation. Ask about transport if medicines or symptoms affect driving. Pace activity and seek help with food, work or caring responsibilities.

The whole treatment plan

Ask how and when the team will assess benefit, which blood tests continue during a pause, and what happens after the final dose. Mineral checks continue monthly for two months after the last antibody treatment.
