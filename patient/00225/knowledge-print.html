When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new breathing difficulty, eye pain or blurred vision, blistering or infected skin, severe diarrhoea or dehydration promptly.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How your treatment works

The aim is to control advanced bowel cancer and symptoms. The team reviews how well treatment is working and whether side effects remain acceptable; there is no single fixed number of cycles for everyone.

EGFR-targeted antibody

Panitumumab

Vectibix
Blocks growth messages at the cell surface

Panitumumab attaches to EGFR, a receptor on the outside of cells. This can interrupt signals that help some bowel cancers grow. A tumour RAS test helps identify whether this approach is suitable. EGFR also supports healthy skin, which explains why skin and nail changes need attention.

EGFR receives a growth signal↓Panitumumab blocks the receptor↓Some growth signals are reduced

What to watch for
Report rash, painful cracks or nail changes early. Eye pain or blurred vision, new breathing symptoms, severe diarrhoea, or blistering skin need prompt assessment. Blood tests check minerals such as magnesium.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Review and infusion

The team checks symptoms, blood tests and treatment suitability, then gives panitumumab through a vein. Tell the nurse immediately about infusion symptoms. Confirm the skin-care plan, contact number and next visit.

Day 2–7: At home

There is no chemotherapy pump to disconnect. Watch skin, bowel and eye symptoms and use the prescribed care plan. Report changes early; serious symptoms can happen on any day.

Day 8–14: Continue care and prepare for review

Rash, nail changes and mineral problems may build over several cycles. Feeling well does not replace blood tests. Bring your symptom record. Do not wait for the next infusion if symptoms are concerning.

Practical treatment guide

Visits, access and care between treatments

Panitumumab on Day 1 every 14 days while beneficial and tolerable. There is no home chemotherapy pump.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the exact regimen, tests, medicines, emergency number and treatment goal. Ask whether a peripheral cannula or an existing line is appropriate; a port is not automatically required.

STEP 2

At the treatment unit

The first panitumumab infusion usually takes about one hour; higher doses or reactions may need longer. Later tolerated infusions may be shorter. Blood review, preparation, premedicines and observation add time; infusion time is not the total appointment length.

STEP 3

After the infusion

There is no fluorouracil home pump and no disconnection visit in this regimen.

STEP 4

Between doses

Use the written symptom and support plan; do not wait for the next appointment if urgent symptoms appear.

STEP 5

The next decision

Confirm the next visit after any delay or dose change. Treatment continues only while the oncology team judges benefit and tolerability acceptable. Follow-up mineral checks continue after the final EGFR-antibody dose where applicable.

Transport and daily life

Plan transport if antihistamines, symptoms or other medicines affect driving. Ask for practical, work, nutrition or emotional support.

If treatment changes

Ask which component has been held and which visits remain. A treatment delay does not automatically cancel blood tests, line care or symptom follow-up.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Start skin care early

Ask for your skin-care plan before the first infusion. Use the recommended moisturiser and sun protection; your team may prescribe a cream or an antibiotic. Do not use ordinary acne treatments without advice.

Minerals and blood tests

Panitumumab can lower magnesium and other minerals even when you feel well. Blood tests continue during treatment and for about eight weeks after it finishes. Take replacement treatment only as prescribed.

Feeling sick or loose stools

Panitumumab has low nausea risk, but symptoms can still occur. Follow the rescue medicine and hydration plan from your team. Severe or persistent diarrhoea, reduced urine or inability to drink needs urgent advice.

Travel and daily life

Allow time for blood tests, review, preparation, infusion and observation. Ask about transport if medicines or symptoms affect driving. Pace activity and seek help with food, work or caring responsibilities.

The whole treatment plan

Ask how and when the team will assess benefit, which blood tests continue during a pause, and what happens after the final dose. Mineral checks continue monthly for two months after the last antibody treatment.

Treatment reference · 01

Your treatment aim and review plan

Understand why this option fits your cancer.

The setting matters

Panitumumab is used for bowel cancer that has spread and has a confirmed wild-type RAS result. It is given after earlier fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin and irinotecan treatment has failed. Your team explains the aim, alternatives and how benefit will be reviewed.

What the tumour tests mean

RAS wild type is required for panitumumab. Your team also considers the cancer location, other molecular results, previous treatment and whether surgery or other local treatment could be useful. A positive eligibility result is not a promise of benefit.

Treatment reference · 02

Before you start and before each cycle

The team checks that the plan remains suitable.

Checks and medicines

Review blood counts, kidney/liver function and minerals, current symptoms, allergies and all medicines. dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (a medicine-processing enzyme) (DPD) testing is not a requirement for panitumumab alone.

Skin, eyes and breathing

Tell the team about an existing rash, skin infection, dry or painful eyes, contact lenses, or previous lung inflammation. Report new eye pain, light sensitivity or visual change promptly.

Treatment reference · 03

Living with treatment

Build the plan around what matters to you.

Skin and nails

Ask for your skin-care plan before the first infusion. Use the recommended moisturiser and sun protection; your team may prescribe a cream or an antibiotic. Do not use ordinary acne treatments without advice. Avoid friction on sore skin and tell the team about painful nail folds.

Food, activity and support

Keep fluids and nourishing food within reach. Pace activity around energy levels and ask for dietetic, emotional or practical support. If symptoms prevent eating, drinking or usual tasks, contact the team.

Pregnancy and fertility

Discuss pregnancy plans and fertility before treatment. Panitumumab requires contraception during treatment and for two months afterwards, and breastfeeding should be avoided for that period. When chemotherapy is also included, its precautions may last longer; ask for the instructions for the whole regimen.

Treatment reference · 04

When treatment changes or finishes

Follow-up continues after the last infusion.

Monitoring and recovery

Continue your skin plan and planned magnesium, calcium and potassium checks for about eight weeks after panitumumab. New symptoms still need review. Ask when to stop prescribed supportive medicines.

Treatment choices

Your team may pause, reduce, change or stop one or more medicines. Ask which medicine changes, why, and what the next review will assess. Supportive and palliative care can help alongside active treatment.

Treatment reference · 05

Recognise changes early

You do not need to identify the responsible medicine before asking for help.

When to call

Report new breathing difficulty, eye pain or blurred vision, blistering or infected skin, severe diarrhoea or dehydration promptly.

Track impact, not just a score

Describe when symptoms began, whether they are worsening, and whether they affect food, fluids, sleep, walking or daily tasks. Your team assigns the clinical grade. Use the passport if it helps; it is not required to access this information.

Radiotherapy or other treatment

This regimen has no routine radiotherapy component. If another treatment is proposed, ask whether it happens before, during or after this course and who will coordinate the appointments.

20100007: panitumumab monotherapy

Studies can help explain why a treatment is offered. The schedule, previous medicines and tumour tests affect how well the evidence fits you.

Panitumumab improved survival in a trial group selected using tumour testing after other bowel-cancer medicines had stopped working. The likely benefit for you depends on your cancer, previous treatment and general health; it cannot be predicted from the trial alone.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Why do I need a RAS test?

RAS is part of the growth-signalling pathway inside cancer cells. If it is changed by a mutation, blocking EGFR is unlikely to help. Your team needs a validated wild-type RAS result before panitumumab. Other tumour tests and the treatment aim also affect the choice.

Is this the same as checkpoint immunotherapy?

Panitumumab is a targeted antibody against EGFR. It works differently from checkpoint treatments used in some other cancer regimens. Ask your team which treatment class and side effects apply to you.

Does a rash mean the treatment is working?

A rash is a recognised drug effect, but it cannot tell you whether your cancer is responding. Report it early so it can be treated. Scans, symptoms and clinical review guide the response assessment.

Will I need radiotherapy as part of this cycle?

These panitumumab regimens do not contain a scheduled radiotherapy course. If radiotherapy is recommended separately, your oncology teams will explain its purpose and timing. Do not assume it occurs on infusion or pump days.

What about pregnancy, breastfeeding or fertility?

Discuss pregnancy plans and fertility before treatment. Panitumumab requires contraception during treatment and for two months afterwards, and breastfeeding should be avoided for that period. When chemotherapy is also included, its precautions may last longer; ask for the instructions for the whole regimen.

How long should I allow for the appointment?

The infusion itself is usually about an hour at first, but blood tests, review, pharmacy preparation and observation add time. Ask your unit for its expected visit length. Larger doses or a previous reaction may need a longer infusion.
