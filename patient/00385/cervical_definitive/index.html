SACTCheck · NCCP 00385 · My treatment at a glance

Weekly cisplatin with radiotherapy for cervical cancer

The aim is to cure cervical cancer using radiotherapy as the main treatment. Cisplatin helps the radiation work.

Definitive chemoradiation · NCCP 00385 v5 · Source checked 2026-11-27

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

My overall treatment

One cisplatin infusion each week with radiotherapy for five planned cycles. Your consultant may extend weekly treatment while radiotherapy continues.

  1. Your next stepExternal-beam radiotherapy and internal radiotherapy (brachytherapy) have their own appointments. Ask the radiation team to mark both on your calendar.

My treatment calendar

Weekly cisplatin with pelvic radiotherapyCisplatin runs into a vein over about one hour. Fluids before and after it protect the kidneys. Your visit also includes blood review and anti-sickness treatment.
Between visitsExternal-beam radiotherapy and internal radiotherapy (brachytherapy) have their own appointments. Ask the radiation team to mark both on your calendar.

Your team confirms the active phase and dates. Symptoms may occur during or after treatment; there are no guaranteed safe days.

Between treatments

  • Contact oncology urgently for fever, heavy bleeding, uncontrolled diarrhoea, very little urine or vomiting that prevents drinking. Tell both teams if you miss an appointment.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
