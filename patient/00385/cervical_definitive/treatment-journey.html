Your treatment journey

Weekly cisplatin with radiotherapy for cervical cancer

The aim is to cure cervical cancer using radiotherapy as the main treatment. Cisplatin helps the radiation work.

Your infusion schedule

Weekly cisplatin with pelvic radiotherapy

Cisplatin runs into a vein over about one hour. Fluids before and after it protect the kidneys. Your visit also includes blood review and anti-sickness treatment.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat your team checksWhat you can do
Before startingBlood count and kidney and liver tests. Your team checks hearing and kidney clearance in more detail when needed.Mention hearing aids, ringing ears, numbness or difficulty drinking before your first infusion.
Before each weekly doseRepeat the blood count and kidney and liver tests. Review fluid intake, hearing, nerve symptoms and the radiotherapy plan.Tell the team about diarrhoea, vomiting or reduced urine. Ask which fluids and anti-sickness medicines to use at home.
After treatmentThe radiotherapy team arranges follow-up and checks recovery, including bowel, bladder and vaginal symptoms.Ask for your follow-up date, a contact for new bleeding and advice on sexual health or menopause symptoms.

Your team books scans and any extra blood tests according to your symptoms and treatment plan.

At review visits

  • Why are some visits missing from my chemotherapy calendar? You may have radiation on days without a cisplatin infusion. Brachytherapy is booked separately. Follow the dates given by radiation oncology.
  • How will the team decide what happens next? External-beam radiotherapy and internal radiotherapy (brachytherapy) have their own appointments. Ask the radiation team to mark both on your calendar.
