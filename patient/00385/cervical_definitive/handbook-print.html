My treatment handbook

Weekly cisplatin with radiotherapy for cervical cancer

Your treatment and how the medicines work

The aim is to cure cervical cancer using radiotherapy as the main treatment. Cisplatin helps the radiation work.

Cisplatin · Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. Damages cancer-cell DNA and makes the cells more sensitive to radiation. Tell the team about ringing in the ears, tingling, reduced urine or vomiting that stops you drinking.

What the research found

People who received cisplatin with radiation lived longer as a group than those given an older medicine with radiation in this study.

The comparator and radiation techniques were historical. This result does not compare weekly cisplatin with modern induction or immunotherapy strategies.

Your infusion schedule

Weekly cisplatin with pelvic radiotherapy

Cisplatin runs into a vein over about one hour. Fluids before and after it protect the kidneys. Your visit also includes blood review and anti-sickness treatment.

Common effects and what to do

What you may notice

Tiredness, nausea and appetite changes can affect activity and meals. Ask for help if symptoms interfere with eating or drinking.

Serious effects: when to call

Low blood counts · Cisplatin

Phone the 24-hour number for fever or shaking chills. Report unexplained bruising or bleeding.

Bowel irritation · Pelvic radiation

Report increasing diarrhoea, urgency, bleeding or trouble keeping up with fluids.

Bladder or vaginal irritation · Pelvic radiation

Tell the radiation team about pain on passing urine, troublesome discharge, bleeding or soreness.

Ringing or hearing change · Cisplatin

Report even mild ringing or muffled hearing before the next dose.

Kidney injury · Cisplatin

Call if you pass much less urine or cannot drink because of vomiting.

Nerve changes · Cisplatin

Report tingling or loss of feeling that affects walking or using your hands.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Contact oncology urgently for fever, heavy bleeding, uncontrolled diarrhoea, very little urine or vomiting that prevents drinking. Tell both teams if you miss an appointment. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Cisplatin

Anti-sickness medicines

Use the signed anti-sickness prescription for this week’s infusion. Call if you cannot keep fluids down.

Radiotherapy appointments

Keep the chemotherapy and radiation calendars together. If either appointment changes, contact both teams.

Pelvic care

Ask about bladder, bowel, vaginal and sexual health symptoms. A specialist nurse can discuss dryness, pain, menopause and pelvic rehabilitation.

Planning your visits

Appointments

One cisplatin infusion each week with radiotherapy for five planned cycles. Your consultant may extend weekly treatment while radiotherapy continues.

At the unit

Cisplatin runs into a vein over about one hour. Fluids before and after it protect the kidneys. Your visit also includes blood review and anti-sickness treatment.

After this part of treatment

External-beam radiotherapy and internal radiotherapy (brachytherapy) have their own appointments. Ask the radiation team to mark both on your calendar.

Preparing for your oncology discussion

  • Why are some visits missing from my chemotherapy calendar? You may have radiation on days without a cisplatin infusion. Brachytherapy is booked separately. Follow the dates given by radiation oncology.
  • How will the team decide what happens next? External-beam radiotherapy and internal radiotherapy (brachytherapy) have their own appointments. Ask the radiation team to mark both on your calendar.
