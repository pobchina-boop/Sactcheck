Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

Cisplatin with radiotherapy after cervical cancer surgery

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

A lymph node removed during surgery contained cancer. Radiation and weekly cisplatin aim to reduce the chance of cancer returning. Postoperative adjuvant chemoradiation for cervical squamous, adenosquamous or adenocarcinoma with unexpected pathological node positivity.

Treatment schedule. One cisplatin infusion each week with radiotherapy for five planned cycles. Your consultant may extend weekly treatment while radiotherapy continues.

Regimen-specific side effects

Cisplatin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Kidneys and hearingFluids and blood tests help protect the kidneys. Report reduced urine, ringing or hearing loss promptly. Hearing changes can persist.
Nerves, nausea and low countsReport numbness, persistent vomiting or bleeding. Call urgently for fever or chills.

Pelvic radiotherapy alongside chemotherapy

Discuss bowel and bladder irritation, vaginal changes and the effects on sexual health, ovarian function and fertility that apply to you. Your radiation oncology team will discuss and document your radiotherapy plan separately.

Treatment discussion and decision

Cisplatin with radiotherapy after cervical cancer surgery

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

A study of people at high risk of recurrence after surgery found that adding chemotherapy to pelvic radiation lowered the chance of recurrence and improved survival. The trial included positive nodes, margins or parametria and used combination chemotherapy. It supports the postoperative treatment principle, without directly testing this weekly cisplatin schedule.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Contact oncology urgently for fever, heavy bleeding, uncontrolled diarrhoea, very little urine or vomiting that prevents drinking. Tell both teams if you miss an appointment. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
