Your treatment journey

Cisplatin with radiotherapy after cervical cancer surgery

A lymph node removed during surgery contained cancer. Radiation and weekly cisplatin aim to reduce the chance of cancer returning.

Your infusion schedule

Weekly cisplatin during postoperative radiotherapy

Cisplatin runs into a vein over about one hour. Fluids before and after it protect the kidneys. Your visit also includes blood review and anti-sickness treatment.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat your team checksWhat you can do
After the operationBlood count and kidney and liver tests. Your team checks hearing and kidney clearance in more detail when needed.Review wound recovery, the pathology result and when the radiation team wants to begin. Report pelvic pain, leg swelling or discharge.
Before each weekly doseRepeat the blood count and kidney and liver tests. Review fluid intake, hearing, nerve symptoms and the radiotherapy plan.Tell the team about diarrhoea, vomiting or reduced urine. Ask which fluids and anti-sickness medicines to use at home.
After treatmentThe radiotherapy team arranges follow-up and checks recovery, including bowel, bladder and vaginal symptoms.Ask for your follow-up date, a contact for new bleeding and advice on sexual health or menopause symptoms.

Your team books scans and any extra blood tests according to your symptoms and treatment plan.

At review visits

  • Why do I need treatment after surgery? The lymph node result suggests that microscopic cancer cells may remain. Radiation treats the area at risk, and cisplatin helps the radiation damage these cells.
  • How will the team decide what happens next? Your radiation plan is based on the surgical pathology. Ask whether a radiation boost is needed and who will review bladder, bowel or pelvic symptoms after treatment.
