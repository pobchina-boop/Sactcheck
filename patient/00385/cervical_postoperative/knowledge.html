Your complete treatment reference

Cisplatin with radiotherapy after cervical cancer surgery

Your treatment and how the medicines work

A lymph node removed during surgery contained cancer. Radiation and weekly cisplatin aim to reduce the chance of cancer returning.

Cisplatin · Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. Damages cancer-cell DNA and makes the cells more sensitive to radiation. Tell the team about ringing in the ears, tingling, reduced urine or vomiting that stops you drinking.

Your infusion schedule

Weekly cisplatin during postoperative radiotherapy

Cisplatin runs into a vein over about one hour. Fluids before and after it protect the kidneys. Your visit also includes blood review and anti-sickness treatment.

Common effects and what to do

What you may notice

Tiredness, nausea and appetite changes can affect activity and meals. Ask for help if symptoms interfere with eating or drinking.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Contact oncology urgently for fever, heavy bleeding, uncontrolled diarrhoea, very little urine or vomiting that prevents drinking. Tell both teams if you miss an appointment. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Practical support

Cisplatin

Tell the team about ringing in the ears, tingling, reduced urine or vomiting that stops you drinking.

Anti-sickness medicines

Use the signed anti-sickness prescription for this week’s infusion. Call if you cannot keep fluids down.

Radiotherapy appointments

Keep the chemotherapy and radiation calendars together. If either appointment changes, contact both teams.

Pelvic care

Ask about bladder, bowel, vaginal and sexual health symptoms. A specialist nurse can discuss dryness, pain, menopause and pelvic rehabilitation.

Planning your visits

Appointments

One cisplatin infusion each week with radiotherapy for five planned cycles. Your consultant may extend weekly treatment while radiotherapy continues.

At the unit

Cisplatin runs into a vein over about one hour. Fluids before and after it protect the kidneys. Your visit also includes blood review and anti-sickness treatment.

After this part of treatment

Your radiation plan is based on the surgical pathology. Ask whether a radiation boost is needed and who will review bladder, bowel or pelvic symptoms after treatment.

What the research found

A study of people at high risk of recurrence after surgery found that adding chemotherapy to pelvic radiation lowered the chance of recurrence and improved survival.

The trial included positive nodes, margins or parametria and used combination chemotherapy. It supports the postoperative treatment principle, without directly testing this weekly cisplatin schedule.
