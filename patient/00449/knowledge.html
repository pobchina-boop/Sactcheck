When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact promptly for new chest symptoms, severe or persistent diarrhoea, reduced urine, inability to drink, severe abdominal pain or a line/pump problem. New neurological symptoms, uncontrolled bleeding or a swollen painful limb also need urgent assessment.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Your medicines and what they do

FOLFIRI + bevacizumab is used for cancer of the colon or rectum that has spread. Treatment aims to control the cancer; the likely benefit and review plan depend on your individual situation. Surgery may be reconsidered in selected cases after multidisciplinary review.

Anti-VEGF targeted antibody

Bevacizumab

Avastin or a prescribed biosimilar
Blocks a blood-vessel growth signal

Bevacizumab binds VEGF, a signal that helps blood vessels grow. Blocking it can restrict the blood supply supporting a tumour. It also affects normal blood vessels and healing, which is why blood pressure, urine and planned procedures matter.

VEGF signal↓Bevacizumab binds VEGF↓New-vessel signalling is reduced

What to watch for
Report bleeding, a wound that is not healing, severe abdominal pain, new swelling, chest symptoms or a severe headache. Tell the team before surgery, dental procedures or port insertion.

Sources: nccp · patient

Topoisomerase I chemotherapy

Irinotecan

Campto
Interrupts DNA copying

The body converts irinotecan into SN-38. This traps an enzyme called topoisomerase I while DNA is being copied, causing damage that can stop cancer cells dividing. Normal bowel and blood-forming cells can also be affected.

Irinotecan becomes SN-38↓DNA-repair enzyme is trapped↓Dividing cells are damaged

What to watch for
Tell the nurse immediately about cramps, sweating or diarrhoea during the infusion. Diarrhoea starting later needs the separate written treatment and contact plan.

Sources: nccp · patient

Chemotherapy modulator

Folinic acid

Calcium leucovorin
Strengthens fluorouracil action

Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself.

Folinic acid↓Stronger enzyme binding↓Fluorouracil effect increases

What to watch for
The combined treatment can increase mouth soreness and diarrhoea. Report these promptly. Rare allergic reactions can occur.

Sources: nccp · patient

Chemotherapy

Fluorouracil

5-FU
Disrupts cell building blocks

Fluorouracil interferes with the production and use of DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. The infusion extends exposure over approximately two days.

Drug enters cell↓DNA / RNA disrupted↓Growth slows

What to watch for
Diarrhoea, mouth sores and skin soreness may become serious. Chest pain during the pump infusion needs emergency assessment. Unexpected severe early toxicity can occur even after DPD testing.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Infusion and pump connection

The team reviews symptoms, blood results and your treatment plan, including blood pressure and urine protein. Day-ward infusions are followed by connection of the fluorouracil pump. Before leaving, confirm the pump-care instructions, disconnection appointment and contact number.

Day 2–3: The pump is delivering treatment

The pump runs for approximately 46 hours from connection. Keep it and the line secure and dry as taught. The team confirms the actual disconnection appointment. Call for a leaking, disconnected or unexpectedly stopped pump. Do not reconnect, alter settings or disconnect it yourself unless specifically trained and instructed.

Day 4–7: After disconnection

Delayed diarrhoea can begin after the first 24 hours and may continue after the pump is removed. Use the written plan and contact the team early. Mouth soreness, nausea and tiredness can overlap. Report poor intake, persistent diarrhoea or mouth pain early. Chest pain or major breathing difficulty requires emergency help.

Day 8–14: Between visits and the next review

Blood counts can fall between visits. Tell the team about bowel symptoms, mouth pain and how you are managing daily activities. Treatment proceeds only after review. Bring the symptom passport and check the next appointment. Day 1 usually repeats 14 days after this cycle started, only if confirmed.

Your treatment, step by step

Your port and treatment pump

From preparing the line to coming home after disconnection. Your team arranges the appointments and shows you what to do.

1 · Before treatment

Choose and prepare the line

The team explains whether a port or PICC suits your treatment. You receive instructions for the insertion appointment.

2 · Insertion and recovery

A separate appointment may be needed

For a port, numbing medicine is used and a small device is placed under the skin. Ask about sedation, transport and wound care.

3 · Day 1

The nurse connects your pump

After the day-ward medicines, the nurse checks and connects the fluorouracil pump. Ask for a demonstration before leaving.

4 · At home

Treatment continues for about 46 hours

Carry the pump as shown. Keep the dressing dry and protect the line from pulling, heat and damage.

5 · As arranged

Return for disconnection

A trained professional checks the infusion, disconnects the pump and cares for the line. The appointment is based on connection time.

6 · Between cycles

The access device usually stays

A port needle can be removed while the port stays under the skin. A PICC remains in the arm with ongoing dressing and line care.

Receiving bevacizumab? Tell the oncology and procedural teams before port insertion, surgery or dental work. They agree treatment timing and wound review.

Port or PICC: what is the difference?

A port is a small chamber beneath the skin, usually on the chest, attached to a tube ending in a large vein. A special needle goes through the skin into the port when it is used. A PICC enters a vein in the upper arm; a short section remains outside under a dressing. Both can deliver a home infusion. Your team helps choose the device and teaches its care.

Before insertion and during recovery

Tell the team about allergies, blood thinners, infections and any other cancer medicines. Follow the instructions about eating, drinking and transport; do not stop medicines yourself. Port placement uses local anaesthetic, sometimes with sedation or general anaesthetic. The team explains the procedure and risks, including infection, bleeding, clots and insertion complications. Ask how to manage discomfort, protect the wound and resume activity. Report increasing pain, redness, swelling, discharge or fever.

Connection: what the nurse does

The nurse checks the line and treatment, secures the connection and dressing, and starts the pump. Before leaving, you should have the disconnection location and time, contact numbers including out of hours, and the written spill instructions. Ask to be shown what normal pump progress looks like. You do not need to calculate a rate.

Carrying it, sleeping and washing

Use the supplied carrier and the position recommended for your pump. Keep tubing free from pulling and kinks. Ask how to place the pump safely for sleep and how to protect the line and dressing when washing. Do not immerse it or swim. Avoid direct heat and cold. An elastomeric pump has a balloon that shrinks slowly and may have no alarm; an electronic pump has different instructions. Follow the instructions for the device you actually receive.

Disconnection is not port removal

Keep the appointment even if the pump looks empty, unless the team changes the plan. Staff check the pump and line and arrange safe disposal. After flushing and other line care as required, the port needle may be removed and a small dressing applied. The implanted port stays in place until a separate removal procedure is arranged. A PICC remains in place and needs scheduled dressing and flushing care.

Leakage, disconnection or unexpected pump progress

Call the team immediately if the pump leaks, is damaged, finishes unexpectedly early, appears not to run, or the line disconnects. Follow the emergency or spill steps you have been specifically taught, including use of a clamp if instructed. Do not reconnect, change settings or disconnect the pump yourself. Keep other people away from leaked medicine. If medicine touches skin, rinse it off and wash with soap and water, then seek advice.

Pain, swelling or feeling unwell

Contact oncology immediately for pain or swelling around the line, a wet/loose dressing, redness, fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell. A painful swollen arm or neck needs urgent advice. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty or collapse: call 112 / 999. Do not wait for pump disconnection or the next clinic visit.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the first treatment

Confirm the aim, likely course length, blood-test arrangements and a working urgent contact number. DPD testing is required before starting the fluoropyrimidine unless already completed.

STEP 2

Line and pump arrangements

Confirm venous access, the 46-hour fluorouracil pump and disconnection arrangements before leaving. Know whom to contact for leakage, an alarm, line pain or a pump that finishes unexpectedly.

STEP 3

If treatment is delayed

Ask the unit to confirm the new treatment and blood-test dates. Re-anchor supportive medicines to the treatment actually given; do not take an extra or catch-up anticancer dose yourself.

STEP 4

Getting help between visits

Follow your oncology unit’s urgent advice for fever, infection, severe diarrhoea, chest pain or difficulty breathing. Bring your treatment card and current medicines list.

STEP 5

Operations and dental procedures

Tell both the oncology team and the procedural team that you receive bevacizumab. Holds and restart need an agreed wound-healing plan; do not stop or restart it yourself.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Nausea prevention and rescue

Take the prescribed prevention medicines at the planned times and keep rescue treatment available. Tell the team if nausea or constipation is not controlled; do not duplicate steroid or anti-sickness medicines.

Bowel symptoms

Irinotecan can cause immediate cramps or sweating during the infusion and delayed diarrhoea afterwards. These need different treatments. Ask for the written rescue and urgent-contact plan before starting.

Counts and recovery

White-cell growth-factor injections are prescribed according to individual risk and previous complications. They are not automatically required for every patient or every cycle.

Blood pressure and wound care

Keep the agreed blood-pressure and urine checks. Report bleeding, poorly healing wounds, new swelling or severe headache promptly.

Bowel symptoms: act early

Keep the written plan with your medicines.

During the infusion

With irinotecan, sudden cramps, sweating, a runny nose or diarrhoea can occur during or soon after treatment. Tell the nurse immediately; this needs a different approach from delayed diarrhoea.

At home

Record changes from your usual bowel pattern or stoma output. Follow your prescribed diarrhoea plan and contact oncology if symptoms persist or you cannot replace lost fluids. Do not wait for the next scheduled visit.

Tests and preparation

Checks support safe treatment even when you feel well.

Blood tests and symptoms

The team reviews blood counts, kidney and liver function and symptoms before another cycle. Missing information is not assumed to be normal.

DPD and fluorouracil

DPD helps the body break down fluorouracil. Deficiency can cause severe toxicity. Testing guides the plan but cannot exclude all serious reactions; unexpectedly severe early diarrhoea, mouth sores or illness needs immediate review.

Sources: nccp · patient

Additional checks

Bevacizumab requires blood-pressure and urine-protein checks. Report any wound, bleeding, clot history or planned procedure.

Treatment reference · 07

Bevacizumab: vessels, wounds and checks

Some effects are found by monitoring before symptoms appear.

Why blood pressure and urine?

Blocking VEGF can affect normal blood vessels and kidney filtering. Blood pressure and urine-protein checks help the team decide whether bevacizumab needs review.

Sources: nccp · patient

Before a procedure

Tell oncology and the procedural team about bevacizumab before port insertion, dental work or surgery. They agree treatment timing and wound review. Do not apply a generic waiting period yourself.

Sources: nccp · patient

Symptoms that need action

Seek urgent advice for uncontrolled bleeding, severe abdominal pain, severe headache or visual change, new weakness, chest pain, breathlessness or a painful swollen limb. Call 112 / 999 for collapse or severe breathing/chest symptoms.

Understanding the evidence

Ask which evidence matches your cancer, treatment aim and medicines.

In selected people fit enough for more intensive treatment, a trial found better results with FOLFOXIRI plus bevacizumab than with FOLFIRI plus bevacizumab, with a different side-effect burden. Both groups received bevacizumab, so this does not measure the effect of adding bevacizumab to FOLFIRI. Treatment intensity is an individual decision.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Does having more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably show whether the cancer is responding. Report them so they can be treated. Your team assesses benefit using the tests and review plan appropriate to your cancer.

Why might a dose be delayed or reduced?

Your team may allow recovery, investigate a symptom or reduce the chance of lasting harm. Ask which drug changes, why, and when review is planned. A delay does not itself mean treatment has failed.

What should I say when calling?

Give the regimen name, last treatment date, symptoms and when they started. Explain whether you can eat, drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Give your temperature if available, but do not delay calling to collect details.

What belongs in the consent conversation?

The individual aim and likely benefit, uncertainty, reasonable alternatives including not proceeding, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and practical concerns. You can ask for an interpreter, more time or another explanation and can reconsider your decision.

How many cycles should I use this passport for?

The passport provides reusable recording pages, not a prescribed course length. Your oncology team sets the duration and can provide extra pages.

Will I need a port or a PICC?

The team chooses the most suitable access device with you. A port is under the skin; a PICC enters through the upper arm and has an external section. Both require specific care, and the pump can be used with either.

Does disconnecting the pump remove my port?

No. At the arranged visit the pump is disconnected and the line cared for. A port needle may be removed, but the implanted port stays under the skin until a separate removal procedure is arranged.

What if the pump finishes early or still looks full?

Contact the team for advice. Do not change delivery settings or disconnect or reconnect it yourself. Follow only the specific emergency steps you have been taught.

Is delayed diarrhoea the same as cramps during infusion?

No. Irinotecan can cause an early reaction with cramps, sweating or diarrhoea, and a later diarrhoea problem. Tell the nurse about early symptoms and follow the separate written plan at home.
