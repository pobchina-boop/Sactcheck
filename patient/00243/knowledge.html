How this medicine supports your treatment

Crizotinib targets abnormal ALK or ROS1 signalling in selected advanced non-small-cell lung cancers. The aim is to control cancer and delay progression; the choice depends on the tumour test, previous treatment, other options and your priorities.

Oral targeted therapy: ALK/ROS1 tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Crizotinib

Blocks growth signals from abnormal ALK or ROS1 proteins
Validated ALK or ROS1 tumour test↓Crizotinib blocks the abnormal signal↓Cancer growth may slow

An ALK or ROS1 rearrangement can drive cancer growth. Crizotinib blocks these signals in suitable cancers. It can also affect healthy tissues, so regular tests and symptom reporting remain important.

Report changes in breathing, vision, heartbeat, swelling, liver symptoms or severe bowel symptoms promptly. Check all other medicines before starting or changing them.

Taking crizotinib at home

The prescription and capsule formulation determine how you take it.

Taking, missing or vomiting a dose

Swallow 200 mg/250 mg hard capsules whole with water, with or without food; do not crush, dissolve or open them. Other formulations have different instructions. If a dose is missed, take it when remembered only if the next dose is at least 6 hours away; otherwise skip it. Never double a dose. If you vomit after a dose, do not take an extra dose; take the next dose at its usual time.

NCCP 00243 v7 · official regimen · EMA Xalkori product information: adult NSCLC and patient leaflet

When a dose changes

A dose may change from twice daily to once daily. Ask for a written strength-and-frequency plan. Do not combine old and new instructions, add missed doses or restart after a clinical hold without advice.

Tests and symptom reviews

Baseline checks and ongoing monitoring do different jobs.

Monitoring plan

Before starting: confirm the tumour test, blood count, kidney/liver tests, chest imaging, symptoms and all medicines; ECG and electrolytes near the first dose. Liver tests are planned weekly for the first 2 months using current product information, then monthly; NCCP says every 2 weeks initially, so confirm the local plan. Blood count and kidney tests are monthly. ECG, electrolytes, pulse and blood pressure need a planned schedule and earlier review if unwell. NCCP lists monthly chest X-ray; other disease imaging follows your consultant plan.

Eyes and breathing

Tell your team about visual symptoms and any new cough or breathlessness. Persistent or worsening vision problems need an eye assessment; severe visual loss needs urgent review. The routine paediatric eye-examination timetable does not apply automatically to adult NSCLC.

Support during treatment

Ask for help with eating, fatigue, travel, work and emotional wellbeing.

A tailored anti-sickness plan

Crizotinib has moderate-to-high oral sickness risk. Agree daily prevention and rescue treatment with your team. The chosen medicine must account for heart-rhythm and drug-interaction risks; do not add anti-sickness medicines or a prolonged steroid course yourself.

Food and medicine interactions

Avoid grapefruit/grapefruit juice and St John’s wort. Check prescription medicines, pharmacy medicines, vitamins and herbal products before taking them. Some antibiotics, antifungals, heart medicines and seizure treatments interact. Do not stop a prescribed medicine without a plan.

Pregnancy, fertility and feeding

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Use an effective agreed contraception plan during treatment and for at least 90 days after the last dose; this applies to men and women. Oral contraceptives may be less effective. Tell the team promptly about pregnancy and avoid breastfeeding during treatment.

Report visual changes. Take care with driving or machinery if vision, dizziness or fatigue affects safety. Avoid prolonged sun exposure and use protective clothing and sunscreen.

When you call

Give the medicine name, when the last dose was taken, what has changed, and whether you can drink and pass urine. Do not delay a call to collect details.

Discuss benefit and choices

In separate ALK studies, crizotinib delayed progression compared with the chemotherapy used at that time. The ROS1 study showed cancer responses but had no comparison group. These studies involve different populations and cannot rank present-day medicines or predict an individual outcome. Ask how the evidence fits your tumour, previous therapy, brain involvement and current alternatives.

Evidence depends on the setting

ALK first-line, ALK after previous treatment and ROS1 disease were studied separately. The chemotherapy comparisons are historical; the ROS1 study was small and had no control group. Ask about the alternatives suitable for you, including supportive care and choosing not to proceed.

A change in the plan

Reviews consider symptoms, tests, cancer response, side effects and your priorities. Ask who will arrange follow-up and how to report symptoms after treatment stops. You may reconsider your consent.
