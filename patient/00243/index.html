Understand the treatment. Keep the plan manageable.

Crizotinib targets abnormal ALK or ROS1 signalling in selected advanced non-small-cell lung cancers. The aim is to control cancer and delay progression; the choice depends on the tumour test, previous treatment, other options and your priorities.

Oral targeted therapy: ALK/ROS1 tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Crizotinib

Blocks growth signals from abnormal ALK or ROS1 proteins
Validated ALK or ROS1 tumour test↓Crizotinib blocks the abnormal signal↓Cancer growth may slow

An ALK or ROS1 rearrangement can drive cancer growth. Crizotinib blocks these signals in suitable cancers. It can also affect healthy tissues, so regular tests and symptom reporting remain important.

Report changes in breathing, vision, heartbeat, swelling, liver symptoms or severe bowel symptoms promptly. Check all other medicines before starting or changing them.

Support throughout treatment

A tailored anti-sickness plan

Crizotinib has moderate-to-high oral sickness risk. Agree daily prevention and rescue treatment with your team. The chosen medicine must account for heart-rhythm and drug-interaction risks; do not add anti-sickness medicines or a prolonged steroid course yourself.

Food and medicine interactions

Avoid grapefruit/grapefruit juice and St John’s wort. Check prescription medicines, pharmacy medicines, vitamins and herbal products before taking them. Some antibiotics, antifungals, heart medicines and seizure treatments interact. Do not stop a prescribed medicine without a plan.

Doses and supply

Swallow 200 mg/250 mg hard capsules whole with water, with or without food; do not crush, dissolve or open them. Other formulations have different instructions. If a dose is missed, take it when remembered only if the next dose is at least 6 hours away; otherwise skip it. Never double a dose. If you vomit after a dose, do not take an extra dose; take the next dose at its usual time. Request repeat supply early and keep a current medicines list. A clinician-held dose needs clinician authorisation to restart.

Pregnancy, fertility and feeding

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Use an effective agreed contraception plan during treatment and for at least 90 days after the last dose; this applies to men and women. Oral contraceptives may be less effective. Tell the team promptly about pregnancy and avoid breastfeeding during treatment.

Vision, driving and sunlight

Report visual changes. Take care with driving or machinery if vision, dizziness or fatigue affects safety. Avoid prolonged sun exposure and use protective clothing and sunscreen.

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Contact your oncology team immediately for new or worsening breathlessness or cough, fainting, palpitations, severe dizziness, rapidly increasing swelling/weight, sudden or severe loss of vision, yellow skin/eyes, dark urine, fever/shivering or sudden illness, severe abdominal pain, or persistent vomiting/diarrhoea with poor drinking or little urine. Do not wait for the next appointment. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
