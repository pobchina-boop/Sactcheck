How your medicines work

Taxane chemotherapy

Docetaxel

Disrupts the cell’s division machinery
Microtubules stabilised→Division disrupted→Cell growth affected

Docetaxel stabilises tiny structures called microtubules. Cells need these to separate their chromosomes during division; disrupting the process can stop cancer cells multiplying. Healthy dividing cells are also affected.

Take prescribed steroid premedication. Report fever, lasting numbness, swelling, sore nails or infusion symptoms.

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Damages DNA inside cells
Platinum enters cell→DNA links form→Copying is disrupted

Carboplatin creates links in DNA, making it harder for cells to copy and repair their genetic instructions. Its dose depends on kidney function as well as the intended regimen.

Keep blood-test appointments. Fever, unusual bleeding, repeated vomiting or infusion symptoms need prompt advice.

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

Targets HER2 growth signals
HER2 on cell surface→Antibody binds→Growth signalling reduced

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

Attend planned echoes. Report breathlessness, ankle swelling, rapid weight gain or infusion symptoms.

HER2-targeted antibody

Pertuzumab

Blocks HER2 pairing with other receptors
HER2 pairing→Pertuzumab blocks pairing→Growth signals reduced

Pertuzumab binds a different area of HER2 from trastuzumab. It limits receptor pairing, so the two antibodies act together at different points on the same growth pathway.

Report diarrhoea early, especially with poor fluid intake. Keep cardiac checks and report infusion symptoms immediately.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

All four medicines are given by infusion on Day 1 of a 21-day cycle, for six cycles before surgery. Surgery findings guide the postoperative treatment. There is no routine take-home chemotherapy pump.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm blood tests, heart assessment, active medicines, route and the consent discussion.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Trained staff check and access the line, give the prescribed premedicines and infusions, and monitor for reactions. Tell the nurse immediately about pain, burning, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit time and transport

First visits can take longer because of loading doses, premedicines and observation. Ask the unit about expected time, transport and any escort needed after sedation or line insertion.

Support around treatment

The correct supportive prescription

Follow the script for the active medicine, route and treatment day. Do not carry chemotherapy anti-sickness medicines automatically into an antibody-only phase.

Tests before treatment

FBC, kidney and liver profile at baseline and before every 21-day cycle. The team reviews the necessary results before a planned dose.

Keep heart checks on the plan

An echo every 12 weeks is separate from blood tests or cancer imaging. Ask who books and reviews it.

Plan for symptoms

Keep your agreed contact numbers and instructions. New symptoms can need assessment before a routine appointment.

Do not wait for the next appointment

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
