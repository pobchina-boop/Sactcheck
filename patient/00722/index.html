01 / treatment identity

What this treatment is doing

Neoadjuvant HER2-positive breast cancer treatment for selected high-risk, locally advanced or inflammatory disease, according to the encoded NCCP 00722 regimen.

Docetaxel

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

Carboplatin

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

Trastuzumab

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

Regimen-specific point
The chemotherapy phase and the longer HER2-directed treatment plan are related but not identical. Ask your team what continues after surgery.
02 / treatment journey

How the schedule fits together

Before cycle 1

Baseline blood tests and cardiac assessment are reviewed.

Cycle day 1

Docetaxel + carboplatin + trastuzumab + pertuzumab are given.

Days 2-21

Recovery, supportive medicines and symptom monitoring.

Every 21 days

The cycle repeats if review and blood tests allow.

After chemotherapy

HER2-directed treatment usually continues according to the post-operative/adjuvant treatment plan.

A delay, dose change or omitted medicine can be appropriate. Your team should explain what changed and why.

03 / visual side-effect guide

Where changes may appear

This larger body map is a memory aid. The cards below are separated by the medicines or treatment phase that make each risk relevant. A picture cannot diagnose the cause of a symptom: report new or worsening changes to your oncology team.

Trastuzumab + pertuzumab

Heart function change

New breathlessness, ankle swelling, rapid weight gain or palpitations should be reported; heart function is monitored.

Diarrhoea

Pertuzumab-containing treatment can cause diarrhoea. Persistent or severe diarrhoea needs advice to prevent dehydration.

Infusion reaction

Fever, chills, flushing, wheeze or dizziness during/after infusion needs immediate assessment.

Docetaxel

Peripheral neuropathy

Tingling, numbness or pain in hands/feet should be reported before it interferes with function.

Fluid retention / skin and nails

New swelling, rapid weight gain, painful rash or significant nail changes should be discussed.

Hypersensitivity reaction

Rash, wheeze, swelling or dizziness during infusion needs immediate assessment.

Carboplatin + regimen-wide

Low blood counts / infection

Fever, shivering, bleeding or sudden illness needs prompt oncology contact.

Nausea and fatigue

Use prescribed supportive medicines and seek advice if unable to maintain fluids.

Carboplatin hypersensitivity

Allergic reactions can occur, particularly after repeated exposure; tell staff immediately about symptoms during infusion.

This is a regimen-specific safety summary, not an exhaustive adverse-effect list. Follow the current treatment plan, medicine leaflets and your oncology team’s SOS instructions.

04 / monitoring and support

What helps make treatment safer

Before and during treatment

  • Follow the prescribed steroid premedication around docetaxel.
  • Growth-factor support may be part of the plan; follow the exact product and timing given by your oncology team.
  • Cardiac function is monitored because trastuzumab/pertuzumab can affect the heart.

Bring to each review

  • Your current medicines, including non-prescription products.
  • A note of new symptoms, when they started and what they stopped you doing.
  • Any temperature, blood-pressure or bowel-count readings your team asked you to record.
  • Questions about the purpose of treatment, scan/review timing or changes to the plan.
05 / when to call

Do not wait for the next appointment

Use your oncology SOS instructionsCall oncology urgently for fever, severe diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling, severe rash, uncontrolled vomiting, significant bleeding or a rapidly worsening symptom. Severe breathing difficulty or chest pain: call 112/999.

If your team has given you different local thresholds or emergency instructions, follow those instructions.
