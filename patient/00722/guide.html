Treatment journey
Before cycle 1

Baseline blood tests and cardiac assessment are reviewed.

Cycle day 1

Docetaxel + carboplatin + trastuzumab + pertuzumab are given.

Days 2-21

Recovery, supportive medicines and symptom monitoring.

Every 21 days

The cycle repeats if review and blood tests allow.

After chemotherapy

HER2-directed treatment usually continues according to the post-operative/adjuvant treatment plan.

Important medicine-specific effects

Docetaxel

  • Severe neutropenia/infection can occur.
  • Fluid retention, swelling, nerve symptoms, nail/skin changes and infusion reactions can occur.

Carboplatin

  • Low blood counts, especially neutropenia and low platelets, can occur.
  • Allergic infusion reactions can occur, particularly after repeated exposure.
  • Nausea and vomiting may occur despite preventative medicines.

Trastuzumab + pertuzumab

  • Heart function can fall and is monitored during HER2-directed treatment.
  • Infusion/administration reactions can occur.
  • Pertuzumab commonly causes diarrhoea; rare serious lung reactions can occur with HER2 therapy.
Other / shared treatment effects: The anatomy page highlights important symptom patterns for this exact regimen, but it cannot show every possible effect. Fatigue, nausea, appetite change, mouth/bowel symptoms or blood-count changes may occur depending on the medicines used. Report new or worsening symptoms even if they are not listed here.

Monitoring / medicines

  • Follow the prescribed steroid premedication around docetaxel.
  • Growth-factor support may be part of the plan; follow the exact product and timing given by your oncology team.
  • Cardiac function is monitored because trastuzumab/pertuzumab can affect the heart.

Bring / track

  • Your current medicines, including non-prescription products.
  • A note of new symptoms, when they started and what they stopped you doing.
  • Any temperature, blood-pressure or bowel-count readings your team asked you to record.
  • Questions about the purpose of treatment, scan/review timing or changes to the plan.

When to call - do not wait for the next appointment

Call oncology urgently for fever, severe diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling, severe rash, uncontrolled vomiting, significant bleeding or a rapidly worsening symptom. Severe breathing difficulty or chest pain: call 112/999. If your team has given you different local thresholds or emergency instructions, follow those instructions.
