TCHP: before surgery and beyond

Neoadjuvant · HER2-positive early breast cancer

Six cycles before surgery are one part of the pathway. Surgery findings determine the treatment afterwards; hormone treatment is added only if the cancer is also hormone-receptor positive.

What each check is for

1Heart ultrasound (echo)Before HER2 therapy; every 12 weeks during it; at completion

Checks heart pumping function, even without symptoms.

Ask / act: Ask when the next echo is due and who arranges it. Report new breathlessness, swelling or palpitations.

Sources: NCCP 00722 · ESC

2Response and operation planningDuring neoadjuvant treatment and before surgery

Checks the local response and helps plan surgery.

Ask / act: Ask which breast imaging is planned and whether the result changes the operation.

Sources: NEO

3After surgery: choose the next pathwayWhen pathology is available

Residual cancer and receptor status can change the postoperative medicines.

Ask / act: Ask for the name, purpose and planned duration of the next treatment.

Sources: NCCP 00722 · EARLY

4Bone health, if endocrine therapy is prescribedAt the endocrine-treatment decision; then by risk

Assess fracture risk and whether a baseline DEXA bone-density scan is needed when starting an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression/treatment-induced menopause. It may already have been done before this regimen.

Ask / act: Ask whether a DEXA result is already available and whether bone protection is recommended.

Sources: BONE · NOGG

5Surveillance after early breast treatmentAfter completion of local treatment

Annual mammography of remaining breast tissue after treatment; the team sets the first date. The mastectomy side is not routinely mammographed. New symptoms need assessment between visits.

Ask / act: Report new symptoms without waiting for the next mammogram.

Sources: EARLY · BONE

At the next review, ask

  • Which treatment phase am I in, and what happens next?
  • Which test is expected next, who arranges it, and who explains the result?
  • Who should I contact if an expected appointment has not arrived?
