Adjuvant dabrafenib and trametinib after removal of BRAF-mutated stage III melanoma

NCCP 00415b: adjuvant treatment after complete resection of stage III melanoma with a BRAF V600 mutation. This route is distinct from treatment of unresectable or metastatic melanoma. · NCCP 00415 · version 5 · 28-day cycle

Treatment context
After eligible stage III melanoma has been completely removed, dabrafenib and trametinib are offered to reduce the chance of recurrence. The expected benefit, alternatives and treatment burden need an individual discussion; treatment cannot guarantee that melanoma will not return.
Schedule
Take dabrafenib twice daily, around 12 hours apart, and trametinib once daily, every day unless the team instructs a hold. The adjuvant course is planned for 12 months, unless stopped earlier for recurrence, side effects or another clinical reason. A four-week review is not a drug holiday or one of 12 chemotherapy cycles.

General treatment discussion

Possible short-term, long-term, unexpected and unlisted effects; benefits are not guaranteed.
Severe complications can require hospital admission and can rarely be fatal.
Infection / sepsis, allergic or infusion reactions and leakage outside a vein (extravasation) should be discussed where applicable to the medicines and route.
Pregnancy, contraception, breastfeeding, sexual health and fertility preservation should be discussed where relevant.
Treatment can be delayed, changed or stopped if risks outweigh benefit. Consent is voluntary and can be withdrawn.
