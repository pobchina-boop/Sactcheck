Shared advanced-cancer protocol · NCCP 00226 v9

Weekly paclitaxel

This weekly paclitaxel protocol is used in specified advanced breast, ovarian, small-cell lung and bladder-cancer settings. The aim is usually disease control and symptom relief. Evidence and alternatives differ between cancers; confirm the indication with your oncologist.

How your medicines work

Taxane chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Locks the cell division machinery
Microtubules form→Structures remain locked→Division is interrupted

Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues.

Report tingling or numbness before the next weekly dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Support around treatment

Premedication for infusion reactions

Steroid and antihistamine premedication reduce the chance or severity of reactions. The team may adjust premedication after tolerated doses. Do not omit or change it yourself; tell the nurse about missed doses.

Aches, nausea and bowel support

Agree a pain-relief and anti-sickness plan. Ask before taking new medicines. Uncontrolled pain or poor intake needs review. Some supportive medicines cause constipation or drowsiness.

Nerve symptoms and fall prevention

Use secure footwear and take care with balance, hot surfaces and sharp objects if sensation is reduced. Tell the team early about functional changes; persistent neuropathy may influence dosing.

Hair, nails and skin

Ask about hair-loss support and whether scalp cooling is appropriate and available. Protect sensitive nails and skin. Report painful nail changes, spreading rash, blistering or infection.
