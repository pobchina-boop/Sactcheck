Your complete treatment reference

Weekly paclitaxel for ovarian cancer · four visits per cycle

Your treatment and how the medicines work

After platinum chemotherapy has stopped controlling metastatic ovarian cancer, weekly paclitaxel can shrink or slow the cancer and help relieve symptoms.

Paclitaxel · Taxol (conventional paclitaxel)

Taxane chemotherapy. Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues. Report tingling or numbness before the next weekly dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Common effects and what to do

Hair and energy

Hair loss and tiredness are common. Plan rest around visits and tell the team if fatigue interferes with daily activities.

Nerves and sensation

Report tingling, burning or numbness before the next dose. Trouble buttoning clothes or walking can mean the dose needs review.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call the 24-hour oncology number immediately for fever, chills, bleeding or feeling suddenly unwell. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, breathlessness, dizziness, throat tightness or pain at the infusion site. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Before treatment

Premedicines reduce the risk of an infusion reaction. Tell your nurse about any previous reaction and which medicines you took. Your team prescribes any changes.

Between visits

Call the 24-hour oncology number immediately for fever, chills, bleeding or feeling suddenly unwell. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, breathlessness, dizziness, throat tightness or pain at the infusion site.

Planning your visits

Appointments

Paclitaxel over one hour on days 1, 8, 15 and 22 of a 28-day cycle. Day 22 is a treatment day unless your consultant specifically prescribes a different calendar.

What to bring

Bring the appointment calendar, medicines list, oncology contact card and any record of new symptoms.

Before the infusion

Premedicines reduce the risk of an infusion reaction. Tell your nurse about any previous reaction and which medicines you took. Your team prescribes any changes.

What the research found

Some participants with previously treated ovarian cancer had tumour shrinkage with paclitaxel.

This was a small, single-arm study. It cannot show a survival advantage or establish the best dose or schedule for you.
