Weekly paclitaxel · relapsed or refractory SCLC

Treatment aims to control small-cell lung cancer after previous therapy and may help symptoms. The evidence is limited, so discuss likely benefit, burdens and alternatives with the team.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

Paclitaxel 80 mg/m² is infused on Days 1, 8, 15 and 22 of a 28-day cycle. The consultant may instead prescribe Days 1, 8 and 15. Each planned weekly dose needs review. Continue while helping and tolerated; National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) does not set a fixed cycle cap.

Tests and checks

full blood count (FBC), kidney and liver profile at baseline and before each treatment; NCCP also explicitly lists Day 8 FBC. Check neuropathy, daily function, infection and previous infusion reactions before every dose. Confirm whether Day 22 is prescribed. Check observations during infusion, especially early in the infusion. Significant conduction problems require a specialist cardiac-monitoring plan. Routine serial echocardiograms are not imposed for this monotherapy. Scans and response review follow the consultant’s plan; no fixed interval is invented.

Between treatment visits

Bars show planned treatment and possible symptom patterns, not measured risk. Side effects can overlap and persist.

There are no guaranteed safe days. Act on fever, sudden illness or a new serious symptom whenever it occurs. Do not wait for the next appointment.
