When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new cough or breathlessness, fever or sudden illness promptly. Tell staff immediately about an infusion reaction or pain at the infusion site. Report numbness, tingling or loss of daily function before the next dose.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Your medicines and what they do

Control advanced cancer and symptoms while balancing side effects and treatment burden.

Chemotherapy

Nab-paclitaxel

Albumin-bound paclitaxel · Abraxane
Disrupts cell division by stabilising microtubules

This formulation carries paclitaxel with albumin, a protein. It interferes with the structures cells use to divide. Its dosing and allergy precautions differ from ordinary paclitaxel.

Albumin-bound medicine↓Microtubules held stable↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report numbness, tingling, weakness or problems with daily tasks before the next dose. Tell staff about an infusion reaction; new cough or breathlessness needs prompt assessment.

Practical treatment guide

Your treatment and access plan

Nab-paclitaxel on Day 1 every 21 days. Review response after two cycles; the NCCP protocol says to discontinue if there is no response. Further treatment depends on benefit and tolerance.

STEP 1

Before treatment

FBC, kidney and liver tests before treatment and each cycle. Review response after two cycles; the source specifies stopping if there is no response.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. A port is not automatically required; insertion and removal are separate procedures with individual instructions.

STEP 3

On the treatment day

Confirm the medicines due that day, any premedicines and the reviewed blood results. Tell staff immediately about pain, burning, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

After the visit

No routine take-home infusion pump is required for this regimen. Keep line-care, urgent-contact and next-review instructions.

Visit length

Nab-paclitaxel is infused over 30 minutes. Full visit time includes preparation and review.

Line care

A PICC or port has its own care plan. Report fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or line damage; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

Treatment changes

A delayed, omitted or changed dose needs an updated plan from the treating team. Do not move doses or restart on your own.

Support for this visit

Routine allergy premedicines are usually not required for this albumin-bound formulation. Low-risk antiemetic support is separate; previous reactions may change the plan.
