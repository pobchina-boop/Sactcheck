When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology 24-hour number immediately for a temperature of 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or sudden illness. Call urgently for new breathlessness, chest pain, bleeding, severe or persistent diarrhoea, vomiting despite medicines, inability to drink, very little urine or a spreading or blistering rash. Do not wait for the next visit; serious infection can occur without a high temperature.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Your chemotherapy medicine

Pemetrexed is chemotherapy used to control selected non-squamous non-small-cell lung cancer that is locally advanced or has spread. Maintenance aims to keep cancer controlled after platinum chemotherapy has finished without the cancer progressing. Second-line treatment is a separate treatment decision after previous chemotherapy. Ask which aim and indication apply to you.

Antifolate chemotherapy

Pemetrexed

Alimta
Interrupts DNA building-block production

Pemetrexed blocks several enzymes that use folate to make the building blocks of DNA and RNA. This can slow or stop cancer-cell growth. Healthy cells can also be affected. Prescribed folic acid and vitamin B12 reduce important toxicities and are part of treatment.

Folate-dependent enzymes blocked↓DNA building blocks reduced↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Take the prescribed folic acid and attend for B12. Report mouth sores, diarrhoea, a rash, breathlessness or reduced urine; check before taking ibuprofen or other non-prescription medicines.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Infusion day

Pemetrexed is infused into a vein over about 10 minutes on Day 1. The whole visit is longer because it includes blood-result review, symptom checks, medicines, preparation and observation. The team can use a cannula or suitable existing line; this regimen has no routine take-home infusion pump. Report any reaction or discomfort around the drip immediately.

Day 2: Day after pemetrexed

Continue the prescribed rash-prevention steroid through the day after treatment, folic acid daily, and anti-sickness medicine as directed. Use one reconciled medicine plan; do not double steroids.

Day 3–20: Between visits

Continue folic acid and monitor symptoms. Your B12 injection is due every three cycles and may be booked on a treatment day. Call promptly for illness, poor intake, reduced urine, bowel or mouth symptoms; do not wait for review.

Day 21: Review before another cycle

Blood count, kidney and liver tests, symptoms, previous toxicity and treatment benefit guide the next decision. Confirm the next dose, vitamins, support medicines and scan/review plan.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Folic acid every day

The source uses folic acid 350–1000 micrograms daily. Take at least five doses in the seven days before the first pemetrexed, continue throughout treatment and for 21 days after the final dose. Follow your own prescription and tell the team about missed doses.

Vitamin B12 injection

The source uses vitamin B12 1000 micrograms into a muscle during the week before the first pemetrexed, then every three cycles. Later injections may be on treatment day. A change from platinum treatment to maintenance does not automatically restart the B12 clock: your team checks the last dose and the next due date.

One steroid prescription

Pemetrexed rash prevention uses dexamethasone-equivalent 4 mg twice daily on the day before, day of and day after treatment. Your pharmacist or prescriber should give one clear steroid schedule that also accounts for any anti-sickness medicines. Do not add a second steroid course yourself.

Low-risk anti-sickness plan

Pemetrexed on its own has a low sickness risk. Take the prescribed prevention and rescue medicines and call if sickness persists. A previous platinum anti-sickness course should be reviewed when moving to pemetrexed alone.

Kidneys and other medicines

Check with the team before using ibuprofen, other anti-inflammatory painkillers, aspirin or any new medicine. Kidney function and the medicine determine whether and when it needs interruption. Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own; ask for a written plan.

Reporting symptoms and getting help

Keep your 24-hour oncology number available, record symptoms and ask for practical or emotional support early.

Evidence depends on the treatment setting

Maintenance and second-line studies answer different questions.

Maintenance studies found longer disease control or survival with pemetrexed than placebo in selected patients whose cancer had not progressed after initial chemotherapy. One studied starting pemetrexed after a different chemotherapy combination; another studied continuing it after pemetrexed and cisplatin. A separate second-line study found similar survival to docetaxel with fewer important blood-count side effects. These historical comparisons do not predict an individual outcome or describe pemetrexed plus immunotherapy.

Sources: hanna · jmen · paramount

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Does having more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably show whether the cancer is responding. Report them so they can be treated. Your team assesses benefit using the tests and review plan appropriate to your cancer.

Why might a dose be delayed or reduced?

Your team may allow recovery, investigate a symptom or reduce the chance of lasting harm. Ask which drug changes, why, and when review is planned. A delay does not itself mean treatment has failed.

What should I say when calling?

Give the regimen name, last treatment date, symptoms and when they started. Explain whether you can eat, drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Give your temperature if available, but do not delay calling to collect details.

What belongs in the consent conversation?

The individual aim and likely benefit, uncertainty, reasonable alternatives including not proceeding, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and practical concerns. You can ask for an interpreter, more time or another explanation and can reconsider your decision.

How many cycles should I use this passport for?

The passport provides reusable recording pages, not a prescribed course length. Your oncology team sets the duration and can provide extra pages.

Can I use this page to decide if blood results are safe?

No. Treatment decisions require the clinical protocol, symptoms, previous toxicity, organ function and the prescriber’s review. The patient page explains the checks and does not authorise treatment.

Is this the same as pemetrexed with pembrolizumab?

This guide covers pemetrexed on its own for maintenance or second-line treatment of selected non-squamous non-small-cell lung cancer. If pembrolizumab or another immunotherapy is also prescribed, use the matching combination regimen and its additional consent, monitoring and urgent advice. Pemetrexed with pembrolizumab maintenance is a different treatment plan.

What is maintenance treatment?

Maintenance means continuing cancer treatment after the initial chemotherapy has finished and the cancer has not progressed. In this regimen pemetrexed is the only anti-cancer medicine. Your team confirms whether it is being continued from your earlier combination or started as a new maintenance medicine.

How many cycles will I have?

Treatment is planned every three weeks while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable. There is no fixed total number of cycles in this regimen. Scans, symptoms, blood results, your priorities and the oncology review guide continuation, delays, changes or stopping.

Can I stop the vitamins when platinum treatment stops?

Do not stop them because platinum stops. Folic acid and B12 protect against pemetrexed toxicity and continue according to the pemetrexed plan. The team checks your existing vitamin schedule at a phase change.

How long will I be at the unit?

Pemetrexed is infused into a vein over about 10 minutes on Day 1. The whole visit is longer because it includes blood-result review, symptom checks, medicines, preparation and observation. The team can use a cannula or suitable existing line; this regimen has no routine take-home infusion pump.

Can I take my usual painkillers?

Check with the team before using ibuprofen, other anti-inflammatory painkillers, aspirin or any new medicine. Kidney function and the medicine determine whether and when it needs interruption. Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own; ask for a written plan.

Try explaining the plan back: Can you explain whether this is maintenance or second-line treatment, which vitamin and steroid medicines continue, when B12 is due, and how to get urgent help?
