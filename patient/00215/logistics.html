NCCP 00215 · version 7

Bevacizumab every 21 days · breast treatment component

Bevacizumab infusion on Day 1 of each 21-day cycle with separately prescribed paclitaxel. Continue while the cancer is controlled and treatment is tolerated, with regular review; there is no fixed number of breast-treatment cycles.

Make treatment fit into daily life

The usual bevacizumab reference dose is 15 mg/kg intravenously every 21 days. The first infusion is over 90 minutes; if tolerated, the second can be over 60 minutes and later infusions over 30 minutes. The team checks the actual prescription, blood pressure, urine and other tests before treatment.

Infusion visits

The usual bevacizumab reference dose is 15 mg/kg intravenously every 21 days. The first infusion is over 90 minutes; if tolerated, the second can be over 60 minutes and later infusions over 30 minutes. The team checks the actual prescription, blood pressure, urine and other tests before treatment.

Checks and samples

Allow time for blood tests, a urine sample and blood-pressure checks. A 24-hour urine collection may be needed between visits; confirm when the team needs the result.

Partner treatment

Paclitaxel may be given on different days from bevacizumab. Bring both treatment plans; a change to one medicine does not automatically change the other.

Access and procedures

Bevacizumab needs an intravenous cannula or existing suitable central line; the team decides access. Tell the team about surgery, dental work or wounds before booking procedures.

Support contacts

Keep the daytime and out-of-hours oncology numbers available. If a visit or test is missed, contact the team for a revised plan rather than rearranging treatment yourself.

Who arranges each check?

Agree the due window, booking team, result reviewer and whom to contact if an appointment or result is missing. This reference page does not book or record a test.

Sources and review
