Bevacizumab every 21 days · breast treatment component

Bevacizumab blocks a signal that helps cancers develop blood vessels. With paclitaxel, it can help delay cancer worsening. The aim in this setting is disease control, with treatment reviewed against benefit and side effects.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

Bevacizumab infusion on Day 1 of each 21-day cycle with separately prescribed paclitaxel. Continue while the cancer is controlled and treatment is tolerated, with regular review; there is no fixed number of breast-treatment cycles.

Tests and checks

Before starting: blood count, kidney and liver tests, urine protein, blood pressure and a heart history/examination. Repeat blood count, kidney/liver tests and urine testing before each cycle; check blood pressure before treatment and after it. Extra checks depend on symptoms and results.

Between treatment visits

This shows one 21-day bevacizumab cycle without calendar dates. Partner paclitaxel days are separately prescribed. The next cycle is reviewed before it starts.

Educational reference; the treating team confirms the prescription and tests. This component guide does not cover all paclitaxel risks or give permission to treat.
