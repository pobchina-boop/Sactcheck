When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team immediately for coughing blood, significant bleeding, severe tummy pain, sudden breathlessness or chest pain, or a new severe headache, confusion, seizure or vision change. Fever or sudden illness also needs urgent advice, especially with chemotherapy.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Treatment reference · 01

Why bevacizumab is included

This is one part of your combination treatment.

Treatment aim

To help control bowel cancer that has spread, alongside the chemotherapy chosen by your oncology team.

How it works

Bevacizumab binds VEGF, a signal that helps new blood vessels grow. It can slow tumour growth but also affect normal blood vessels and wound healing.

Which evidence applies?

NCCP lists bevacizumab 15 mg/kg every three weeks with fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy for metastatic colorectal cancer. A survival estimate from a different bevacizumab dose, chemotherapy combination or cancer setting is not assigned to this schedule. Your team explains the evidence for the exact combination and line of treatment.

Treatment reference · 02

Before treatment starts

Some problems are found by tests before symptoms develop.

Baseline checks

Blood count, kidney and liver tests, urine protein, blood pressure and a heart history/examination are checked. A heart ultrasound is considered after chest-wall radiotherapy or previous anthracycline treatment.

Medicines, wounds and procedures

Bring all medicines and supplements. Report bleeding, clotting problems, poorly controlled blood pressure, unhealed wounds and planned operations or dental procedures. Oncology must coordinate any treatment pause.

The complete combination

Confirm the chemotherapy partner, its tests, infusion or tablet calendar, any pump arrangements and prescribed anti-sickness plan. This bevacizumab pack cannot replace those instructions.

Treatment reference · 03

During and between cycles

Your next dose depends on review, not just the date.

Blood pressure and urine

Blood pressure is checked before each cycle and after treatment. Regular blood-count, kidney/liver and urine-protein tests continue. Extra tests are arranged for abnormal results.

Warfarin

If taking warfarin, INR is checked weekly until the warfarin dose is stable, then before each cycle. Tell the team about bleeding or any medicine changes.

Symptoms that need urgent help

Call your oncology team immediately for coughing blood, significant bleeding, severe tummy pain, sudden breathlessness or chest pain, or a new severe headache, confusion, seizure or vision change. Fever or sudden illness also needs urgent advice, especially with chemotherapy.

Support medicines

Bevacizumab alone has minimal sickness risk. The chemotherapy partner determines additional anti-sickness treatment and other supportive medicines; use the issued prescription.

Treatment reference · 04

Living with treatment

Keep information and contacts available on paper as well as online.

Before any procedure

Tell every treating clinician that you receive bevacizumab. It can affect wound healing; your oncology team must agree timing around operations and check healing before restarting.

Keeping a record

This is a rolling record, not a six-cycle limit. Record actual doses and interruptions. A stamp records completed treatment and does not authorise the next dose.

Pregnancy and fertility

Discuss fertility and pregnancy prevention with your team before treatment. Check the duration of contraception and breastfeeding restrictions for every medicine in the combination; contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible.

Treatment reference · 05

Review and follow-up

There is no universal final cycle or scan timetable.

Assessing benefit

Your consultant sets scans and any tumour-marker tests according to your cancer, symptoms and treatment plan. These are separate from routine blood-pressure and laboratory safety checks.

After a pause or stopping

Continue arranged blood-pressure checks and follow-up of unresolved effects. Ask who reviews results, what treatment follows and whom to contact if symptoms develop.

Evidence in this colorectal setting

The chemotherapy partner, dose schedule and previous treatment determine how trial results apply.

NCCP lists bevacizumab 15 mg/kg every three weeks with fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy for metastatic colorectal cancer. A survival estimate from a different bevacizumab dose, chemotherapy combination or cancer setting is not assigned to this schedule. Your team explains the evidence for the exact combination and line of treatment.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Who confirms my next dose?

Your treating team checks the prescription, tests and symptoms. A completed-cycle stamp records past treatment; it does not authorise the next dose.

What if the plan changes?

Ask the team to update your phase, medicines, test appointments and cycle record. Use the updated prescription.

When are my scans?

Your team sets a response and follow-up plan for your cancer and symptoms. This document does not invent a fixed scan or tumour-marker timetable.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are you having now? What changes after induction? What would make you call today? Ask your team to fill any gaps.
