Your treatment journey

Bevacizumab every three weeks for ovarian cancer

Your team may add bevacizumab to initial chemotherapy for advanced ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer and then continue it alone. A different use is with five-day topotecan if the cancer has returned and is resistant to platinum.

Your infusion schedule

First-line · with carboplatin and paclitaxel

Bevacizumab starts with cycle 2. Confirm wound healing and the chemotherapy premedicines for this visit.

First-line · bevacizumab alone

After the chemotherapy phase, bevacizumab can continue every three weeks within the first-line course limit.

Recurrence · with five-day topotecan

Bevacizumab is due on day 1. Topotecan is due on days 1 to 5. Confirm the written anti-sickness plan for each return visit.

At review visits

  • What do I need to check before my next visit? Tell the team before dental surgery or an operation. Starting or restarting bevacizumab after major surgery requires adequate healing and at least 28 days.
  • When is my next treatment due? First-line treatment starts with chemotherapy. Bevacizumab starts in cycle 2 and is given every 21 days. After up to six chemotherapy cycles it can continue alone, up to 15 months (22 cycles) in total. In platinum-resistant recurrence it is given every 21 days with five-day topotecan until progression or unacceptable side effects.
