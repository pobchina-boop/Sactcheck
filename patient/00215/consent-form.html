Bevacizumab every 21 days · breast treatment component

Bevacizumab infusion on Day 1 of each 21-day cycle with separately prescribed paclitaxel. Continue while the cancer is controlled and treatment is tolerated, with regular review; there is no fixed number of breast-treatment cycles.

Discussion aid: review with the patient and complete the approved local consent process.

Purpose and plan

□ Exact indication, treatment aim and uncertainty explained
□ Expected duration and review / switch points discussed
□ Alternatives and the option not to proceed discussed
□ Patient priorities, capacity and support needs explored

Practical safety

□ Medicine, route, schedule and missed-dose instructions
□ Menopause and ovarian-suppression requirements if relevant
□ Pregnancy, contraception / fertility and breastfeeding
□ Monitoring, symptom reporting and contact plan

Selected material risks

□ Raised blood pressure and protein in the urine
□ Bleeding, blood clots and problems with wound healing
□ Rare bowel perforation, abnormal internal connections called fistulae, or serious wound infection
□ Rare severe headache, confusion, seizures or vision changes associated with a brain syndrome
□ Infusion reactions; fatigue and other effects may be increased by the chemotherapy partner

Discuss disease-control goals and alternatives, the lack of established overall-survival improvement in the supporting paclitaxel study, blood-pressure and proteinuria monitoring, bleeding and clotting, bowel perforation/fistula, wound healing, infusion reactions, reproductive precautions and the additional risks of paclitaxel.

Individual benefit, alternatives and concerns

Agreed checks and next review

Before starting: blood count, kidney and liver tests, urine protein, blood pressure and a heart history/examination. Repeat blood count, kidney/liver tests and urine testing before each cycle; check blood pressure before treatment and after it. Extra checks depend on symptoms and results.
