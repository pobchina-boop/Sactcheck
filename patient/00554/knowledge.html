Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Desmoid tumours do not spread to distant organs but can grow locally, cause pain and impair function. This treatment aims to control troublesome or progressive local disease and preserve daily function; it is not a metastatic-cancer survival treatment.

Vinca alkaloid chemotherapy

Vinblastine

Disrupts cell-division structures

Vinblastine interferes with microtubules, preventing cells from dividing normally. Blood production, bowel movement and nerves may also be affected. It is given only into a vein by trained staff; other administration routes can be fatal.

Microtubules are disrupted↓Cell division slows↓Blood, bowel and nerve checks

What to watch for
Report fever, painful constipation with vomiting or abdominal swelling, new weakness or numbness, hearing changes and infusion-site pain promptly.

Sources: nccp · vinblastine-pi

Antimetabolite chemotherapy

Methotrexate

Blocks folate-dependent cell processes

Methotrexate interferes with folate-dependent processes needed to make genetic material and support cell growth. Mouth, bowel and blood cells can be affected. Reduced kidney clearance or fluid collections can prolong exposure and increase toxicity.

Folate-dependent processes are blocked↓Cell growth is disrupted↓Mouth, kidney, liver, blood and lung checks

What to watch for
Report severe mouth ulcers or diarrhoea, dry cough or breathlessness, fever, yellow eyes, reduced urine or new neurological symptoms urgently. Ask the team before starting any medicine or supplement.

Individual prescribed course

Vinblastine 6 mg/m² and methotrexate 30 mg/m² are given on days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle. Day 22 is a planned break. National Cancer Control Programme describes 12 cycles unless progression or unacceptable toxicity requires a change.

This injected regimen gives three weekly doses followed by a one-week break, for up to 12 cycles. Follow the written injection calendar; never substitute daily oral methotrexate. Kidney or liver problems, ulcers or new breathing symptoms require individual oncology/pharmacy review before further treatment. Desmoid treatment is outside the listed licensed indications of the examined vinblastine and methotrexate products. The national regimen supports this use; your team should explain the product-specific off-label rationale and document consent under local policy.

Full blood count

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle as specified by National Cancer Control Programme. The team also reviews current blood counts before each planned dose, including days 8 and 15, and confirms when samples are needed; count recovery is required before further vinblastine.

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle; sooner for dehydration, reduced urine, vomiting, diarrhoea or new fluid collections.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Ask oncology/pharmacy before taking anti-inflammatory painkillers, trimethoprim or co-trimoxazole, other antibiotics, proton-pump inhibitors, folate supplements or new medicines. Avoid alcohol. Tell anaesthesia staff about methotrexate before procedures, including possible nitrous oxide use. Do not self-stop essential medicines; have the complete list reconciled.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Vinblastine is given intravenously over 10 minutes and methotrexate as an intravenous bolus by trained staff. Vinblastine is for intravenous use only. The appointment also includes safety review, prescribed support and observation.

STEP 3

Intravenous access and line care

The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements.

STEP 4

Home support

Methotrexate has low and vinblastine minimal nausea/vomiting risk. National Cancer Control Programme lists no usual separate premedication or routine additional prophylaxis. Use individual anti-sickness, mouth and constipation support. The team prescribes any rescue medicines, extra hydration or white-cell growth-factor injections individually; the separate high-dose methotrexate support programme does not automatically apply.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Fertility and pregnancy precautions

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Your wider treatment plan

A specialist desmoid team weighs observation, systemic options, selected local treatment, symptoms, tumour location and function. Some desmoid tumours can stabilise without immediate treatment. Your treatment decision

Read the general chemotherapy risks and the individual medicine risks together.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Use this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk below.

Methotrexate and vinblastine in advanced aggressive fibromatosis

A small historical phase II study using methotrexate 30 mg/m² and vinblastine 6 mg/m² weekly found tumour shrinkage or stability and symptom benefit in some patients with advanced inoperable aggressive fibromatosis. It supports activity but had no randomised comparator.

The study used continuous weekly treatment, rather than the current National Cancer Control Programme days 1, 8 and 15 of a 28-day cycle. DESMOPAZ used vinblastine 5 mg/m² and a different weekly-then-fortnightly schedule; its non-comparative randomised design does not establish a definitive head-to-head superiority result. Do not present trial rates as validated estimates for this exact schedule.

Sources: trial · nccp · desmopaz

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Desmoid tumours do not spread to distant organs but can grow locally, cause pain and impair function. This treatment aims to control troublesome or progressive local disease and preserve daily function; it is not a metastatic-cancer survival treatment. A specialist desmoid team weighs observation, systemic options, selected local treatment, symptoms, tumour location and function. Some desmoid tumours can stabilise without immediate treatment. Surgery and radiotherapy are individual decisions because local treatment can itself affect function. Discuss pain management, rehabilitation and psychological support alongside tumour control.

When do I attend?

Vinblastine 6 mg/m² and methotrexate 30 mg/m² are given on days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle. Day 22 is a planned break. National Cancer Control Programme describes 12 cycles unless progression or unacceptable toxicity requires a change.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Can I restart after a delay or safety hold?

Only after the treating team gives an explicit plan. A normal result does not erase a previous severe reaction or permanent-stop decision.

Are safety tests the same as checking benefit?

No. Safety tests look for treatment effects on the body. Scans, symptoms and function help the specialist assess tumour control.

How do other treatments fit?
