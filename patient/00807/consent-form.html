Treatment context
Reduce the risk of bladder cancer returning or worsening, and treat suitable carcinoma in situ, using local immune treatment.
Schedule
BCG is placed in the bladder once weekly for six weeks. Maintenance uses three weekly instillations at months 3,6 and12 for intermediate-risk disease; high/very-high-risk plans may add months 18,24,30 and36. Your urologist confirms the course according to risk, response, tolerance and supply.

General risks of bladder instillation

Discuss risks in the context of local bladder treatment, the selected medicine and this patient. The agent-specific section follows.

Urinary catheter discomfort or injury; burning, frequency, urgency, bladder pain or spasm
Blood in urine, urinary infection or difficulty passing urine
Allergic reactions and possible serious or lasting effects; local treatment does not exclude systemic reactions
Serious BCG infection or inflammation outside the bladder, potentially fatal; delayed infection can occur months or years later
Possible bladder contracture, genital inflammation and fertility effects

General treatment discussion

Possible short-term, long-term, unexpected and unlisted effects; benefits are not guaranteed.
Severe complications can require hospital admission and can rarely be fatal.
Infection / sepsis, allergic or infusion reactions and leakage outside a vein (extravasation) should be discussed where applicable to the medicines and route.
Pregnancy, contraception, breastfeeding, sexual health and fertility preservation should be discussed where relevant.
Treatment can be delayed, changed or stopped if risks outweigh benefit. Consent is voluntary and can be withdrawn.

Source: NCCP 00807 regimen and the current product-specific BCG information. This is a discussion aid; use the approved local consent process.

2. Regimen / agent-specific material risks

BCG

Intravesical live bacterial immunotherapy
Burning, urgency, frequent urination, bladder discomfort and blood in urine
Flu-like symptoms, tiredness and fever
Uncommon or rare serious infection spreading beyond the bladder, including sepsis; this can be fatal
Inflammation of the prostate, testis/epididymis, joints or eyes; possible fertility effects
Bladder shrinkage/contracture and lasting urinary problems
Delayed BCG infection may appear months or years after treatment

Discuss the intended benefit, alternatives, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and individual priorities. Record the patient’s questions and the agreed plan. Live BCG placed inside the bladder. It is not intravenous chemotherapy or PD1/PDL1 checkpoint immunotherapy.
