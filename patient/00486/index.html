FLOX: fluorouracil, folinic acid and oxaliplatin

FLOX is given after surgery for selected stage II or III colon cancer to reduce the chance of recurrence. It combines weekly fluorouracil/folinic acid with oxaliplatin on alternate treatment weeks.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

More about my medicines

Oxaliplatin

Oxaliplatin makes links within DNA. These interfere with copying and repair, which can stop a cancer cell dividing. Healthy tissues can also be affected.

Folinic acid

Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself.

Fluorouracil

Fluorouracil interferes with DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. The same drug has different schedules; follow the prescription for this regimen.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1–42: Six weekly treatment visits

Attend on Days 1, 8, 15, 22, 29 and 36; oxaliplatin only on Days 1, 15 and 29. Side effects can occur on any day; the calendar does not predict when you will feel well.

What to do: Call the oncology team if unwell. Report symptoms before the next dose; do not wait for your next appointment if urgent.

Day 43–56: Planned two-week break

No catch-up doses; symptoms can continue during the break. Confirm the next plan. Side effects can occur on any day; the calendar does not predict when you will feel well.

What to do: Call the oncology team if unwell. Report symptoms before the next dose; do not wait for your next appointment if urgent.

Support at home

Supportive medicines

Use the prescribed anti-sickness and diarrhoea plan; check the medicine, timing and rescue duration. Do not duplicate steroids or anti-sickness medicines.

Mouth, bowel and skin care

Report soreness and diarrhoea early. Use gentle mouth and skin care and ask for help with food or fluids.

Medicine checks

Bring all prescription medicines, supplements and non-prescription products. Warfarin and phenytoin may need closer monitoring; brivudine/sorivudine interactions require pharmacy review.

Cold precautions

Avoid cold exposure as advised after oxaliplatin. Do not suck ice chips during this regimen.
