Carboplatin + etoposide + chest radiotherapy

Chemotherapy and chest radiotherapy work together to treat lung cancer. Your team will explain the aim, possible benefit and alternatives for you.

This guide is for Stage III non-small-cell lung cancer. Your radiation team supplies the exact radiation calendar; later treatment is a separate decision.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.

My treatment

My overall treatment

Carboplatin on Days 1 and 8; etoposide on Days 1–5. Each cycle lasts 28 days. Two cycles are planned. Radiotherapy usually starts within 24 hours of the first chemotherapy dose; follow your separate radiation appointment plan.

  1. Chemotherapy + radiotherapyTwo 28-day chemotherapy cycles; radiation runs alongside on its own appointment plan.
  2. After treatmentRecovery checks and the next treatment decision follow your individual plan.

A typical 28-day cycle

Day 1Both chemotherapy medicines
Day 2–5Etoposide each day
Day 6–7No planned chemotherapy
Day 8Carboplatin only
Day 9–28No further chemotherapy this cycle

Timing may change after review. Side effects can occur on any day; there are no guaranteed safe days.

More about my medicines

Carboplatin

Carboplatin damages the DNA that cancer cells need to divide. It can also affect healthy cells.

Etoposide

Etoposide interferes with an enzyme used to copy and repair DNA. This can stop cancer cells growing and can also lower healthy blood counts.

Chest radiotherapy

Radiotherapy directs radiation at the planned chest area. Its appointments and dose are set by the radiation oncology team.

Consent discussion aid

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Both chemotherapy medicines

Treatment after blood-test and symptom review. Radiotherapy usually begins close to this first treatment.

What to do: Confirm both treatment plans and take only the prescribed support medicines.

Day 2–5: Etoposide each day

Return for etoposide on each of these four days. Radiotherapy appointments may also be planned.

What to do: The team checks blood results and symptoms before dosing. Report swallowing or breathing changes.

Day 6–7: No planned chemotherapy

Follow the separate radiotherapy calendar. Anti-sickness medicines may continue as prescribed.

What to do: Call if unwell; do not wait for the next infusion.

Day 8: Carboplatin only

Return for the second carboplatin dose after safety checks. Etoposide is not given today.

What to do: Confirm today’s anti-sickness plan with the team.

Day 9–28: No further chemotherapy this cycle

Continue any planned radiotherapy appointments and safety checks. Tiredness and swallowing symptoms can build up.

What to do: Report new symptoms and keep the urgent contact plan available, including after treatment ends.

Support at home

Your anti-sickness plan

Carboplatin days need a different plan from etoposide-only days. The team combines prevention and rescue medicines into one clear schedule. Do not repeat or add courses yourself.

Eating, drinking and swallowing

Report painful swallowing, weight loss or difficulty drinking early. The team can arrange pain relief, dietitian support or other help.

Fluids and kidney protection

Follow your individual drinking advice and report much less urine. Kidney tests help the team calculate your carboplatin dose; do not adjust it yourself.

Other medicines and prevention

Bring a full medicine list. Ask before starting medicines, supplements or vaccines. Fertility and contraception advice should be agreed before treatment.
