My treatment handbook

EMA/CO for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds.

Dactinomycin · Dactinomycin

Cytotoxic antibiotic. Interferes with the way tumour cells use DNA to make RNA. Tell the nurse immediately if the injection site burns or stings.

Etoposide · Etoposide

Topoisomerase inhibitor. Prevents tumour cells repairing DNA during division. Tell the nurse if you feel faint or breathless during the infusion.

Methotrexate · Methotrexate

Antifolate. Prevents rapidly dividing trophoblastic cells using folate to make new DNA. Check new medicines with oncology pharmacy and follow the timed rescue plan.

Folinic acid · Folinic acid

Rescue medicine. Supplies a usable form of folate to protect healthy cells after methotrexate. Ring the unit for any missed, late or vomited rescue dose.

Vincristine · Vincristine

Vinca alkaloid. Interferes with the structures that cells use to divide. Report tingling, weakness or severe constipation. This medicine is for IV use only.

Cyclophosphamide · Cyclophosphamide

Alkylating chemotherapy. Damages DNA so tumour cells cannot keep dividing. Tell the team about painful urination or blood in your urine.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · EMA

Dactinomycin and etoposide are followed by methotrexate over 12 hours. Write down the infusion start time so you know when rescue begins.

Day 2 · EMA continues

Return for dactinomycin and etoposide. Oral folinic acid starts today. The day 1 and 2 anti-sickness medicines form one course.

Day 3 · rescue continues

Start the first oral folinic acid tablet 24 hours after the methotrexate infusion STARTS. Take four doses 12 hours apart unless your team gives you a different written rescue plan. If a tablet is late, missed or vomited, phone the unit straight away.

Day 8 · CO

Vincristine is given only into a vein, followed by cyclophosphamide. Tell the team about constipation or numbness before treatment. The full sequence repeats every 14 days.

Common effects and what to do

How you feel between visits

Tiredness, nausea or appetite changes can make everyday activities harder. Ask your team for help if symptoms interfere with eating or drinking.

Serious effects: when to call

IV site injury · Dactinomycin

Report pain, swelling or blistering at the injection site immediately.

Sore mouth · Dactinomycin

Phone if mouth ulcers prevent you drinking.

Infection or bleeding · Dactinomycin

Contact the unit urgently for fever, chills or unusual bleeding.

Low blood counts · Etoposide

Phone urgently for fever, chills or unexplained bleeding.

Infusion reaction · Etoposide

Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, faintness or breathing problems.

Mouth ulcers · Methotrexate

Ask for advice if sores make eating or drinking difficult.

Slower clearance · Methotrexate

Attend kidney blood tests. Report vomiting or difficulty drinking.

Rescue tablets · Folinic acid

Check each time against your written schedule. Phone about a missed or vomited dose.

Nerve changes · Vincristine

Report numbness, tingling or weakness before treatment.

Constipation · Vincristine

Phone urgently for severe abdominal pain, swelling or inability to pass stool.

Bladder irritation · Cyclophosphamide

Report blood in the urine, pain or difficulty passing urine.

Low blood counts · Cyclophosphamide

Phone urgently for fever, chills or unexpected bleeding.

Bring to your visit

Between visits

Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Call for severe constipation, abdominal pain, weakness, or a missed folinic acid dose. Report stinging at an IV site immediately.

Preparing for your oncology discussion

  • What happens at my next visit? Two treatment days in week one, oral rescue, then two different IV medicines on day 8.
  • Which symptoms should I phone about? Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Call for severe constipation, abdominal pain, weakness, or a missed folinic acid dose. Report stinging at an IV site immediately.
  • What should I plan before starting? Bring your medicines list and your GTN centre contact details.
  • How should I plan follow-up? After remission your specialist arranges hCG every two weeks for six months, then monthly for six months, then every two months for two years.
  • Why are there more cycles after hCG returns to normal? Treatment continues to clear disease too small to detect. Low-risk disease usually needs three more courses. With high-risk disease the usual plan is three more cycles (six weeks), or four (eight weeks) with adverse features such as liver metastases.
  • Should I call about a dry cough between visits? Phone your 24-hour oncology number straight away for a persistent dry cough or new breathlessness. Methotrexate can cause lung inflammation, and infection can cause similar symptoms. Your team needs to assess you even if you have no fever.
