Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

EMA/CO for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds. High-risk or resistant gestational trophoblastic neoplasia.

Treatment schedule. Two treatment days in week one, oral rescue, then two different IV medicines on day 8.

Regimen-specific side effects

Dactinomycin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Low counts, mouth sores and IV leakagePhone urgently for fever or bleeding. Report painful ulcers. Tell the nurse immediately if the line burns or stings.

Etoposide

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Low counts and infusion reactionPhone for fever or bleeding. Report flushing or faintness during infusion. Hair loss and a small later leukaemia risk are possible.

Methotrexate

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Mouth, blood counts, liver and kidneysReport mouth ulcers, fever or poor fluid intake. Check new medicines and take folinic acid at the written times.

Folinic acid

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Rescue and allergyPhone immediately if a tablet is missed, late or vomited. Seek emergency help for swelling or wheeze.

Vincristine

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Nerves, bowel and IV siteReport numbness, weakness or severe constipation. Tell the nurse if the line stings. Vincristine is for IV use only.

Cyclophosphamide

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Blood counts, bladder and fertilityPhone about fever, unusual bleeding, painful urination or blood in the urine. Discuss future fertility before treatment.

Treatment discussion and decision

EMA/CO for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

Long-term follow-up documents responses and outcomes with EMA/CO in high-risk GTN. Single-centre cohort including previously treated patients.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Call for severe constipation, abdominal pain, weakness, or a missed folinic acid dose. Report stinging at an IV site immediately. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
