EMA/CO for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

My treatment record

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds.

PhaseWhenWhat changes
Day 1 · EMADay 1 · EMADactinomycin and etoposide are followed by methotrexate over 12 hours. Write down the infusion start time so you know when rescue begins.
Day 2 · EMA continuesDay 2 · EMA continuesReturn for dactinomycin and etoposide. Oral folinic acid starts today. The day 1 and 2 anti-sickness medicines form one course.
Day 3 · rescue continuesDay 3 · rescue continuesStart the first oral folinic acid tablet 24 hours after the methotrexate infusion STARTS. Take four doses 12 hours apart unless your team gives you a different written rescue plan. If a tablet is late, missed or vomited, phone the unit straight away.

My questions and next steps

  • What happens at my next visit? Two treatment days in week one, oral rescue, then two different IV medicines on day 8.
  • Which symptoms should I phone about? Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Call for severe constipation, abdominal pain, weakness, or a missed folinic acid dose. Report stinging at an IV site immediately.
  • What should I plan before starting? Bring your medicines list and your GTN centre contact details.
  • How should I plan follow-up? After remission your specialist arranges hCG every two weeks for six months, then monthly for six months, then every two months for two years.
  • Why are there more cycles after hCG returns to normal? Treatment continues to clear disease too small to detect. Low-risk disease usually needs three more courses. With high-risk disease the usual plan is three more cycles (six weeks), or four (eight weeks) with adverse features such as liver metastases.
  • Should I call about a dry cough between visits? Phone your 24-hour oncology number straight away for a persistent dry cough or new breathlessness. Methotrexate can cause lung inflammation, and infection can cause similar symptoms. Your team needs to assess you even if you have no fever.
