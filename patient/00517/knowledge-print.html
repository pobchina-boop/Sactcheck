Act on symptoms, not the calendar

When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Fever, shivering, sudden illness, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, persistent vomiting or inability to drink needs urgent oncology advice. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure is an emergency: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

The medicines act together to shrink or control nasopharyngeal cancer. Your team records how this course fits any planned radiation treatment and your overall treatment aim.

Antimetabolite chemotherapy

Gemcitabine

intravenous (IV) infusion
Interferes with deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) synthesis

Gemcitabine is converted inside cells into forms that disrupt DNA building blocks and DNA copying. It can affect normal blood-cell production as well as cancer cells.

Activated inside cells↓DNA production disrupted↓Cell division slowed

What to watch for
Report fever, unusual bruising, breathlessness, swelling or reduced urine urgently. Flu-like symptoms cannot safely be assumed to be a harmless drug effect.

Platinum chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Cisplatin
Damages cancer-cell DNA

Cisplatin links strands of DNA, the instructions inside cells, making it harder for cancer cells to repair damage and divide. Healthy cells may also be affected. Kidney function, hearing, blood counts and nerve symptoms help the team decide whether the next dose is safe.

DNA damage↓Cell repair disrupted↓Cancer growth reduced

What to watch for
Report reduced urine, persistent vomiting, tinnitus, hearing change or new tingling.

The drawings are simplified explanations of drug action, not anatomical pictures or a prediction of response.

Understanding the evidence

The national regimen brings together research and specialist guidance for a specific treatment setting.

A small single-arm study used the same drug doses for three induction cycles before chemoradiotherapy. It does not establish a comparative survival benefit for the linked NCCP 4–6-cycle course, and it is not the 1,000 mg/m² gemcitabine randomised induction regimen.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before your first visit

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests; review medicines, allergies, weight, oral intake and symptoms. Review nerve symptoms and hearing; audiology when indicated. Cisplatin eligibility includes adequate kidney function.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Gemcitabine runs over 30 minutes, followed on Day 1 by cisplatin over 2 hours. Cisplatin requires prescribed fluids before and after treatment. Day 8 is gemcitabine alone.

STEP 4

Leaving the unit

Confirm the next tests, medicines, treatment dates and verified daytime and out-of-hours contact numbers.

STEP 5

Practical help

Ask about transport, work, costs, communication needs and whether a support person can attend.

Between-treatment notes

Day 1: Day 1: treatment

Gemcitabine runs over 30 minutes, followed on Day 1 by cisplatin over 2 hours. Cisplatin requires prescribed fluids before and after treatment. Day 8 is gemcitabine alone. Report new symptoms before dosing.

Day 2–7: Between the first two visits

Follow prescribed support medicines. Blood counts and side effects may change between visits; this is not a guaranteed safe period. Call the team promptly if unwell. Keep the Day-8 blood-test and treatment appointment.

Day 8: Day 8: gemcitabine

Day 1: full blood count, kidney and liver tests. Day 8: full blood count and creatinine. Hearing assessment and measured kidney clearance are arranged when indicated. Day-8 decisions use their own blood-count and toxicity rules.

Day 9–21: Recovery and next review

Keep the next test and treatment dates. Fatigue and low blood counts may occur between visits. Do not wait for the next visit if unwell.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Preventing and treating sickness

Use the prescribed medicines for the active treatment day. The cisplatin combination visit has high sickness risk; Day 8 is gemcitabine alone and has a separate plan.

Fluids with cisplatin

Cisplatin needs prescribed fluids before and after treatment. Follow your individual drinking plan for the next 24 hours; heart or kidney conditions may change it. Call if vomiting prevents drinking or urine falls.

Mouth care and nutrition

Follow your unit’s oral-care plan. Tell the team early about mouth pain, weight loss, choking or difficulty eating and drinking. Dietetic and swallowing support can help.

Medicine review

Bring prescription, non-prescription and herbal medicines. Check interactions, allergies and any changes with the oncology pharmacist.

Recovery and practical support

Ask about fatigue, work, transport, caring responsibilities, emotional support and help with appointments.

Understanding your diagnosis

Tumour tests and safety tests

Your team explains cancer-specific tests, including Epstein–Barr virus tests where relevant in nasopharyngeal cancer. These are distinct from blood tests used for medicine safety. No universal tumour-marker schedule is prescribed here.

Your treatment aim and choices

Deciding together

Discuss expected benefit, possible lasting effects, suitable alternatives and the option of declining treatment. Tell the team what matters most to you.

Symptoms and when to call

Between visits

Keep a record of symptoms and medicines taken. Do not wait for a routine appointment when symptoms are worsening.

Mouth, eating and swallowing

Speak up early

Tell the team about pain, dry mouth, taste changes, choking, reduced intake or weight loss. Ask for a personalised oral-care, dietetic and swallowing plan.

Radiation-related care

If you are having head and neck radiation, follow the radiation team’s dental, skin, saliva and swallowing advice during and after treatment. Radiation appointments and late-effect checks have their own plan.

Living with treatment

Activity and rest

Pace activity according to symptoms. Ask for help with fatigue, sleep, work, travel or caring responsibilities.

Sexual health and fertility

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment where appropriate. Ask about contraception, pregnancy, breastfeeding and medicine-specific timeframes.

Infusions and access

Your infusion arrangement

Line problems

Report pain, redness, swelling, leaking or a wet dressing. Do not adjust infusion equipment yourself; contact the unit.

After this course

Recovery and follow-up

The team follows recovery, response and persistent side effects. Scans and tumour tests follow the individual plan; there is no universal scan or tumour-marker schedule in this regimen.
