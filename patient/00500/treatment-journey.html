Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenBefore first treatment and before each cycle. Extra counts may be needed for symptoms or abnormal results.

Check white cells for infection risk, haemoglobin for anaemia and platelets for bleeding risk.

Kidney and liver tests

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle; repeat sooner for symptoms, abnormal results or clinical concerns.

Check safe medicine handling and identify kidney or liver effects.

Heart assessment and lifetime exposure

If part of your plan
Whenelectrocardiogram (ECG) before treatment. echocardiogram (heart ultrasound) (ECHO) or multigated acquisition (heart pumping) scan (MUGA) if over 65 or clinically indicated; the source baseline left ventricular ejection fraction (heart pumping measurement) (LVEF) criterion is at least 50%. Review cardiac symptoms and cumulative anthracycline exposure during treatment; further heart tests follow clinical risk or symptoms.

Assess anthracycline-related heart risk and account for previous doxorubicin or related treatment.

Mouth and throat examination

WhenBefore starting; review mouth symptoms during treatment and examine as indicated.

Identify soreness, infection or problems affecting nutrition.

Medicines and symptoms

WhenBefore treatment and at each treatment review; report new symptoms or medicine changes between visits.

Check interactions, treatment toxicity and whether support prescriptions are complete.

Cancer response and follow-up

If part of your plan
WhenYour consultant sets the scan and clinical-review schedule according to the sarcoma and treatment aim.

Assess cancer control separately from safety blood tests.

The whole treatment journey

Doxorubicin for soft tissue sarcoma: treatment and checks

Locally advanced unresectable or metastatic soft tissue sarcoma

NCCP 00500 gives doxorubicin 75 mg/m² on day 1 of a 21-day cycle, usually for 3–6 cycles. The source allows consideration of 60 mg/m² in people over 70; the prescribed dose and number of cycles are individual decisions.

What each check is for

1Treatment reviewDoxorubicin day 1, every 21 days

The aim is usually to control advanced sarcoma, reduce cancer-related symptoms or slow growth. Your consultant explains the realistic goal for your own tumour and circumstances.

Ask / act: Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. New breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations or ankle swelling needs prompt assessment. Pain or burning at the infusion site requires immediate nursing review.

Sources: NCCP
