Additional phase-specific support

  • No separate routine drug premedication is specified by National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP), but prescribed anti-sickness treatment is required. Review all medicines, prior anthracyclines, heart symptoms and pregnancy/fertility plans before starting.
  • This is a high nausea/vomiting-risk treatment. Use one written prevention and rescue plan before and after treatment. Confirm any prescribed stomach protection and local mouth-care measures; do not add duplicate support courses.
  • The team chooses suitable intravenous access and explains line care. Tell the nurse immediately about burning, pain, redness or swelling during an injection or drip. Do not wait for the visit to finish. If you have a port or peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC), agree dressing, flushing and removal arrangements.
  • Caution: metoclopramide with olanzapine may increase extrapyramidal adverse effects.
  • Dexamethasone may be modified or omitted where the systemic anticancer therapy (SACT) regimen already includes a steroid.
  • Metoclopramide: adult maximum 10 mg up to TDS (30 mg/day) and maximum treatment duration 5 days under current EMA/Irish product information; avoid prolonged repeated use without review.
  • Use one agreed support prescription. Reconcile local adjuncts and avoid duplicate steroid or anti-sickness courses.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
