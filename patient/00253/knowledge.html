How this medicine supports your treatment

After early breast cancer treatment, endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence. In advanced disease, the aim is to control cancer and symptoms. Your setting determines the duration and follow-up.

Hormone therapy · oestrogen-receptor modulator

Tamoxifen

Blocks oestrogen signalling at breast-cancer receptors
Oestrogen can stimulate ER-positive cells↓Tamoxifen binds the receptor↓Cancer-cell growth signals are reduced

Tamoxifen acts at oestrogen receptors. Its effects differ between tissues, which helps explain the distinct blood-clot and womb risks. It is not a cytotoxic chemotherapy infusion.

Report abnormal vaginal bleeding, clot symptoms and visual changes promptly. Discuss hot flushes, sleep and sexual symptoms early.

The exact treatment being discussed

After early breast cancer treatment, endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence. In advanced disease, the aim is to control cancer and symptoms. Your setting determines the duration and follow-up.

Who this card covers

Adjuvant or advanced oestrogen-receptor-positive breast cancer in pre- or postmenopausal women, within the exact NCCP eligibility criteria.

NCCP 00253 v6 · official regimen

Dose and administration

20 mg orally once daily. Swallow the tablet whole, with or without food. If a dose is missed or vomited, do not replace it; take the next scheduled daily dose.

NCCP 00253 v6 · official regimen

Duration and decision points

For early breast cancer, discuss the initial endocrine course and a later decision about extending or changing treatment; some patients continue endocrine therapy to 10 years. Advanced treatment continues according to benefit and tolerance. A five-year review is not an automatic instruction to stop.

NCCP 00253 v6 · official regimen

Individual safety

History of significant thromboembolic disease is an NCCP exclusion. Severe hepatic impairment is not recommended. Interactions, including potent CYP2D6 inhibitors, need medication review.

NCCP 00253 v6 · official regimen

Checks throughout treatment

Baseline, ongoing safety checks and cancer scans have different purposes.

Before treatment

FBC, renal and liver profile before treatment. Check clot history, medicines and pregnancy possibility. ECG and electrolytes are added when cardiac / QT risk makes them clinically indicated.

NCCP 00253 v6 · official regimen

During treatment

Liver tests, INR, ophthalmology and ECG are repeated when clinically indicated. NCCP recommends an annual gynaecological examination; new abnormal vaginal bleeding needs prompt assessment.

NCCP 00253 v6 · official regimen

Heart checks

ECG is risk-based. For patients with QT risk, the cited tamoxifen product information recommends baseline ECG / electrolytes and repeat ECG after at least four weeks, with further checks as indicated. Routine echo surveillance is not required for tamoxifen alone.

Your setting changes the pathway

Choose the pathway that your team has confirmed; these are not all extra treatments you must have.

Early-breast follow-up

After curative treatment, annual mammography is generally used for remaining breast tissue: both breasts after conservation or the opposite breast after unilateral mastectomy. The mastectomy side is not routinely mammographed. Routine CT, bone scans and tumour markers are not recommended for asymptomatic early-breast surveillance. New symptoms need assessment between appointments.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO early breast cancer guidelines · NICE NG 101: early and locally advanced breast cancer

Surgery, chemotherapy and radiotherapy

In an early-breast pathway, chemotherapy, if indicated, precedes endocrine treatment. Surgery and its pathology guide adjuvant choices. Postoperative radiotherapy is usually delivered after recovery and any adjuvant chemotherapy; aromatase inhibitors may overlap radiotherapy under the agreed plan. Tamoxifen timing around radiotherapy is agreed by the treating teams. None of these additional treatments is automatically required by an endocrine prescription.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO early breast cancer guidelines

Advanced-disease scans

For advanced breast cancer, response assessment is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease pace, sites, symptoms and treatment. Indolent disease may be reviewed less often; new symptoms can prompt earlier scans. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not a universal three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO metastatic breast cancer guidelines

Tumour markers

Tumour markers may supplement imaging and clinical assessment in advanced disease if they are informative, especially if elevated at baseline. They do not replace scans, and a marker change alone should not determine treatment failure. There is no universal fixed marker timetable.

ESO-ESMO ABC 5 consensus: tumour-marker interpretation

Menopause, pregnancy and contraception

Tamoxifen is not contraception. Discuss non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for nine months after stopping, where relevant; absent periods do not prove infertility.

Evidence for a different decision

Each study applies to its own population and treatment setting.

ATLAS: duration review after five years

6,846 ER-positive women within 12,894 randomized participants who had completed 5 years of tamoxifen. Years 5–14 recurrence:21.4% versus 25.1%; breast-cancer mortality:12.2% versus 15.0%. Extended treatment increased pulmonary embolus risk (RR 1.87) and endometrial-cancer risk (3.1% versus 1.6% during years 5–14). A planned risk–benefit discussion, not automatic ten-year treatment for everyone. Benefit and harm populations differ: cancer efficacy is ER-positive, safety analyses include all receptor groups. Published correction is listed.

What the evidence can tell us

EBCTCG: adjuvant tamoxifen benefit in ER-positive breast cancer: population, findings and limits

Individual-patient meta-analysis of 20 randomized trials,21,457 women; ER-positive efficacy population 10,645. About 5 years of tamoxifen versus no adjuvant tamoxifen.

In ER-positive disease, recurrence rate ratios were 0.53 during years 0–4 and 0.68 during years 5–9; breast-cancer mortality was reduced by about one-third through 15 years.

Relative effects are not an individual absolute benefit. Absolute benefit depends on baseline recurrence risk. Historical adjuvant evidence does not quantify benefit in metastatic disease or ER-negative disease.
