Understand the treatment. Keep the plan manageable.

After early breast cancer treatment, endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence. In advanced disease, the aim is to control cancer and symptoms. Your setting determines the duration and follow-up.

Hormone therapy · oestrogen-receptor modulator

Tamoxifen

Blocks oestrogen signalling at breast-cancer receptors
Oestrogen can stimulate ER-positive cells↓Tamoxifen binds the receptor↓Cancer-cell growth signals are reduced

Tamoxifen acts at oestrogen receptors. Its effects differ between tissues, which helps explain the distinct blood-clot and womb risks. It is not a cytotoxic chemotherapy infusion.

Report abnormal vaginal bleeding, clot symptoms and visual changes promptly. Discuss hot flushes, sleep and sexual symptoms early.

What each check is for

1Bloods and symptomsBaseline FBC, renal and liver profile; later blood tests are clinically indicated, not automatic chemotherapy-cycle tests. Annual gynaecological examination; report bleeding or visual changes promptly.

Checks treatment safety.

Ask / act: Agree the due window, booking owner and result reviewer. Report new symptoms between appointments.

Sources: NCCP 00253

2Heart monitoringECG is risk-based. For patients with QT risk, the cited tamoxifen product information recommends baseline ECG / electrolytes and repeat ECG after at least four weeks, with further checks as indicated. Routine echo surveillance is not required for tamoxifen alone.

ECG and echo answer different questions.

Ask / act: Agree the due window, booking owner and result reviewer. Report new symptoms between appointments.

Sources: NCCP 00253 · SMPC

3Bone healthAssess personal fracture and menopausal risks. Tamoxifen has a different effect on bone from aromatase inhibitors; do not automatically copy an AI bone-density schedule.

Checks fracture risk and whether treatment is needed.

Ask / act: Agree the due window, booking owner and result reviewer. Report new symptoms between appointments.

Sources: NICE_NG101

4Cancer scansEarly / curative: annual mammography of remaining breast tissue; no routine mammography on the mastectomy side, CT, bone scans or tumour markers if asymptomatic. Advanced: response assessment generally every 2–4 months, adapted to disease pace and symptoms; less often may suit indolent disease. The team selects imaging, not automatically CT chest/abdomen/pelvis. New symptoms can bring assessment forward.

The treatment setting determines surveillance or response assessment.

Ask / act: Agree the due window, booking owner and result reviewer. Report new symptoms between appointments.

Sources: ESMO_EBC2024 · ESMO_MBC2023

5MarkersTumour markers may supplement imaging and clinical assessment in advanced disease if they are informative, especially if elevated at baseline. They do not replace scans, and a marker change alone should not determine treatment failure. There is no universal fixed marker timetable.

A marker result alone does not establish treatment failure.

Ask / act: Agree the due window, booking owner and result reviewer. Report new symptoms between appointments.

Sources: ABC5_MARKERS

Support throughout treatment

Symptoms and daily life

Report abnormal vaginal bleeding, clot symptoms and visual changes promptly. Discuss hot flushes, sleep and sexual symptoms early.

Support medicines

No routine antiemetic, steroid premedication or G-CSF is required by this endocrine monotherapy. Ask for individual symptom support.

Tests and appointments

Ask who books each check, when it is due and who reviews it. Tell the team if an expected appointment has not arrived.

Your practical treatment guide

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Seek prompt assessment for new one-sided leg swelling, unexpected vaginal bleeding, new visual changes or jaundice. New chest pain, breathlessness or stroke symptoms can signal a serious problem.
