Hormone therapy · oestrogen-receptor modulator

Tamoxifen

Occupies oestrogen receptors in breast-cancer cells and reduces the growth signals they receive
Oestrogen can signal through the receptor↓Tamoxifen and active breakdown products occupy the receptor↓Less oestrogen-driven signalling reaches the cancer cell

Tamoxifen is a selective oestrogen-receptor modulator. It blocks the effects of oestrogen in breast tissue but has different effects in some other tissues. Your liver converts it into active forms, including endoxifen. Some medicines affect that conversion, so a pharmacist should review prescriptions, supplements and over-the-counter products.

Ask for help with hot flushes, sleep, mood, sexual symptoms or side effects that make daily doses difficult. Report clot symptoms, unusual vaginal bleeding or new visual changes promptly.

1Confirm the planBefore starting or changing treatment

Adjuvant or advanced oestrogen-receptor-positive breast cancer in pre- or postmenopausal women, within the exact NCCP eligibility criteria.

Sources: NCCP 00253

2Checks and symptomsAt the agreed review points

Before treatment, check blood count, kidney and liver function, clot history, medicines and pregnancy possibility. The current Irish Tamox product information also recommends an eye examination before treatment and regular blood-count, liver and calcium checks; the team agrees their timing for your product. ECG and electrolyte checks depend on heart-rhythm risks or symptoms. NCCP recommends an annual gynaecological examination. Report unusual vaginal bleeding or visual changes promptly.

Ask / act: Agree who books tests, reviews results and responds to new symptoms.

Sources: NCCP 00253

Support throughout treatment

Help with daily treatment

Ask early for help with flushes, sweats, sleep, mood, vaginal or sexual symptoms, nausea or fatigue. A difficult side effect matters even if it is not medically severe. The team can discuss symptom treatment and the endocrine plan.

Support medicines

This oral hormone treatment does not require routine steroid premedication, injections to raise white cells or a standard anti-sickness course. Symptom medicines are chosen individually and checked for interactions.

Bloods, eyes and reviews

Agree who arranges the recommended eye and blood checks, their due windows and who reviews the results. Report bleeding or visual changes between appointments.

Bone and activity

The effect on bone differs before and after menopause. Discuss your own fracture risks and whether a bone-density test is appropriate; activity and bone care should suit your circumstances.

Your practical treatment guide

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Get urgent help for a swollen painful leg, chest pain, new breathlessness, stroke symptoms, fainting with palpitations, swelling of the face or throat, or a severe blistering rash. Tell the team promptly about unusual vaginal bleeding, visual changes, jaundice, unusual bruising or symptoms that make treatment difficult.
