How this medicine supports your treatment

After early breast cancer treatment, endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence. In advanced disease, the aim is to control cancer and symptoms. Your setting determines the duration and follow-up.

Hormone therapy · oestrogen-receptor modulator

Tamoxifen

Occupies oestrogen receptors in breast-cancer cells and reduces the growth signals they receive
Oestrogen can signal through the receptor↓Tamoxifen and active breakdown products occupy the receptor↓Less oestrogen-driven signalling reaches the cancer cell

Tamoxifen is a selective oestrogen-receptor modulator. It blocks the effects of oestrogen in breast tissue but has different effects in some other tissues. Your liver converts it into active forms, including endoxifen. Some medicines affect that conversion, so a pharmacist should review prescriptions, supplements and over-the-counter products.

Ask for help with hot flushes, sleep, mood, sexual symptoms or side effects that make daily doses difficult. Report clot symptoms, unusual vaginal bleeding or new visual changes promptly.

Medicine and schedule

Take tamoxifen once daily continuously. There is no planned week off. The prescriber confirms the duration for the treatment setting. Swallow the tablet whole, with or without food. If a dose is missed or vomited, do not replace it; take the next scheduled daily dose.

Individual risks and support

Discuss the expected benefit in your treatment setting alongside clot, womb, eye and liver risks and effects on daily life. Tell the team about previous clots, severe liver disease and all other medicines. Tamoxifen is not contraception: discuss an effective non-hormonal method during treatment and for nine months after stopping, where relevant. Absent periods do not prove infertility. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are excluded from this NCCP pathway. Do not change antidepressants, blood thinners or other regular medicines yourself.

Your setting changes the pathway

Choose the pathway that your team has confirmed; these are not all extra treatments you must have.

Early-breast follow-up

After curative treatment, annual mammography is generally used for remaining breast tissue: both breasts after conservation or the opposite breast after unilateral mastectomy. The mastectomy side is not routinely mammographed. Routine CT, bone scans and tumour markers are not recommended for asymptomatic early-breast surveillance. New symptoms need assessment between appointments.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO early breast cancer guidelines · NICE NG 101: early and locally advanced breast cancer

Surgery, chemotherapy and radiotherapy

In an early-breast pathway, chemotherapy, if indicated, precedes endocrine treatment. Surgery and its pathology guide adjuvant choices. Postoperative radiotherapy is usually delivered after recovery and any adjuvant chemotherapy; aromatase inhibitors may overlap radiotherapy under the agreed plan. Tamoxifen timing around radiotherapy is agreed by the treating teams. None of these additional treatments is automatically required by an endocrine prescription.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO early breast cancer guidelines

Advanced-disease scans

For advanced breast cancer, response assessment is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease pace, sites, symptoms and treatment. Indolent disease may be reviewed less often; new symptoms can prompt earlier scans. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not a universal three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO metastatic breast cancer guidelines

Tumour markers

Tumour markers may supplement imaging and clinical assessment in advanced disease if they are informative, especially if elevated at baseline. They do not replace scans, and a marker change alone should not determine treatment failure. There is no universal fixed marker timetable.

ESO-ESMO ABC 5 consensus: tumour-marker interpretation

Menopause, pregnancy and contraception

Tamoxifen is not contraception. Discuss an effective non-hormonal method during treatment and for nine months after stopping, where relevant. Absent periods do not prove infertility. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are excluded from this NCCP pathway.

Practical support

Help with daily treatment

Ask early for help with flushes, sweats, sleep, mood, vaginal or sexual symptoms, nausea or fatigue. A difficult side effect matters even if it is not medically severe. The team can discuss symptom treatment and the endocrine plan.

Support medicines

This oral hormone treatment does not require routine steroid premedication, injections to raise white cells or a standard anti-sickness course. Symptom medicines are chosen individually and checked for interactions.

Bloods, eyes and reviews

Agree who arranges the recommended eye and blood checks, their due windows and who reviews the results. Report bleeding or visual changes between appointments.

Bone and activity

The effect on bone differs before and after menopause. Discuss your own fracture risks and whether a bone-density test is appropriate; activity and bone care should suit your circumstances.

Taking tablets / attending injections

Why tamoxifen is offered

Taking the planned course after early breast cancer reduces the chance of the cancer returning and lowers the risk of dying from breast cancer. A longer course may bring further benefit for some people.

The size of the benefit depends on your cancer, previous treatment and remaining risk. Early-breast study results do not predict how an individual with advanced breast cancer will respond. Longer treatment also carries risks and should be reviewed with the team.

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Get urgent help for a swollen painful leg, chest pain, new breathlessness, stroke symptoms, fainting with palpitations, swelling of the face or throat, or a severe blistering rash. Tell the team promptly about unusual vaginal bleeding, visual changes, jaundice, unusual bruising or symptoms that make treatment difficult.

Questions to ask

Do I have a treatment-free week?

Take tamoxifen once daily continuously. There is no planned week off. The prescriber confirms the duration for the treatment setting.

When are bloods, ECGs or echoes needed?

Before treatment, check blood count, kidney and liver function, clot history, medicines and pregnancy possibility. The current Irish Tamox product information also recommends an eye examination before treatment and regular blood-count, liver and calcium checks; the team agrees their timing for your product. ECG and electrolyte checks depend on heart-rhythm risks or symptoms. NCCP recommends an annual gynaecological examination. Report unusual vaginal bleeding or visual changes promptly. There is no routine echo schedule for tamoxifen alone.

Which scans apply to me?

After curative treatment, annual mammography is generally used for remaining breast tissue: both breasts after conservation or the opposite breast after unilateral mastectomy. The mastectomy side is not routinely mammographed. Routine CT, bone scans and tumour markers are not recommended for asymptomatic early-breast surveillance. New symptoms need assessment between appointments. For advanced breast cancer, response assessment is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease pace, sites, symptoms and treatment. Indolent disease may be reviewed less often; new symptoms can prompt earlier scans. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not a universal three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

What is my bone-health plan?

Discuss your menopausal state and personal fracture risks. Tamoxifen can affect bone differently before and after menopause. Your team will advise whether a bone-density test is needed.

How long will treatment continue?

For early breast cancer, discuss the initial endocrine course and a later decision about extending or changing treatment; some patients continue endocrine therapy to 10 years. Advanced treatment continues according to benefit and tolerance. A five-year review is not an automatic instruction to stop.

Do I need a port or home pump?

No port or home infusion pump is needed for this medicine itself. Any separately prescribed medicine has its own access requirements.

Can I keep taking my other medicines?

Give the team a complete list, including antidepressants, blood thinners, hormone preparations and supplements. A pharmacist will check whether changes are needed. Do not stop a regular medicine yourself.

What if side effects make me want to stop?

Contact the team early. Help with symptoms or a review of the endocrine plan may make treatment easier. Do not wait until you miss several doses to ask for support.
