The whole treatment journey

AC → weekly paclitaxel + weekly trastuzumab: treatment and checks

HER2-positive neoadjuvant or adjuvant high-risk breast cancer

Four AC cycles 21 days apart, then 12 weekly paclitaxel doses with weekly trastuzumab. Continue trastuzumab to complete one year total, without overlapping it with AC.

What each check is for

1Before first doseBaseline

Baseline FBC, kidney and liver tests; review before each AC cycle and each paclitaxel administration under the regimen and local testing plan.

Ask / act: Confirm tests and consent before prescribing.

Sources: NCCP 00432

2Heart-monitoring planBaseline → cycles → after treatment

ESC supplementary guidance: baseline echo before anthracycline treatment. High/very-high cardiac risk: echo every two anthracycline cycles (for example before cycles 3 and 5 when continuing), and within 3 months after completion. All adults: echo within 12 months after completion. Other repeat tests depend on risk, cumulative exposure and symptoms. Reassess before the first trastuzumab, then echo/MUGA every 12 weeks during trastuzumab. In an uninterrupted weekly taxane phase,12 weeks is near its end; in21-day continuation this is about every 4 cycles. Keep the original clock through the transition. Earlier assessment is needed for concern.

Ask / act: Name the team responsible for booking and reviewing results.

Sources: NCCP 00432 · ESC

3Treatment-phase reviewBefore each transition

Four AC cycles 21 days apart, then 12 weekly paclitaxel doses with weekly trastuzumab. Continue trastuzumab to complete one year total, without overlapping it with AC.

Ask / act: Confirm active medicines, support script and the next test checkpoint.

Sources: NCCP 00432

4Response and follow-upPlanned review points

For early breast cancer, breast imaging and clinical review follow the agreed pathway. Routine CT/PET scans and blood tumour markers are not used to look for distant recurrence when there are no symptoms or clinical concerns.

Ask / act: Review symptoms and imaging together; do not change treatment on tumour markers alone.

Sources: NCCP 00432

At the next review, ask

  • Which treatment phase am I in, and what happens next?
  • Which test is expected next, who arranges it, and who explains the result?
  • Who should I contact if an expected appointment has not arrived?

This map does not show whether a test has been ordered, booked or completed. Do not wait for a scheduled test if you develop new symptoms.
