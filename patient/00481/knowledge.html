Understand your treatment

How this hormone treatment works

This is androgen deprivation therapy (ADT): it lowers testosterone to help control prostate cancer. With radiotherapy, the aim may be cure; in advanced disease, it is part of ongoing cancer control.

GnRH receptor antagonist

Degarelix

Firmagon
Directly blocks testosterone-stimulating signals

Degarelix is a GnRH receptor antagonist. It blocks the pituitary signal to the testes and lowers testosterone rapidly, without an initial testosterone flare. A depot under the skin releases the medicine between injections.

GnRH receptor blocked↓Pituitary signal falls↓Testosterone falls

What to watch for
Book the next injection before leaving. If an appointment is missed, contact the team promptly for a new date; do not arrange an extra loading dose yourself. Degarelix does not produce an initial testosterone surge, so anti-androgen treatment solely for flare prevention is not normally needed.

Long-acting hormone treatment

Duration set by your treatment plan

The first dose is 240 mg as two 120 mg injections under the skin. The first 80 mg maintenance injection is 28 days later, then 80 mg every 28 days. The team sets the total duration.

Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) lowers testosterone, a hormone that can drive prostate cancer. This medicine is your ADT. Additional cancer tablets, chemotherapy or radiotherapy are separate decisions based on your cancer and overall health. Keep treatment going until your team changes the plan; do not stop because prostate-specific antigen (PSA) has fallen.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Treatment visit

A trained healthcare professional gives the injections under the skin of the abdomen. Injection sites are changed and kept away from pressure from belts or waistbands. This formulation is not given into a vein or muscle. The first dose is 240 mg as two 120 mg injections under the skin. The first 80 mg maintenance injection is 28 days later, then 80 mg every 28 days. The team sets the total duration.

Day 2–28: Between appointments

The depot continues working between injections. Symptoms can occur on any day. Keep your next booked appointment; contact the team promptly if it is missed.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Sickness support

This treatment has minimal sickness risk. Routine preventive anti-sickness medicines are not usually required; agree a plan if symptoms develop.

Bone and muscle health

Discuss fracture risk, bone-density testing when indicated, calcium intake, vitamin D and suitable resistance and aerobic exercise. Supplements and bone medicines are individual decisions.

Sexual health, mood and hot flushes

Tell the team which effects matter most. Support is available for sexual function, sleep, mood and vasomotor symptoms.

Treatment-specific instructions

Book the next injection before leaving. If an appointment is missed, contact the team promptly for a new date; do not arrange an extra loading dose yourself. Degarelix does not produce an initial testosterone surge, so anti-androgen treatment solely for flare prevention is not normally needed.

Medicine review

Bring all prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines and supplements. Ask pharmacy before starting or stopping anything.

Urgent help

Treatment reference · 04

Treatment goals and choices

Your goal

This is androgen deprivation therapy (ADT): it lowers testosterone to help control prostate cancer. With radiotherapy, the aim may be cure; in advanced disease, it is part of ongoing cancer control.

Sources: nccp · smPC

Additional treatment

In metastatic hormone-sensitive cancer, many suitable patients benefit from an additional androgen-pathway medicine, and sometimes chemotherapy. Radiotherapy can have a different role in localised disease, selected metastatic disease or symptom relief. Your specialist explains the options for your setting.

Sources: nccp · smPC

Deciding together

Discuss expected benefit, uncertainty, side effects, alternatives including surgical castration where appropriate, and the option of declining treatment. No one duration fits every setting.

Sources: nccp · smPC

What the evidence can tell you

Trials support the discussion but do not predict an individual outcome.

In the pivotal comparative study, sustained testosterone suppression from Day 28 to Day 364 occurred in 97.2% with the 240/80 mg degarelix regimen and 96.4% with leuprorelin. This supports hormone suppression; it does not prove a survival advantage or a cardiovascular advantage for an individual.

Sources: nccp · smPC

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the aim and how long is this planned for?

This is androgen deprivation therapy (ADT): it lowers testosterone to help control prostate cancer. With radiotherapy, the aim may be cure; in advanced disease, it is part of ongoing cancer control.

Do I need another cancer medicine or radiotherapy alongside this?

Ask how this hormone treatment fits the plan for your disease. Additional hormone-pathway medicines, chemotherapy and radiotherapy have different roles; they are not automatically needed in every setting.

When is my next dose and what if I miss it?

Book the next injection before leaving. If an appointment is missed, contact the team promptly for a new date; do not arrange an extra loading dose yourself. Degarelix does not produce an initial testosterone surge, so anti-androgen treatment solely for flare prevention is not normally needed.

How can we protect bone, heart, sexual and mental health?

Ask for a plan covering exercise, bone and fracture risk, blood pressure and glucose, sexual symptoms, mood and sleep. Tell the team about changes early so support can be offered.

Who can I contact urgently?

Use the verified daytime and out-of-hours numbers supplied by your treating hospital. For severe breathing difficulty, collapse, a seizure or severe chest pain, call 112 or 999. Do not use the example numbers in this draft.

Try explaining the plan back: Explain your starting dose and usual schedule. Explain what to do after a missed dose or appointment. Name your next tests and your urgent contact route.
