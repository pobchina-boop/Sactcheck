Additional phase-specific support

  • Use one written anti-sickness plan, including medicines before treatment, tablets for home and rescue treatment. Your team reconciles steroids, interactions and any stomach-protection medicine from your local prescription. Cisplatin needs prescribed intravenous fluids and electrolyte monitoring/replacement. Follow your own fluid advice; tell the team about heart failure or fluid restriction. Review kidney function, hearing, nerves and other medicines before treatment. Day 8 gemcitabine has a separate lower-risk anti-sickness plan.
  • Suitable intravenous access is needed. This schedule does not routinely require a take-home infusion pump.
  • Reconcile all prescribed adjuncts including any local stomach-protection medicine. Follow one prescription; do not duplicate steroid or antiemetic courses.
  • Caution: metoclopramide with olanzapine may increase extrapyramidal adverse effects.
  • Dexamethasone may be modified or omitted where the systemic anticancer therapy (SACT) regimen already includes a steroid.
  • Metoclopramide: adult maximum 10 mg up to TDS (30 mg/day) and maximum treatment duration 5 days under current EMA/Irish product information; avoid prolonged repeated use without review.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
