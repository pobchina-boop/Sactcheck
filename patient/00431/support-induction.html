Additional phase-specific support

  • Combination induction is low emetic risk; nivolumab maintenance is minimal risk. Follow the individual/local anti-sickness plan. Routine premedication is not usually required; steroid immune-toxicity rescue is not routine prophylaxis.
  • Discuss fertility and pregnancy precautions before starting. Immunotherapy may harm a pregnancy. Use the contraception plan and after-treatment duration specified for the medicines you receive; ask about breastfeeding restrictions. Tell the team promptly about possible pregnancy. Do not stop necessary hormone replacement or prescribed steroids without advice.
  • Immunotherapy can cause inflammation in healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new diarrhoea, blood in stools, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell any clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy.
  • This is not an issued prescription. The clinician and pharmacist must complete and authorise any supportive medicines.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
