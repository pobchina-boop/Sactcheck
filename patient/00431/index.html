When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Up to 4 combination visits, 3 weeks apart; then nivolumab alone every 2 or 4 weeks as prescribed.

  1. Combination inductionNivolumab 1 mg/kg and ipilimumab 3 mg/kg are given by intravenous (IV) infusion, subject to review before every dose. Four is the planned combination limit, not the total number of all treatment visits.
  2. Nivolumab aloneAfter the last melanoma combination dose, the first two-weekly nivolumab dose starts three weeks later or the first four-weekly dose six weeks later. The team confirms dose, route, interval and start date. Treatment continues while beneficial and tolerable; previous immune-toxicity decisions still apply.

More about my medicines

Nivolumab

Nivolumab prevents the PD-1 checkpoint from dampening T-cell activity. This can strengthen an immune attack on melanoma. It can also trigger inflammation in healthy organs, including after treatment has ended.

Ipilimumab

Ipilimumab blocks the CTLA-4 checkpoint that limits T-cell activity. In this plan it is combined with nivolumab during induction. The stronger immune effect can also increase inflammation in healthy organs.

Getting ready for treatment visits

Consent discussion aid

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Treatment visit

At each combination visit, nivolumab is given before ipilimumab, as separate IV infusions. The source schedule gives each over 30 minutes; allow extra time for assessment, preparation and observation. Maintenance may be IV or subcutaneous nivolumab with its own dose and appointment interval. The route and interval are recorded in your plan; they are not interchangeable on your own.

What to do: Report symptoms and previous reactions before treatment.

Day 2–21: Between visits

Immunotherapy can cause inflammation in healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new diarrhoea, blood in stools, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell any clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy.

What to do: Do not wait for the next infusion to report a possible immune effect.

Support at home

An individual symptom plan

Ask which anti-sickness or other symptom medicines are prescribed, when to take them and when to call if they do not help. Do not start a borrowed medicine or alter treatment to manage a side effect yourself.

Your urgent contacts

Keep your actual oncology, out-of-hours and pharmacy contacts on the wallet card. Example numbers on a template must be replaced before you use it.

Pregnancy and fertility precautions

Discuss fertility and pregnancy precautions before starting. Immunotherapy may harm a pregnancy. Use the contraception plan and after-treatment duration specified for the medicines you receive; ask about breastfeeding restrictions. Tell the team promptly about possible pregnancy. Do not stop necessary hormone replacement or prescribed steroids without advice.

Eating, activity and practical help

Let the team know if cramps, altered taste or poor appetite make eating or moving difficult. Ask for practical food and activity advice that fits your symptoms and any other illness.

Steroids and hormone replacement

Some immune effects need steroids or lasting hormone replacement. Follow the exact prescription and taper. Carry the relevant alert information, and do not stop or reduce these medicines yourself when anticancer treatment is held or completed.
