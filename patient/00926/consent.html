Treatment discussion

First-line advanced or unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma

PhaseWhenWhat changes
First visitCycle 1 day 1 · Two separate infusionsTremelimumab is given once over about one hour. Durvalumab begins 60–120 minutes after the first infusion finishes and is given over about one hour. Allow extra time for checks.
Later visitsCycle 2 onward · Day 1 every 28 daysOnly durvalumab is repeated, every four weeks if your team advises continuing. Tremelimumab is not repeated.

Possible benefit, alternatives and your priorities

HIMALAYA compared one dose of tremelimumab plus regular durvalumab with sorafenib. Median survival was about 16.4 months with the combination and 13.8 months with sorafenib. Median means half the group lived longer and half lived for less time.

These are results in selected trial groups. They cannot predict an individual response; ask how the findings apply to you.

Discuss the aim, alternatives including supportive care alone, possible benefit, immune effects that may be serious or permanent, possible need for steroids or long-term hormone treatment, and what matters most to the patient. Discuss that only the first visit includes tremelimumab.

Other options discussed and what matters most to me

Important risks we discussed

  • Lung inflammation (pneumonitis) — Contact oncology urgently for a new cough or breathlessness. Severe trouble breathing or chest pain needs 112 or 999.
  • Bowel inflammation (colitis) — Call oncology urgently for new diarrhoea, blood in stools or increasing abdominal pain.
  • Liver inflammation or bile drainage problems — Call urgently for yellow eyes or skin, dark urine or worsening upper abdominal pain.
  • Hormone problems — Unusual thirst, frequent urination, dizziness, vomiting, marked weakness or severe headache need urgent assessment. Collapse or confusion with severe illness needs emergency help.
  • Skin, muscles or heart — Call urgently for blistering rash, new weakness, chest discomfort or palpitations. Severe chest pain, collapse or trouble breathing needs 112 or 999.
