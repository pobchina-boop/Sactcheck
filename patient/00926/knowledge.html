Your complete treatment reference

Tremelimumab + durvalumab

Your treatment and how the medicines work

First-line immunotherapy to control advanced liver cancer and help people live longer.

Tremelimumab · Imjudo

CTLA-4 immune checkpoint inhibitor. Blocks a different brake, CTLA-4, on immune cells early in their response. This can strengthen their response to cancer. Given once on cycle 1 day 1; immune side effects can appear later.

Durvalumab · Imfinzi

PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitor. Blocks a signal called PD-L1 that some cancers use to switch off immune cells. This can help immune cells recognise and attack cancer. Immune cells can inflame healthy organs, including after the final infusion. Carry your immunotherapy alert card.

Your infusion schedule

Cycle 1 day 1 only

Tremelimumab is given once over about one hour. Durvalumab begins 60–120 minutes after the first infusion finishes and is given over about one hour. Allow extra time for checks.

From cycle 2

Only durvalumab is repeated, every four weeks if your team advises continuing. Tremelimumab is not repeated.

Common effects and what to do

Tiredness or changes in appetite

Mention ongoing symptoms. Sudden exhaustion with dizziness may need urgent assessment.

Itch or rash

Tell your team early so they can see the skin. Blisters or peeling need urgent assessment.

Loose stools

Call the team about new diarrhoea even if mild; immune inflammation can worsen quickly.

Serious effects: when to call

Lung inflammation (pneumonitis)

Contact oncology urgently for a new cough or breathlessness. Severe trouble breathing or chest pain needs 112 or 999.

Bowel inflammation (colitis)

Call oncology urgently for new diarrhoea, blood in stools or increasing abdominal pain.

Liver inflammation or bile drainage problems

Call urgently for yellow eyes or skin, dark urine or worsening upper abdominal pain.

Hormone problems

Unusual thirst, frequent urination, dizziness, vomiting, marked weakness or severe headache need urgent assessment. Collapse or confusion with severe illness needs emergency help.

Skin, muscles or heart

Call urgently for blistering rash, new weakness, chest discomfort or palpitations. Severe chest pain, collapse or trouble breathing needs 112 or 999.

Contact your oncology service urgently. New cough, breathlessness, diarrhoea, jaundice, severe headache or marked weakness: contact oncology urgently, even after treatment ends. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Carry the official immunotherapy alert card

Keep the card with you during treatment and afterwards. Show it to an out-of-hours doctor or emergency team and tell them when you last received immunotherapy.

If you become unwell

Use the 24-hour oncology number supplied by your hospital. If the team cannot be reached, seek urgent assessment. Follow the emergency action shown on your card.

Medicines and steroids

Show the team all prescription, over-the-counter and herbal medicines. Take prescribed steroids exactly as directed and ask before changing them.

Infusion visits

Tell the nurse at once about throat tightness, rash, sudden dizziness or difficulty breathing during an infusion. Ask if you need any prescribed medicine at home.

Planning your visits

Your first visit

Tremelimumab is given once over about one hour. Durvalumab begins 60–120 minutes after the first infusion finishes and is given over about one hour. Allow extra time for checks.

Later visits

Only durvalumab is repeated, every four weeks if your team advises continuing. Tremelimumab is not repeated.

Before leaving the unit

Write down the next infusion, blood tests and the 24-hour oncology number. Ask which appointments change if a dose is delayed. Bring the wallet and passport to your next review.

What the research found

HIMALAYA compared one dose of tremelimumab plus regular durvalumab with sorafenib. Median survival was about 16.4 months with the combination and 13.8 months with sorafenib. Median means half the group lived longer and half lived for less time.

These are results in selected trial groups. They cannot predict an individual response; ask how the findings apply to you.
