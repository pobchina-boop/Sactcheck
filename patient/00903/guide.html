Gemcitabine and cisplatin induction before chemoradiotherapy

This chemotherapy is given before chemoradiotherapy to improve cancer control. Induction is the first stage of the planned pathway, and does not replace the later treatment.

Head and neck: nasopharyngeal cancer · NCCP 00903 v1 · Document revised 11 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

The NCCP plan starts chemoradiotherapy 21–28 days after Day 1 of the last induction cycle, subject to review and recovery. This is measured from the first day, not from Day 8 or the end of that cycle. The later regimen has its own prescription.

My overall treatment

Day 1: both drugs; Day 8: gemcitabine. Every 21 days, up to 3 cycles.

Between treatments

  • Side effects can occur on any day. Keep prescribed support medicines and contact details to hand.
  • Call promptly for fever or sudden illness, new breathing symptoms, persistent vomiting or inability to drink.

Bring your medicines list, appointment calendar and verified urgent contacts.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
