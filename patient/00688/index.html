How your medicines work

PD-L1 checkpoint immunotherapy

Atezolizumab

Removes an immune checkpoint signal
PD-L1 blocked→Immune inhibition reduced→Immune cells can attack cancer

Atezolizumab blocks PD-L1 signalling that can restrain immune cells. This may help the immune system attack cancer, but it can also inflame healthy organs. Immune effects can begin during treatment or after it has stopped.

Report new cough, diarrhoea, jaundice, severe rash, unusual weakness, headache, thirst or changes in urination promptly.

Chemotherapy

Nab-paclitaxel

Disrupts cell division by stabilising microtubules
Albumin-bound medicine→Microtubules held stable→Cell division disrupted

This formulation carries paclitaxel with albumin, a protein. It interferes with the structures cells use to divide. Its dosing and allergy precautions differ from ordinary paclitaxel.

Report numbness, tingling, weakness or problems with daily tasks before the next dose. Tell staff about an infusion reaction; new cough or breathlessness needs prompt assessment.

Practical treatment guide

Your treatment and access plan

Atezolizumab 840 mg is given on Days 1 and 15, before nab-paclitaxel 100 mg/m² on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle. Nab-paclitaxel is planned for up to six cycles; extension needs consultant review. Atezolizumab may continue after chemotherapy stops.

STEP 1

Before treatment

Baseline FBC, kidney/liver function, glucose, thyroid function, hepatitis B/C testing and morning cortisol. FBC, kidney/liver tests and glucose before each cycle; check the count results needed for Day 8 and Day 15 decisions.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. A port is not automatically required; insertion and removal are separate procedures with individual instructions.

STEP 3

On the treatment day

Atezolizumab is given first, then nab-paclitaxel; Day 8 has nab-paclitaxel only.

STEP 4

After the visit

No routine take-home infusion pump is required for this regimen. Keep line-care, urgent-contact and next-review instructions.

Visit length

Atezolizumab: 60 minutes initially, then 30 minutes if tolerated; nab-paclitaxel: 30 minutes. Full visits also include preparation, review and observation.

Line care

A PICC or port has its own care plan. Report fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or line damage; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

Treatment changes

A delayed, omitted or changed dose needs an updated plan from the treating team. Do not move doses or restart on your own.

Support for this visit

Separate combination, Day 8 chemotherapy-only and immunotherapy-only support; maintain immune and blood-count monitoring.

Atezolizumab

  • Immune inflammation of lungs, bowel, liver or kidneys
  • Thyroid, adrenal or pituitary problems and diabetes
  • Rare serious heart, nerve or muscle inflammation
  • Infusion reactions and severe skin reactions

Nab-paclitaxel

  • Numbness / tingling or weakness, sometimes lasting
  • Low blood counts, infection and sepsis
  • Hair loss, fatigue and muscle / joint aches
  • Infusion reactions; uncommon serious lung inflammation

Support around treatment

Know the treatment day

Day 8 is nab-paclitaxel only. Confirm which medicines are due at every visit.

Immune symptoms

Report new symptoms promptly, even after treatment ends. Do not assume diarrhoea or breathlessness is an ordinary chemotherapy effect.

Nerves and blood counts

Report numbness before the next dose; fever or unusual bleeding needs urgent advice.

Medicines and vaccines

Discuss steroids, new medicines and vaccinations with the team.

Do not wait for the next appointment

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
