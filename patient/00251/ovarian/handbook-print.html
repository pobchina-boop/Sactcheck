Carboplatin every four weeks for ovarian, fallopian tube or peritoneal cancer

Your treatment and how the medicines work

After surgery, treatment may reduce the risk of the cancer returning. For advanced or recurrent disease, it aims to control cancer and symptoms. Ask which aim applies to you.

Carboplatin · Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. Links strands of DNA together. Cancer cells then have difficulty dividing. The kidneys clear the medicine, so kidney function affects the prescribed dose. Phone urgently for fever, chills or unusual bleeding. During an infusion, tell the nurse immediately about itching, flushing, wheeze or throat tightness.

What the research found

Studies compared carboplatin alone with combinations in people receiving their first treatment and in people whose cancer had returned. Some combinations controlled the cancer longer. Your team weighs this against which treatment you can tolerate.

The studies involve different settings and do not compare 21-day with 28-day administration. Neither provides an individual prognosis. EWOC-1 was stopped early in a selected vulnerable population; age alone does not establish that combination treatment is unsuitable.

Common effects and what to do

What you may notice

Nausea, tiredness, altered taste and reduced appetite may follow treatment. Blood counts can fall between visits even when you feel well.

Serious effects: when to call

Infection or bleeding · Carboplatin

Ring urgently if you have a fever, chills, new bruising, nosebleeds or feel suddenly unwell.

Allergic reaction · Carboplatin

Tell the nurse right away about itching, flushing, throat tightness, wheeze or dizziness during treatment, even if earlier infusions went well.

Kidney function · Carboplatin

Report reduced urine and vomiting. Blood tests help your team calculate each dose.

Nausea and vomiting · Carboplatin

Take the anti-sickness treatment as written. Call if you cannot keep down fluids.

Nerve or hearing changes · Carboplatin

Report new tingling, numbness or ringing ears, particularly after previous platinum therapy.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call the 24-hour oncology line for fever, chills, feeling suddenly unwell, unusual bleeding, persistent vomiting or markedly reduced urine. Tell the nurse immediately about itching, wheeze, throat tightness or faintness during treatment. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Planning your visits

Appointments

One infusion on day 1 every 28 days (four weeks). Bring your written next appointment date. Your team plans the number of cycles and reviews response, blood results and treatment tolerance.

On the day

Carboplatin is given into a vein over about 30 minutes on day 1 of each 28-day cycle. Blood checks and anti-sickness medicine add time to the visit. The team works out your dose from kidney function and a prescribed target exposure.

Between visits

Use the recovery days to take prescribed medicines and note symptoms. Contact the 24-hour number for urgent problems.

Preparing for your oncology discussion

  • Why do I need another kidney test? The kidneys clear carboplatin. The team checks how well they are working to choose an appropriate dose for this cycle.
  • How do I know when the next dose is due? Your booked schedule is every four weeks. A blood test or clinic visit is separate from an infusion. Use the date printed on your treatment plan.
  • Which blood counts does my team look for? The usual minimum is 1 for neutrophils and 100 for platelets, both in billions per litre. A result exactly at either minimum meets that count threshold. Your team may choose a higher minimum for you and reviews the rest of your results and symptoms before giving treatment.
