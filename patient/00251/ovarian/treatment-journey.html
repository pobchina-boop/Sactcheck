Your treatment journey

Carboplatin every four weeks for ovarian, fallopian tube or peritoneal cancer

After surgery, treatment may reduce the risk of the cancer returning. For advanced or recurrent disease, it aims to control cancer and symptoms. Ask which aim applies to you.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · carboplatin every 28 days

One infusion is due this cycle. Confirm the next day-1 appointment is 28 days later unless your team changes it.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat your team checksWhat you can do
Before startingBlood count, kidney and liver tests. A measured kidney filtration test is preferred where feasible because carboplatin dose depends on kidney clearance.Mention previous platinum treatment or allergy, bleeding, infections and medicines that may affect the kidneys.
Early cycles · days 13–15 and day 21Additional blood counts around days 13–15 and on day 21 help identify when your counts reach their lowest point.Write down both appointments. These are blood tests and do not indicate another infusion.
Before each cycleThe team checks blood counts and kidney and liver function, reviews symptoms and signs of platinum allergy, and decides whether to give the next infusion.Call if fever or bleeding develops, even if a recent blood test was satisfactory.
Response reviewSymptoms, examination and scans or tumour markers when appropriate help the team judge benefit.Ask which measures will be used for your cancer and when you will discuss the results.

The team individualises response assessments and extra tests according to symptoms and treatment aim.

At review visits

  • Why do I need another kidney test? The kidneys clear carboplatin. The team checks how well they are working to choose an appropriate dose for this cycle.
  • How do I know when the next dose is due? Your booked schedule is every four weeks. A blood test or clinic visit is separate from an infusion. Use the date printed on your treatment plan.
  • Which blood counts does my team look for? The usual minimum is 1 for neutrophils and 100 for platelets, both in billions per litre. A result exactly at either minimum meets that count threshold. Your team may choose a higher minimum for you and reviews the rest of your results and symptoms before giving treatment.
