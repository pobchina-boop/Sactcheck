1. Treatment and purpose

Carboplatin · 28-day breast schedule · NCCP 00251 v6

Carboplatin is infused on Day 1 of each 28-day cycle. The team selects the dose using the prescribed AUC and kidney function. Treatment continues while helpful and tolerated; there is no fixed breast course length.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Carboplatin · Platinum chemotherapy

Contact the oncology emergency service immediately for fever or shaking chills, sudden illness, bleeding, new breathlessness, chest pain or severe allergy. Report reduced urine, hearing changes and new numbness promptly. Do not wait for the next visit.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss fertility preservation before treatment if relevant. Carboplatin may affect periods and fertility, sometimes permanently. Do not breastfeed during treatment. Discuss reliable contraception and contact the team immediately if pregnancy is possible. The linked Irish SmPC advises effective contraception for women during treatment and at least 6 months afterwards; men should avoid fathering a child during treatment and at least 3 months afterwards. Confirm the actual product/local advice and any longer requirement from other medicines. Discuss low blood counts and sepsis, bleeding, nausea/vomiting, fatigue, kidney, hearing and nerve effects, and infusion allergy. Benefit is uncertain and alternatives depend on your cancer and priorities. Carboplatin for metastatic breast cancer is an off-label use under this Irish NCCP protocol. The treating team must explain this, obtain informed consent and follow local Drugs and Therapeutics Committee approval requirements.
