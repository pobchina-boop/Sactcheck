NCCP 00733 · version 2a

Fortnightly cetuximab + modified FOLFOX-6

Cetuximab on Day 1. Day 1: oxaliplatin, folinic acid and fluorouracil, then a 46-hour fluorouracil pump; planned disconnection around Day 3. A repeating 14-day cycle. The number of cycles depends on benefit, side effects and your treatment plan.

NCCP 00733 · practical support

Your treatment, at home.

Important medicine-specific effects

Cetuximab

  • Rash, dry skin, cracks and sore nails
  • Eye inflammation or corneal injury
  • Low magnesium, calcium or potassium
  • Diarrhoea, dehydration and kidney problems
  • Infusion reaction; rare serious lung or skin effects

Oxaliplatin

  • Cold-triggered tingling / throat discomfort
  • Persistent sensory neuropathy, sometimes lasting after treatment
  • Allergic / infusion reaction
  • Low blood counts and infection
  • Nausea, diarrhoea and fatigue
  • Rare serious lung or blood complications

Folinic acid

  • Enhances fluorouracil activity and toxicity
  • Rare allergic reaction
  • Diarrhoea / mouth soreness in combination

Fluorouracil

  • Diarrhoea, dehydration and mouth ulcers
  • Low blood counts / infection
  • Hand-foot skin soreness
  • Cardiotoxicity including chest pain / ischaemia
  • Severe early toxicity including DPD-related toxicity
  • Pump / central-line complications

Make the plan practical

Skin, nails and minerals

Start the prescribed skin-care plan early, use moisturiser and sun protection, and report painful or infected skin or nails. Keep mineral blood-test appointments during treatment and for two months after cetuximab.

Bowel symptoms and fluids

Follow your written rescue plan. Fever, blood, severe pain, reduced urine or inability to drink needs urgent advice.

Line and pump plan

Confirm port/PICC preparation, pump connection, home care and professional disconnection around Day 3.

Comfort and daily life

Avoid cold drinks and cold exposure as instructed after oxaliplatin; do not suck ice chips during fluorouracil in this regimen. Use prescribed anti-sickness medicines, pace activities and ask for nutritional, emotional or practical support.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
