When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Understand your treatment

How your medicines work

The aim is to control advanced bowel cancer and symptoms. Treatment continues while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable; your team reviews the plan with you.

EGFR-targeted antibody

Cetuximab

Erbitux
Blocks growth messages at the cell surface

Cetuximab attaches to EGFR on the outside of cells and blocks some growth messages. In this bowel-cancer plan, validated extended RAS testing helps identify whether treatment is suitable. Healthy skin also uses EGFR, so skin and nail effects need early care.

EGFR receives a growth signal↓Cetuximab blocks the receptor↓Some growth signals are reduced

What to watch for
Report rash or sore nails early. Tell the nurse immediately about infusion symptoms. Eye pain, new breathing problems or severe diarrhoea need prompt advice. Blood tests check minerals during treatment and for two months afterwards.

Platinum chemotherapy

Oxaliplatin

Eloxatin
Links deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) strands

Oxaliplatin makes links within DNA. These interfere with copying and repair, which can stop a cancer cell dividing. Healthy tissues can also be affected.

Platinum enters cell↓DNA links form↓Copying is disrupted

What to watch for
Cold can trigger tingling or throat discomfort. Tell the team about persistent numbness, difficulty with buttons or walking, or infusion symptoms. New breathing difficulty always needs assessment.

Chemotherapy modulator

Folinic acid

Calcium leucovorin
Strengthens fluorouracil action

Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself.

Folinic acid↓Stronger enzyme binding↓Fluorouracil effect increases

What to watch for
The combined treatment can increase mouth soreness and diarrhoea. Report these promptly. Rare allergic reactions can occur.

Chemotherapy

Fluorouracil

5-FU
Disrupts cell building blocks

Fluorouracil interferes with the production and use of DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. The infusion extends exposure over approximately two days.

Drug enters cell↓DNA / RNA disrupted↓Growth slows

What to watch for
Diarrhoea, mouth sores and skin soreness may become serious. Chest pain during the pump infusion needs emergency assessment. Unexpected severe early toxicity can occur even after dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (a medicine-processing enzyme) (DPD) testing.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Treatment and checks

The team reviews blood tests, symptoms and your prescription before treatment. Premedicines and observation are part of cetuximab visits. Know the contact number and the next planned visit.

Day 2–3: Pump completion

Keep the arranged professional pump-disconnection appointment. Do not adjust, reconnect or remove it yourself. Call early for symptoms; do not wait for the next appointment.

Day 4–14: Recovery and the next review

The team uses symptoms, tests and your priorities to decide what happens next. Bring questions and a symptom record if useful; this is optional.

Your treatment, step by step

Your port and treatment pump

From preparing the line to coming home after disconnection. Your team arranges the appointments and shows you what to do.

1 · Before treatment

Choose and prepare the line

The team explains whether a port or peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) suits your treatment. You receive instructions for the insertion appointment.

2 · Insertion and recovery

A separate appointment may be needed

For a port, numbing medicine is used and a small device is placed under the skin. Ask about sedation, transport and wound care.

3 · Day 1

The nurse connects your pump

After the day-ward medicines, the nurse checks and connects the fluorouracil pump. Ask for a demonstration before leaving.

4 · At home

Treatment continues for about 46 hours

Carry the pump as shown. Keep the dressing dry and protect the line from pulling, heat and damage.

5 · As arranged

Return for disconnection

A trained professional checks the infusion, disconnects the pump and cares for the line. The appointment is based on connection time.

6 · Between cycles

The access device usually stays

A port needle can be removed while the port stays under the skin. A PICC remains in the arm with ongoing dressing and line care.

Port or PICC: what is the difference?

A port is a small chamber beneath the skin, usually on the chest, attached to a tube ending in a large vein. A special needle goes through the skin into the port when it is used. A PICC enters a vein in the upper arm; a short section remains outside under a dressing. Both can deliver a home infusion. Your team helps choose the device and teaches its care.

Before insertion and during recovery

Tell the team about allergies, blood thinners, infections and any other cancer medicines. Follow the instructions about eating, drinking and transport; do not stop medicines yourself. Port placement uses local anaesthetic, sometimes with sedation or general anaesthetic. The team explains the procedure and risks, including infection, bleeding, clots and insertion complications. Ask how to manage discomfort, protect the wound and resume activity. Report increasing pain, redness, swelling, discharge or fever.

Connection: what the nurse does

The nurse checks the line and treatment, secures the connection and dressing, and starts the pump. Before leaving, you should have the disconnection location and time, contact numbers including out of hours, and the written spill instructions. Ask to be shown what normal pump progress looks like. You do not need to calculate a rate.

Carrying it, sleeping and washing

Use the supplied carrier and the position recommended for your pump. Keep tubing free from pulling and kinks. Ask how to place the pump safely for sleep and how to protect the line and dressing when washing. Do not immerse it or swim. Avoid direct heat and cold. An elastomeric pump has a balloon that shrinks slowly and may have no alarm; an electronic pump has different instructions. Follow the instructions for the device you actually receive.

Disconnection is not port removal

Keep the appointment even if the pump looks empty, unless the team changes the plan. Staff check the pump and line and arrange safe disposal. After flushing and other line care as required, the port needle may be removed and a small dressing applied. The implanted port stays in place until a separate removal procedure is arranged. A PICC remains in place and needs scheduled dressing and flushing care.

Leakage, disconnection or unexpected pump progress

Call the team immediately if the pump leaks, is damaged, finishes unexpectedly early, appears not to run, or the line disconnects. Follow the emergency or spill steps you have been specifically taught, including use of a clamp if instructed. Do not reconnect, change settings or disconnect the pump yourself. Keep other people away from leaked medicine. If medicine touches skin, rinse it off and wash with soap and water, then seek advice.

Pain, swelling or feeling unwell

Contact oncology immediately for pain or swelling around the line, a wet/loose dressing, redness, fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell. A painful swollen arm or neck needs urgent advice. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty or collapse: call 112 / 999. Do not wait for pump disconnection or the next clinic visit.

The drawings show general steps, not instructions for inserting or accessing a line. Your hospital confirms its own arrangements and the care instructions for your device.

Practical treatment guide

Visits, access and care between treatments

Cetuximab and modified FOLFOX6 start on Day 1, followed by one 46-hour fluorouracil infusion with planned disconnection around Day 3. Repeat every 14 days while beneficial and tolerable. No weekly Day 8 cetuximab is scheduled.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the exact regimen, tests, medicines, emergency number and treatment goal. Discuss which central line is suitable and arrange pump training.

STEP 2

At the treatment unit

Fortnightly cetuximab infusion time is set from the dose and safe infusion rate; the first dose may take longer. Oxaliplatin and folinic acid are usually infused over about two hours, before fluorouracil. Blood review, preparation, premedicines and observation add time; infusion time is not the total appointment length.

STEP 3

After the infusion

The fluorouracil pump runs for about 46 hours. Confirm where and when it will be disconnected, usually around Day 3; do not disconnect it yourself unless specifically trained.

STEP 4

Between doses

Use the written symptom and support plan; do not wait for the next appointment if urgent symptoms appear.

STEP 5

The next decision

Confirm the next visit after any delay or dose change. Treatment continues only while the oncology team judges benefit and tolerability acceptable. Follow-up mineral checks continue after the final EGFR-antibody dose where applicable.

Transport and daily life

Plan transport if antihistamines, symptoms or other medicines affect driving. Ask for practical, work, nutrition or emotional support.

If treatment changes

Ask which component has been held and which visits remain. A treatment delay does not automatically cancel blood tests, line care or symptom follow-up.

Observation at every antibody visit

Premedicines and monitoring add time to every cetuximab visit. Your team will confirm the observation period before you leave; do not assume that later visits can omit observation.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Skin, nails and minerals

Start the prescribed skin-care plan early, use moisturiser and sun protection, and report painful or infected skin or nails. Keep mineral blood-test appointments during treatment and for two months after cetuximab.

Bowel symptoms and fluids

Follow your written rescue plan. Fever, blood, severe pain, reduced urine or inability to drink needs urgent advice.

Line and pump plan

Confirm port/PICC preparation, pump connection, home care and professional disconnection around Day 3.

Comfort and daily life

Avoid cold drinks and cold exposure as instructed after oxaliplatin; do not suck ice chips during fluorouracil in this regimen. Use prescribed anti-sickness medicines, pace activities and ask for nutritional, emotional or practical support.

The whole treatment plan

Ask how and when the team will assess benefit, which blood tests continue during a pause, and what happens after the final dose. Mineral checks continue monthly for two months after the last antibody treatment.

Nerves and cold exposure

Tell the team about numbness, tingling, cold-triggered throat symptoms or difficulty with buttons or walking before the next dose. Avoid cold exposure as instructed; do not use ice chips during fluorouracil in this oxaliplatin regimen. Breathing difficulty always needs urgent assessment.

Recovery and the next dose

A delay or reduction is individual. Tell the team about ongoing side effects and previous dose changes; do not assume a calendar date or a stamped card means that treatment can restart.

Previous infusion reactions

Tell the team about any previous severe antibody reaction, even if you feel well now. Premedication does not remove all reaction risk. Report breathing problems, swelling, faintness or rapidly worsening symptoms immediately.

Medicines that can change fluorouracil safety

Show the team every prescribed medicine, over-the-counter treatment and supplement before treatment. Tell them specifically about recent antiviral treatment such as brivudine or sorivudine. The pharmacist must check combinations and timing before fluorouracil. Do not stop or restart treatment yourself.

Nerve symptoms after a dose is held

Tell the team about the worst numbness, tingling or functional difficulty you have had, even if it has improved. Recovery does not automatically make restarting oxaliplatin safe. The team reviews the previous severity and dose before deciding whether it can be used again.

Treatment reference · 05

Visits and treatment sequence

Know which steps happen in the clinic and at home.

The exact delivery plan

Cetuximab on Day 1. Day 1: oxaliplatin, folinic acid and fluorouracil, then a 46-hour fluorouracil pump; planned disconnection around Day 3. A repeating 14-day cycle. The number of cycles depends on benefit, side effects and your treatment plan.

Radiotherapy relationship

This NCCP regimen does not contain a routine radiotherapy course. If radiation is recommended separately, ask whether it is before, during or after these medicines.

Treatment reference · 06

When treatment changes or ends

Follow-up continues.

Symptoms and monitoring

Continue the cetuximab skin plan and mineral checks for two months after the last dose. Persistent numbness after oxaliplatin needs follow-up. Ask which medicines to continue, when to seek help and how response will be assessed.

Next decisions

The team reviews benefit, side effects and your priorities. Supportive and palliative care can help alongside active treatment. Ask for a clear next-step and contact plan.

Treatment reference · 07

Pauses, recovery and follow-up

Your team may pause or change one or more medicines. Do not restart or change doses yourself.

Decisions before restarting

The team checks the severity and recovery of side effects, previous episodes and the doses you actually received. A standard table dose does not automatically override a prior individual reduction.

Keep testing appointments

Blood tests may continue during a treatment pause. Keep monthly electrolyte, magnesium and calcium checks for two months after the last cetuximab dose. Scans and tumour-marker timing follow the consultant plan; these documents do not impose a universal scan interval.

The interval before chemotherapy

Cetuximab is given before chemotherapy. Product information requires at least one hour between the end of cetuximab and the start of chemotherapy. Your appointment includes preparation, premedication and observation as well as time connected to a drip.

Check every medicine and supplement

Show the team all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines before treatment and before starting anything new. Certain antiviral medicines, including brivudine and sorivudine, must not be combined with fluorouracil. Tell the team if you take warfarin or phenytoin; extra monitoring may be needed. Do not stop or change medicines yourself.

OPUS: exploratory extended RAS analysis

Studies can help explain why a treatment is offered. The schedule, previous medicines and tumour tests affect how well the evidence fits you.

Adding cetuximab to FOLFOX4 improved tumour shrinkage in a small selected trial group. That analysis did not establish a clear survival advantage. Tumour shrinkage is not the same as cure. The trial used weekly cetuximab and FOLFOX4, so its results are indirect for this fortnightly modified FOLFOX6 plan.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

How is my treatment chosen?

This combination is used for RAS wild-type bowel cancer that has spread. Your team explains the aim, alternatives and how benefit will be reviewed.

Do I need to enter dates to understand the cycle?

No. The graph explains a general pattern. Your hospital arranges appointments; the guide and its safety information are available without entering anything.

Will I have a pump at home?

Confirm port/PICC preparation, pump connection, home care and professional disconnection around Day 3.

What happens if a dose is delayed?

The team checks the cause and may delay, reduce, change or stop one medicine. Ask which component changes and when you should next attend; do not restart yourself.

How will I know whether it is helping?

Scans, symptoms and clinical review assess benefit. A rash, side effect or good day alone cannot show whether the cancer is responding.

What about radiotherapy?

This NCCP regimen does not contain a routine radiotherapy course. If radiation is recommended separately, ask whether it is before, during or after these medicines.

What about fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding?

Discuss fertility preservation and reproductive plans before treatment. Ask for the contraception and breastfeeding instructions for every medicine; the whole regimen may require longer precautions than one medicine alone.

Why is RAS testing mentioned?

RAS testing selects cetuximab treatment in colorectal cancer. It is not the selection test for the separate head-and-neck radiotherapy indication. Confirm the cancer-specific plan with your team.

If one medicine is held, do the other medicines continue?

Only the treating team can decide. Folinic acid is linked to the fluorouracil bolus. A held medicine does not automatically cancel all clinic visits, blood tests, pump care or skin-care treatment. Ask for the revised written plan.
