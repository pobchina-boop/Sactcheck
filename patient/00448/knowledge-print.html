Understand your treatment

How your treatment works

The aim is to control advanced bowel cancer and symptoms. Treatment continues while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable; your team reviews the plan with you.

EGFR-targeted antibody

Panitumumab

Vectibix
Blocks growth messages at the cell surface

Panitumumab attaches to EGFR, a receptor on the outside of cells. This can interrupt signals that help some bowel cancers grow. A tumour RAS test helps identify whether this approach is suitable. EGFR also supports healthy skin, which explains why skin and nail changes need attention.

EGFR receives a growth signal↓Panitumumab blocks the receptor↓Some growth signals are reduced

What to watch for
Report rash, painful cracks or nail changes early. Eye pain or blurred vision, new breathing symptoms, severe diarrhoea, or blistering skin need prompt assessment. Blood tests check minerals such as magnesium.

Topoisomerase I chemotherapy

Irinotecan

Campto
Interrupts deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) copying

The body converts irinotecan into SN-38. This traps an enzyme called topoisomerase I while DNA is being copied, causing damage that can stop cancer cells dividing. Normal bowel and blood-forming cells can also be affected.

Irinotecan becomes SN-38↓DNA-repair enzyme is trapped↓Dividing cells are damaged

What to watch for
Tell the nurse immediately about cramps, sweating or diarrhoea during the infusion. Diarrhoea starting later needs the separate written treatment and contact plan.

Chemotherapy modulator

Folinic acid

Calcium leucovorin
Strengthens fluorouracil action

Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself.

Folinic acid↓Stronger enzyme binding↓Fluorouracil effect increases

What to watch for
The combined treatment can increase mouth soreness and diarrhoea. Report these promptly. Rare allergic reactions can occur.

Chemotherapy

Fluorouracil

5-FU
Disrupts cell building blocks

Fluorouracil interferes with the production and use of DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. The infusion extends exposure over approximately two days.

Drug enters cell↓DNA / RNA disrupted↓Growth slows

What to watch for
Diarrhoea, mouth sores and skin soreness may become serious. Chest pain during the pump infusion needs emergency assessment. Unexpected severe early toxicity can occur even after dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (a medicine-processing enzyme) (DPD) testing.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Infusion and pump connection

The team reviews symptoms, blood tests including minerals and your treatment plan. Panitumumab and the day-ward chemotherapy are followed by connection of the fluorouracil pump. Before leaving, confirm the pump-care instructions, disconnection appointment and contact number.

Day 2–3: The pump is delivering treatment

The pump runs for approximately 46 hours from connection. Keep it and the line secure and dry as taught. The team confirms the actual disconnection appointment. Call for a leaking, disconnected or unexpectedly stopped pump. Do not reconnect, alter settings or disconnect it yourself unless specifically trained and instructed.

Day 4–7: After disconnection

Delayed diarrhoea can begin after the first 24 hours and may continue after the pump is removed. Use the written plan and contact the team early. Mouth soreness, nausea and tiredness can overlap. Report poor intake, persistent diarrhoea or mouth pain early. Chest pain or major breathing difficulty requires emergency help.

Day 8–14: Between visits and the next review

Blood counts can fall between visits. Tell the team about bowel symptoms, mouth pain and how you are managing daily activities. Treatment proceeds only after review. Bring the symptom passport and check the next appointment. Day 1 usually repeats 14 days after this cycle started, only if confirmed.

Your treatment, step by step

Your port and treatment pump

From preparing the line to coming home after disconnection. Your team arranges the appointments and shows you what to do.

1 · Before treatment

Choose and prepare the line

The team explains whether a port or peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) suits your treatment. You receive instructions for the insertion appointment.

2 · Insertion and recovery

A separate appointment may be needed

For a port, numbing medicine is used and a small device is placed under the skin. Ask about sedation, transport and wound care.

3 · Day 1

The nurse connects your pump

After the day-ward medicines, the nurse checks and connects the fluorouracil pump. Ask for a demonstration before leaving.

4 · At home

Treatment continues for about 46 hours

Carry the pump as shown. Keep the dressing dry and protect the line from pulling, heat and damage.

5 · As arranged

Return for disconnection

A trained professional checks the infusion, disconnects the pump and cares for the line. The appointment is based on connection time.

6 · Between cycles

The access device usually stays

A port needle can be removed while the port stays under the skin. A PICC remains in the arm with ongoing dressing and line care.

Port or PICC: what is the difference?

A port is a small chamber beneath the skin, usually on the chest, attached to a tube ending in a large vein. A special needle goes through the skin into the port when it is used. A PICC enters a vein in the upper arm; a short section remains outside under a dressing. Both can deliver a home infusion. Your team helps choose the device and teaches its care.

Before insertion and during recovery

Tell the team about allergies, blood thinners, infections and any other cancer medicines. Follow the instructions about eating, drinking and transport; do not stop medicines yourself. Port placement uses local anaesthetic, sometimes with sedation or general anaesthetic. The team explains the procedure and risks, including infection, bleeding, clots and insertion complications. Ask how to manage discomfort, protect the wound and resume activity. Report increasing pain, redness, swelling, discharge or fever.

Connection: what the nurse does

The nurse checks the line and treatment, secures the connection and dressing, and starts the pump. Before leaving, you should have the disconnection location and time, contact numbers including out of hours, and the written spill instructions. Ask to be shown what normal pump progress looks like. You do not need to calculate a rate.

Carrying it, sleeping and washing

Use the supplied carrier and the position recommended for your pump. Keep tubing free from pulling and kinks. Ask how to place the pump safely for sleep and how to protect the line and dressing when washing. Do not immerse it or swim. Avoid direct heat and cold. An elastomeric pump has a balloon that shrinks slowly and may have no alarm; an electronic pump has different instructions. Follow the instructions for the device you actually receive.

Disconnection is not port removal

Keep the appointment even if the pump looks empty, unless the team changes the plan. Staff check the pump and line and arrange safe disposal. After flushing and other line care as required, the port needle may be removed and a small dressing applied. The implanted port stays in place until a separate removal procedure is arranged. A PICC remains in place and needs scheduled dressing and flushing care.

Leakage, disconnection or unexpected pump progress

Call the team immediately if the pump leaks, is damaged, finishes unexpectedly early, appears not to run, or the line disconnects. Follow the emergency or spill steps you have been specifically taught, including use of a clamp if instructed. Do not reconnect, change settings or disconnect the pump yourself. Keep other people away from leaked medicine. If medicine touches skin, rinse it off and wash with soap and water, then seek advice.

Pain, swelling or feeling unwell

Contact oncology immediately for pain or swelling around the line, a wet/loose dressing, redness, fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell. A painful swollen arm or neck needs urgent advice. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty or collapse: call 112 / 999. Do not wait for pump disconnection or the next clinic visit.

The drawings show general steps, not instructions for inserting or accessing a line. Your hospital confirms its own arrangements and the care instructions for your device.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Treatment phases and appointments

A 14-day cycle: panitumumab, irinotecan, folinic acid and fluorouracil start on Day 1, followed by a fluorouracil pump for about 46 hours. Arrange disconnection around Day 3. Continue according to benefit and tolerance.

STEP 2

Checks after treatment

Monthly electrolytes, magnesium and calcium for two months after the last panitumumab dose. Disease assessment follows the consultant plan. Escalate persistent diarrhoea, fever or dehydration between visits. Check INR if taking warfarin, as clinically indicated.

STEP 3

Access and practical support

Central venous access, a 46-hour fluorouracil pump, planned disconnect and emergency contacts. Provide written delayed-diarrhoea instructions separately from atropine for acute cholinergic symptoms.

STEP 4

Paper copies, wallet QR and contacts

The example telephone numbers on the wallet card are placeholders. Your team must replace them with working daytime and out-of-hours numbers before patient use. Keep the card with you; the QR opens the regimen information, not your confidential medical record.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Panitumumab + FOLFIRI

No routine panitumumab premedication unless previous hypersensitivity/local policy indicates. Written delayed-diarrhoea plan and urgent fever/dehydration advice; do not confuse with acute cholinergic management. Skin care and electrolytes; monthly Mg/Ca/electrolytes for two months after last panitumumab.

Act at the first liquid stool

Use the written diarrhoea rescue prescription supplied by your oncology team at the first liquid stool and drink the advised electrolyte-containing fluids. Contact the team early; if no plan was supplied, call immediately. Severe pain, fever, blood, inability to drink or reduced urine needs urgent assessment. Do not treat early infusion cramps or sweating as the same problem.

Recovery and the next dose

A delay or reduction is individual. Tell the team about ongoing side effects and previous dose changes; do not assume a calendar date or a stamped card means that treatment can restart.

Treatment reference · 01

Your treatment aim and review plan

Understand why this option fits your cancer.

The setting matters

Panitumumab + FOLFIRI is used for bowel cancer that has spread and has a confirmed wild-type RAS result. This NCCP regimen covers first-line treatment and selected second-line treatment after a fluoropyrimidine regimen without irinotecan. Your team explains the aim, alternatives and how benefit will be reviewed.

What the tumour tests mean

RAS wild type is required for panitumumab. Your team also considers the cancer location, other molecular results, previous treatment and whether surgery or other local treatment could be useful. A positive eligibility result is not a promise of benefit.

Treatment reference · 02

Before you start and before each cycle

The team checks that the plan remains suitable.

Checks and medicines

Review blood counts, kidney/liver function and minerals, current symptoms, allergies and all medicines. DPD testing is needed before fluorouracil unless already documented. Tell the team about severe reactions to previous chemotherapy.

Skin, eyes and breathing

Tell the team about an existing rash, skin infection, dry or painful eyes, contact lenses, or previous lung inflammation. Report new eye pain, light sensitivity or visual change promptly.

Treatment reference · 03

Living with treatment

Build the plan around what matters to you.

Skin and nails

Ask for your skin-care plan before the first infusion. Use the recommended moisturiser and sun protection; your team may prescribe a cream or an antibiotic. Do not use ordinary acne treatments without advice. Avoid friction on sore skin and tell the team about painful nail folds.

Food, activity and support

Keep fluids and nourishing food within reach. Pace activity around energy levels and ask for dietetic, emotional or practical support. If symptoms prevent eating, drinking or usual tasks, contact the team.

Pregnancy and fertility

Discuss pregnancy plans and fertility before treatment. Panitumumab requires contraception during treatment and for two months afterwards, and breastfeeding should be avoided for that period. When chemotherapy is also included, its precautions may last longer; ask for the instructions for the whole regimen.

Treatment reference · 04

When treatment changes or finishes

Follow-up continues after the last infusion.

Monitoring and recovery

Continue your skin plan and planned magnesium, calcium and potassium checks for about eight weeks after panitumumab. New symptoms still need review. Ask when to stop prescribed supportive medicines.

Treatment choices

Your team may pause, reduce, change or stop one or more medicines. Ask which medicine changes, why, and what the next review will assess. Supportive and palliative care can help alongside active treatment.

Treatment reference · 05

Recognise changes early

You do not need to identify the responsible medicine before asking for help.

When to call

Report new breathing difficulty, eye pain or blurred vision, blistering or infected skin, severe diarrhoea or dehydration promptly. Also report new chest pain, fever or a line/pump problem immediately.

Track impact, not just a score

Describe when symptoms began, whether they are worsening, and whether they affect food, fluids, sleep, walking or daily tasks. Your team assigns the clinical grade. Use the passport if it helps; it is not required to access this information.

Two bowel-symptom patterns

Cramps, sweating or early diarrhoea during irinotecan need immediate nursing attention. Delayed diarrhoea begins more than 24 hours later and can occur before the next cycle. Follow your written treatment and escalation plan; fever, blood, severe pain or dehydration requires urgent assessment.

Treatment reference · 06

Visits, access and coordination

Know what happens in the clinic and what happens at home.

The delivery plan

Day 1: panitumumab, irinotecan, folinic acid and fluorouracil, followed by a fluorouracil pump for about 46 hours; disconnection is arranged around Day 3. A cycle lasts 14 days. Treatment continues while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable.

Radiotherapy or other treatment

This regimen has no routine radiotherapy component. If another treatment is proposed, ask whether it happens before, during or after this course and who will coordinate the appointments.

Treatment reference · 07

Testing continues after panitumumab

Some changes can outlast the infusion course.

Before and during treatment

Confirm the DPD result before fluorouracil unless already tested. Blood count, kidney and liver tests are required before each cycle. Skin, eyes and electrolytes including magnesium and calcium need monitoring during panitumumab treatment.

For two months after the last dose

Arrange monthly electrolytes, magnesium and calcium for two months after the final panitumumab dose. Report cramps, weakness, palpitations or other new symptoms; do not wait for a routine test if unwell.

Conditional heart and anticoagulant checks

A baseline ECG is needed if cardiac function is compromised; later heart investigations depend on symptoms. If taking warfarin, INR is checked as clinically indicated. Confirm who arranges and reviews your INR tests.

Treatment reference · 08

Pauses, recovery and follow-up

Your team may pause or change one or more medicines. Do not restart or change doses yourself.

Decisions before restarting

The team checks the severity and recovery of side effects, previous episodes and the doses you actually received. A standard table dose does not automatically override a prior individual reduction.

Keep testing appointments

Blood tests may continue during a treatment pause. Keep monthly electrolyte, magnesium and calcium checks for two months after the last panitumumab dose. Scans and tumour-marker timing follow the consultant plan; these documents do not impose a universal scan interval.

Check every medicine and supplement

Show the team all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines before treatment and before starting anything new. Certain antiviral medicines, including brivudine and sorivudine, must not be combined with fluorouracil. Tell the team if you take warfarin or phenytoin; extra monitoring may be needed. Do not stop or change medicines yourself.

Irinotecan medicine interactions

Ask the oncology pharmacist before taking grapefruit, herbal products or any new medicine. Some medicines change irinotecan exposure. Prochlorperazine should not be taken on the same day as irinotecan; confirm your alternative prescribed nausea plan with the team. Tell the team about known Gilbert syndrome before the first treatment.

Why this treatment is offered

For selected people whose bowel cancer had already received treatment, adding panitumumab improved tumour shrinkage and delayed progression. A longer overall survival was not established in this analysis.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Why do I need a RAS test?

RAS is part of the growth-signalling pathway inside cancer cells. If it is changed by a mutation, blocking EGFR is unlikely to help. Your team needs a validated wild-type RAS result before panitumumab. Other tumour tests and the treatment aim also affect the choice.

Is this the same as checkpoint immunotherapy?

Panitumumab is a targeted antibody against EGFR. It works differently from checkpoint treatments used in some other cancer regimens. Ask your team which treatment class and side effects apply to you.

Does a rash mean the treatment is working?

A rash is a recognised drug effect, but it cannot tell you whether your cancer is responding. Report it early so it can be treated. Scans, symptoms and clinical review guide the response assessment.

Will I need radiotherapy as part of this cycle?

These panitumumab regimens do not contain a scheduled radiotherapy course. If radiotherapy is recommended separately, your oncology teams will explain its purpose and timing. Do not assume it occurs on infusion or pump days.

What about pregnancy, breastfeeding or fertility?

Discuss pregnancy plans and fertility before treatment. Panitumumab requires contraception during treatment and for two months afterwards, and breastfeeding should be avoided for that period. When chemotherapy is also included, its precautions may last longer; ask for the instructions for the whole regimen.

Does having more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably show whether the cancer is responding. Report them so they can be treated. Your team assesses benefit using the tests and review plan appropriate to your cancer.

Why might a dose be delayed or reduced?

Your team may allow recovery, investigate a symptom or reduce the chance of lasting harm. Ask which drug changes, why, and when review is planned. A delay does not itself mean treatment has failed.

What should I say when calling?

Give the regimen name, last treatment date, symptoms and when they started. Explain whether you can eat, drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Give your temperature if available, but do not delay calling to collect details.

Which checks continue when treatment changes or finishes?

Monthly electrolytes, magnesium and calcium for two months after the last panitumumab dose. Disease assessment follows the consultant plan. Escalate persistent diarrhoea, fever or dehydration between visits. Check INR if taking warfarin, as clinically indicated.
