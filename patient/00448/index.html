Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

More about my medicines

Panitumumab

Panitumumab attaches to EGFR, a receptor on the outside of cells. This can interrupt signals that help some bowel cancers grow. A tumour RAS test helps identify whether this approach is suitable. EGFR also supports healthy skin, which explains why skin and nail changes need attention.

Irinotecan

The body converts irinotecan into SN-38. This traps an enzyme called topoisomerase I while DNA is being copied, causing damage that can stop cancer cells dividing. Normal bowel and blood-forming cells can also be affected.

Folinic acid

Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself.

Fluorouracil

Fluorouracil interferes with the production and use of DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. The infusion extends exposure over approximately two days.

Getting ready for treatment visits

Consent discussion aid

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Infusion and pump connection

The team reviews symptoms, blood tests including minerals and your treatment plan. Panitumumab and the day-ward chemotherapy are followed by connection of the fluorouracil pump.

What to do: Before leaving, confirm the pump-care instructions, disconnection appointment and contact number.

Day 2–3: The pump is delivering treatment

The pump runs for approximately 46 hours from connection. Keep it and the line secure and dry as taught. The team confirms the actual disconnection appointment.

What to do: Call for a leaking, disconnected or unexpectedly stopped pump. Do not reconnect, alter settings or disconnect it yourself unless specifically trained and instructed.

Day 4–7: After disconnection

Delayed diarrhoea can begin after the first 24 hours and may continue after the pump is removed. Use the written plan and contact the team early. Mouth soreness, nausea and tiredness can overlap.

What to do: Report poor intake, persistent diarrhoea or mouth pain early. Chest pain or major breathing difficulty requires emergency help.

Day 8–14: Between visits and the next review

Blood counts can fall between visits. Tell the team about bowel symptoms, mouth pain and how you are managing daily activities. Treatment proceeds only after review.

What to do: Bring the symptom passport and check the next appointment. Day 1 usually repeats 14 days after this cycle started, only if confirmed.

Support at home

Panitumumab + FOLFIRI

No routine panitumumab premedication unless previous hypersensitivity/local policy indicates. Written delayed-diarrhoea plan and urgent fever/dehydration advice; do not confuse with acute cholinergic management. Skin care and electrolytes; monthly Mg/Ca/electrolytes for two months after last panitumumab.

Act at the first liquid stool

Use the written diarrhoea rescue prescription supplied by your oncology team at the first liquid stool and drink the advised electrolyte-containing fluids. Contact the team early; if no plan was supplied, call immediately. Severe pain, fever, blood, inability to drink or reduced urine needs urgent assessment. Do not treat early infusion cramps or sweating as the same problem.

Recovery and the next dose

A delay or reduction is individual. Tell the team about ongoing side effects and previous dose changes; do not assume a calendar date or a stamped card means that treatment can restart.
