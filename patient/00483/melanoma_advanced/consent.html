1. Treatment and purpose

Nivolumab · every 2 weeks · advanced melanoma · NCCP 00483 v13a

Nivolumab is given as 240 mg by intravenous (IV) infusion over 30 minutes OR 600 mg by subcutaneous injection over 3–5 minutes, on Day 1 every 2 weeks. Allow extra time for assessment, preparation and observation. The team selects one formulation. A subcutaneous injection is given into the thigh or abdomen, alternating sites and avoiding damaged skin, scars or moles. IV and subcutaneous doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Treatment continues until progression or unacceptable toxicity, with individual benefit and safety review.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Nivolumab · PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy

Carry your immunotherapy alert card and report new symptoms early, including after treatment ends.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss the exact diagnosis, treatment intent, expected benefits and uncertainty, suitable alternatives, the regimen-specific risks and monitoring, fertility and pregnancy precautions, and practical burdens. Confirm the treatment phase and route. This guide supports the conversation and does not replace individual consent.
