Nivolumab every 2 weeks for head and neck cancer

Nivolumab aims to control head and neck cancer that has returned or spread, relieve symptoms and help some people live longer. Benefit and side effects are reviewed for you.

Recurrent or metastatic squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck after platinum treatment; the cancer cannot be treated with curative local therapy and has progressed or returned within six months of the last platinum dose. · NCCP 00483 v13a · Document revised 9 November 2026

My overall treatment

Nivolumab alone every 2 weeks; duration individually reviewed.

14day view

Treatment timing in this window

Nivolumab treatmentDay 1
  1. Nivolumab monotherapyNivolumab is given as 240 mg through a vein over 30 minutes OR 600 mg as an injection under the skin over 3–5 minutes, on Day 1 every 2 weeks. Your team selects one formulation; these doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Allow extra time for safety checks, preparation and observation. Treatment continues until the cancer progresses or side effects become unacceptable, with individual benefit and safety review. There is no fixed number of cycles in this head and neck treatment course.

My tests and checks

WhenWhat is checkedTiming / reason
BeforeBaseline safety and preparationNCCPFull blood count, kidney and liver tests, glucose and thyroid tests; review autoimmune disease, transplants, previous immune side effects and all medicines.
DuringBloods, symptoms and supportNCCPFull blood count, kidney and liver tests and glucose before every 2-week cycle. Thyroid tests once a month and when indicated; cortisol when clinically indicated.
After / responseFollow-up and testsNCCP / individual planKeep reporting new symptoms after treatment ends. Immune side effects require monitoring for at least five months after the last dose. Steroid tapers, hormone replacement or specialist review may continue longer. Scans and examinations follow your individual response and follow-up plan.

Between treatments

  • Immune side effects may start between visits or after the final dose. Report changes promptly.
  • A longer gap between treatments does not remove scheduled tests or the need to report symptoms.

Bring your medicine list, test dates, treatment calendar and actual urgent contacts.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
