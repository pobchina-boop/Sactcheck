Nivolumab · every 2 weeks · adjuvant melanoma

Nivolumab helps immune cells act against melanoma. After complete surgery, its aim is to lower the risk of recurrence; this does not guarantee that melanoma will not return.

Completely resected melanoma with lymph-node involvement or metastatic disease; the team confirms the stage III or resected stage IV indication. Uveal melanoma is excluded from this adjuvant pathway. · NCCP 00483 v13a · Document revised 6 November 2026

My overall treatment

Every 2 weeks; up to 12 months after surgery.

14day view

Treatment timing in this window

Nivolumab treatmentDay 1
  1. Adjuvant treatment after surgeryNivolumab is given as 240 mg by intravenous (IV) infusion over 30 minutes OR 600 mg by subcutaneous injection over 3–5 minutes, on Day 1 every 2 weeks. Allow extra time for assessment, preparation and observation. The team selects one formulation. A subcutaneous injection is given into the thigh or abdomen, alternating sites and avoiding damaged skin, scars or moles. IV and subcutaneous doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Adjuvant treatment continues for up to 12 months, subject to safety review.

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Immunotherapy can cause inflammation in healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new diarrhoea, blood in stools, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell any clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
