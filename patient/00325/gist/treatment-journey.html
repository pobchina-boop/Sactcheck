Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle.

Detect infection, anaemia and bleeding risk.

Kidney and liver tests

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle. Repeat sooner for symptoms or abnormal results.

Detect organ effects and guide treatment review.

Thyroid function

WhenAt baseline and every 12 weeks under this National Cancer Control Programme source; sooner if symptoms suggest a thyroid problem.

Identify underactive or overactive thyroid function, which may need treatment.

Blood pressure

WhenAt baseline and each attendance; additional home or clinic checks as prescribed.

Detect high blood pressure even before symptoms occur.

Heart function and rhythm

If part of your plan
WhenReview cardiac history and whether electrocardiogram (heart tracing), echocardiogram (heart ultrasound) or multigated acquisition scan (heart pumping scan) are indicated; further assessment follows risk, symptoms or abnormal findings. Electrolytes and medicines that affect the QT electrical recovery interval are reviewed when relevant.

Identify heart weakness or electrical-rhythm risk.

Urine protein

WhenProduct information supports baseline urine testing and monitoring for protein loss during treatment; the team specifies repeat timing.

Detect protein leakage and kidney complications.

Blood glucose in people with diabetes

If part of your plan
WhenCheck regularly if diabetes is present and promptly with symptoms suggesting low glucose.

Detect low glucose and review diabetes medicines.

Mouth, dental health, skin and wound review

WhenReview symptoms throughout treatment; consider dental assessment before treatment and coordinate surgery or invasive dental care with oncology.

Identify hand–foot effects, mouth problems or healing concerns early.

Tumour response review

If part of your plan
WhenYour gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) specialist sets imaging and clinical-review timing. This advanced-disease plan assesses tumour control, symptoms and whether treatment remains useful.

Assess benefit separately from treatment-safety tests.

The whole treatment journey

Sunitinib for gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST): 28 days on, 14 days off: treatment and checks

Unresectable or metastatic gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) after imatinib resistance or intolerance

National Cancer Control Programme 00325: sunitinib 50 mg by mouth once daily on days 1–28, followed by 14 days without sunitinib. Repeat the 42-day cycle only as confirmed by your team. Dose reductions or additional holds may be needed; symptoms and monitoring continue during the planned break.

What each check is for

1gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) treatment reviewSunitinib days 1–28; break days 29–42

Sunitinib aims to slow gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) growth and help symptoms after imatinib has stopped working or cannot be tolerated. The team reviews previous treatment, tumour biology, your health and priorities before choosing the schedule.

Ask / act: Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Chest pain, new breathlessness, fainting, severe headache, confusion, significant bleeding, severe abdominal pain or blistering skin requires urgent assessment.
