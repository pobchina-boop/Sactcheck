Your practical treatment plan

Know the next step.

Taking treatment

Take palbociclib for 21 days, then have 7 days without palbociclib. The aromatase-inhibitor tablet continues every day, or fulvestrant injections follow their separate schedule. The week off applies to palbociclib, not automatically to the hormone treatment.

Check whether the pharmacy supplied capsules or tablets: capsules are taken with food; tablets can be taken with or without food. Swallow whole at a consistent time. Avoid grapefruit. If you miss or vomit a dose, do not take an extra dose; take the next scheduled dose. Do not restart after a clinical hold until the treating team confirms.

Checks and follow-up

Before the first dose: FBC, kidney and liver bloods; review other medicines, pregnancy possibility, receptor results, menopausal status and the exact endocrine partner. Confirm ovarian suppression when relevant. FBC on Day 1 and Day 15 of cycles 1–2, then Day 1 from cycle 3. Kidney/liver bloods before each cycle. If counts are low, extra blood tests and a pause may be needed. Only after no worse than grade 1/2 neutropenia in the first 6 cycles may FBC frequency reduce to every 3 months before a cycle, with additional tests when clinically indicated.

Support between reviews

Nausea support

Palbociclib is classified as oral minimal–low emetic risk. Routine premedication is not usually needed; discuss rescue medication if nausea occurs. A generic intensive chemotherapy antiemetic schedule is not automatically added.

Low counts and infection

Blood-count monitoring, dose interruption and dose reduction are central to support. Growth-factor use is an individual clinical decision, not an automatic routine injection on this card.

Hormone-related symptoms

Discuss hot flushes, joint symptoms, vaginal symptoms, sleep and bone health according to the actual endocrine partner. Support can be adjusted without silently changing either cancer medicine.

Mouth, bowel and energy symptoms

Report mouth soreness, troublesome nausea/diarrhoea or fatigue so supportive treatment and hydration can be reviewed. New breathing symptoms must not be dismissed as expected fatigue.
