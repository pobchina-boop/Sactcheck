How this medicine supports your treatment

Palbociclib slows cell-cycle progression while the hormone partner reduces oestrogen signalling. The aim in this advanced-disease pathway is cancer control, with treatment and response reviewed over time.

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Palbociclib

Blocks cell-cycle signals that help cancer cells divide
CDK4/6 drives cell-cycle entry↓Palbociclib blocks CDK4/6↓Cell division slows

Hormone signalling can activate cyclin-D/CDK4/6. Palbociclib blocks this control point, helping keep susceptible cells from progressing through the cell cycle. Combining it with endocrine therapy acts on both the hormonal signal and downstream cell division.

The key early issue is a falling white-cell count, which may cause no symptoms. Keep blood-test appointments and report infection symptoms or new cough/breathlessness promptly.

Either an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant

Your endocrine partner

Reduces oestrogen signalling to hormone-sensitive cells
Oestrogen can stimulate cancer growth↓AI lowers oestrogen; fulvestrant targets its receptor↓Hormone-driven growth is reduced

An aromatase inhibitor lowers oestrogen production; fulvestrant acts at the oestrogen receptor. These are alternative partners here, not an instruction to take both. The partner’s dose, monitoring and injection or tablet schedule must be confirmed.

Discuss joint/hot-flush and bone symptoms with an AI, or injection-site/bleeding symptoms with fulvestrant. Pre/perimenopausal women require ovarian suppression in the prescribed combination.

The exact treatment being discussed

Palbociclib slows cell-cycle progression while the hormone partner reduces oestrogen signalling. The aim in this advanced-disease pathway is cancer control, with treatment and response reviewed over time.

Who this covers

NCCP 00414 a: HR-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced/metastatic disease with an aromatase inhibitor. NCCP 00414 b: the same receptor/disease setting with fulvestrant in women who have received prior endocrine therapy. Pre/perimenopausal women also need an agreed ovarian-suppression plan.

NCCP 00414 Palbociclib Therapy–28 day, version 5 a, 11 August 2026, pp 1–5 · EMA Ibrance SmPC / patient leaflet; product information updated 7 March 2025, sections 4.1–4.8 and 5.1

Dose and administration

Usual palbociclib dose: 125 mg once daily on Days 1–21 of a 28-day cycle; reduced prescribed doses are 100 mg or 75 mg.. Check whether the pharmacy supplied capsules or tablets: capsules are taken with food; tablets can be taken with or without food. Swallow whole at a consistent time. Avoid grapefruit. If you miss or vomit a dose, do not take an extra dose; take the next scheduled dose. Do not restart after a clinical hold until the treating team confirms.

NCCP 00414 Palbociclib Therapy–28 day, version 5 a, 11 August 2026, pp 1–5 · EMA Ibrance SmPC / patient leaflet; product information updated 7 March 2025, sections 4.1–4.8 and 5.1

Duration and decisions

Treatment continues while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable. There is no fixed adjuvant course in NCCP 00414. Blood results or symptoms may change the dose or postpone the next cycle.

NCCP 00414 Palbociclib Therapy–28 day, version 5 a, 11 August 2026, pp 1–5 · EMA Ibrance SmPC / patient leaflet; product information updated 7 March 2025, sections 4.1–4.8 and 5.1

Individual safety and source differences

The same low count can lead to a different action at Day 1 and Day 15 of the first two cycles. Dose decisions also depend on fever/infection, recovery, severity and current dose. A clinician must confirm a restart; the patient timeline is an explanation, not permission to self-adjust treatment.

Your wider treatment plan

Additional treatments depend on the confirmed setting and indication.

Advanced-disease scans

Response assessment in advanced breast cancer is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease dynamics, sites, symptoms and treatment. Less frequent monitoring may suit indolent disease. New symptoms or suspected progression need earlier assessment. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not an automatic three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO metastatic breast cancer guidelines

Tumour markers

If informative, particularly when elevated at baseline or disease is not readily measurable, tumour markers may supplement clinical assessment and imaging. A marker rise alone should not trigger a treatment change. There is no universal fixed marker timetable.

ESO-ESMO ABC 5 consensus: tumour-marker interpretation

Pregnancy, fertility and contraception

Do not use during pregnancy or breastfeeding. EMA specifies effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3 weeks after the last dose for women and 14 weeks for men; the endocrine partner may require a longer interval. Discuss fertility preservation before treatment when relevant.

Evidence depends on the population

Different studies answer different questions; do not compare their results as if populations and follow-up were identical.

PALOMA-3, palbociclib + fulvestrant: EMA efficacy table 9

521 patients with HR-positive/HER2-negative advanced cancer after endocrine progression; 347 palbociclib/fulvestrant vs 174 placebo/fulvestrant, with ovarian suppression for pre/perimenopausal women. Updated investigator-assessed PFS, 23 October 2015 cutoff: 11.2 vs 4.6 months; HR 0.497 (95% CI 0.398–0.620). Adding palbociclib improved PFS after prior endocrine therapy. The prespecified final OS analysis was not statistically significant: 34.9 vs 28.0 months; HR 0.814 (95% CI 0.644–1.029). Separate later exploratory follow-up from this final analysis; do not compare PFS numerically with PALOMA-2 as if populations matched.

Practical support

Nausea support

Palbociclib is classified as oral minimal–low emetic risk. Routine premedication is not usually needed; discuss rescue medication if nausea occurs. A generic intensive chemotherapy antiemetic schedule is not automatically added.

Low counts and infection

Blood-count monitoring, dose interruption and dose reduction are central to support. Growth-factor use is an individual clinical decision, not an automatic routine injection on this card.

Hormone-related symptoms

Discuss hot flushes, joint symptoms, vaginal symptoms, sleep and bone health according to the actual endocrine partner. Support can be adjusted without silently changing either cancer medicine.

Mouth, bowel and energy symptoms

Report mouth soreness, troublesome nausea/diarrhoea or fatigue so supportive treatment and hydration can be reviewed. New breathing symptoms must not be dismissed as expected fatigue.

What the evidence can tell us

PALOMA-2, palbociclib + letrozole: EMA efficacy tables 6 and 8: population, findings and limits

666 postmenopausal patients with HR-positive/HER2-negative advanced breast cancer without prior systemic treatment for advanced disease; 444 palbociclib/letrozole vs 222 placebo/letrozole.

Investigator-assessed PFS at the 2016 primary analysis: 24.8 vs 14.5 months; HR 0.576 (95% CI 0.463–0.718). Adding palbociclib delayed progression in the studied population.

PFS is not survival. Final overall survival was not statistically significant: 53.9 vs 51.2 months; HR 0.956 (95% CI 0.777–1.177), p=0.6755, cutoff 15 November 2021. Do not present this as a proven OS benefit.
