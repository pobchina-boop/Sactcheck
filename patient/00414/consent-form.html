Palbociclib with endocrine therapy

Take palbociclib for 21 days, then have 7 days without palbociclib. The aromatase-inhibitor tablet continues every day, or fulvestrant injections follow their separate schedule. The week off applies to palbociclib, not automatically to the hormone treatment.

Selected material risks

□ Low neutrophils and infection risk
□ Fatigue
□ Mouth soreness
□ Nausea or diarrhoea
□ Hair thinning
□ Rare but serious interstitial lung disease / pneumonitis
□ Hot flushes and other hormone-related symptoms
□ AI: joint symptoms and bone loss
□ Fulvestrant: injection-site pain and bruising
□ Partner-specific liver, interaction and pregnancy considerations

The same low count can lead to a different action at Day 1 and Day 15 of the first two cycles. Dose decisions also depend on fever/infection, recovery, severity and current dose. A clinician must confirm a restart; the patient timeline is an explanation, not permission to self-adjust treatment. Do not use during pregnancy or breastfeeding. EMA specifies effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3 weeks after the last dose for women and 14 weeks for men; the endocrine partner may require a longer interval. Discuss fertility preservation before treatment when relevant.

Individual benefit, alternatives and concerns

Agreed checks and next review

Before the first dose: FBC, kidney and liver bloods; review other medicines, pregnancy possibility, receptor results, menopausal status and the exact endocrine partner. Confirm ovarian suppression when relevant. FBC on Day 1 and Day 15 of cycles 1–2, then Day 1 from cycle 3. Kidney/liver bloods before each cycle. If counts are low, extra blood tests and a pause may be needed. Only after no worse than grade 1/2 neutropenia in the first 6 cycles may FBC frequency reduce to every 3 months before a cycle, with additional tests when clinically indicated.
