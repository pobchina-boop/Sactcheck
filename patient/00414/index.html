Palbociclib with endocrine therapy

Palbociclib slows cell-cycle progression while the hormone partner reduces oestrogen signalling. The aim in this advanced-disease pathway is cancer control, with treatment and response reviewed over time.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

The usual palbociclib starting dose is 125 mg once daily; a lower dose may be prescribed. Palbociclib is taken once daily on Days 1–21, then paused on Days 22–28 of each 28-day cycle. The aromatase inhibitor continues daily, or fulvestrant follows its injection schedule. Ovarian suppression has its own plan when required.

Tests and checks

full blood count (FBC), renal and liver profile. Confirm receptor status, exact endocrine partner, previous endocrine therapy, menopause/ovarian suppression, pregnancy precautions and interacting medicines. FBC on Day 1 and Day 15 of cycles 1–2; Day 1 from cycle 3 and extra checks as indicated. Only after at most grade 1–2 neutropenia throughout the first six cycles may FBC monitoring reduce to every three months, before the start of a cycle and as indicated. Renal and liver profile before each cycle; review fever/infection, mouth/bowel symptoms, fatigue, pulmonary symptoms and adherence. No routine palbociclib-specific Day-14 electrocardiogram (ECG) or echo timetable; investigate when clinically indicated.

Between treatment visits

Treatment days are specified by the regimen. Symptom timing varies; the full-cycle symptom bands show that an effect can occur at any point, not a measured probability or severity.

Contact the treatment team promptly if you feel feverish or unwell, have shaking chills or infection symptoms, or develop new cough or breathlessness. Follow the local emergency temperature instructions; do not wait for a planned blood test or for the 38.5°C value used in one clinician dose-adjustment table. Severe breathing difficulty needs emergency care.
