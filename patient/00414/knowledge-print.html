Palbociclib with endocrine therapy

The usual palbociclib starting dose is 125 mg once daily; a lower dose may be prescribed. Palbociclib is taken once daily on Days 1–21, then paused on Days 22–28 of each 28-day cycle. The aromatase inhibitor continues daily, or fulvestrant follows its injection schedule. Ovarian suppression has its own plan when required.

How this medicine supports your treatment

Palbociclib slows cell-cycle progression while the hormone partner reduces oestrogen signalling. The aim in this advanced-disease pathway is cancer control, with treatment and response reviewed over time.

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Palbociclib

Blocks cell-cycle signals that help cancer cells divide
CDK4/6 drives cell-cycle entry↓Palbociclib blocks CDK4/6↓Cell division slows

Hormone signalling can activate cyclin-D/CDK4/6. Palbociclib blocks this control point, helping keep susceptible cells from progressing through the cell cycle. Combining it with endocrine therapy acts on both the hormonal signal and downstream cell division.

The key early issue is a falling white-cell count, which may cause no symptoms. Keep blood-test appointments and report infection symptoms or new cough/breathlessness promptly.

Either an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant

Your endocrine partner

Reduces oestrogen signalling to hormone-sensitive cells
Oestrogen can stimulate cancer growth↓aromatase inhibitor (AI) lowers oestrogen; fulvestrant targets its receptor↓Hormone-driven growth is reduced

An aromatase inhibitor lowers oestrogen production; fulvestrant acts at the oestrogen receptor. These are alternative partners here, not an instruction to take both. The partner’s dose, monitoring and injection or tablet schedule must be confirmed.

Discuss joint/hot-flush and bone symptoms with an AI, or injection-site/bleeding symptoms with fulvestrant. Pre/perimenopausal women require ovarian suppression in the prescribed combination.

Your treatment aim

Palbociclib slows cell-cycle progression while the hormone partner reduces oestrogen signalling. The aim in this advanced-disease pathway is cancer control, with treatment and response reviewed over time.

Who this treatment is for

HR-positive/HER2-negative locally advanced/metastatic disease with an AI, or with fulvestrant after previous endocrine therapy as defined in NCCP. Pre/perimenopausal endocrine treatment requires ovarian suppression; the exact partner and its risks must be confirmed.

NCCP 00414 v5a · official regimen

Schedule and duration

The usual palbociclib starting dose is 125 mg once daily; a lower dose may be prescribed. Palbociclib is taken once daily on Days 1–21, then paused on Days 22–28 of each 28-day cycle. The aromatase inhibitor continues daily, or fulvestrant follows its injection schedule. Ovarian suppression has its own plan when required. Repeat while beneficial and tolerated. The planned week off concerns palbociclib, not automatically the endocrine partner.

Checks before and during treatment

Baseline tests and ongoing tests have different purposes.

Before the first dose

FBC, renal and liver profile. Confirm receptor status, exact endocrine partner, previous endocrine therapy, menopause/ovarian suppression, pregnancy precautions and interacting medicines.

NCCP 00414 v5a · official regimen · EMA Ibrance SmPC / patient leaflet; product information updated 7 March 2025, sections 4.1–4.8 and 5.1

During treatment

FBC on Day 1 and Day 15 of cycles 1–2; Day 1 from cycle 3 and extra checks as indicated. Only after at most grade 1–2 neutropenia throughout the first six cycles may FBC monitoring reduce to every three months, before the start of a cycle and as indicated. Renal and liver profile before each cycle; review fever/infection, mouth/bowel symptoms, fatigue, pulmonary symptoms and adherence. No routine palbociclib-specific Day-14 ECG or echo timetable; investigate when clinically indicated.

What the studies tell us

Palbociclib with hormone therapy can delay cancer growth. Studies used different hormone partners and different prior-treatment groups. The final overall trial results did not establish a clear survival improvement with palbociclib; later exploratory follow-up needs to be interpreted separately.

How to interpret this

Study results describe groups with defined treatment and eligibility. Ask about your own likely benefits, important harms, alternatives and remaining uncertainty.

PALOMA-2 primary report, Finn et al 2016 · PALOMA-2 final OS, Slamon et al 2024 · PALOMA-3 protocol-specified final OS, Turner et al 2018 · PALOMA-3 exploratory extended follow-up, Cristofanilli et al 2022

Questions to ask

How long does treatment last?

Do I need to enter dates?

No. This map shows general timing without personal information. Your hospital arranges actual appointments; ask who books each test and who reviews its result.

What if a dose is missed or treatment is held?

Check whether the pharmacy supplied capsules or tablets: capsules are taken with food; tablets can be taken with or without food. Swallow whole at a consistent time. Avoid grapefruit. If you miss or vomit a dose, do not take an extra dose; take the next scheduled dose. Do not restart after a clinical hold until the treating team confirms. A hold may affect one medicine rather than the whole combination; confirm each component.

Palbociclib with hormone therapy can delay cancer growth. Studies used different hormone partners and different prior-treatment groups. The final overall trial results did not establish a clear survival improvement with palbociclib; later exploratory follow-up needs to be interpreted separately.

What happens after treatment changes or ends?

Repeat while beneficial and tolerated. The planned week off concerns palbociclib, not automatically the endocrine partner. Keep the separately agreed disease follow-up, endocrine and bone-health plan.
