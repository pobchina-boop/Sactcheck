My treatment at a glance · National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) 00317

Pemetrexed + Cisplatin

These two chemotherapy medicines aim to control the cancer. Your team will explain the expected benefit for you.

Check your treatment: This summary covers the two chemotherapy medicines. If nivolumab is part of your treatment before surgery, use the full nivolumab combination plan.

Your treatment plan

Pemetrexed and cisplatin are given by drip on Day 1, then repeated every 21 days (3 weeks), if your team confirms it is safe.

  1. Before startingBlood tests and a review of your health. Start folic acid and arrange your vitamin B12 injection.
  2. Treatment and time at homeBoth medicines on Day 1. Extra intravenous (IV) fluids help protect your kidneys. Follow your medicines plan at home.
  3. Review and next stepsUsually 4–6 cycles for the chemotherapy-only course. Your team confirms the number and the next step. Pemetrexed maintenance, if planned, is a separate phase.

Before and between visits

  • Start folic acid and arrange vitamin B12 before treatment; follow your prescription.
  • Ask for one clear steroid and anti-sickness schedule; do not add duplicate steroid courses.
  • Tell your team about hearing problems, numbness, other medicines and any infection.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately for temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions.

Cannot reach the team? Seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

What to watch for

Use the picture and symptom descriptions together. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

1. Ears / hearingCisplatin

Report tinnitus or hearing change promptly; describe any existing problem before starting.

3. Chest / breathingBoth chemotherapy medicines

New breathlessness, wheeze or chest pain needs urgent assessment; do not assume it is an expected side effect.

5. Stomach / bowelBoth chemotherapy medicines

Use prescribed anti-sickness medicines. Severe vomiting, diarrhoea or poor intake needs urgent advice.

7. Skin / blood countsBoth chemotherapy medicines

Report a spreading rash, unusual bruising or bleeding. Fever or sudden illness needs urgent advice.

2. MouthPemetrexed

Mouth ulcers or painful swallowing can limit eating and drinking. Seek advice early.

4. Kidneys / fluidsBoth chemotherapy medicines

6. Hands / feetCisplatin
