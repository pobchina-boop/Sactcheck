Your testing and review plan

A reference cycle is not a personalised calendar. Tests listed as symptom-triggered or individual are not presented as mandatory tests at every visit. No universal imaging or tumour-marker interval is invented.

Record symptoms and the disease sites being followed on scans.Full blood count, kidney and liver function before every cycle. Blood-count suppression can be delayed: the product information describes the lowest counts often three to four weeks after a dose, so a 21-day appointment does not guarantee recovery. Extra blood tests are arranged when clinically needed.Follow your unit’s fever threshold and urgent-contact instructions; call immediately if you develop fever, shaking chills or feel suddenly unwell, even without a high temperature. Also report unusual bleeding, persistent vomiting, inability to drink, reduced urine, jaundice or severe abdominal pain. Fever with abdominal pain and jaundice needs urgent assessment for rare severe liver injury. Tell the nurse immediately about infusion-site burning or swelling.Keep reporting fever, bleeding, worsening fatigue or other symptoms after the last infusion while counts and organs recover. Response scans, later blood tests and the next treatment decision are individual; the National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) source gives no universal imaging or tumour-marker schedule.Do not restart held treatment until the team confirms the medicine, dose and timing. The whole treatment journey

Dacarbazine — metastatic melanoma: treatment and checks

Advanced melanoma

Dacarbazine by intravenous (IV) infusion on day 1, repeated every 21 days for up to six cycles, or stopped earlier for progression or unacceptable toxicity.

Read from left to right. Columns show stages, not equal lengths of time. This is a general plan: no dates or personal information are needed. Your team confirms which steps apply and arranges appointments.
