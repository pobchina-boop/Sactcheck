When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology promptly for new breathlessness, swelling, severe diarrhoea, inability to drink or sudden illness. Tell staff immediately about rash, chest tightness or breathing difficulty during the injection. Fever, shivers, unusual bleeding or worsening numbness during chemotherapy also need prompt advice.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Two antibodies in one injection with chemotherapy

Shrink the cancer before surgery and contribute to reducing recurrence risk as part of a complete early-breast plan.

Taxane chemotherapy

Docetaxel

Disrupts the cell’s division machinery

Docetaxel stabilises tiny structures called microtubules. Cells need these to separate their chromosomes during division; disrupting the process can stop cancer cells multiplying. Healthy dividing cells are also affected.

Microtubules stabilised↓Division disrupted↓Cell growth affected

What to watch for
Take prescribed steroid premedication. Report fever, lasting numbness, swelling, sore nails or infusion symptoms.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Damages DNA inside cells

Carboplatin creates links in DNA, making it harder for cells to copy and repair their genetic instructions. Its dose depends on kidney function as well as the intended regimen.

Platinum enters cell↓DNA links form↓Copying is disrupted

What to watch for
Keep blood-test appointments. Fever, unusual bleeding, repeated vomiting or infusion symptoms need prompt advice.

Sources: nccp · smpc

HER2-targeted antibody

Pertuzumab

Part of the combined Phesgo injection
Blocks HER2 pairing with other receptors

Pertuzumab binds a different area of HER2 from trastuzumab. It limits receptor pairing, so the two antibodies act together at different points on the same growth pathway.

HER2 pairing↓Pertuzumab blocks pairing↓Growth signals reduced

What to watch for
Keep echo appointments. Report diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling, palpitations or an injection reaction.

Sources: nccp · smpc

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

Part of the combined Phesgo injection
Targets HER2 growth signals

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

HER2 on cell surface↓Antibody binds↓Growth signalling reduced

What to watch for
Keep echo appointments. Report diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling, palpitations or an injection reaction.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Phesgo is one combined injection of pertuzumab and trastuzumab under the skin of the thigh. It is not two separate injections and is never given into a vein.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm blood results, cardiac checks, active phase, previous dual HER2 dose and the consent discussion.

STEP 2

The thigh injection

Loading/reloading 1200 mg/600 mg over 8 minutes with 30 minutes observation; maintenance 600 mg/600 mg over 5 minutes with 15 minutes observation. Complete the observation before chemotherapy. Alternate thighs; avoid sore, bruised or damaged skin.

STEP 3

Chemotherapy and access

After Phesgo observation, chemotherapy is given through a cannula, PICC or port. The team explains access insertion and care. No take-home infusion pump is routinely used.

STEP 4

Leaving the unit

Confirm supportive medicines, next visits and due tests. Injection time is shorter than the whole appointment; preparation, assessment and observation still take time.

Switching / a missed appointment

The team checks the last dual HER2 dose, including IV treatment: a gap of less than 6 weeks uses the maintenance dose; 6 weeks or longer needs reloading. A first Phesgo visit after IV treatment does not automatically require a loading dose.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Antiemetic plan

Carboplatin Day 1 uses its high-risk antiemetic plan. Docetaxel steroid premedication must be reconciled with antiemetic steroids.

Premedicines

Docetaxel: prescribed dexamethasone starts the day before each dose. Ask for one reconciled steroid schedule.

Appointments and results

Echo before treatment and every 12 weeks (around cycle 5 if on time), including maintenance. Bloods before each cycle. Ask who books and reviews each test; report a missing appointment.

Phase transition

After surgery, your team reviews the pathology and chooses the next HER2 medicine. The usual plan aims to complete about one year of HER2 treatment from the first dose. Remaining invasive cancer can change the medicine offered. The selected treatment has its own blood tests and supportive medicines.

Why this treatment?

The aim is to treat higher-risk HER2-positive breast cancer before surgery and reduce the chance of it returning. Docetaxel and carboplatin are chemotherapy medicines; Phesgo contains two medicines that target HER2.

Why this plan rather than another?

The choice depends on the cancer stage, lymph nodes, HER2 result, general health and the likely side effects. This plan does not contain an anthracycline. Treatment reference · 05

Surgery, radiotherapy and hormone treatment

Other treatment depends on your cancer and personal plan.

How surgery and radiation fit

Systemic neoadjuvant treatment comes before surgery. Lumpectomy versus mastectomy is agreed with the surgeon. Radiotherapy, if indicated, follows surgery/recovery and is coordinated with postoperative systemic treatment; it may overlap HER2-only treatment when both teams agree.

Sources: early

Endocrine treatment and bone health

If hormone-receptor-positive disease leads to an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression, assess fracture risk and baseline DEXA when indicated. Repeat according to results, risk and the endocrine plan; DEXA is not a scan at each Phesgo cycle.

Sources: bone

Receptor and genomic tests

Hormone-receptor status can affect endocrine treatment. Oncotype-type chemotherapy triage is not applied to these HER2-positive regimens; treatment selection requires the appropriate indication-specific MDT assessment.

Sources: early

Questions worth asking

Is Phesgo two injections?

Phesgo is one combined injection of pertuzumab and trastuzumab under the skin of the thigh. It is not two separate injections and is never given into a vein.

When is my next heart scan?

Echo or MUGA before starting and every 12 weeks during treatment, including maintenance. At an uninterrupted 21-day schedule, the first 12-week check is around cycle 5; count elapsed time from the first HER2 dose. A delay or route/phase change does not restart the clock. Cardiac concern may require 4–8-week checks or urgent earlier assessment.

What changes after chemotherapy?

After surgery, your team reviews the pathology and chooses the next HER2 medicine. The usual plan aims to complete about one year of HER2 treatment from the first dose. Remaining invasive cancer can change the medicine offered. The selected treatment has its own blood tests and supportive medicines.

Where does radiotherapy fit?

Systemic neoadjuvant treatment comes before surgery. Lumpectomy versus mastectomy is agreed with the surgeon. Radiotherapy, if indicated, follows surgery/recovery and is coordinated with postoperative systemic treatment; it may overlap HER2-only treatment when both teams agree.

Will I need CT scans, markers or DEXA?

Repeat according to results, risk and the endocrine plan; DEXA is not a scan at each Phesgo cycle.

Why was this treatment chosen for me?

The aim is to treat higher-risk HER2-positive breast cancer before surgery and reduce the chance of it returning. Docetaxel and carboplatin are chemotherapy medicines; Phesgo contains two medicines that target HER2. The choice depends on the cancer stage, lymph nodes, HER2 result, general health and the likely side effects. This plan does not contain an anthracycline.
