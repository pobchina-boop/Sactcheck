My overall treatment

Mitomycin day 1 · five-day pumps on days 1–5 and 22–26

28day view

Treatment timing in this window

MitomycinDay 1
Fluorouracil pump 1Day 1–5 · Continuous 120-hour infusion.
Fluorouracil pump 2Day 22–26 · Second continuous 120-hour infusion.
  1. TreatmentMitomycin is given once on day 1. Fluorouracil runs continuously through a pump over 120 hours on days 1–5 and again on days 22–26, alongside the prescribed radiotherapy course. This is one chemoradiation course, not a repeating 28-day chemotherapy cycle.

WhenWhat is checkedTiming / reason
BeforeBaseline checksNCCP sourceFull blood count, kidney and liver tests. Confirm dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) testing before first fluorouracil unless previously tested. Review the heart history, medicines, intravenous access and radiotherapy plan.
DuringSafety monitoringNCCP sourceFull blood count, kidney and liver tests weekly. Review mouth, bowel, skin, urinary and cardiac symptoms and the pump/line. Additional tests depend on symptoms or abnormal results.

Between treatments

  • Side effects may occur on any day, including during a break. Report problems promptly.
  • Use one written anti-sickness plan, including medicines before treatment, tablets for home and rescue treatment. Your team reconciles steroids, interactions and any stomach-protection medicine from your local prescription. Ask for a written mouth-care and diarrhoea plan, pump contacts and line-care instructions. Stomach protection and other local adjuncts should be reconciled with the actual prescription.

Confirm the DPD result, pump training, connection/disconnection appointments and the radiotherapy department’s bladder-preparation instructions.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
