Consent discussion aid

Durvalumab, paclitaxel and carboplatin

The aim is to control the cancer, ease or prevent symptoms and delay worsening. Your team can explain the benefit they expect for you and your other options.

Treatment discussion

First treatment for womb (endometrial) cancer that is advanced or has returned, when curative surgery or radiotherapy is unsuitable and a tumour test shows deficient mismatch repair (dMMR) or high microsatellite instability (MSI-H). These changes can make the tumour more visible to immune cells.

PhaseWhenWhat changes
CombinationDay 1 of each cycle · 4–6 cycles if toleratedDay 1: durvalumab, then paclitaxel, then carboplatin. Repeat every 21 days for 4–6 cycles unless the team pauses or stops earlier.
MaintenanceFirst dose 3 weeks after last combination · Then every 4 weeksThe first durvalumab-only dose is 3 weeks after the final combination infusion. It then repeats every 4 weeks while helping and tolerated.

Possible benefit, alternatives and your priorities

The dMMR subgroup receiving durvalumab had a lower rate of progression or death over follow-up (hazard ratio 0.42, 95% confidence interval 0.22–0.80). This compares groups over time and does not predict how long treatment will help one person.

This was a prespecified exploratory subgroup. A separate study arm also used olaparib; olaparib is not part of this NCCP regimen.

Discuss your treatment aim, alternatives including supportive care, the expected benefit and uncertainties, duration, infusion reactions, infection, low blood counts, hair loss, neuropathy and delayed immune inflammation. Ask about possible lifelong hormone treatment, fertility, contraception, breastfeeding, and what would prompt a pause or stop. You can ask more questions or reconsider your choice.

Other options discussed and what matters most to me

Important risks we discussed

  • Pneumonitis · lung inflammation — New cough or breathlessness needs an immediate oncology call. Severe breathing difficulty needs 112 or 999.
  • Infection or low blood counts — Fever, chills or sudden illness can be serious even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately.
  • Colitis · bowel inflammation — New or worsening diarrhoea, belly pain or blood in stools needs urgent oncology advice.
  • Hormone problems — Severe weakness, dizziness, vomiting or extreme thirst can signal changes in hormone or blood-sugar levels. Call oncology immediately.
  • Infusion allergy — Tell the nurse immediately if you feel flushed, wheezy, dizzy or unwell during a drip.
