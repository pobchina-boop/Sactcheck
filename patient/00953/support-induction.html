Support during combination

Durvalumab, paclitaxel and carboplatin

Day 1: durvalumab, then paclitaxel, then carboplatin. Repeat every 21 days for 4–6 cycles unless the team pauses or stops earlier.

Sickness plan for combination days

Carboplatin has high risk of sickness. Your team prescribes medicines to prevent sickness and rescue medicines for home. Confirm names, times and what to do if you vomit or cannot drink.

Before the taxane infusion

The team gives medicines to reduce allergy risk before paclitaxel, according to local policy. Tell the nurse straight away about flushing, wheezing, pain or dizziness during an infusion.

Home and urgent help

Call oncology immediately for fever or sudden illness, new cough or breathing changes, persistent diarrhoea or unusual bleeding. Bring your immune alert card. Report tingling or trouble walking before the next cycle.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call the oncology team immediately for a fever or feeling suddenly unwell, new cough or breathlessness, diarrhoea or bloody stool, blistering rash, marked weakness or dizziness. Do this even after treatment ends. Call 112 or 999 for severe breathing trouble, severe chest pain, collapse or confusion. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.
