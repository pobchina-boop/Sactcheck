Preparing for treatment visits

Durvalumab, paclitaxel and carboplatin

Planning your visits

On a combination day

All medicines are given into a vein. On combination days durvalumab is given first over about 1 hour, then paclitaxel over about 3 hours and carboplatin over about 30 minutes. Allow extra time for review, premedicines, blood results and preparation. Durvalumab alone takes about an hour.

Before your visit

Bring your medicines list, alert card and 24-hour oncology contact. Tell the team about prior allergic reactions, hearing issues, diabetes, autoimmune disease, steroids and nerve symptoms.

Leaving the unit

Write down the date of the next blood test, the next infusion and the 24-hour phone number. Confirm whether the next visit is for three drugs or one.

Write down your next appointments

  • Bring the medication list and alert card. Confirm when to take anti-sickness medicines.
Contact your oncology service urgently. Call the oncology team immediately for a fever or feeling suddenly unwell, new cough or breathlessness, diarrhoea or bloody stool, blistering rash, marked weakness or dizziness. Do this even after treatment ends. Call 112 or 999 for severe breathing trouble, severe chest pain, collapse or confusion. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.
