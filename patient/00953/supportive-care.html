Your supportive care plan

Durvalumab, paclitaxel and carboplatin

Practical support

Preventing sickness

Combination treatment has high sickness risk, mainly from carboplatin. Take your prescribed prevention and rescue medicines as directed. Durvalumab alone has minimal sickness risk, so the plan may change.

Before paclitaxel

The team gives medicines to lower the chance of an infusion reaction, including a steroid and antihistamine according to its plan. Tell the nurse immediately about wheeze, flushing or faintness.

Nerve or muscle symptoms

Contact the team early about numb or painful hands or feet, difficulty with buttons or walking, or troublesome muscle or joint aches.

Immune alert card

Carry your immunotherapy alert card during and after treatment. Show it to any urgent-care clinician. Do not start, stop or change steroids yourself.

Fertility and pregnancy

Discuss pregnancy, contraception and fertility before treatment. Durvalumab requires effective contraception during treatment and for at least three months after the last dose; ask your team for the longest interval required by any medicine you receive. Do not breastfeed during treatment.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call the oncology team immediately for a fever or feeling suddenly unwell, new cough or breathlessness, diarrhoea or bloody stool, blistering rash, marked weakness or dizziness. Do this even after treatment ends. Call 112 or 999 for severe breathing trouble, severe chest pain, collapse or confusion. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.
