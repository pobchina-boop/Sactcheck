Your complete treatment reference

Jemperli with paclitaxel and carboplatin

Your treatment and how the medicines work

The aim is to control womb cancer that is advanced or has returned, and delay it getting worse.

Dostarlimab · Jemperli

PD-1 immunotherapy. It removes a brake on immune cells so they can recognise and attack cancer. It can also cause inflammation in healthy organs. Report new cough, diarrhoea, severe fatigue or rash even after the final dose.

Paclitaxel · Paclitaxel

Taxane chemotherapy. It disrupts cell division. Hair follicles, blood cells and nerves can also be affected. Report numbness and tell the nurse immediately if you flush or wheeze during treatment.

Carboplatin · Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. It damages DNA in dividing cells. Kidney function helps determine the prescribed amount. Report fever, bleeding, hearing changes or an infusion reaction.

Your infusion schedule

Cycles 1–6

Three infusions every three weeks if your team confirms you are ready.

Cycle 7 onwards

Jemperli alone, then every six weeks. Your team reviews benefit and side effects. NCCP allows up to three years of monotherapy.

Common effects and what to do

Nausea and fatigue

Use the anti-sickness plan supplied for each phase. Call if vomiting prevents drinking.

Hair and nerves

Paclitaxel can cause hair loss and numbness. Report nerve changes before each infusion.

Serious effects: when to call

Dostarlimab and immune inflammation

Lung, bowel, liver, kidney, skin and hormone problems can begin after treatment stops. Call urgently for new breathlessness, diarrhoea, jaundice or marked weakness.

Paclitaxel and carboplatin

Fever, chills, bleeding or sudden illness need an immediate call. Report flushing, dizziness or wheeze during any infusion immediately.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Fever, new breathlessness, diarrhoea or unusual bleeding? Call oncology now. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Combination phase

Carboplatin carries high sickness risk. Follow the scheduled anti-sickness medicines and the rescue plan given by your team.

Paclitaxel premedicines

Before paclitaxel the unit provides medicines to reduce allergic reactions. Confirm any doses you must take at home before attending.

Jemperli alone

Sickness risk is minimal with Jemperli alone. Ask which medicines continue once chemotherapy ends. Keep carrying your immunotherapy alert card.

Planning your visits

Day-unit visits

On combination days, dostarlimab goes into a vein first, then paclitaxel, then carboplatin. The paclitaxel infusion usually takes about three hours. Allow extra time for checks, premedicines and observation.

After cycle six

Confirm the actual date of cycle seven with the unit. Delays can move subsequent appointments.

Family planning

Discuss fertility, contraception and breastfeeding with the oncology team before treatment.

What the research found

In RUBY, adding dostarlimab to chemotherapy and then continuing dostarlimab reduced the hazard of progression or death in the dMMR/MSI-H group (HR 0.28, 95% CI 0.16–0.50).

These results compare groups over time. They cannot predict the duration of benefit for one person. The NCCP indication shown here is biomarker-specific.
