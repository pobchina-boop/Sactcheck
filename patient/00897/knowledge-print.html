SACTCheck · NCCP 00897 v4 · Unresectable or metastatic biliary tract cancer

Your treatment reference

Durvalumab + gemcitabine + cisplatin

Your treatment and how the medicines work

First-line treatment to slow advanced bile duct or gallbladder cancer and help people live longer.

Durvalumab · Imfinzi

PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitor. Blocks a signal called PD-L1 that some cancers use to switch off immune cells. This can help immune cells recognise and attack cancer. Immune cells can inflame healthy organs, including after the final infusion. Carry your immunotherapy alert card.

Gemcitabine · Generic preparations

Antimetabolite chemotherapy. Resembles a building block for DNA. It interferes with making new DNA in dividing cells. Low blood counts can increase the risk of infection, bruising and bleeding.

Cisplatin · Generic preparations

Platinum chemotherapy. Links strands of DNA together, making it difficult for dividing cancer cells to copy their instructions. Tell the team about reduced urine, ringing in the ears, hearing loss or tingling.

Your infusion schedule

Up to eight 21-day cycles

On day 1 you receive durvalumab, gemcitabine and cisplatin. On day 8 you receive gemcitabine and cisplatin only. Blood results or symptoms may change dates.

From cycle 9 when suitable

Chemotherapy finishes after up to eight cycles. If treatment continues as planned, only durvalumab is given on day 1 of a 28-day cycle. Your team reviews benefit and side effects.

Common effects and what to do

Tiredness or low energy

Rest when needed and tell your team if tiredness suddenly worsens.

Nausea or loss of appetite

Use prescribed anti-sickness medicines. Ask for help if eating or drinking becomes difficult.

Low blood counts

These may be found on blood tests. Report bruising or bleeding and call urgently for fever or shivering.

Serious effects: when to call

Lung inflammation (pneumonitis)

Contact oncology urgently for a new cough or breathlessness. Severe trouble breathing or chest pain needs 112 or 999.

Bowel inflammation (colitis)

Call oncology urgently for new diarrhoea, blood in stools or increasing abdominal pain.

Liver inflammation or bile drainage problems

Call urgently for yellow eyes or skin, dark urine or worsening upper abdominal pain.

Hormone problems

Unusual thirst, frequent urination, dizziness, vomiting, marked weakness or severe headache need urgent assessment. Collapse or confusion with severe illness needs emergency help.

Infection or blocked bile duct

Fever, shivering, jaundice or upper abdominal pain need urgent assessment, especially with a biliary stent.

Kidneys, hearing or nerves

Report much less urine, ringing in the ears, hearing changes or tingling promptly.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Fever, shaking chills or jaundice may signal serious infection, especially with a bile duct stent. Contact oncology immediately. New cough, breathlessness or diarrhoea is also urgent. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Carry the official immunotherapy alert card

Keep the card with you during treatment and afterwards. Show it to an out-of-hours doctor or emergency team and tell them when you last received immunotherapy.

If you become unwell

Use the 24-hour oncology number supplied by your hospital. If the team cannot be reached, seek urgent assessment. Follow the emergency action shown on your card.

Medicines and steroids

Show the team all prescription, over-the-counter and herbal medicines. Take prescribed steroids exactly as directed and ask before changing them.

During the chemotherapy phase

The unit gives fluids before cisplatin to help protect your kidneys. Your team will prescribe anti-sickness medicines and advise what to take at home. Ask about fluid intake if you have a heart or kidney condition.

During durvalumab maintenance

The chemotherapy medicines and their routine pre-infusion fluids finish. Bring your immunotherapy alert card and check your prescription for any continuing supportive medicine.

Planning your visits

Your first visit

On day 1 you receive durvalumab, gemcitabine and cisplatin. On day 8 you receive gemcitabine and cisplatin only. Blood results or symptoms may change dates.

Later visits

Chemotherapy finishes after up to eight cycles. If treatment continues as planned, only durvalumab is given on day 1 of a 28-day cycle. Your team reviews benefit and side effects.

Before leaving the unit

Write down the next infusion, blood tests and the 24-hour oncology number. Ask which appointments change if a dose is delayed. Bring the wallet and passport to your next review.

What the research found

The TOPAZ-1 trial compared adding durvalumab to gemcitabine and cisplatin with adding a placebo. At the three-year analysis, about 15 in 100 people in the durvalumab group and 7 in 100 in the placebo group were alive after three years.

These are results in selected trial groups. They cannot predict an individual response; ask how the findings apply to you.
