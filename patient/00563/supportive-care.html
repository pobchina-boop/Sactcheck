Additional phase-specific support

  • Assess tumour lysis risk when tumour burden, kidney problems or dehydration raise concern; fluids and additional tests are individual prescriptions. Both oral components are moderate-to-high emetic risk in the source.
  • Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least one month after the last dose. Hormonal contraception needs an additional or alternative barrier method because interactions can reduce effectiveness. Discuss fertility beforehand and follow the team’s breastfeeding instructions. Report a possible pregnancy promptly.
  • Contact the team promptly for new visual symptoms, chest pain, breathlessness, palpitations, faintness, severe muscle pain or weakness, dark urine, bleeding, severe rash, persistent diarrhoea or severe abdominal pain. Do not continue one medicine at an unchanged dose if the other has been held without a specific team instruction. Report markedly reduced urine, persistent vomiting, new muscle cramps or weakness, or palpitations promptly; the team may need to check salts, kidney function and tumour lysis risk.
  • This is not an issued prescription. The clinician and pharmacist must complete and authorise any supportive medicines.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
