When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call promptly for unusual bleeding, yellow eyes / skin, worsening numbness or new cough / breathlessness.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

The medicine in this card

T-DM1 links HER2 targeting to a chemotherapy payload to help control previously treated advanced breast cancer. Prior trastuzumab and a taxane, plus the full NCCP prior-treatment or early-relapse criteria, must be checked.

HER2-directed antibody with a chemotherapy payload

Trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1)

Kadcyla · antibody–drug conjugate
HER2 targeting carries DM1 into cancer cells.

The antibody attaches to HER2. After the complex enters a cell, its DM1 payload disrupts structures needed for cell division. This is a single linked medicine, with its own dosing and safety rules.

Attach to HER2↓Enter the cell↓Release DM1 to disrupt division

What to watch for
Report bleeding, numbness, jaundice, new cough or breathlessness. Keep blood and heart checks.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

T-DM1 3.6 mg/kg IV every 21 days, adjusted if required, until progression or unacceptable toxicity. This metastatic course has no fixed 14-cycle endpoint; the postoperative early-breast regimen is a different card.

STEP 1

Before your visit

Confirm the dose, symptoms, required blood results and echo due window.

STEP 2

Access and administration

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used according to the treatment plan. A port insertion is a separate procedure.

STEP 3

Treatment and observation

First infusion over 90 minutes, then observe for at least 90 minutes. Later infusions may take 30 minutes if tolerated, with at least 30 minutes observation.

STEP 4

After the visit

Contact the unit to rearrange a missed dose; the next schedule preserves a 3-week interval. This medicine does not routinely require a take-home pump.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit length

First infusion over 90 minutes, then observe for at least 90 minutes. Later infusions may take 30 minutes if tolerated, with at least 30 minutes observation.

Missed or delayed visit

Contact the unit to rearrange a missed dose; the next schedule preserves a 3-week interval.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Support for this medicine

T-DM1 has low emetogenic risk; review the low-risk antiemetic draft and individual contraindications.

Blood and heart appointments

FBC, renal and liver profile before every cycle. Echo / MUGA every 12 weeks; around C5 for uninterrupted 21-day cycles.

Other treatments and bone health

Radiotherapy or local procedures are used only if indicated. Any endocrine or other systemic treatment needs a separate oncology plan. Bone-health checks depend on the individual risk and treatment context.

Pregnancy / contraception

Discuss pregnancy, fertility and contraception before treatment. Report pregnancy promptly; do not breastfeed during treatment or for 7 months afterwards. The team confirms the medicine-specific contraception duration.

Treatment reference · 01

Why this treatment?

This treatment is for HER2-positive breast cancer that cannot be removed by surgery or has spread, after specified earlier treatment with trastuzumab and a taxane. T-DM1 carries a chemotherapy medicine attached to an antibody that targets HER2.

Why T-DM1 may be selected

This treatment is for HER2-positive breast cancer that cannot be removed by surgery or has spread, after specified earlier treatment with trastuzumab and a taxane. T-DM1 carries a chemotherapy medicine attached to an antibody that targets HER2.

Sources: nccp

Why this medicine rather than another HER2 treatment?

The decision depends on the treatments already received, when the cancer returned or changed, organ function, side effects and other suitable options. T-DM1 is different from trastuzumab alone and from trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd). Ask your team to explain why it fits your current plan.

Sources: nccp

Cardiac checks

Echo / MUGA every 12 weeks; around C5 for uninterrupted 21-day cycles. Baseline cardiac assessment and LVEF are required. ECG is guided by the cardiac / clinical assessment; NCCP does not prescribe a separate fixed-cycle ECG timetable for T-DM1.

The wider pathway

Coordinate cancer medicines with local treatment and follow-up.

Scans and markers

Response imaging commonly every 2–4 months, adapted to sites, disease course and treatment. A new symptom can need earlier imaging. Tumour markers may supplement assessment, never determine a treatment change alone.

Sources: early · metastatic · markers

Radiotherapy relationship

Radiotherapy is used for a defined site or symptom when indicated. The oncology and radiation teams agree whether this medicine continues or pauses; do not infer a concurrent schedule from this card.

Sources: early · metastatic

Bone health

If an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression is planned, assess fracture risk and DEXA need. Repeat timing depends on results and bone treatment; this is not an automatic HER2-therapy scan.

Questions worth asking

How long will I receive treatment?

T-DM1 3.6 mg/kg IV every 21 days, adjusted if required, until progression or unacceptable toxicity. This metastatic course has no fixed 14-cycle endpoint; the postoperative early-breast regimen is a different card.

When is my next echo?

Echo / MUGA every 12 weeks; around C5 for uninterrupted 21-day cycles. Existing HER2 exposure and delays affect cycle numbering; the team should give the actual due window.

How does radiotherapy fit?

Radiotherapy is used for a defined site or symptom when indicated. The oncology and radiation teams agree whether this medicine continues or pauses; do not infer a concurrent schedule from this card.

Does a tumour marker replace a scan?

No. In metastatic disease it may add information to symptoms and imaging. It should not alone determine treatment changes. In symptom-free early disease it is not routine recurrence screening.

Why was this treatment chosen for me?
