When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Follow your oncology team’s emergency instructions for fever, shivering or sudden illness, including with normal temperature. Report new breathlessness/cough, diarrhoea or abdominal pain, reduced urine, severe rash, unusual weakness, headache or marked thirst promptly. Severe chest pain, collapse, confusion or major breathing difficulty needs emergency care.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Three medicines. Different jobs.

This treatment aims to control pleural mesothelioma that cannot be removed with curative surgery, help symptoms and extend life. It is usually given without an expectation of cure. Mesothelioma starts in the lining around the lung and is different from non-small-cell lung cancer.

Immunotherapy

Nivolumab

Opdivo
Blocks the PD-1 immune checkpoint

Nivolumab binds PD-1 on T cells. It prevents signals that can reduce the immune attack on cancer. This can also let immune cells inflame healthy tissues. It is infused every 21 days in this plan.

T cell↓PD-1 blocked↓Immune response

What to watch for
New breathing, bowel, skin, hormone or other symptoms need prompt advice during or after treatment.

Sources: nivo · nccp

Immunotherapy

Ipilimumab

Yervoy
Blocks the CTLA-4 immune checkpoint

Ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4, another control on T-cell activity. It works alongside nivolumab through a different immune checkpoint. It is given every 42 days, so it is absent from each Day 22 visit. Healthy organs can also become inflamed.

T cell↓CTLA-4 blocked↓Immune activity

What to watch for
Report diarrhoea, tummy pain, rash, severe tiredness, headache or other new symptoms promptly. Keep your immune alert card.

Sources: ipi · nccp

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Day 1 visit

Nivolumab and ipilimumab are due. Report symptoms and confirm the current prescription.

Day 2–21: Between visits

Follow your prescribed support plan and report new symptoms promptly. Immune effects can occur on any day. Keep the oncology contact instructions and immune alert card accessible.

Day 22: Day 22 · no ipilimumab

Nivolumab is due. Confirm active medicines and the right support plan.

Day 23–42: Before the next cycle

Continue symptom reporting. Later Day 1 visits again include both immune medicines if prescribed. Confirm the next appointment and blood tests; do not wait for it if unwell.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Visits and duration

Each cycle lasts 42 days: nivolumab and ipilimumab on Day 1, then nivolumab alone on Day 22. Nivolumab is every three weeks and ipilimumab every six weeks. This regimen contains no chemotherapy. Continue while appropriate until progression, unacceptable toxicity or up to 24 months without progression. Your team reviews the plan and may delay or stop treatment.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Nivolumab over 30 minutes, then ipilimumab over 30 minutes on Day 1 only. Ipilimumab vital signs are checked every 30 minutes during infusion and for an hour afterwards. Blood tests, preparation and observation add to time in the unit.

STEP 3

Medicines and monitoring

Blood count, kidney, liver, glucose and thyroid tests before each 42-day cycle; symptoms reviewed before each infusion and additional tests when indicated. Cortisol if clinically indicated. Carry treatment details and the immune alert card. Describe your usual bowel pattern, breathing, energy, skin and hormone symptoms, and tell the team about autoimmune illness, infections and all medicines.

STEP 4

Transport and support

Arrange travel and help if needed. Keep urgent contacts and alert information with you.

Treatment reference · 05

Treatment effects

Both immune medicines can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Keep your immune alert card and report new symptoms promptly, including for at least five months after the last dose.

Bowel, lungs and skin

New diarrhoea, tummy pain, blood/mucus in stool, cough, breathlessness, blistering or peeling skin need prompt assessment.

Sources: nccp

Hormones, heart, muscles and nerves

Unusual weakness, headache, dizziness, marked thirst, frequent urination, chest pain, palpitations, drooping eyelids or difficulty swallowing/breathing need urgent advice. Several organs can be inflamed together.

Sources: nccp

Liver and kidneys

Yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, reduced urine or swelling need prompt review; blood tests can find changes before symptoms.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 06

Support medicines and self-care

Steroids may be prescribed to treat immune inflammation. Follow the exact dose and taper; do not start, stop or change them without your team. Hormone replacement can sometimes be long term.

Sources: nccp

Treatment reference · 07

After treatment and daily life

Both immune medicines can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Keep your immune alert card and report new symptoms promptly, including for at least five months after the last dose.

Reproductive advice

Discuss pregnancy, breastfeeding, fertility and contraception before treatment. Effective contraception is needed for at least five months after the last nivolumab dose; ask about your individual plan.

Sources: nccp

Living with treatment

Ask for help with fatigue, appetite, pain, breathlessness, work and emotional concerns. Follow-up continues for unresolved effects and any long-term hormone replacement.

Sources: nccp

Why this treatment is offered

In CheckMate 743, nivolumab plus ipilimumab improved overall survival compared with platinum/pemetrexed chemotherapy in previously untreated unresectable pleural mesothelioma.

Trial results compare groups and cannot predict your personal benefit. Discuss how the study population relates to your cancer and health.

Questions worth asking

Why are the visits different?

Each cycle lasts 42 days: nivolumab and ipilimumab on Day 1, then nivolumab alone on Day 22. Nivolumab is every three weeks and ipilimumab every six weeks. This regimen contains no chemotherapy.

How long is treatment planned?

Continue while appropriate until progression, unacceptable toxicity or up to 24 months without progression. Your team reviews the plan and may delay or stop treatment.

What if I miss a support medicine?

Ask your oncology team or pharmacist for advice; do not double doses or combine steroid courses yourself.

How is benefit checked?

Your consultant sets the scan plan and reviews symptoms, results and priorities. Side effects do not reliably show cancer response.

Can I decide from this guide whether treatment is safe today?

No. The team must assess symptoms, blood results, previous reactions and the prescribing protocol.

What should I report after treatment stops?

Both immune medicines can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Keep your immune alert card and report new symptoms promptly, including for at least five months after the last dose.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are you having now? What changes after induction? What would make you call today? Ask your team to fill any gaps.
