When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Help control advanced kidney cancer using immunotherapy and a blood-vessel-growth inhibitor.

PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy

Pembrolizumab

Keytruda
Releases an immune brake

Pembrolizumab blocks PD-1, a signal that can limit the activity of immune cells. This can help them attack cancer, but can also cause inflammation in healthy organs during treatment or after it ends.

PD-1 blocked↓Immune cells more active↓Healthy organs can inflame

What to watch for
Report new diarrhoea, cough, breathlessness, rash, headache, unusual weakness, thirst or changes in urine promptly.

Sources: nccp

Targeted therapy: VEGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Axitinib

Inlyta
Axitinib blocks vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFRs), signals that help tumours grow new blood vessels. Reducing this blood supply can slow cancer growth. The same action explains effects on blood pressure, kidneys, bleeding and wound healing.

What to watch for
Collect a prescription for diarrhoea medicine, usually loperamide, before starting. Follow the supplied instructions and contact the team for persistent diarrhoea, dizziness, poor intake or reduced urine.

Pembrolizumab every 21 days · axitinib twice daily

Individual prescribed course

Pembrolizumab 200 mg is given intravenously every 21 days. Axitinib starts at 5 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart, continuously, with or without food. Only your team may change the dose. Do not take an extra axitinib dose after a missed or vomited dose. Duration depends on benefit, tolerance and the agreed plan.

Pembrolizumab is held or stopped for toxicity, never dose-reduced. Axitinib has separate dose levels and holding rules. Liver injury or diarrhoea can involve either medicine, so do not attribute symptoms or change tablets without advice.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm infusion appointments, daily tablet dose, home blood-pressure instructions and urgent contact numbers.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Pembrolizumab runs over 30 minutes with observation according to local policy. Swallow axitinib tablets whole with water; follow the prescribed twice-daily dose and pharmacy instructions.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

Pembrolizumab needs suitable intravenous access. Axitinib is taken at home; no infusion pump is used.

STEP 4

Home support

Routine anti-sickness prophylaxis is not usually required; use the individual symptom plan. Bring an immunotherapy alert card. Follow blood-pressure treatment, skin/foot care and the diarrhoea plan. Steroids for suspected immune toxicity require medical direction. Review CYP3A-interacting medicines, grapefruit and herbal products with pharmacy.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

KEYNOTE-426 · first interim report, 2019

KEYNOTE-426 randomised 861 people to pembrolizumab/axitinib or sunitinib. In the first interim analysis, 12-month overall survival was 89.9% versus 78.3%; median progression-free survival was 15.1 versus 11.1 months.

These are the dated initial trial results, not a personalised prognosis or the latest follow-up. The trial compared with sunitinib, not every current first-line combination. Its treatment-duration rules do not automatically define the NCCP prescription.

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Help control advanced kidney cancer using immunotherapy and a blood-vessel-growth inhibitor. This NCCP combination is first-line treatment for advanced RCC with a clear-cell component. Treatment after progression, surgery or radiotherapy for selected problems is agreed individually. Ongoing hormone replacement may be needed after an immune effect, even after treatment stops.

When do I attend?

Pembrolizumab 200 mg is given intravenously every 21 days. Axitinib starts at 5 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart, continuously, with or without food. Only your team may change the dose. Do not take an extra axitinib dose after a missed or vomited dose. Duration depends on benefit, tolerance and the agreed plan.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
