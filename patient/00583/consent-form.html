Treatment context
Help control advanced kidney cancer using immunotherapy and a blood-vessel-growth inhibitor.
Schedule
Pembrolizumab 200 mg is given intravenously every 21 days. Axitinib starts at 5 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart, continuously, with or without food. Only your team may change the dose. Do not take an extra axitinib dose after a missed or vomited dose. Duration depends on benefit, tolerance and the agreed plan.

Further immune-related risks and lasting effects

Brain or nerve inflammation, severe weakness or paralysis (including encephalitis, Guillain–Barré or myasthenic syndromes)
Eye inflammation or changes in vision
Inflammation of the pancreas (pancreatitis)
New type 1 diabetes, which can present with dangerous blood-acid build-up (ketoacidosis)
Severe blistering or peeling skin reactions
Immune blood disorders, such as destruction of red blood cells; other rare inflammatory disorders can occur
Immune effects can start after treatment stops; some hormone deficiencies or other effects can be permanent
Treatment may need to be held or stopped; steroids, other immune-suppressing medicines, hospital care or lifelong hormone replacement may be needed
Immune reactions can become life-threatening or fatal; report new or persistent symptoms promptly

General treatment discussion

Possible short-term, long-term, unexpected and unlisted effects; benefits are not guaranteed.
Severe complications can require hospital admission and can rarely be fatal.
Infection / sepsis, allergic or infusion reactions and leakage outside a vein (extravasation) should be discussed where applicable to the medicines and route.
Pregnancy, contraception, breastfeeding, sexual health and fertility preservation should be discussed where relevant.
Treatment can be delayed, changed or stopped if risks outweigh benefit. Consent is voluntary and can be withdrawn.

2. Regimen / agent-specific material risks

Pembrolizumab

PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy
Immune inflammation can affect the lungs, bowel, liver, kidneys, skin, eyes, heart, muscles or nervous system and can be severe or fatal
Hormone-gland inflammation can affect the thyroid, pituitary or adrenal glands; diabetes can occur and hormone replacement may be lifelong
Severe skin reactions, including blistering or peeling; infusion reactions
Immune effects can start during treatment or months after stopping; tell every healthcare professional about immunotherapy

Axitinib

Targeted therapy: VEGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor
High blood pressure, sometimes severe; rare heart failure, heart attack, stroke or clots
Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, constipation, reduced appetite and weight loss
Sore mouth, taste changes, fatigue and voice changes
Painful, red or peeling palms and soles; rash and dry skin
Thyroid changes, liver-test abnormalities and protein in the urine
Bleeding, occasionally serious; rare bowel perforation or abnormal connections (fistulas)
Delayed wound healing; rare reversible brain swelling syndrome (PRES)
Anaemia and changes in other blood cells; possible fertility and pregnancy harm

Discuss the intended benefit, alternatives, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and individual priorities. Record the patient’s questions and the agreed plan. Pembrolizumab is held or stopped for toxicity, never dose-reduced. Axitinib has separate dose levels and holding rules. Liver injury or diarrhoea can involve either medicine, so do not attribute symptoms or change tablets without advice.
