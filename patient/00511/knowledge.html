When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Severe upper abdominal pain, jaundice, rapidly increasing abdominal swelling, persistent vomiting or bleeding needs urgent assessment. Report burning at the infusion site immediately.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Dacarbazine may be used to control metastatic sarcoma and help symptoms. Expected benefit depends on tumour subtype, previous treatment and your overall condition; your consultant explains why this option is appropriate.

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

Dacarbazine

DTIC
Damages cancer-cell deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)

Dacarbazine is converted in the body into substances that damage DNA and make it harder for cancer cells to divide. It also affects some healthy cells, including blood-forming cells. This explains why nausea prevention and blood-count checks are central to the plan.

Activated in the body↓DNA damage limits division↓Blood and liver checks

What to watch for
Report fever, bleeding, persistent vomiting, severe upper abdominal pain, new jaundice or rapidly increasing abdominal swelling urgently. Tell the nurse about burning at the infusion site.

Individual prescribed course

NCCP 00511 gives dacarbazine 1.2 g/m² on day 1 every 21 days. The source describes 4 cycles or treatment until progression or unacceptable toxicity; your consultant must specify the planned duration and review point.

This is dacarbazine 1.2 g/m² over 1 hour, not the 1 g/m² comparator used in some later trials. The current catalogue-linked NCCP document is version 2, reviewed March 2021 with a printed review-due date in March 2026; the treating team should confirm the current local protocol.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

NCCP lists no separate routine premedication or additional regimen-specific prophylaxis, but anti-sickness treatment is required. Bring an up-to-date medicine list and report new abdominal pain, jaundice, fever or bleeding before treatment.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Dacarbazine is infused intravenously over 1 hour with protection from light. The total visit also includes safety review, prescribed anti-sickness medicines and observation.

STEP 3

Intravenous access and line care

The team chooses suitable intravenous access and explains line care. Tell the nurse immediately about burning, pain, redness or swelling during an injection or drip. Do not wait for the visit to finish. If you have a port or peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC), agree dressing, flushing and removal arrangements.

STEP 4

Home support

This is a high nausea/vomiting-risk treatment. Follow the prescribed prevention, delayed-sickness and rescue plan, and contact the team if vomiting prevents drinking. Confirm any stomach protection and local mouth care. Avoid alcohol and have the team review potentially liver-toxic medicines and phenytoin.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Fertility and pregnancy precautions

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Avoid pregnancy and breastfeeding as directed. Your team must give written contraception and post-treatment precautions for every medicine, using current product information; the required duration may differ by medicine and between patients.

Your wider treatment plan

A sarcoma multidisciplinary team reviews the exact pathology, disease extent, prior treatment, fitness and your priorities. Surgery and radiotherapy may help local control in selected circumstances; their role and timing are decided separately. Chemotherapy does not automatically replace an operation or radiotherapy. Supportive and palliative care can be used alongside cancer treatment to help symptoms and daily life.

Delayed blood-count and liver effects

Counts can fall several weeks after dacarbazine. Report fever, bleeding or sudden illness throughout the cycle. Severe upper abdominal pain, yellow eyes or increasing abdominal swelling needs urgent assessment.

Your consent discussion

Use the general treatment risks and the separate medicine-specific risks together.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste change, diarrhoea or constipation; tiredness, hair and skin changes; infusion reactions and intravenous-line complications; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. Risks differ between medicines and people. The medicine-specific section explains the main additional risks for this regimen.

Historical EORTC phase II study of high-dose dacarbazine

A small historical single-arm EORTC study evaluated dacarbazine 1.2 g/m² every three weeks after prior chemotherapy and observed responses in some patients. It supports activity in that setting but does not show a survival advantage over another treatment.

The historical study used a 20-minute infusion, whereas NCCP 00511 specifies 1 hour. Its small, previously treated population and lack of a randomised comparator limit inference. Do not copy response or survival figures from studies using 1 g/m² or mixed dacarbazine doses as estimates for this exact regimen.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Are blood tests the same as checking whether treatment works?

No. Blood counts and organ tests mainly check safety. Your sarcoma team uses the clinical review and planned imaging to assess cancer control.
