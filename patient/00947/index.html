NCCP 00947 · version 1

Capivasertib + fulvestrant

Capivasertib is taken twice daily for four days, then stopped for three days, each week. Fulvestrant injections are given on Days 1 and 15 of the first 28-day cycle, then Day 1 of later cycles. Treatment continues while helpful and tolerated.

Understand the treatment. Keep the plan manageable.

This combination aims to control advanced breast cancer. Capivasertib blocks a growth signal inside cancer cells and fulvestrant reduces signalling through the oestrogen receptor. Tumour testing helps identify people whose cancer has a relevant pathway alteration.

Open your complete treatment reference
Targeted AKT inhibitor

Capivasertib

Blocks an internal growth signal called AKT
The tumour has a relevant pathway alteration↓Capivasertib blocks AKT signalling↓Growth and survival signals are reduced

Blocks AKT1, AKT2 and AKT3, proteins that help carry growth and survival signals inside cells. PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN alterations can activate this pathway. AKT also helps the body respond to insulin. Blocking it can raise blood sugar, which is why glucose checks start early.

Keep the glucose plan and report diarrhoea, rash or high-glucose symptoms early.

Oestrogen-receptor antagonist and degrader

Fulvestrant

Blocks and reduces oestrogen receptors
ER-positive cancer uses oestrogen signals↓Fulvestrant binds and reduces ER↓Less oestrogen-driven signalling reaches the cell

Attaches to oestrogen receptors and reduces their number, making it harder for oestrogen to stimulate hormone-sensitive breast cancer.

Report unusual injection-site bleeding, severe pain, numbness or pain travelling down the leg.

What each check is for

1Confirm the selected pathwayBefore starting

For adults whose ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer has a PIK3CA, AKT1 or PTEN change and has grown during or after hormone treatment. The team also checks general health and earlier treatment.

Ask / act: Confirm biomarker, endocrine history, ovarian suppression and individual suitability.

Sources: NCCP 00947

2Glucose and HbA1cBaseline; weeks 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8; then ongoing

Check blood count, kidney and liver function before treatment and each cycle. Check fasting glucose and HbA1c before starting. Fasting glucose is checked at weeks 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8, then monthly; ideally before the tablet dose on day 3 or 4 of a dosing week. HbA1c is checked every three months, with an extra week-4 check for diabetes, prediabetes or high baseline glucose. Diabetes, new high glucose or metformin initiation needs extra checks under a written plan.

Ask / act: Agree who performs, reviews and acts on every result; specify additional home checks.

Sources: NCCP 00947

3Symptoms and treatment changesThroughout, including tablet-free days

Seek urgent assessment for unusual thirst or urination, severe diarrhoea, inability to drink, or a new rash. Vomiting with abdominal pain, breathing difficulty, fruity breath, confusion or marked sleepiness can signal ketoacidosis: seek emergency help without waiting for a high glucose reading. Blistering or peeling skin also needs urgent help.

Ask / act: Use the urgent contact plan; review the actual capivasertib dose and fulvestrant schedule separately.

Sources: NCCP 00947

Support throughout treatment

Glucose plan

Agree when to check glucose and when to call. Use your written diabetes-medicine plan on tablet-taking and tablet-free days; do not change existing diabetes medicines yourself.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Have the prescribed diarrhoea medicine available. Follow the agreed early-treatment instructions and contact the team for persistent symptoms, dizziness or reduced urine.

Skin and injections

Report rash early and use skin treatments as advised. Tell the injector about anticoagulants, bruising and any previous leg nerve symptoms.
