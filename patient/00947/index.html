Capivasertib + fulvestrant

This combination aims to control advanced breast cancer. Capivasertib blocks a growth signal inside cancer cells and fulvestrant reduces signalling through the oestrogen receptor. Tumour testing helps identify people whose cancer has a relevant pathway alteration.

Capivasertib is taken twice daily for four days, then stopped for three days, each week. Fulvestrant injections are given on Days 1 and 15 of the first 28-day cycle, then Day 1 of later cycles. Treatment continues while helpful and tolerated.

Tests and checks

Check blood count, kidney and liver function before treatment and each cycle. Check fasting glucose and glycated haemoglobin (longer-term blood glucose) (HbA1c) before starting. Fasting glucose is checked at weeks 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8, then monthly; ideally before the tablet dose on day 3 or 4 of a dosing week. HbA1c is checked every three months, with an extra week-4 check for diabetes, prediabetes or high baseline glucose. Diabetes, new high glucose or metformin initiation needs extra checks under a written plan.

Between treatment visits

Tablet days and injection days follow the prescribed regimen. Side effects can occur at any time and are not predicted by the width of a band.

Tablet-free days still require symptom reporting and the agreed glucose checks. The treatment calendar does not authorise a dose or restart.

Seek urgent assessment for unusual thirst or urination, severe diarrhoea, inability to drink, or a new rash. Vomiting with abdominal pain, breathing difficulty, fruity breath, confusion or marked sleepiness can signal ketoacidosis: seek emergency help without waiting for a high glucose reading. Blistering or peeling skin also needs urgent help. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms: 112 / 999.
