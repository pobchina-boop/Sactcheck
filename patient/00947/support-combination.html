Additional phase-specific support

  • None usually required. Do not import cytotoxic antiemetic dexamethasone/aprepitant routines into this regimen.
  • Report new rash promptly. Emollients, antihistamines and topical steroids depend on grade and dermatology advice. Early dermatology advice is recommended. Ongoing antihistamines and/or topical steroids may be considered as secondary prevention after persistent or previous grade 3 rash; this is not mandatory routine prophylaxis for everyone.
  • Use the individual written glucose and diabetes-medicine plan. The source suggests metformin on capivasertib dosing days and withdrawal when capivasertib is withdrawn unless otherwise clinically indicated. This must be reconciled with pre-existing diabetes treatment: do not tell all patients to stop their usual diabetes medicines on the three capivasertib-free days. Watch for low glucose as well as high glucose on non-dosing days.
  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors should be avoided where possible; strong/moderate inhibitors require capivasertib dose review. Strong/moderate CYP3A4 inducers should be avoided. Reconcile medicines affecting narrow-therapeutic-index CYP or UGT substrates and sensitive transport substrates. Dose decisions must preserve earlier toxicity reductions.
  • Agree glucose equipment, testing windows, result review and urgent contacts before the first dose.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
