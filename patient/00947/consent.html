Capivasertib + fulvestrant

Capivasertib is taken twice daily for four days, then stopped for three days, each week. Fulvestrant injections are given on Days 1 and 15 of the first 28-day cycle, then Day 1 of later cycles. Treatment continues while helpful and tolerated.

Purpose, setting and alternatives

This combination aims to control advanced breast cancer. Capivasertib blocks a growth signal inside cancer cells and fulvestrant reduces signalling through the oestrogen receptor. Tumour testing helps identify people whose cancer has a relevant pathway alteration. For adults whose ER-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer has a PIK3CA, AKT1 or PTEN change and has grown during or after hormone treatment. The team also checks general health and earlier treatment.

Material risks and support

  • Diarrhoea, dehydration and salt imbalance
  • Raised blood sugar or diabetes; rarely a dangerous ketone/acidity problem called diabetic ketoacidosis
  • Rash, itch or severe skin reactions
  • Nausea, vomiting, sore mouth, reduced appetite and tiredness
  • Changes in blood counts or kidney/liver tests, assessed by the team
  • Pain, bruising or bleeding at the injection sites; rarely nerve injury symptoms
  • Hot flushes and joint or muscle discomfort
  • Nausea, tiredness and changes in liver tests
  • Allergic reactions; discuss pregnancy prevention and fertility

Discuss glucose/diabetes and ketoacidosis, diarrhoea/dehydration, skin reactions and injection risks. Agree the tablet pattern, glucose plan and contact route. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are excluded. Contraception advice must cover the longer fulvestrant period: two years after its last dose for women; capivasertib male advice extends sixteen weeks after stopping. Discuss fertility and the individual plan.

Monitoring and understanding

Check blood count, kidney and liver function before treatment and each cycle. Check fasting glucose and HbA1c before starting. Fasting glucose is checked at weeks 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8, then monthly; ideally before the tablet dose on day 3 or 4 of a dosing week. HbA1c is checked every three months, with an extra week-4 check for diabetes, prediabetes or high baseline glucose. Diabetes, new high glucose or metformin initiation needs extra checks under a written plan.
