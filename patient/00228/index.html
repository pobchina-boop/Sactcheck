How your medicines work

Chemotherapy

Eribulin

Stops microtubules growing
Cell-division structures→Growth blocked→Cell division disrupted

Eribulin blocks growth of the tiny structures cells need to divide. It can slow cancer growth, but can also affect blood cells and nerves.

Report numbness, weakness or difficulty with walking or hand tasks before the next dose. Fever or sudden illness needs immediate advice; palpitations or fainting need urgent assessment.

Practical treatment guide

Your infusion and access plan

Eribulin on Days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle. Day 15 is a planned treatment-free week. Treatment continues while beneficial and tolerated.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the indication, baseline bloods, required cardiac checks, medicines review and consent before the first infusion.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Eribulin is given by IV infusion over 5 minutes; blood review, preparation and access make the full visit longer. Staff observe for reactions. Report burning, pain, swelling or leakage at the infusion site immediately.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit length

Eribulin is given by IV infusion over 5 minutes; blood review, preparation and access make the full visit longer. A port is optional access, not a requirement for a take-home pump. This regimen has no routine ambulatory pump.

Premedication

No routine hypersensitivity premedication is specified. Low-risk antiemetic support is reviewed separately, including QT interactions.

Support around treatment

Supportive medicines

No routine hypersensitivity premedication is specified. Low-risk antiemetic support is reviewed separately, including QT interactions.

Everyday care

Keep gently active as tolerated, pace rest and activity, and report nerve symptoms before the next dose.

Tests and appointments

Ask who books each test, when it is due and who reviews the result. Tell the team if an expected appointment has not arrived.

Correct medicine and schedule

EU dose labels use eribulin base. 1.23 mg/m² base corresponds to 1.4 mg/m² mesylate in some trial reports; these are not two alternative doses.

Review the full medicine plan

Low emetic risk; choose preventive/rescue medicines after considering QT prolongation and interactions. No routine hypersensitivity premedication is usually required. Review potassium/magnesium and correct abnormalities; monitor ECG when cardiac/QT risks or symptoms warrant it. Severe neutropenia may be managed with G-CSF under the treating team. Avoid driving or machinery when tired or dizzy.

Do not wait for the next appointment

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
