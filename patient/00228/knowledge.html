When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. New chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, fever or sudden illness needs urgent assessment. Report worsening numbness, severe constipation, mouth ulcers or inability to drink promptly.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

How this medicine works

The aim is to control the cancer and its symptoms. The team reviews benefit, side effects and your priorities before continuing.

Chemotherapy

Eribulin

Halaven · eribulin base
Stops microtubules growing

Eribulin blocks growth of the tiny structures cells need to divide. It can slow cancer growth, but can also affect blood cells and nerves.

Cell-division structures↓Growth blocked↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report numbness, weakness or difficulty with walking or hand tasks before the next dose. Fever or sudden illness needs immediate advice; palpitations or fainting need urgent assessment.

Practical treatment guide

Your infusion and access plan

Eribulin on Days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle. Day 15 is a planned treatment-free week. Treatment continues while beneficial and tolerated.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the indication, baseline bloods, required cardiac checks, medicines review and consent before the first infusion.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Eribulin is given by IV infusion over 5 minutes; blood review, preparation and access make the full visit longer. Staff observe for reactions. Report burning, pain, swelling or leakage at the infusion site immediately.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit length

Eribulin is given by IV infusion over 5 minutes; blood review, preparation and access make the full visit longer. A port is optional access, not a requirement for a take-home pump. This regimen has no routine ambulatory pump.

Premedication

No routine hypersensitivity premedication is specified. Low-risk antiemetic support is reviewed separately, including QT interactions.

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines

No routine hypersensitivity premedication is specified. Low-risk antiemetic support is reviewed separately, including QT interactions.

Everyday care

Keep gently active as tolerated, pace rest and activity, and report nerve symptoms before the next dose.

Tests and appointments

Ask who books each test, when it is due and who reviews the result. Tell the team if an expected appointment has not arrived.

Correct medicine and schedule

EU dose labels use eribulin base. 1.23 mg/m² base corresponds to 1.4 mg/m² mesylate in some trial reports; these are not two alternative doses.

Review the full medicine plan

Low emetic risk; choose preventive/rescue medicines after considering QT prolongation and interactions. No routine hypersensitivity premedication is usually required. Review potassium/magnesium and correct abnormalities; monitor ECG when cardiac/QT risks or symptoms warrant it. Severe neutropenia may be managed with G-CSF under the treating team. Avoid driving or machinery when tired or dizzy.

Treatment reference · 01

The exact treatment being discussed

The aim is to control the cancer and its symptoms. The team reviews benefit, side effects and your priorities before continuing.

Indication

Advanced breast cancer after at least one chemotherapy regimen for advanced disease, with prior anthracycline and taxane unless unsuitable; or adult unresectable liposarcoma after anthracycline unless unsuitable.

Sources: nccp

Dose and formulation

1.23 mg/m² eribulin base. EU dose labels use eribulin base. 1.23 mg/m² base corresponds to 1.4 mg/m² mesylate in some trial reports; these are not two alternative doses.

Sources: nccp

Evidence limits

In heavily pretreated advanced breast cancer, eribulin helped people live longer on average than the comparison treatments chosen by their doctors. This does not predict an individual result or establish the same benefit in every treatment line or another cancer. EU dose is 1.23 mg/m² eribulin base; 1.4 mg/m² mesylate in trial reports is the equivalent amount, not an alternative numeric prescription. Day 8 may be delayed or omitted after review.

Sources: trial

Eribulin versus dacarbazine: advanced liposarcoma evidence

452 adults with advanced liposarcoma or leiomyosarcoma after at least two systemic regimens including anthracycline, using Days 1 and 8 every 21 days. Separate sarcoma trial and its liposarcoma subgroup support the liposarcoma indication; breast-trial benefit figures do not apply. The licensed liposarcoma indication must not be expanded to all sarcomas because the parent trial also enrolled leiomyosarcoma. Use current NCCP eligibility and EMA indication wording. Do not apply breast imaging or CA 15-3/CEA pathways to liposarcoma.

Sources: ERIBULIN_SARCOMA · ERIBULIN_LPS

Treatment reference · 02

Checks throughout treatment

Ask who arranges each test and who reviews it.

Bloods and symptoms

FBC, renal and liver profile before starting and at the start of each cycle. The EMA product information specifies FBC before every dose, including Day 8; additional renal / liver tests follow clinical need.

Sources: nccp

ECG and heart function

ECG before starting and during treatment when there is cardiac disease, a slow / abnormal rhythm, QT-prolonging medicines or electrolyte disturbance. Correct low potassium, calcium or magnesium before treatment; repeat monitoring follows risk and findings. No fixed routine echo interval is specified for eribulin alone. Cardiac symptoms or risk prompt individual assessment. If trastuzumab is separately prescribed, use its own cardiac-monitoring plan.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Cancer response

For metastatic breast cancer, ESMO recommends response evaluation generally every 2–4 months, adjusted to disease, treatment and symptoms. Agree the scan type, due window and result-review appointment; symptoms can prompt earlier assessment. For the other cancer indications on this card, use the cancer-specific monitoring plan; the breast interval is not transferred automatically.

Sources: nccp · metastatic

Markers

Tumour markers, when useful for the cancer being treated, supplement clinical assessment and imaging. A marker result alone should not determine treatment failure or a treatment change.

Treatment reference · 04

How other care fits around chemotherapy

There is no automatic surgery, radiotherapy or maintenance phase in this card.

Surgery and radiation

These are advanced-disease regimens. Surgery or radiotherapy is used only for a defined local or symptom-control purpose. The oncology and radiation teams decide whether chemotherapy continues, pauses or precedes radiation; no routine overlap is implied.

Sources: metastatic

Bone health

DEXA is not a routine test for this chemotherapy alone. Assess fracture risk and bone density when indicated by endocrine treatment, ovarian suppression or other clinical risks; repeat according to the relevant plan.

Sources: bone

Changing treatment

Discuss benefit, adverse effects, other treatment options and your preferences at each review. New symptoms can bring review forward.

Questions worth asking

Which treatment days and breaks should I expect?

Eribulin on Days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle. Day 15 is a planned treatment-free week. Treatment continues while beneficial and tolerated.

When do I need blood tests, an ECG or echo?

FBC, renal and liver profile before starting and at the start of each cycle. The EMA product information specifies FBC before every dose, including Day 8; additional renal / liver tests follow clinical need. ECG before starting and during treatment when there is cardiac disease, a slow / abnormal rhythm, QT-prolonging medicines or electrolyte disturbance. When will we know whether treatment is helping?

For metastatic breast cancer, ESMO recommends response evaluation generally every 2–4 months, adjusted to disease, treatment and symptoms. Agree the scan type, due window and result-review appointment; symptoms can prompt earlier assessment. For the other cancer indications on this card, use the cancer-specific monitoring plan; the breast interval is not transferred automatically.

Where do surgery or radiotherapy fit?

These are advanced-disease regimens. Surgery or radiotherapy is used only for a defined local or symptom-control purpose. The oncology and radiation teams decide whether chemotherapy continues, pauses or precedes radiation; no routine overlap is implied.

Do tumour markers replace scans?

Tumour markers, when useful for the cancer being treated, supplement clinical assessment and imaging. A marker result alone should not determine treatment failure or a treatment change.

Does a port mean a pump at home?

No. A port is a route into a vein. Staff disconnect the infusion and remove the port needle after the visit when appropriate; removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure.
