Additional phase-specific support

  • This is a low nausea/vomiting-risk regimen. Agree individual anti-sickness and bowel support; National Cancer Control Programme lists no usual separate premedication. White-cell growth-factor support may be chosen for severe neutropenia, but there is no universal routine course.
  • Bring all medicines and report numbness, weakness, constipation, infection or fainting before treatment. Review medicines that affect heart rhythm. Arrange written contraception instructions and avoid breastfeeding during treatment.
  • The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements.
  • An improved test result does not authorise restarting after a safety hold; confirm the new prescription.
  • Metoclopramide: adult maximum 10 mg up to three times daily (30 mg/day) and maximum treatment duration 5 days under current European Medicines Agency/Irish product information; avoid prolonged repeated use without review.
  • Review heart-rhythm risk and electrolytes before choosing anti-sickness medicines that can affect the QT electrical interval.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
