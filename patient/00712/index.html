How your medicines work

Immunotherapy

Nivolumab

Blocks the PD-1 immune checkpoint
T cell→PD-1 blocked→Immune response

Nivolumab binds PD-1 on T cells. It prevents signals that can reduce the immune attack on cancer. This can also let immune cells inflame healthy tissues. It is infused every 21 days in this plan.

New breathing, bowel, skin, hormone or other symptoms need prompt advice during or after treatment.

Immunotherapy

Ipilimumab

Blocks the CTLA-4 immune checkpoint
T cell→CTLA-4 blocked→Immune activity

Ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4, another control on T-cell activity. It works alongside nivolumab through a different immune checkpoint. It is given every 42 days, so it is absent from each Day 22 visit. Healthy organs can also become inflamed.

Report diarrhoea, tummy pain, rash, severe tiredness, headache or other new symptoms promptly. Keep your immune alert card.

Chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Disrupts microtubules and cell division
Microtubules→Division disrupted→Cancer cells affected

Paclitaxel stabilises microtubules, structures cells use during division. It can slow cancer growth but also harm healthy nerves, blood-forming cells and hair follicles. There are two planned doses.

Tell the nurse immediately about infusion symptoms. Report numbness, tingling, fever, painful muscles, mouth sores or diarrhoea.

Chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Damages deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)
DNA→Platinum links→Copying disrupted

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that hinder copying and repair. Healthy tissues can also be harmed. Its dose depends on target exposure (AUC) and measured or estimated kidney function; AUC is not a fixed number of milligrams. There are two planned doses in this regimen.

Report fever, bleeding, nausea, hearing changes or numbness. Tell the nurse immediately about possible allergy during the infusion.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Visits and duration

Cycle 1 lasts 42 days: nivolumab, ipilimumab, paclitaxel and carboplatin on Day 1; nivolumab, paclitaxel and carboplatin on Day 22. These are the only two planned chemotherapy administrations. From Cycle 2, nivolumab is given on Days 1 and 22 and ipilimumab on Day 1. Continue while appropriate until progression, unacceptable toxicity or up to 24 months without progression. Your team reviews the plan and may delay or stop treatment.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Nivolumab over 30 minutes, then ipilimumab over 30 minutes on Day 1 only. Paclitaxel over three hours, then carboplatin over 30 minutes on both Cycle 1 treatment days. Ipilimumab vital signs are checked every 30 minutes during infusion and for an hour afterwards. Blood tests, preparation and observation add to time in the unit.

STEP 3

Medicines and monitoring

Blood count, kidney and liver tests before each treatment; glucose and thyroid tests before each 42-day cycle. Cortisol and neuropathy checks when indicated. Paclitaxel premedication includes corticosteroid and antihistamine treatment, with H2-blocker decisions made by the team. The source allows consultant-directed changes; ask for a written plan for each of the two chemotherapy visits.

STEP 4

Transport and support

Arrange travel and help if needed. Keep urgent contacts and alert information with you.

Support around treatment

Cycle 1 Days 1 and 22 · chemotherapy-containing visits

Cycle 1 lasts 42 days: nivolumab, ipilimumab, paclitaxel and carboplatin on Day 1; nivolumab, paclitaxel and carboplatin on Day 22. These are the only two planned chemotherapy administrations. From Cycle 2, nivolumab is given on Days 1 and 22 and ipilimumab on Day 1. Paclitaxel premedication includes corticosteroid and antihistamine treatment, with H2-blocker decisions made by the team. The source allows consultant-directed changes; ask for a written plan for each of the two chemotherapy visits. The team must reconcile paclitaxel allergy-prevention steroids with anti-sickness steroids and the aprepitant interaction into one prescription. Do not add courses or take leftover steroids yourself. Use dedicated carboplatin AUC≥4 NCIS row; no routine olanzapine in that row. Myalgia/arthralgia may need an individual analgesia plan. Both immune medicines can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Keep your immune alert card and report new symptoms promptly, including for at least five months after the last dose.

Cycle 2 onwards · Day 1 nivolumab + ipilimumab

Both immune medicines can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Keep your immune alert card and report new symptoms promptly, including for at least five months after the last dose. Chemotherapy is complete; review and stop induction prophylaxis rather than automatically repeating it.

Cycle 2 onwards · Day 22 nivolumab alone

Both immune medicines can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Keep your immune alert card and report new symptoms promptly, including for at least five months after the last dose.
