01 / treatment identity

What this treatment is doing

A 14-day chemotherapy regimen used across several gastrointestinal cancer treatment plans. Your oncology team should confirm your treatment purpose and planned number of cycles.

Oxaliplatin

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

Folinic acid

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

5-fluorouracil (5-FU)

Part of this regimen. Your team will explain the dose, route and role in your treatment.

Regimen-specific point
Cold sensitivity after oxaliplatin is distinctive. Avoid cold drinks, freezer items and cold air when these trigger symptoms.
02 / treatment journey

How the schedule fits together

Day 1

Oxaliplatin and folinic acid are given by drip, then 5-FU is started.

Days 1-3

A portable pump gives 5-FU continuously for about 46 hours.

Around day 3

Pump disconnect. Follow your local line and pump instructions.

Days 4-14

Recovery and monitoring. Symptoms can occur on any day.

Day 15

Next cycle if review and blood tests allow.

A delay, dose change or omitted medicine can be appropriate. Your team should explain what changed and why.

03 / visual side-effect guide

Where changes may appear

This larger body map is a memory aid. The cards below are separated by the medicines or treatment phase that make each risk relevant. A picture cannot diagnose the cause of a symptom: report new or worsening changes to your oncology team.

Oxaliplatin

Peripheral neuropathy / cold sensitivity

Tingling, numbness or pain, often triggered by cold. Avoid cold exposure if advised and report symptoms before the next dose.

Acute throat or jaw sensation

A tight throat, jaw discomfort or difficulty taking a breath can occur soon after oxaliplatin. Tell staff immediately; severe breathing difficulty is an emergency.

Hypersensitivity reaction

Flushing, rash, wheeze, swelling or dizziness during/after infusion needs immediate assessment.

5-FU + leucovorin

Diarrhoea and mouth inflammation

Persistent diarrhoea, mouth ulcers or inability to drink can lead to dehydration and needs prompt oncology advice.

Hand-foot skin reaction

Soreness, redness, peeling or pain of palms/soles should be reported.

Rare heart toxicity

New chest pain, pressure, palpitations or breathlessness during 5-FU needs urgent assessment.

Regimen-wide

Low blood counts / infection

Fever, shivering or sudden illness can occur when white cells are low. Follow your oncology SOS temperature instructions.

Severe fluoropyrimidine toxicity

Unexpectedly severe diarrhoea, mouth ulcers, confusion or rapid deterioration can signal serious 5-FU toxicity and needs urgent review.

This is a regimen-specific safety summary, not an exhaustive adverse-effect list. Follow the current treatment plan, medicine leaflets and your oncology team’s SOS instructions.

04 / monitoring and support

What helps make treatment safer

Before and during treatment

  • DPD status is checked before fluoropyrimidine treatment.
  • Before each cycle: blood count, kidney/liver bloods and assessment of nerve symptoms.
  • Follow your own labelled anti-sickness, mouth-care and diarrhoea medicines.

Bring to each review

  • Your current medicines, including non-prescription products.
  • A note of new symptoms, when they started and what they stopped you doing.
  • Any temperature, blood-pressure or bowel-count readings your team asked you to record.
  • Questions about the purpose of treatment, scan/review timing or changes to the plan.
05 / when to call

Do not wait for the next appointment

Use your oncology SOS instructionsCall your oncology SOS service for fever or shivers, sudden illness, 4 or more diarrhoea episodes in a day, vomiting despite medicines, mouth sores stopping fluids, bleeding, new chest pain or breathlessness. Severe chest pain or breathing difficulty: call 112/999.

If your team has given you different local thresholds or emergency instructions, follow those instructions.
