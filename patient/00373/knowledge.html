When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact the team promptly for new blurred or lost vision, eye pain, breathlessness or ankle swelling, palpitations or fainting, severe muscle pain or weakness with dark urine, unusual bleeding, severe diarrhoea, a blistering rash or yellow skin or eyes.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

To control advanced BRAF V600-mutated melanoma by blocking BRAF and MEK in the same growth pathway; the two medicines have different daily schedules.

MEK targeted treatment

Cobimetinib

Cotellic
Blocks a second growth signal

Cobimetinib inhibits MEK, further along the growth-signalling pathway affected by BRAF. It is combined with vemurafenib. Cobimetinib has a seven-day planned break in each 28-day cycle; vemurafenib continues through that break unless the team holds it.

Blocks a second growth signal↓Treatment plan and safety checks↓Report new symptoms early

What to watch for
Report new vision changes, muscle pain with dark urine, breathlessness, ankle swelling or unusual bleeding.

Sources: nccp · cotellic-pi

BRAF targeted treatment

Vemurafenib

Zelboraf
Blocks an altered BRAF signal

Vemurafenib blocks the altered BRAF V600 protein that can drive melanoma-cell growth. A confirmed tumour BRAF V600 result is needed. Healthy tissues can also be affected; tablets need the same careful monitoring as treatment given in hospital.

What to watch for
Report new skin growths, sunburn-like reactions, eye pain or blurred vision, palpitations or fainting.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1–21: At home each day

Usual starting doses are cobimetinib 60 mg once daily on days 1–21 and vemurafenib 960 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart, continuously. Cobimetinib can be taken with or without food. Avoid regularly taking both daily vemurafenib doses on an empty stomach. Swallow both medicines whole and follow the separate labels. For cobimetinib, take a missed dose only if at least 12 hours remain before the next dose. For vemurafenib the interval is at least four hours. Otherwise skip the missed dose; do not double up. If you vomit after either medicine, do not take an extra dose. A hold or reduction may apply to one medicine or both: obtain a written component-specific restart plan.

Day 22–28: Planned cobimetinib break

Vemurafenib continues twice daily. The seven-day break applies to cobimetinib only. Record any additional treatment hold separately and confirm the next cobimetinib start with the team.

Taking or receiving treatment

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines and symptom plan

NCCP lists each component as minimal-to-low emetogenic risk; follow local and individual anti-sickness policy. Agree a diarrhoea, fluid-intake and nausea plan; contact the team if symptoms persist or you cannot drink. Check all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines for interactions before taking them. The seven-day cobimetinib break is planned; extra breaks or restarting doses require the team’s instructions.

Skin and daily care

Vemurafenib can make skin very sensitive to sunlight. Use UVA/UVB protection, protective clothing and SPF 30 or higher with lip protection. Continue skin checks for new growths or changing lesions. Do not assume combining the medicines removes the need for skin surveillance.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

The cobimetinib source specifies two effective contraception methods during treatment and for at least three months afterwards; vemurafenib requires at least six months after its last dose. The combined plan therefore needs to cover the longer six-month vemurafenib interval and account for reduced hormonal-contraceptive effectiveness. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding before treatment.

Your diagnosis and stage

Keep the pathology report, original melanoma site, stage and known sites of spread. This guide concerns advanced disease that cannot be removed completely by surgery or has spread; it does not describe a postoperative recurrence-prevention course.

The tumour test

A BRAF V600 test helps identify whether BRAF-targeted medicines are an option. A tumour mutation does not by itself mean an inherited family condition. Keep the exact result and ask how it changes your choices.

Your medicines and overall plan

How treatment works

Cobimetinib inhibits MEK, further along the growth-signalling pathway affected by BRAF. It is combined with vemurafenib. Cobimetinib has a seven-day planned break in each 28-day cycle; vemurafenib continues through that break unless the team holds it. Sources: nccp · cotellic-pi · zelboraf-pi

Important boundaries

Only cobimetinib has the planned days 22–28 break. Vemurafenib continues. ECG measures rhythm; an echocardiogram or other LVEF test measures pumping function, and one does not replace the other. Radiation treatment needs a separate coordinated plan.

Sources: nccp · cotellic-pi · zelboraf-pi

Evidence and choices

Median progression-free survival: 12.3 versus 7.2 months (HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.46–0.72). Median overall survival: 22.3 versus 17.4 months (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.55–0.90). These are group results from that study, not a prediction for you. Direct comparison was against vemurafenib alone. It does not rank this regimen against other BRAF/MEK pairs or immunotherapy.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests, results and treatment decisions

Safety tests and scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Confirm BRAF V600, blood count, kidney and liver function, LDH, and baseline CPK (muscle enzyme) and creatinine. Check ECG, LVEF (heart pumping function), medicines and skin; record vision symptoms.

Sources: nccp · cotellic-pi · zelboraf-pi

During treatment

Blood count, kidney/liver tests and LDH before each cycle. Current Cotellic product information adds CPK and creatinine monthly, and LVEF after the first month then at least every three months until treatment stops. ECG is every four weeks for the first 12 weeks, then every 12 weeks and after dose changes. Arrange monthly skin checks under current vemurafenib product information. New visual symptoms need prompt eye assessment; symptoms can require extra tests between scheduled checks.

Sources: nccp · cotellic-pi · zelboraf-pi

What a result means

Ask which result is abnormal, whether the finding needs a repeat test and whether treatment is held, changed or stopped. Do not use a reference range or one normal result to decide independently that a dose is safe.

Sources: nccp · cotellic-pi · zelboraf-pi

Response and follow-up

After restarting reduced-dose cobimetinib, NCCP and product information specify LVEF at two, four, ten and sixteen weeks, then as indicated. Vemurafenib skin surveillance continues up to six months after stopping or until another anticancer therapy starts. Its other-cancer surveillance includes head-and-neck checks and chest CT, coordinated separately from melanoma-response imaging. A routine cobimetinib break is not evidence of toxicity recovery.

After restarting reduced-dose cobimetinib, NCCP and product information specify LVEF at two, four, ten and sixteen weeks, then as indicated. Vemurafenib skin surveillance continues up to six months after stopping or until another anticancer therapy starts. Its other-cancer surveillance includes head-and-neck checks and chest CT, coordinated separately from melanoma-response imaging. A routine cobimetinib break is not evidence of toxicity recovery.
