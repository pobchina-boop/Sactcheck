When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact the team promptly for new blurred or lost vision, eye pain, breathlessness or ankle swelling, palpitations or fainting, severe muscle pain or weakness with dark urine, unusual bleeding, severe diarrhoea, a blistering rash or yellow skin or eyes.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

To control advanced BRAF V600-mutated melanoma by blocking BRAF and MEK in the same growth pathway; the two medicines have different daily schedules.

MEK targeted treatment

Cobimetinib

Cotellic
Blocks a second growth signal

Cobimetinib inhibits MEK, further along the growth-signalling pathway affected by BRAF. It is combined with vemurafenib. Cobimetinib has a seven-day planned break in each 28-day cycle; vemurafenib continues through that break unless the team holds it.

Blocks a second growth signal↓Treatment plan and safety checks↓Report new symptoms early

What to watch for
Report new vision changes, muscle pain with dark urine, breathlessness, ankle swelling or unusual bleeding.

BRAF targeted treatment

Vemurafenib

Zelboraf
Blocks an altered BRAF signal

Vemurafenib blocks the altered BRAF V600 protein that can drive melanoma-cell growth. A confirmed tumour BRAF V600 result is needed. Healthy tissues can also be affected; tablets need the same careful monitoring as treatment given in hospital.

What to watch for
Report new skin growths, sunburn-like reactions, eye pain or blurred vision, palpitations or fainting.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1–21: At home each day

Usual starting doses are cobimetinib 60 mg once daily on days 1–21 and vemurafenib 960 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart, continuously. Cobimetinib can be taken with or without food. Avoid regularly taking both daily vemurafenib doses on an empty stomach. Swallow both medicines whole and follow the separate labels. For cobimetinib, take a missed dose only if at least 12 hours remain before the next dose. For vemurafenib the interval is at least four hours. Otherwise skip the missed dose; do not double up. If you vomit after either medicine, do not take an extra dose. A hold or reduction may apply to one medicine or both: obtain a written component-specific restart plan.

Day 22–28: Planned cobimetinib break

Vemurafenib continues twice daily. The seven-day break applies to cobimetinib only. Record any additional treatment hold separately and confirm the next cobimetinib start with the team.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Taking or receiving treatment

Usual starting doses are cobimetinib 60 mg once daily on days 1–21 and vemurafenib 960 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart, continuously. Cobimetinib can be taken with or without food. Avoid regularly taking both daily vemurafenib doses on an empty stomach. Swallow both medicines whole and follow the separate labels.

STEP 2

Missed, vomited or held treatment

For cobimetinib, take a missed dose only if at least 12 hours remain before the next dose. For vemurafenib the interval is at least four hours. Otherwise skip the missed dose; do not double up. If you vomit after either medicine, do not take an extra dose. A hold or reduction may apply to one medicine or both: obtain a written component-specific restart plan.

STEP 3

Appointments and supply

Before each visit, check four things: the blood-test booking, how you will get there, your medicine supply and any help you need. Ask who will review the results and tell you whether the next dose can go ahead.

STEP 4

Paper copies and contacts

Keep the handbook, short guide and treatment card in one place. Ask your team to fill in the real daytime and emergency numbers and print a replacement when the plan changes. A phone is optional.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines and symptom plan

NCCP lists each component as minimal-to-low emetogenic risk; follow local and individual anti-sickness policy. Agree a diarrhoea, fluid-intake and nausea plan; contact the team if symptoms persist or you cannot drink. Check all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines for interactions before taking them. The seven-day cobimetinib break is planned; extra breaks or restarting doses require the team’s instructions.

Skin and daily care

Vemurafenib can make skin very sensitive to sunlight. Use UVA/UVB protection, protective clothing and sun protection factor (SPF) 30 or higher with lip protection. Continue skin checks for new growths or changing lesions. Do not assume combining the medicines removes the need for skin surveillance.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

The cobimetinib source specifies two effective contraception methods during treatment and for at least three months afterwards; vemurafenib requires at least six months after its last dose. The combined plan therefore needs to cover the longer six-month vemurafenib interval and account for reduced hormonal-contraceptive effectiveness. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding before treatment.

Treatment reference · 01

Understanding your diagnosis and treatment goal

Use your own pathology, stage and agreed plan alongside this guide.

Your diagnosis and stage

Record where the melanoma began, its pathology result and the sites involved now. This pathway treats melanoma that cannot be fully removed or has spread. Treatment after complete removal has a different aim and a separate plan.

The tumour test

The laboratory checks a sample of the melanoma for a BRAF V600 change. The result can identify a target for certain medicines. A change found in the tumour does not, by itself, establish an inherited condition.

Scans and local treatments

Add planned scans, any operation and any radiotherapy to the same calendar. Ask what each part is intended to achieve and which team arranges it.

New changes still matter

Tell your team about a changing mole, a persistent sore, a new lump or an unexplained symptom. Improvement in a known tumour does not explain a new problem elsewhere.

Treatment reference · 02

Your medicines and overall plan

To control advanced BRAF V600-mutated melanoma by blocking BRAF and MEK in the same growth pathway; the two medicines have different daily schedules.

The schedule

Every 28 days: cobimetinib once daily on days 1–21, then seven days off; vemurafenib twice daily continues on all 28 days unless held by the team.

How treatment works

Cobimetinib inhibits MEK, further along the growth-signalling pathway affected by BRAF. It is combined with vemurafenib. Cobimetinib has a seven-day planned break in each 28-day cycle; vemurafenib continues through that break unless the team holds it. Vemurafenib blocks the altered BRAF V600 protein that can drive melanoma-cell growth. A confirmed tumour BRAF V600 result is needed. Healthy tissues can also be affected; tablets need the same careful monitoring as treatment given in hospital.

Important boundaries

Only cobimetinib has the planned days 22–28 break. Vemurafenib continues. ECG measures rhythm; an echocardiogram or other LVEF test measures pumping function, and one does not replace the other. Radiation treatment needs a separate coordinated plan.

Evidence and choices

Median progression-free survival: 12.3 versus 7.2 months (HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.46–0.72). Median overall survival: 22.3 versus 17.4 months (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.55–0.90). These are group results from that study, not a prediction for you. Direct comparison was against vemurafenib alone. It does not rank this regimen against other BRAF/MEK pairs or immunotherapy.

Treatment reference · 03

Taking or receiving treatment safely

Keep the written prescription and actual treatment record together.

Administration

Usual starting doses are cobimetinib 60 mg once daily on days 1–21 and vemurafenib 960 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart, continuously. Cobimetinib can be taken with or without food. Avoid regularly taking both daily vemurafenib doses on an empty stomach. Swallow both medicines whole and follow the separate labels.

Missed or held treatment

For cobimetinib, take a missed dose only if at least 12 hours remain before the next dose. For vemurafenib the interval is at least four hours. Otherwise skip the missed dose; do not double up. If you vomit after either medicine, do not take an extra dose. A hold or reduction may apply to one medicine or both: obtain a written component-specific restart plan.

Other medicines

Give the team a complete list of prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines, supplements and allergies. Ask before adding a new medicine; treatment interactions and symptom-masking can matter.

Practical arrangements

Write down where blood samples are taken, who receives the results and how you obtain the next appointment or medicine supply. Request a printed plan if that is easier to use.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests, results and treatment decisions

Safety tests and scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Confirm BRAF V600, blood count, kidney and liver function, LDH, and baseline CPK (muscle enzyme) and creatinine. Check ECG, LVEF (heart pumping function), medicines and skin; record vision symptoms.

During treatment

Blood count, kidney/liver tests and LDH before each cycle. Current Cotellic product information adds CPK and creatinine monthly, and LVEF after the first month then at least every three months until treatment stops. ECG is every four weeks for the first 12 weeks, then every 12 weeks and after dose changes. Arrange monthly skin checks under current vemurafenib product information. New visual symptoms need prompt eye assessment; symptoms can require extra tests between scheduled checks.

What a result means

Ask which result is abnormal, whether the finding needs a repeat test and whether treatment is held, changed or stopped. Do not use a reference range or one normal result to decide independently that a dose is safe.

Response and follow-up

After restarting reduced-dose cobimetinib, NCCP and product information specify LVEF at two, four, ten and sixteen weeks, then as indicated. Vemurafenib skin surveillance continues up to six months after stopping or until another anticancer therapy starts. Its other-cancer surveillance includes head-and-neck checks and chest CT, coordinated separately from melanoma-response imaging. A routine cobimetinib break is not evidence of toxicity recovery.

Treatment reference · 05

What to expect between visits

A quiet treatment day does not rule out later problems.

Act on new symptoms

Contact the team promptly for new blurred or lost vision, eye pain, breathlessness or ankle swelling, palpitations or fainting, severe muscle pain or weakness with dark urine, unusual bleeding, severe diarrhoea, a blistering rash or yellow skin or eyes.

Supportive medicines

NCCP lists each component as minimal-to-low emetogenic risk; follow local and individual anti-sickness policy. Agree a diarrhoea, fluid-intake and nausea plan; contact the team if symptoms persist or you cannot drink. Check all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines for interactions before taking them. The seven-day cobimetinib break is planned; extra breaks or restarting doses require the team’s instructions.

A useful daily record

Record symptoms, temperature when unwell, bowel changes, fluid intake when reduced, missed doses and any medicines used for relief. Note how symptoms affect eating, walking, sleep and normal activities; this helps the team judge severity.

When life is affected

Bring up difficulties with eating, sleep, pain, energy, work or caring for someone. The team can arrange symptom support while cancer treatment continues.

Treatment reference · 06

Skin, body changes and daily care

Use the detailed body map to locate symptoms, not to diagnose them.

Skin and sun

Vemurafenib can make skin very sensitive to sunlight. Use UVA/UVB protection, protective clothing and SPF 30 or higher with lip protection. Continue skin checks for new growths or changing lesions. Do not assume combining the medicines removes the need for skin surveillance.

The body map

The organ anchors show possible locations and warning symptoms. They do not show how common each effect is, and a symptom may have another cause. New symptoms should be assessed in their clinical context.

Food, fluids and activity

Choose food, fluids and movement that you can manage, within any personal restrictions. Ask for help early if you are losing weight, drinking less or finding everyday activity harder.

Keep checking the plan

A new medicine, fever, hospital admission, procedure or radiotherapy may change treatment timing. Tell the oncology team and take the wallet card or medicine list to other appointments.

Treatment reference · 07

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss these issues before the first dose whenever possible.

Contraception and pregnancy

The cobimetinib source specifies two effective contraception methods during treatment and for at least three months afterwards; vemurafenib requires at least six months after its last dose. The combined plan therefore needs to cover the longer six-month vemurafenib interval and account for reduced hormonal-contraceptive effectiveness. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding before treatment.

Fertility preservation

Ask whether treatment or the cancer may affect fertility and whether fertility preservation is appropriate before treatment. A change in periods or sexual function does not reliably establish infertility.

Written dates and methods

Record the agreed method, last treatment date and how long precautions continue. Different medicines have different intervals, so ask for clarification after a switch or combination change.

Sexual health

You can request a private discussion about intimacy, sexual symptoms or relationships. Ask for help if the agreed pregnancy precautions are difficult to follow.

Treatment reference · 08

Decisions, support and useful records

Your priorities belong in the treatment discussion.

Benefits and alternatives

Ask what the treatment aims to achieve, what alternatives are suitable, how likely important benefits are in your situation and how treatment burden or uncertainty affects that choice. A trial average cannot promise an individual outcome.

Support alongside treatment

Supportive care addresses symptoms and daily life during cancer treatment. Palliative care can be part of that support; accepting it does not mean you must stop anticancer treatment.

A person you trust

Choose whether to involve a relative or supporter and what may be shared with them. Ask for an interpreter or a different information format. Check that you can explain the next dose, tests and urgent-contact plan in your own words.

Your document set

Keep the current treatment summary, medicine list, test plan and contact card together. Use the printed instructions when you need them; the QR code is an optional link to further information.
