Cobimetinib + vemurafenib — advanced melanoma

To control advanced BRAF V600-mutated melanoma by blocking BRAF and MEK in the same growth pathway; the two medicines have different daily schedules.

Advanced melanoma · NCCP 00373 v3 · Document revised 6 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

Only cobimetinib has the planned days 22–28 break. Vemurafenib continues. electrocardiogram (ECG) measures rhythm; an echocardiogram or other left ventricular ejection fraction (heart pumping measurement) (LVEF) test measures pumping function, and one does not replace the other. More about my medicines

Cobimetinib

Cobimetinib inhibits MEK, further along the growth-signalling pathway affected by BRAF. It is combined with vemurafenib. Cobimetinib has a seven-day planned break in each 28-day cycle; vemurafenib continues through that break unless the team holds it.

Vemurafenib

Vemurafenib blocks the altered BRAF V600 protein that can drive melanoma-cell growth. A confirmed tumour BRAF V600 result is needed. Healthy tissues can also be affected; tablets need the same careful monitoring as treatment given in hospital.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1–21: At home each day

Usual starting doses are cobimetinib 60 mg once daily on days 1–21 and vemurafenib 960 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart, continuously. Cobimetinib can be taken with or without food. Avoid regularly taking both daily vemurafenib doses on an empty stomach. Swallow both medicines whole and follow the separate labels.

What to do: For cobimetinib, take a missed dose only if at least 12 hours remain before the next dose. For vemurafenib the interval is at least four hours. Otherwise skip the missed dose; do not double up. If you vomit after either medicine, do not take an extra dose. A hold or reduction may apply to one medicine or both: obtain a written component-specific restart plan.

Day 22–28: Planned cobimetinib break

Vemurafenib continues twice daily. The seven-day break applies to cobimetinib only.

What to do: Record any additional treatment hold separately and confirm the next cobimetinib start with the team.

Support at home

Supportive medicines and symptom plan

NCCP lists each component as minimal-to-low emetogenic risk; follow local and individual anti-sickness policy. Agree a diarrhoea, fluid-intake and nausea plan; contact the team if symptoms persist or you cannot drink. Check all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines for interactions before taking them. The seven-day cobimetinib break is planned; extra breaks or restarting doses require the team’s instructions.

Skin and daily care

Vemurafenib can make skin very sensitive to sunlight. Use UVA/UVB protection, protective clothing and sun protection factor (SPF) 30 or higher with lip protection. Continue skin checks for new growths or changing lesions. Do not assume combining the medicines removes the need for skin surveillance.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

The cobimetinib source specifies two effective contraception methods during treatment and for at least three months afterwards; vemurafenib requires at least six months after its last dose. The combined plan therefore needs to cover the longer six-month vemurafenib interval and account for reduced hormonal-contraceptive effectiveness. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding before treatment.
