Consent discussion aid

Liposomal doxorubicin and carboplatin

The aim is to control recurrent cancer and review how it responds.

Treatment discussion

This combination uses the pegylated liposomal formulation of doxorubicin with carboplatin for cancer that has returned and is considered sensitive to platinum.

PhaseWhenWhat changes
Day 1Day 1Both medicines every four weeks, usually for six cycles. Extra cycles may be considered if the cancer shrinks or stays stable.

Possible benefit, alternatives and your priorities

In platinum-sensitive recurrent ovarian cancer, median time to progression was 11.3 months with this combination versus 9.4 months with carboplatin and paclitaxel. The combinations had different side effects.

This comparison concerned recurrent disease. A median describes the trial group.

Discuss the expected benefit, side effects, fertility, alternatives and symptom-directed care with the treating clinician. You may ask for more time or change your decision.

Other options discussed and what matters most to me

Important risks we discussed

  • Infusion reaction · Infused medicines — Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, wheeze or chest tightness during treatment.
  • Infection or bleeding · Chemotherapy — Fever, shivering, unexplained bleeding or suddenly feeling unwell? Call the oncology team now.
  • Kidneys · Carboplatin — Tell the team if you pass much less urine or cannot keep fluids down. Kidney checks guide treatment.
  • Nausea and vomiting · Chemotherapy — Use your prescribed anti-sickness plan. Call if vomiting continues or you cannot drink.
  • Heart · Liposomal doxorubicin — Report swelling, worsening breathlessness or a racing heartbeat promptly. Severe chest pain needs emergency help.
  • Hands and feet · Liposomal doxorubicin — Report painful redness, peeling or blisters early. Tell the team if walking is affected.
  • Mouth · Liposomal doxorubicin — Report ulcers, pain on swallowing or difficulty drinking.
  • Drip site · Liposomal doxorubicin — Tell the nurse immediately if the infusion stings or the area swells.

Risks to discuss for each medicine

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin

Hand and foot soreness, Mouth ulcers, Infusion reactions, Heart muscle damage. Report sore hands, feet or mouth before the next infusion, and report swelling or breathlessness promptly.

Carboplatin

Low blood counts, Nausea, Infusion reaction. Report fever, bleeding, hearing changes or an infusion reaction.
