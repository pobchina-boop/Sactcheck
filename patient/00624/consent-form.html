Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

Liposomal doxorubicin and carboplatin

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

The aim is to control recurrent cancer and review how it responds. This combination uses the pegylated liposomal formulation of doxorubicin with carboplatin for cancer that has returned and is considered sensitive to platinum.

Treatment schedule. Both medicines every four weeks, usually for six cycles. Extra cycles may be considered if the cancer shrinks or stays stable.

Regimen-specific side effects

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Hands and feetReport burning pain, redness, peeling or blisters early. Your team may delay or adjust treatment.
Mouth and blood countsReport mouth ulcers or difficulty eating. Fever or chills require an urgent call for possible infection.
Heart function and infusion reactionsReport ankle swelling, new breathlessness or difficulty lying flat. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest tightness or back pain during infusion.

Carboplatin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Low blood counts and infectionCall urgently for fever, shivering or unexpected bleeding. Red cells and platelets can fall as well as white cells.
Nausea and vomitingTake the prescribed anti-sickness medicines. Call if vomiting continues or you cannot keep fluids down.
Allergy, kidneys and hearingTell the nurse immediately about an infusion reaction. Kidney function guides dosing. Report new ringing in the ears or hearing changes.

Treatment discussion and decision

Liposomal doxorubicin and carboplatin

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

In platinum-sensitive recurrent ovarian cancer, median time to progression was 11.3 months with this combination versus 9.4 months with carboplatin and paclitaxel. The combinations had different side effects. This comparison concerned recurrent disease. A median describes the trial group.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Fever, shaking chills, unusual bleeding or sudden illness? Call the oncology unit now. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
