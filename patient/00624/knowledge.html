Your complete treatment reference

Liposomal doxorubicin and carboplatin

Your treatment and how the medicines work

The aim is to control recurrent cancer and review how it responds.

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin · Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin

Liposomal chemotherapy. A fatty coating carries doxorubicin in the blood. The medicine damages DNA in dividing cells. Report sore hands, feet or mouth before the next infusion, and report swelling or breathlessness promptly.

Carboplatin · Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. It damages DNA in dividing cells. Kidney function helps determine the prescribed amount. Report fever, bleeding, hearing changes or an infusion reaction.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1

Both medicines every four weeks, usually for six cycles. Extra cycles may be considered if the cancer shrinks or stays stable.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Fever, shaking chills, unusual bleeding or sudden illness? Call the oncology unit now. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Skin and mouth care

Avoid rubbing or overheating sore hands and feet. Ask about moisturiser and mouth care. Report symptoms early, particularly when walking or eating becomes difficult.

Sickness prevention

Follow the written carboplatin anti-sickness plan. Contact the team if vomiting continues despite your prescribed rescue medicine.

Planning your visits

At the day unit

Liposomal doxorubicin is given first, slowly at the start to watch for a reaction. Carboplatin follows. Report burning, swelling or pain around your drip immediately.

If a visit changes

Your team will tell you if a visit moves after a delay. Do not work out a new date yourself.

Before starting treatment

Discuss fertility, contraception and breastfeeding with your team. Bring your current medicines and details of previous infusion reactions.

What the research found

In platinum-sensitive recurrent ovarian cancer, median time to progression was 11.3 months with this combination versus 9.4 months with carboplatin and paclitaxel. The combinations had different side effects.

This comparison concerned recurrent disease. A median describes the trial group.
