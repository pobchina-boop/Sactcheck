Hormone therapy · aromatase inhibitor

Anastrozole

Reduces the oestrogen available to stimulate cancer cells
Aromatase normally makes oestrogen↓The medicine blocks aromatase↓Less oestrogen reaches cancer-cell receptors

Anastrozole blocks aromatase, an enzyme that makes oestrogen in fat and other tissues after menopause. Less oestrogen is available to stimulate hormone-sensitive breast cancer cells. This also explains effects such as hot flushes, vaginal dryness and bone thinning. It does not reliably switch off functioning ovaries; any ovarian-suppression treatment needs a separate prescription and checks.

Tell the team if joint pain, hot flushes, vaginal dryness or sleep disturbance affects daily life. Bone thinning may cause no early symptoms, so keep the agreed bone-health reviews.

What each check is for

1Confirm the planBefore starting or changing treatment

Postmenopausal hormone-sensitive breast cancer in the NCCP adjuvant, switch-after-tamoxifen or advanced settings. The exact receptor and prior-treatment criteria remain a prescribing decision.

Ask / act: Confirm treatment setting, medicines, menopause and the planned review.

Sources: NCCP 00254

2Checks and symptomsAt the agreed review points

FBC, renal and liver profile. Confirm menopause; hormone tests may be needed if it is uncertain. Assess fracture risk and baseline bone density, particularly for adjuvant AI treatment. The NICE adjuvant baseline-DXA recommendation has an exception for patients already receiving adjuvant bisphosphonates. Assess symptoms, adherence and bone health at reviews. Repeat lipids and other blood tests when clinically indicated. Bone-density timing follows baseline results and fracture risk.

Ask / act: Agree who books tests, reviews results and responds to new symptoms.

Sources: NCCP 00254

3Review ongoing benefitAt setting-specific decision points

After early breast cancer treatment, endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence. In advanced disease, the aim is to control cancer and symptoms. Your setting determines the duration and follow-up.

Ask / act: Review duration, alternatives and patient priorities; do not stop or extend treatment automatically.

Sources: NCCP 00254

Support throughout treatment

Symptoms and daily life

Tell the team if joint pain, hot flushes, vaginal dryness or sleep disturbance affects daily life. Bone thinning may cause no early symptoms, so keep the agreed bone-health reviews.

Support medicines

No routine antiemetic, steroid premedication or G-CSF is required by this endocrine monotherapy. Ask for individual symptom support.

Tests and appointments

Ask who books each check, when it is due and who reviews it. Tell the team if an expected appointment has not arrived.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
