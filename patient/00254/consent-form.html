NCCP 00254 · version 5

Anastrozole · daily hormone therapy

Take anastrozole once daily continuously. There is no planned week off. The treatment setting determines the intended duration.

Discussion aid: review with the patient and complete the approved local consent process.

□ Exact indication, treatment aim and uncertainty explained
□ Expected duration and review / switch points discussed
□ Alternatives and the option not to proceed discussed
□ Patient priorities, capacity and support needs explored

Practical safety

□ Medicine, route, schedule and missed-dose instructions
□ Menopause and ovarian-suppression requirements if relevant
□ Pregnancy, contraception / fertility and breastfeeding
□ Monitoring, symptom reporting and contact plan

Anastrozole: material risks

□ Joint and muscle pain or stiffness
□ Hot flushes, sleep disturbance and fatigue
□ Vaginal dryness and sexual symptoms
□ Bone thinning and fractures
□ Lipid / cardiovascular risk changes
□ Uncommon liver abnormalities or serious allergic reactions

A functioning premenopausal ovarian state is not compatible with AI monotherapy. Severe hepatic impairment is not recommended by NCCP; moderate impairment requires caution. Confirm menopausal status rather than assuming that absent periods after chemotherapy mean permanent menopause. Any ovarian-suppression treatment must have its own prescribed schedule.

Individual benefit, alternatives and concerns

Agreed checks and next review

FBC, renal and liver profile. Confirm menopause; hormone tests may be needed if it is uncertain. Assess fracture risk and baseline bone density, particularly for adjuvant AI treatment. The NICE adjuvant baseline-DXA recommendation has an exception for patients already receiving adjuvant bisphosphonates. Assess symptoms, adherence and bone health at reviews. Repeat lipids and other blood tests when clinically indicated. Bone-density timing follows baseline results and fracture risk.
