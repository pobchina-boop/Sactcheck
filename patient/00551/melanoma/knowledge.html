When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Immunotherapy can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening diarrhoea, blood or mucus in stools, abdominal pain, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell every treating clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

To help immune cells control advanced melanoma with two checkpoint medicines, followed when appropriate by nivolumab alone. The chosen dose combination has specific licensing and evidence limitations to discuss during consent.

PD-1 checkpoint immunotherapy

Nivolumab

Opdivo
Releases a PD-1 immune brake

Nivolumab blocks PD-1, helping T cells remain active against melanoma. With ipilimumab it acts on two immune checkpoints. This can also cause healthy-organ inflammation; changing to nivolumab alone does not erase earlier immune side effects.

What to watch for
Carry the immunotherapy alert card and report new symptoms during treatment and after it stops.

CTLA-4 checkpoint immunotherapy

Ipilimumab

Yervoy
Releases a CTLA-4 immune brake

Ipilimumab blocks CTLA-4, one of the signals that normally restrain T cells. This may help immune cells attack melanoma. The same immune activity can inflame healthy organs, sometimes after the last infusion; some hormone problems need lasting treatment.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Treatment day

During combination treatment, nivolumab 3 mg/kg is infused first over 30 minutes, then ipilimumab 1 mg/kg over 30 minutes. Maintenance may be nivolumab IV 240 mg every two weeks or IV 480 mg every four weeks, or the prescribed subcutaneous alternative of 600 mg every two weeks or 1200 mg every four weeks. In this melanoma source, IV 240 mg takes 30 minutes and IV 480 mg takes 60 minutes; subcutaneous administration takes about three to five minutes. Report symptoms and previous reactions before treatment.

Day 2–21: Between treatment visits

Immunotherapy can inflame healthy organs during treatment or after it stops. Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening diarrhoea, blood or mucus in stools, abdominal pain, cough or breathlessness, yellow skin or eyes, severe headache, vision changes, marked thirst, unusual weakness or a spreading rash. Tell every treating clinician that you are receiving or have received immunotherapy. Do not wait for the next appointment to report urgent symptoms.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Taking or receiving treatment

During combination treatment, nivolumab 3 mg/kg is infused first over 30 minutes, then ipilimumab 1 mg/kg over 30 minutes. Maintenance may be nivolumab IV 240 mg every two weeks or IV 480 mg every four weeks, or the prescribed subcutaneous alternative of 600 mg every two weeks or 1200 mg every four weeks. In this melanoma source, IV 240 mg takes 30 minutes and IV 480 mg takes 60 minutes; subcutaneous administration takes about three to five minutes.

STEP 2

Missed, vomited or held treatment

If a dose is delayed or held, obtain a revised written plan. The first two-weekly maintenance dose starts three weeks after the last combination dose; the first four-weekly dose starts six weeks afterwards. Do not calculate a different start from the maintenance interval alone. Earlier immune toxicity and permanent-stop decisions still apply.

STEP 3

Appointments and supply

Arrange blood tests, transport, medicine supply and interpreter or carer support. Check who reviews results and how you receive the decision before the next dose.

STEP 4

Paper copies and contacts

Keep the full handbook, three-page summary and wallet card together. The QR code is optional. Ask the team to replace every labelled example contact with your real oncology and out-of-hours details; ask for a new printed plan when treatment changes.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines and symptom plan

NCCP classifies the combination phase as low emetogenic risk and nivolumab alone as minimal risk. Anti-sickness treatment is individual. Routine infusion premedication is not usually required; a previous reaction may alter that plan. Steroid or hormone treatment for immune effects must have a written follow-up plan.

Skin and daily care

Use gentle skin care and sun protection. Report rash early and obtain urgent assessment for spreading painful rash, blisters, peeling or mouth/eye/genital sores. Continue melanoma self-checks and planned skin reviews during maintenance and after treatment.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least five months after the final nivolumab dose. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding before starting and tell the team promptly if pregnancy is possible. Keep the last-dose date on the treatment record.

Understanding your diagnosis and treatment goal

Use your own pathology, stage and agreed plan alongside this guide.

Your diagnosis and stage

Keep the pathology report, original melanoma site, stage and known sites of spread. This guide concerns advanced disease that cannot be removed completely by surgery or has spread; it does not describe a postoperative recurrence-prevention course.

Sources: nccn-patient

The tumour test

A BRAF V600 test helps identify whether BRAF-targeted medicines are an option. A tumour mutation does not by itself mean an inherited family condition. Keep the exact result and ask how it changes your choices.

Sources: nccn-patient

Scans and local treatments

Record the scans used to follow known disease. Surgery or radiotherapy may have a separate role for a particular site; ask for their timing and risks to be added to the overall plan.

Sources: nccn-patient

New changes still matter

Report a new lump, changing mole, persistent skin sore or unexplained symptom. A response at one site does not establish that every other symptom is harmless.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests, results and treatment decisions

Safety tests and scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Blood count, kidney and liver function, glucose, thyroid tests and BRAF tumour status. Review autoimmune disease, previous immune reactions, transplantation, infection and all medicines. Confirm informed consent for this unlicensed melanoma dosing schedule and the local governance requirements.

Sources: nccp

During treatment

Blood count, kidney/liver tests, glucose and thyroid tests before every cycle, with symptom review during combination and maintenance phases. Cortisol and other organ tests are added when indicated. Recheck route, dose, interval and the maintenance start date when phases change.

Sources: nccp

What a result means

Ask which result is abnormal, whether the finding needs a repeat test and whether treatment is held, changed or stopped. Do not use a reference range or one normal result to decide independently that a dose is safe.

Sources: nccp

Response and follow-up

NCCP specifies ongoing monitoring for at least five months after the last dose. Immune effects can require longer follow-up, steroid tapering or lasting hormone replacement. Response scans and any further melanoma treatment follow the individual plan.

Sources: nccp
