How your medicines work

Immunotherapy

Atezolizumab

Blocks the PD-L1 immune brake
Immune cell→PD-L1 blocked→Cancer cell

Atezolizumab is an antibody against PD-L1, a signal that can reduce an immune cell’s activity. Blocking that signal can help immune cells attack cancer. It works differently from chemotherapy. The same immune activation can inflame healthy organs, so new symptoms need assessment even when they look like ordinary treatment tiredness or a minor infection. IV and SC formulations use different doses but both carry immune risks.

Report new cough, breathlessness, diarrhoea, rash, severe headache, weakness, thirst, frequent urination or chest symptoms promptly. Effects can appear during treatment or after the final dose.

Chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Damages cancer-cell DNA
DNA strands→Platinum links→Copying disrupted

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop dividing cancer cells but also affects healthy tissues such as bone marrow. Kidney function is used to calculate its dose. It is given during the four-cycle induction phase and stops before atezolizumab-only maintenance.

Report fever, shivering, unexpected bruising, bleeding or persistent vomiting. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, itching, chest tightness, breathing changes or pain around the drip.

Chemotherapy

Etoposide

Interferes with DNA repair
DNA copying→Repair interrupted→Cell division slows

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme that helps manage DNA during copying. DNA damage can then accumulate and stop cell division. Healthy rapidly renewing tissues can also be affected, explaining low blood counts, hair loss and mouth or bowel problems. Oral absorption differs from IV delivery; the prescribed oral dose must not be inferred from the IV dose.

During an infusion, report dizziness, breathing changes, rash or pain at the drip. At home report fever, mouth ulcers, diarrhoea or poor intake. If capsules are prescribed, follow the exact labelled doses and contact the team about a missed or vomited dose rather than replacing it yourself.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Appointments and time in the unit

Day 1 order: atezolizumab, carboplatin, then etoposide. Atezolizumab IV first infusion is 60 minutes, later 30 minutes if tolerated; SC injection takes about 7 minutes. Carboplatin infusion is 30 minutes and etoposide IV is 60 minutes. Total unit time also includes checks, preparation and observation. SC atezolizumab does not remove the chemotherapy drips during induction.

STEP 2

Your written formulation plan

Confirm your written route plan. Atezolizumab may be a drip (IV) or an injection under the skin of the thigh (SC). Etoposide may be IV on Days 1–3, or IV on Day 1 with prescribed capsules twice daily on Days 2–3. These schedules use different doses; do not change route or convert doses yourself. A separately prescribed four-week IV atezolizumab maintenance plan uses NCCP 00593 and is outside this 21-day calendar.

STEP 3

Travel, support and medicines

Induction usually involves treatment on three consecutive days or one IV day plus prescribed capsules at home. Ask about transport, work, caring responsibilities and who to call about capsules or symptoms. Carry the immunotherapy alert card.

STEP 4

Tests and maintenance

FBC, liver, renal and glucose profile before every cycle; thyroid tests every 3–6 weeks. Scans and other disease assessment follow the consultant plan. Continue symptom review during maintenance and after atezolizumab stops because immune effects may be delayed. Confirm which team books each test and who reviews it before treatment.

Support around treatment

Day 1 carboplatin anti-sickness plan

Carboplatin has a high sickness risk. Your prescription usually includes several anti-sickness medicines with some doses on later days. Follow the written plan even if you initially feel well; contact the team if it is not effective.

Days 2–3 etoposide and the days afterwards

The team should account for each chemotherapy day and for two days after the last dose. The later-day plan may overlap the medicines already started on Day 1, so obtain one clear schedule and do not add duplicate doses. Confirm the plan for oral etoposide separately.

Maintenance support changes

When only atezolizumab remains, routine chemotherapy anti-sickness steroids and other prophylaxis are not automatically continued. Keep any individual rescue or hormone-replacement plan and the immunotherapy alert card. Do not stop prescribed long-term steroids yourself.

Do not wait for the next appointment

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
