When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

My treatment

My overall treatment

Every 21 days: atezolizumab and carboplatin on Day 1, etoposide on Days 1–3, for up to four cycles. Maintenance uses atezolizumab alone.

  1. InductionConfirm IV or injected atezolizumab and whether etoposide Days2–3 means drips or prescribed capsules.
  2. MaintenanceChemotherapy stops. Atezolizumab continues every three weeks while appropriate.

More about my medicines

Atezolizumab

Atezolizumab is an antibody against PD-L1, a signal that can reduce an immune cell’s activity. Blocking that signal can help immune cells attack cancer. It works differently from chemotherapy. The same immune activation can inflame healthy organs, so new symptoms need assessment even when they look like ordinary treatment tiredness or a minor infection. IV and SC formulations use different doses but both carry immune risks.

Carboplatin

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop dividing cancer cells but also affects healthy tissues such as bone marrow. Kidney function is used to calculate its dose. It is given during the four-cycle induction phase and stops before atezolizumab-only maintenance.

Etoposide

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme that helps manage DNA during copying. DNA damage can then accumulate and stop cell division. Healthy rapidly renewing tissues can also be affected, explaining low blood counts, hair loss and mouth or bowel problems. Oral absorption differs from IV delivery; the prescribed oral dose must not be inferred from the IV dose.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Day 1: treatment and checks

The team checks symptoms and blood tests, then gives atezolizumab, carboplatin and etoposide during induction. Atezolizumab may be IV or SC. Tell staff immediately if you feel unwell during treatment.

What to do: Before leaving, confirm whether Days 2–3 mean IV visits or oral etoposide at home, and check the anti-sickness plan.

Day 2–3: Days 2–3: etoposide during induction

You either attend for etoposide drips or take the separately prescribed etoposide capsules twice daily, according to your confirmed plan. Capsules are taken on an empty stomach. Maintenance does not include etoposide.

What to do: Do not switch between the IV and oral schedules or infer capsule amounts. Contact the team about missed or vomited doses.

Day 4–7: After the chemotherapy days

Sickness, reduced appetite, fatigue or bowel changes may occur. Take the anti-sickness medicines on your written schedule, including later days. The team should check coverage after the last chemotherapy day.

What to do: Ask for advice if vomiting continues or you cannot drink. Report new diarrhoea promptly because immune bowel inflammation is also possible.

Day 8–14: Keep checking how you feel

Blood counts may fall between visits. The day number does not tell you whether your counts are safe. Hair loss, mouth soreness or fatigue can affect daily life; ask for symptom support.

What to do: Use the urgent contact plan for fever, shivers or sudden illness, including when your temperature is normal.

Day 15–21: Prepare for the next review

Some symptoms may settle while others persist or appear for the first time. The next dose depends on your review and tests. Around the end of induction, the team discusses whether to move to atezolizumab-only maintenance.

What to do: Bring your symptom record and medicines list. Confirm the next date and which phase and routes are planned.

Support at home

Day 1 carboplatin anti-sickness plan

Carboplatin has a high sickness risk. Your prescription usually includes several anti-sickness medicines with some doses on later days. Follow the written plan even if you initially feel well; contact the team if it is not effective.

Days 2–3 etoposide and the days afterwards

The team should account for each chemotherapy day and for two days after the last dose. The later-day plan may overlap the medicines already started on Day 1, so obtain one clear schedule and do not add duplicate doses. Confirm the plan for oral etoposide separately.

Maintenance support changes

When only atezolizumab remains, routine chemotherapy anti-sickness steroids and other prophylaxis are not automatically continued. Keep any individual rescue or hormone-replacement plan and the immunotherapy alert card. Do not stop prescribed long-term steroids yourself.
