1. Treatment and purpose

Atezolizumab + carboplatin + etoposide · NCCP 00689 v4

Day 1 all three medicines; etoposide also Days 2–3. A cycle is 21 days. Confirm the exact IV/SC/oral route plan. Chemotherapy stops. Atezolizumab continues every 21 days while appropriate; there is no fixed total number here. Review benefit, symptoms and supportive prescriptions.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Atezolizumab · Immunotherapy

Report new cough, breathlessness, diarrhoea, rash, severe headache, weakness, thirst, frequent urination or chest symptoms promptly. Effects can appear during treatment or after the final dose.

Carboplatin · Chemotherapy

Report fever, shivering, unexpected bruising, bleeding or persistent vomiting. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, itching, chest tightness, breathing changes or pain around the drip.

Etoposide · Chemotherapy

During an infusion, report dizziness, breathing changes, rash or pain at the drip. At home report fever, mouth ulcers, diarrhoea or poor intake. If capsules are prescribed, follow the exact labelled doses and contact the team about a missed or vomited dose rather than replacing it yourself.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss the aim of disease control and symptom relief, expected benefits, alternatives including a different systemic plan or supportive care, and the option to revisit decisions. Include immune effects that can be serious, delayed or require long-term hormone replacement, chemotherapy infection/bleeding risks, hair loss, fatigue and practical route choices.
