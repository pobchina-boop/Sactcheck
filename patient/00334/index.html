More about my medicines

Temozolomide

Temozolomide crosses into the brain and creates chemical changes in DNA. If the damage cannot be repaired, a cell may stop dividing or die. Healthy blood-forming cells can also be affected, which helps explain blood-count changes, infection and bleeding risks. Nausea prevention and liver monitoring are important too.

Radiotherapy

Radiotherapy directs high-energy radiation at the tumour and a planned surrounding area. It aims to damage tumour-cell DNA while limiting dose to nearby healthy tissue. Fatigue, scalp irritation, hair loss and swelling can occur; longer-term effects depend on the treatment area and dose.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1–5: Five daily capsule doses

Check the prescribed capsule strengths and number each day. Take fasting with the anti-sickness plan.

What to do: Do not use leftover capsules to change the dose or replace a vomited dose.

Day 6–21: No temozolomide; recovery continues

Rest days allow recovery but blood counts can still fall. Continue other medicines only as individually prescribed.

What to do: Contact the team for fever, sudden illness, bruising or persistent vomiting.

Day 22–28: Blood count and next-cycle decision

Have the Day 22 blood count and any follow-up blood tests arranged. Before the next cycle, the team reviews counts, kidney/liver tests and symptoms.

What to do: Do not start the next cycle just because the calendar reaches Day 1.

Support at home

Nausea prevention

Temozolomide has moderate-to-high oral emetic potential. Take the prescribed anti-sickness plan before chemotherapy and use rescue medicine as instructed. Ask what to do if nausea prevents taking medicines or fluids.

PJP infection prevention

PJP-prevention medicine is required during temozolomide with radiotherapy in this NCCP regimen. Your team supplies the prescription and decides how long to continue, including if lymphocyte counts remain low. Do not stop it just because radiotherapy has finished.

Bowels, food and fluids

Anti-sickness medicines and other medicines can worsen constipation. Ask for your bowel plan, and call for persistent vomiting, severe constipation or difficulty drinking. Do not double a dose after vomiting.

Steroid and seizure plans

Keep steroids and seizure medicines on their own written schedules if prescribed. They are not routine medicines for every patient, and chemotherapy rest days do not automatically mean stopping them. Ask the pharmacist to check interactions.
