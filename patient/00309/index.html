My treatment

My overall treatment

Paclitaxel then carboplatin by drip once every 7 days for seven weeks, alongside separately scheduled radiotherapy. Test results or side effects may change treatment. Any treatment after this course needs a separate decision and prescription.

  1. ChemotherapyBoth drugs at each weekly visit, after blood tests, symptom review and premedicines.
  2. RadiotherapyThe radiation team sets the dose and appointments. A chemotherapy delay does not automatically stop radiation.
  3. After treatmentYour team reviews recovery, persistent symptoms and response. Scan or marker timing is individual.

More about my medicines

Paclitaxel

Paclitaxel stabilises structures needed for cell division. It can also affect nerves, blood cells and healthy tissues. Premedicines help reduce infusion reactions.

Carboplatin

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with cell copying. Its dose uses kidney filtration and target exposure(AUC), not a fixed number of milligrams.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Weekly treatment day

Blood results and symptoms are reviewed. Paclitaxel comes first, followed by carboplatin.

What to do: Tell staff immediately about flushing, breathing trouble, pain or swelling during infusion.

Day 2–3: Support at home

Use the anti-sickness medicines prescribed for these days. Radiation visits may continue on their own schedule.

What to do: Call for fever, poor intake, vomiting or new breathing symptoms.

Day 4–7: Before the next weekly dose

Keep track of swallowing, fatigue, numbness and muscle/joint symptoms. Arrange the next weekly blood tests.

What to do: Report problems before the next dose; do not wait if acutely unwell.

Support at home

Weekly medicines before treatment

Paclitaxel needs premedicines to reduce allergic reactions. The first week and later weeks can differ. Your team also provides anti-sickness medicines for carboplatin; do not double up steroid doses.

Eating, swallowing and breathing

Chest radiation can make swallowing sore. Ask early for pain relief and nutrition support. New breathlessness, cough or inability to drink needs urgent advice, even after the course finishes.

Nerves and aches

Tell the team about numbness, tingling or muscle/joint pain before each weekly visit. Follow the agreed pain-relief plan and check before starting medicines or supplements.
