Weekly carboplatin + paclitaxel with radiotherapy

Chemotherapy and radiation treat stage III non-small-cell lung cancer when cisplatin is unsuitable. Your team explains your treatment aim and options.

Stage III NSCLC weekly7-week regimen. This is distinct from oesophageal CROSS(5 weeks), metastatic chemotherapy and any separately prescribed consolidation.

My overall treatment

Paclitaxel then carboplatin by drip once every 7 days for seven weeks, alongside separately scheduled radiotherapy. Test results or side effects may change treatment. Any treatment after this course needs a separate decision and prescription.

  1. ChemotherapyBoth drugs at each weekly visit, after blood tests, symptom review and premedicines.
  2. RadiotherapyThe radiation team sets the dose and appointments. A chemotherapy delay does not automatically stop radiation.
  3. After treatmentYour team reviews recovery, persistent symptoms and response. Scan or marker timing is individual.

Between treatments

  • Use the prescribed anti-sickness medicines; report numbness, swallowing pain, fatigue or muscle and joint symptoms before treatment.
  • Fever, new breathlessness, uncontrolled vomiting or inability to drink needs urgent advice, including after the course ends.

Confirm your prescription, radiation appointments and blood tests. Bring your medicine list and daytime / overnight contact numbers.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
