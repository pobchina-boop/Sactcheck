When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, sudden illness, new breathlessness, uncontrolled vomiting, inability to swallow fluids, much less urine or unusual bleeding. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or heavy bleeding needs emergency care. Tell staff immediately about infusion pain, swelling or allergy symptoms.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

My chemotherapy medicines

Chemotherapy is given alongside radiotherapy to treat stage III non-small-cell lung cancer when cisplatin is unsuitable. Your team explains the treatment aim and choices.

Taxane chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Taxane chemotherapy
Disrupts cell division

Paclitaxel stabilises structures needed for cell division. It can also affect nerves, blood cells and healthy tissues. Premedicines help reduce infusion reactions.

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Damages DNA

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with cell copying. Its dose uses kidney filtration and target exposure(AUC), not a fixed number of milligrams.

Both medicines are given by drip once every 7 days for seven weeks, alongside a separate radiotherapy course. The plan may change for side effects or test results. Further chemotherapy after radiotherapy is a separate decision.

Separate appointments

Radiation course

Team-agreed

Radiation is given during the chemotherapy course. Its dose, number of fractions and precise timing are prescribed by radiation oncology. A chemotherapy delay does not automatically stop radiation.

Review then next plan

After chemoradiation

Review

Your team reviews recovery and response. NCCP describes optional separate two-cycle carboplatin/paclitaxel consolidation under00304; it is not automatically included and any next treatment needs a separate prescription.

The chemotherapy course is finite; radiotherapy timing and next treatment are confirmed separately.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Weekly treatment day

Blood results and symptoms are reviewed. Paclitaxel comes first, followed by carboplatin. Tell staff immediately about flushing, breathing trouble, pain or swelling during infusion.

Day 2–3: Support at home

Use the anti-sickness medicines prescribed for these days. Radiation visits may continue on their own schedule. Call for fever, poor intake, vomiting or new breathing symptoms.

Day 4–7: Before the next weekly dose

Keep track of swallowing, fatigue, numbness and muscle/joint symptoms. Arrange the next weekly blood tests. Report problems before the next dose; do not wait if acutely unwell.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Confirm your plan

Paclitaxel is infused first over one hour, then carboplatin over 30 minutes, plus checks and premedicines. The consultant may prescribe a lower paclitaxel dose. RT appointments are separate and are not counted as chemotherapy doses.

STEP 2

Keep the contact card

The team supplies the daytime and out-of-hours numbers. Keep the card with you and show it when seeking medical care.

STEP 3

Two linked treatment teams

Confirm which team to contact for radiation or chemotherapy changes. Report swallowing problems early and ask about transport, nutrition and fatigue support.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Weekly medicines before treatment

Paclitaxel needs premedicines to reduce allergic reactions. The first week and later weeks can differ. Your team also provides anti-sickness medicines for carboplatin; do not double up steroid doses.

Eating, swallowing and breathing

Chest radiation can make swallowing sore. Ask early for pain relief and nutrition support. New breathlessness, cough or inability to drink needs urgent advice, even after the course finishes.

Nerves and aches

Tell the team about numbness, tingling or muscle/joint pain before each weekly visit. Follow the agreed pain-relief plan and check before starting medicines or supplements.

Sources: nccp · ncis

Why chemotherapy and radiotherapy are combined

Studies support combining systemic treatment with radiation in the relevant lung-cancer setting.

The evidence comes from specific treatment schedules and patient groups. Your team explains how it applies to your situation; study averages cannot predict an individual outcome.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Which drugs and routes are prescribed for me?

Confirm the exact drug names, routes and interval against your written prescription. The team must explain any change from this reference plan.

How do my radiation appointments fit around chemotherapy?

Radiation has its own calendar. Ask for the combined plan and whom to contact if a chemotherapy or radiation visit changes.

Which blood tests are required before the next dose?

Use the checks table as a prompt. Your team confirms which tests are needed, where they are done and who reviews the results before treatment.

Who do I call during the day and overnight?

Ask the team to replace the example numbers on your card with the correct daytime, overnight and backup contact routes before you leave.

When will recovery and response be reviewed?

Agree the follow-up appointments and any scans with your team. This regimen does not set a universal scan or tumour-marker timetable.

Try explaining the plan back: Explain back your chemotherapy days, how radiation is scheduled separately, the tests before treatment and whom you will call if unwell.
