When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, sudden illness, breathing difficulty, chest pain, bleeding, uncontrolled vomiting or severe constipation with tummy pain. Report new numbness or weakness before the next dose. Tell staff immediately about infusion pain or swelling.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Your medicines

This chemotherapy aims to control lung cancer and symptoms when cisplatin is unsuitable. The team reviews benefit, side effects and your priorities.

Vinca alkaloid chemotherapy

Vinorelbine

Prescribed IV or oral route
Disrupts microtubules needed for cell division

Vinorelbine disrupts the cell structures that help divide chromosomes. This can slow cancer growth and also affect blood cells, nerves and the bowel.

Binds cell-division structures↓Microtubules cannot function normally↓Cell division is disrupted

What to watch for
Report fever, constipation, numbness or pain at the infusion site promptly.

Sources: nccp · smpc-vinorelbine

Chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Damages DNA

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that hinder copying and repair. Healthy tissues can also be harmed. Its dose depends on target exposure (AUC) and measured or estimated kidney function; AUC is not a fixed number of milligrams. There are two planned doses in this regimen.

DNA↓Platinum links↓Copying disrupted

What to watch for
Report fever, bleeding, nausea, hearing changes or numbness. Tell the nurse immediately about possible allergy during the infusion.

Sources: nccp

Four 21 day cycles are planned, unless treatment stops or changes earlier. Day 1: carboplatin plus vinorelbine. Day 8: vinorelbine only. Your prescription specifies vinorelbine by drip OR capsules; these are alternatives, not extra doses.

Review

Four cycles take about 12 weeks without delays. Your team agrees response scans and what happens next. Surgery or radiotherapy is not automatically included.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Confirm the prescribed route

Vinorelbine by drip and vinorelbine capsules are alternatives. Ask the team to mark your route.

STEP 2

Day 1 and Day 8

Day 1 includes carboplatin; Day 8 contains vinorelbine only. Arrange blood tests and clearance for each dose.

STEP 3

Capsules only when prescribed

Keep in the original packaging and follow pharmacy storage/handling instructions. Do not repeat a vomited dose or make up a missed dose.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Different support on Day 1 and Day 8

Day 1 carboplatin needs a stronger anti-sickness plan. Day 8 depends on whether vinorelbine is by drip or capsules. Use the written prescription for that day and route; do not repeat the entire Day 1course automatically.

Bowel plan

Vinorelbine and some anti-sickness medicines can cause constipation. Follow the prescribed bowel plan and call for severe tummy pain, swelling or vomiting.

If capsules are prescribed

Swallow whole with water and some food. Do not chew or open capsules. Do not take an extra dose after vomiting; contact the team for missed-dose advice. Return damaged capsules to pharmacy.

Check medicine interactions

The team should check all medicines, including anti-sickness treatment. Do not start new tablets, supplements or vaccines without advice.

Sources: nccp · ncis

Understanding the evidence

Carboplatin with vinorelbine has been studied in advanced lung cancer.

Historical phase II evidence used alternating IV/oral vinorelbine, not every route variant, and cannot quantify comparative benefit.

Sources: obrien-2004

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Am I having both oral and IV vinorelbine?

No. Your written plan must identify the prescribed route for each dose. Do not treat capsule instructions as additional treatment.

Why is Day 8different?

Only vinorelbine is planned. Blood results, symptoms and the route determine whether dosing and the support plan are appropriate.

What if treatment changes?

Ask your team to update the cycle record, prescription and contact plan. A stamp is not permission for the next dose.

Try explaining the plan back: Explain back your treatment days, checks and whom to contact when unwell.
