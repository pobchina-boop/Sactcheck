SACTCheck · NCCP 00300 v7b · Ovarian dysgerminoma and other germ-cell tumours

Your treatment journey

BEP · bleomycin, etoposide and cisplatin

Treat the germ-cell tumour with curative intent.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · all three medicines

Bleomycin, etoposide and cisplatin are given on day 1. IV fluids and an anti-sickness plan cover the five-day cisplatin block.

Days 2–5 · etoposide and cisplatin

Return on each of days 2, 3, 4 and 5 for etoposide, cisplatin and fluids. Keep using the same dated anti-sickness prescription for this block.

Days 8 and 15 · bleomycin

Bleomycin alone on days 8 and 15. The cycle repeats every 21 days. The consultant confirms your total number of cycles based on diagnosis and risk.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat is checkedWhat you can do
Before day 1Blood count, renal and liver function, germ-cell markers, chest assessment, lung function and hearing as indicated.Ask about fertility preservation before the first dose.
Before each cycleReview counts and tumour markers, renal and liver profile, chest X-ray and cumulative bleomycin.Tell the team about any cough or change in exercise tolerance before the next dose.
During infusionsReview hydration, urine output, magnesium and other salts, hearing and nerve symptoms.Tell the nurse promptly about chills, rash, swelling or trouble breathing.

Scan dates and tumour-marker testing are individualised by your oncology team.

At review visits

  • What happens at my next visit? Five consecutive infusion days, then bleomycin on days 8 and 15.
  • Which symptoms should I phone about? Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shivering or sudden illness. Report new dry cough or breathlessness immediately, even after the last dose.
  • What should I plan before starting? Ask about fertility preservation before the first dose.
