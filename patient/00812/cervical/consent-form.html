Treatment and purpose

Cemiplimab helps the immune system recognise cancer that has grown during or after platinum chemotherapy. The aim is to slow cancer growth and help you live longer. Adults with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer that progressed on or after platinum chemotherapy, ECOG 0–2 and adequate organ function. NCCP 00812a specifies no PD-L1 threshold. Review prior checkpoint inhibitor exposure under current Irish sequencing guidance.

Treatment schedule. One infusion every three weeks (21 days), while it is helping and side effects remain manageable.

Treatment discussion and decision

Cemiplimab for cervical cancer after platinum chemotherapy

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

In a study of 608 people, those assigned cemiplimab lived longer as a group than those assigned another chemotherapy medicine. The figures describe groups and cannot predict one person’s outcome. Evidence applies to the post-platinum trial population. Benefit after progression on a prior PD-1 inhibitor is unestablished in this comparison.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call the 24-hour oncology team immediately about new breathlessness, persistent cough, new diarrhoea, blood in stools, severe weakness, yellow eyes or a severe headache. Symptoms can start after treatment ends. Carry your immunotherapy alert card. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
