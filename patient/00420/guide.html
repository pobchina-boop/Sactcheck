Doxorubicin and cisplatin for osteosarcoma

Chemotherapy treats osteosarcoma cells throughout the body, including cells too small to see on scans, alongside surgery intended to remove the visible cancer.

Chemotherapy before and after surgery for osteosarcoma · NCCP 00420 v4 · Document revised 18 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

This is the six-cycle doxorubicin/cisplatin course. It is distinct from the surgery-linked high-dose methotrexate combination. The national source gives no numeric age cutoff. Lifetime anthracycline exposure and individual heart risk must be reconciled.

My overall treatment

  1. TreatmentDoxorubicin 25 mg/m² is given on days 1, 2 and 3; cisplatin 100 mg/m² on day 1. The reference course contains six 21-day cycles, usually three before surgery and three afterwards, with pathology review. Treatment dates and doses depend on recovery and the specialist plan.

Between treatments

  • Side effects can occur on any day. Use the verified oncology contact plan promptly if unwell.
  • Cisplatin has high nausea/vomiting risk. Agree prevention and rescue anti-sickness medicines, prescribed hydration and electrolyte replacement. A separate white-cell growth-factor plan may be chosen for clinical need. Do not add diuretics or change fluid intake yourself.

Bring the medicine list and treatment/contact record. Report new symptoms before treatment.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
