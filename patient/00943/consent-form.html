Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

Pembrolizumab with cisplatin and radiotherapy for cervical cancer

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

The aim is to cure the cancer. Radiation treats the cervix and nearby areas. Cisplatin helps radiation work, and pembrolizumab helps the immune system recognise cancer cells. Adults with FIGO 2014 stage III–IVA cervical cancer who have not had definitive treatment. PD-L1 testing is not required for this indication.

Treatment schedule. Cycles 1–5 last 21 days. Cisplatin is planned weekly for five doses in cycles 1–2, with a possible sixth dose. Pembrolizumab continues alone from cycle 3 and changes to every 42 days for cycles 6–20. Treatment can end earlier and lasts up to 24 months.

Pelvic radiotherapy alongside chemotherapy

Discuss bowel and bladder irritation, vaginal changes and the effects on sexual health, ovarian function and fertility that apply to you. Your radiation oncology team will discuss and document your radiotherapy plan separately.

Treatment discussion and decision

Pembrolizumab with cisplatin and radiotherapy for cervical cancer

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

Among people with stage III–IVA disease, the group receiving immunotherapy with chemoradiation had fewer deaths during follow-up. Subgroup findings are exploratory. The overall trial also included earlier node-positive stages. It did not test pembrolizumab alone against chemoradiotherapy or directly compare induction chemotherapy.

Discuss cure intent, fertility before pelvic radiation, early menopause, vaginal narrowing and sexual health, hearing and kidney injury, and immune effects that may persist or need hormone replacement.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call the 24-hour oncology line for fever or shaking chills, new breathlessness, frequent diarrhoea, very little urine, severe headache, heavy bleeding or severe weakness. If breathing becomes difficult at rest, seek emergency help. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
