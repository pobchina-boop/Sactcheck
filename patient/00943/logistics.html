Preparing for treatment visits

Pembrolizumab with cisplatin and radiotherapy for cervical cancer

Planning your visits

Appointments

Cycles 1–5 last 21 days. Cisplatin is planned weekly for five doses in cycles 1–2, with a possible sixth dose. Pembrolizumab continues alone from cycle 3 and changes to every 42 days for cycles 6–20. Treatment can end earlier and lasts up to 24 months.

On the day

When both drugs are due, pembrolizumab is infused over 30 minutes before cisplatin over 60 minutes. IV fluids protect the kidneys. Radiation follows cisplatin that day. External-beam radiation and the later internal treatment (brachytherapy) use a separately booked calendar.

Between visits

Radiation appointments are booked separately. Keep both calendars together. Tell the chemotherapy and radiation teams about any dose or appointment change.

Write down your next appointments

  • Tell the team about hearing loss, immune disease, steroid use or problems drinking.
Contact your oncology service urgently. Call the 24-hour oncology line for fever or shaking chills, new breathlessness, frequent diarrhoea, very little urine, severe headache, heavy bleeding or severe weakness. If breathing becomes difficult at rest, seek emergency help. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.
