When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Stop capecitabine and contact oncology for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth or hand-foot symptoms affecting activity, vomiting that prevents drinking, fever or sudden illness. Do not restart after a toxicity interruption until the team advises. Severe abdominal pain, significant bleeding, sudden weakness or a severe unusual headache also need urgent assessment.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the treatment works

This combination treats metastatic or unresectable colorectal cancer when an irinotecan- or oxaliplatin-based combination is unsuitable. Its aims may include cancer control, symptom relief and longer survival.

Oral chemotherapy

Capecitabine

Xeloda
Becomes fluorouracil in the body

After you swallow capecitabine, your body changes it into fluorouracil. This interferes with the DNA and RNA processes cells use to grow. Normal cells can also be affected, which helps explain mouth soreness, bowel symptoms and changes in blood counts.

Swallowed tablet↓Converted to fluorouracil↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Stop tablets and contact the team for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth ulcers, hand-foot soreness affecting activities, fever or dehydration. Chest pain needs urgent assessment. Do not restart until instructed.

Sources: nccp · patient · smpc

Anti-VEGF targeted antibody

Bevacizumab

Avastin or a prescribed biosimilar
Blocks a blood-vessel growth signal

Bevacizumab binds VEGF, a signal that helps blood vessels grow. Blocking it can restrict the blood supply supporting a tumour. It also affects normal blood vessels and healing, which is why blood pressure, urine and planned procedures matter.

VEGF signal↓Bevacizumab binds VEGF↓New-vessel signalling is reduced

What to watch for
Report bleeding, a wound that is not healing, severe abdominal pain, new swelling, chest symptoms or a severe headache. Tell the team before surgery, dental procedures or port insertion.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1–14: Treatment days

Bevacizumab infusion on Day 1; capecitabine twice daily on Days 1–14, then no capecitabine on Days 15–21. Follow your individual prescription. Use the current tablet label. Swallow whole with water with or within 30 minutes of food; do not crush or cut tablets.

Day 15–21: Planned tablet-free days

Do not take leftover tablets to replace doses missed earlier. Side effects may continue during this interval. Keep the oncology contact plan available and confirm the next review before restarting.

Practical treatment guide

Tablets, visits and practical planning

A simple guide to what happens before treatment, on treatment days and at home. Your own service confirms appointments and prescriptions.

STEP 1

Before the first dose

Confirm DPD results, blood tests, kidney/liver checks and the treatment aim. Keep a current medicine list.

STEP 2

Get the tablet plan

Check the number of 150 mg and/or 500 mg tablets in each dose and exactly which days to take them. Bring questions to the oncology pharmacist.

STEP 3

Plan your visits

The first bevacizumab infusion usually runs over 90 minutes; later infusions may shorten if tolerated. Checks and preparation add time. A cannula or existing suitable line is used; there is no take-home pump in this regimen.

STEP 4

Take tablets with food

Take the prescribed morning and evening doses with or within 30 minutes of food, swallowed whole with water. If swallowing is difficult, contact pharmacy for a safe plan.

STEP 5

Know when to pause

Significant diarrhoea, painful mouth or hands/feet, fever, sudden illness or dehydration: stop tablets and call. A missed or vomited dose is not replaced.

STEP 6

Review and next steps

Do not restart after a toxicity break without instructions. Bring your symptom record and remaining tablets if requested. Return unused tablets to pharmacy.

Keeping tablets safe

Keep the original labelled packaging away from children. Follow the storage label and pharmacy handling instructions. Do not share tablets or discard them in household waste.

If treatment is interrupted

Tell the team which doses were missed and why. Never extend the course, double a dose or move tablet days yourself. Ask for a revised written plan.

Questions about your appointments

Ask about transport, blood-test location, pharmacy collection and a named contact for appointment changes. Booking arrangements differ between centres.

Venous access and procedures

Bevacizumab may be given through a cannula or a suitable existing central line. A port is not automatically required. Discuss any planned line insertion, dental work or surgery with oncology because bevacizumab affects wound healing; the team sets any treatment pause.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Written tablet instructions

Ask for the number of each tablet strength per dose, the start and stop days, and the contact number. This page does not calculate a personal dose.

Missed or vomited dose

Skip that dose; take the next scheduled dose at its usual time unless the team has told you to stop. Never double up. Record missed doses and report repeated vomiting.

Skin, mouth and bowel care

Use the agreed skin and mouth-care plan. Keep your prescribed anti-sickness and diarrhoea medicines available. Significant symptoms still require stopping tablets and calling.

Checks and medicine review

Confirm DPD testing and regular blood tests. Tell the team about warfarin, phenytoin, allopurinol, brivudine and any new medicines or supplements. These need an interaction check.

Treatment reference · 02

Taking capecitabine safely

Tablets are active chemotherapy, with a specific schedule.

Meals and doses

Use your current prescription. Swallow tablets whole with water with or within 30 minutes after food. Do not cut, chew or crush them; ask pharmacy if swallowing is difficult.

Sources: nccp · patient

Missed doses or vomiting

Do not replace a missed or vomited dose. Never double the next dose. Contact the team for repeated vomiting or uncertainty about the schedule.

Sources: nccp · patient

Pause and restart

Stop tablets for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth/hand-foot symptoms affecting activity, fever or dehydration and seek advice. A toxicity-related pause requires instructions before restart.

Sources: nccp · patient

Different tablet strengths

Different strengths may be combined to make your dose. A reduction changes tablet numbers: use the new label and do not calculate a change from this page.

Sources: nccp · patient

Treatment reference · 03

Tests and checks

Monitoring helps the team decide whether treatment can continue.

DPD testing

DPD helps break down fluoropyrimidine medicines. Low activity can cause severe toxicity; testing before treatment helps guide the plan but cannot prevent every serious reaction.

Sources: nccp · patient

Blood, kidney and liver results

Counts, kidney and liver function are checked before each cycle. Diarrhoea and poor intake can worsen kidney function and make tablets harder to tolerate.

Sources: nccp · patient

Your symptoms matter

Report bowel frequency, stoma output, mouth soreness, hand-foot pain, chest symptoms and what daily activities you can no longer manage.

Sources: nccp · patient

Additional checks

Blood pressure, urine protein, bleeding/clot symptoms and healing of wounds are checked with bevacizumab.

Sources: nccp · patient

Treatment reference · 04

Recognise symptoms and act

Do not wait for the next appointment if you feel unwell.

Bowel and hydration

Report diarrhoea or increased stoma output early. Inability to drink, dizziness, very little urine, severe pain or blood in stool needs prompt assessment.

Sources: nccp · patient

Hands, feet and mouth

Pain, cracking, peeling or ulcers that affect normal activities or eating/drinking need advice. Gentle preventive care helps, but does not replace reporting.

Sources: nccp · patient

Chest symptoms and infection

Stop capecitabine and seek urgent assessment for chest pain. Fever, shaking chills or sudden illness needs immediate oncology contact, even if the temperature is normal.

Sources: nccp · patient

Treatment-specific effects

Bevacizumab can cause high blood pressure, urine protein, bleeding, clotting and healing problems. Report severe unusual headache, weakness, significant bleeding or severe abdominal pain urgently.

Sources: nccp · patient

Treatment reference · 05

Daily life and other medicines

Make the plan fit your practical needs.

Eating, drinking and activity

Aim for tolerable meals and fluids; contact the team if intake falls. Gentle activity and rest can be adjusted to your energy. Ask for dietetic help if needed.

Medicine and supplement checks

Check new medicines with oncology pharmacy. Warfarin can require closer INR checks; phenytoin levels may need monitoring. Brivudine is an important prohibited interaction.

Ask about infection exposure, work, intimacy, contraception, fertility and any caring responsibilities. Use the medicine-specific duration of precautions given by your team.

Understanding the evidence

AVEX: benefit and treatment burden

The study compared adding bevacizumab with capecitabine alone in an older, selected population.

Show trial results and what the numbers mean

AVEX randomised 280 people aged 70 or older with previously untreated metastatic colorectal cancer who were unsuitable for oxaliplatin- or irinotecan-based combinations.

Bevacizumab + capecitabineCapecitabine alone

Progression-free survival HR 0.53 (95% CI 0.41–0.69); the median difference was four months, with more severe treatment-related toxicity. This is not a guaranteed individual gain in survival. AVEX used capecitabine 1000 mg/m² twice daily; NCCP 00623 lists 1250 mg/m² with a lower-start option.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the aim of my treatment?

This combination treats metastatic or unresectable colorectal cancer when an irinotecan- or oxaliplatin-based combination is unsuitable. Its aims may include cancer control, symptom relief and longer survival.

Which days do I take the tablets?

Bevacizumab infusion on Day 1; capecitabine twice daily on Days 1–14, then no capecitabine on Days 15–21. Follow your individual prescription.

What if I forget a dose or vomit?

Do not take an extra dose. Take the next planned dose at its usual time unless treatment has been stopped; report repeated vomiting and check with the team if unsure.

When should I stop tablets and call?

Stop capecitabine and contact oncology for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth or hand-foot symptoms affecting activity, vomiting that prevents drinking, fever or sudden illness. Do not restart after a toxicity interruption until the team advises. Severe abdominal pain, significant bleeding, sudden weakness or a severe unusual headache also need urgent assessment.

Why do I need blood tests if I take tablets at home?

Blood counts, kidney and liver results, together with symptoms, guide whether to continue and at what dose.

Do I need a port or a pump?

No take-home chemotherapy pump is used. Bevacizumab needs an IV infusion; your team chooses a cannula or suitable existing line.

What happens after treatment?

Ask for your review and follow-up plan. Continue reporting symptoms after the last dose; further therapy depends on the treatment aim and response.
