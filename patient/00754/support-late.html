CUH Department of Medical Oncology: Adjuvant medication prescription for chemotherapy High Risk for nausea and vomiting, Version 2 (05/05/2021)

Complete historical CUH reference for reconciliation, not an issued prescription. Preserve one individual treatment-day, delayed and rescue plan. Do not automatically repeat a start-day course, combine competing courses, or transfer fixed adult reference doses to a child. The treating team confirms each item, actual days, interactions and duration. Ifosfamide remains high emetic risk in all three IVADo/IVA phases, including after doxorubicin stops. This reference applies to the combination treatment, not a new course for vincristine-only days8/15 in cycles1/2; retain the individual whole-cycle plan. The local CUH source is retained as a reference list, not as the national antiemetic standard. Nystatin/chlorhexidine are displayed as local mouth-care adjuncts, not universal antiemetic or mucositis prophylaxis. Verify current local oral-care policy. Inherited reference safety notes: Caution: metoclopramide with olanzapine may increase extrapyramidal adverse effects. Dexamethasone may be modified or omitted where the systemic anticancer therapy regimen already includes a steroid. Metoclopramide: adult maximum 10 mg up to three times daily (30 mg/day) and maximum treatment duration 5 days under current European/Irish product information; avoid prolonged repeated use without review.

Additional phase-specific support

  • Follow one written prevention, home and rescue plan. The team reconciles all steroids and interacting medicines; no generic reference is an additional prescription.
  • On each of days 1 and 2, mesna 900 mg/m² intravenously over ten minutes immediately before ifosfamide, then 3000 mg/m² continuously over 24 hours starting with ifosfamide. It binds bladder-damaging metabolites; kidney and brain monitoring are still required.
  • Hospital-prescribed fluids support urine flow and clearance. The team adjusts them to urine output, blood tests, weight and heart/kidney status; continue the prescribed protection after chemotherapy finishes. Follow individual home fluid advice.
  • Cycles 1–2: daily injections on days 4–7 and 9–11 at the prescribed 5 micrograms/kg. Cycles 3–9: pegylated growth factor may be considered from day 4. These support marrow recovery; the later plan is not automatically the same as the early plan.
  • Use the prescribed mouth-care and constipation-prevention plan. Vincristine slows bowel movement through nerve effects; report painful constipation, vomiting or abdominal swelling promptly. Mouth care helps detect and manage damaged lining before drinking becomes difficult.
  • The team considers Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia and antifungal prevention, and any tumour-lysis precautions. These are individual decisions here. Azole antifungals can increase vincristine toxicity; pharmacy must reconcile the plan.
  • Confirm whether this is cycle 1–2, 3–4 or 5–9, and which vincristine appointments and doxorubicin doses apply. Check the full mesna/hydration prescription and the growth-factor calendar. Ask about individual infection prevention, bowel support and medicines that may interact.
  • Follow the current prescription; an improved test result does not authorise restarting after a hold.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
