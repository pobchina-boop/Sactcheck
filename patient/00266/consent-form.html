Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

TP/TE for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds. For high-risk GTN resistant to or recurring after EMA/CO.

Treatment schedule. Day 1 is paclitaxel and cisplatin. Day 15 is paclitaxel and etoposide. One full cycle lasts 28 days.

Regimen-specific side effects

Paclitaxel

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Nerves, marrow and infusion reactionsReport numbness, fever or unexpected bleeding. Tell the nurse at once about chest tightness or breathlessness. Hair loss is common.

Cisplatin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Kidneys, hearing, nerves and sicknessReport reduced urine, persistent vomiting, tinnitus, hearing loss or numbness. Attend for fluids and electrolyte checks.

Etoposide

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Low counts and infusion reactionPhone for fever or bleeding. Report flushing or faintness during infusion. Hair loss and a small later leukaemia risk are possible.

Treatment discussion and decision

TP/TE for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

A specialist series reports activity with alternating paclitaxel-based doublets in relapsed or resistant GTN. Small non-randomised cohort. Prior platinum exposure and individual response affect interpretation.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Report new tingling or hearing changes before your next infusion. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest tightness or breathing difficulty. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
