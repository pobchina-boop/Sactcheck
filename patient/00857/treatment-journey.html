Pembrolizumab and chemotherapy: the whole breast pathway

Neoadjuvant → surgery → adjuvant · triple-negative early breast cancer

This is NCCP 00857: four 3-week cycles of carboplatin/weekly paclitaxel with pembrolizumab, then four 3-week AC cycles with pembrolizumab, surgery, and postoperative pembrolizumab when appropriate.

What each check is for

1Know when the chemotherapy changesAfter four 3-week cycles

The weekly paclitaxel visits stop and the AC phase starts.

Ask / act: Confirm which medicines and supportive medicines change.

Sources: NCCP 00857

2Thyroid and other immune checksThyroid tests every 3–6 weeks; glucose each cycle; other tests as indicated

Pembrolizumab can inflame hormone glands and other organs.

Ask / act: Report diarrhoea, breathlessness, severe fatigue, thirst, headache, rash or jaundice promptly.

Sources: NCCP 00857

3Heart assessment around doxorubicinBefore and during the anthracycline phase, according to risk

Doxorubicin can affect the heart; ECG and echo answer different questions.

Ask / act: Ask whether a baseline echo and follow-up cardiac tests are needed.

Sources: NCCP 00857 · ESC

4Postoperative medicines and radiotherapyAfter pathology and recovery

The team combines recurrence-risk information, tolerance and the operation result.

Ask / act: Ask whether radiotherapy overlaps the remaining pembrolizumab and which clinician coordinates the plan.

Sources: NCCP 00857 · EARLY

5Surveillance and lasting effectsAfter early breast treatment

Annual mammography of remaining breast tissue after treatment; the team sets the first date. The mastectomy side is not routinely mammographed. New symptoms need assessment between visits.

Ask / act: Report new symptoms early, including after immunotherapy has stopped.

Sources: EARLY

At the next review, ask

  • Which treatment phase am I in, and what happens next?
  • Which test is expected next, who arranges it, and who explains the result?
  • Who should I contact if an expected appointment has not arrived?
