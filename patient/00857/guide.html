Treatment journey
Cycles 1-4

Every 21 days: pembrolizumab + carboplatin on day 1; paclitaxel on days 1, 8 and 15.

Cycles 5-8

Every 21 days: pembrolizumab + doxorubicin + cyclophosphamide on day 1.

Surgery

Definitive breast surgery follows the neoadjuvant phase when clinically appropriate.

Adjuvant phase

Pembrolizumab continues after surgery: 200 mg every 21 days for 9 cycles or 400 mg every 42 days for 5 cycles, according to the treatment plan.

Throughout

Bloods and toxicity review guide whether treatment is given, delayed or modified.

Important medicine-specific effects

Pembrolizumab - immune effects

  • Immune inflammation can affect lungs, bowel, liver, hormone glands, kidneys, skin, heart, nerves or muscles.
  • New symptoms can begin during treatment or after treatment has stopped.

Paclitaxel

  • Peripheral neuropathy can cause numbness, tingling or pain.
  • Infusion reactions can occur despite premedication.
  • Low blood counts and hair loss are common treatment effects.

Carboplatin

  • Low blood counts, especially neutropenia and low platelets, can occur.
  • Allergic infusion reactions can occur, particularly after repeated exposure.
  • Nausea and vomiting may occur despite preventative medicines.

Doxorubicin + cyclophosphamide

  • Low blood counts and infection risk can be significant.
  • Doxorubicin can reduce heart function; cyclophosphamide can irritate the bladder.
  • Mouth soreness and hair loss are common treatment effects.
Other / shared treatment effects: The anatomy page highlights important symptom patterns for this exact regimen, but it cannot show every possible effect. Fatigue, nausea, appetite change, mouth/bowel symptoms or blood-count changes may occur depending on the medicines used. Report new or worsening symptoms even if they are not listed here.

Monitoring / medicines

  • Blood count, kidney/liver profile and glucose are checked regularly; thyroid testing is repeated during pembrolizumab treatment.
  • Paclitaxel requires premedication according to the regimen/local protocol.
  • Cardiac assessment may be required before the doxorubicin phase.

Bring / track

  • Your current medicines, including non-prescription products.
  • A note of new symptoms, when they started and what they stopped you doing.
  • Any temperature, blood-pressure or bowel-count readings your team asked you to record.
  • Questions about the purpose of treatment, scan/review timing or changes to the plan.

When to call - do not wait for the next appointment

Call oncology urgently for fever, new breathlessness, persistent diarrhoea, jaundice, severe rash, significant bleeding, severe weakness or a new neurological symptom. For severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizure call 112/999. If your team has given you different local thresholds or emergency instructions, follow those instructions.

Consent discussion support

Use the approved consent process to document treatment aim, expected benefit, material risks, alternatives, questions and the patient's decision.
