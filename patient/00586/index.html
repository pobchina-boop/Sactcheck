More about my medicines

Capecitabine

After you swallow capecitabine, your body changes it into fluorouracil. This interferes with the DNA and RNA processes cells use to grow. Normal cells can also be affected, which helps explain mouth soreness, bowel symptoms and changes in blood counts.

Pelvic radiotherapy

Radiation is directed at the planned area. Capecitabine helps sensitise cancer cells to it. Nearby bowel, bladder and skin may also become irritated. Effects can build over the weeks and may continue after the final session.

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1–5: Treatment days

Capecitabine twice daily on Days 1–5 of each 7-day cycle, alongside radiotherapy, for up to 5–6 cycles. Follow the written plan for treatment days and interruptions.

What to do: Use the current tablet label. Swallow whole with water with or within 30 minutes of food; do not crush or cut tablets.

Day 6–7: Planned tablet-free days

Do not take leftover tablets to replace doses missed earlier. Side effects may continue during this interval.

What to do: Keep the oncology contact plan available and confirm the next review before restarting.

Support at home

Concurrent capecitabine and pelvic radiotherapy

No automatic high/moderate IV antiemetic course for capecitabine. Written tablet-day and missed/held-dose instructions; do not double doses or extend to weekends without the team’s plan. Diarrhoea/dehydration, hand-foot and pelvic skin care; nutrition and urgent-contact support.
