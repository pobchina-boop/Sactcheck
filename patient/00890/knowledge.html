When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Get urgent help for severe or persistent tummy pain, black or bloody stools, coughing or vomiting blood, new severe headache, confusion, seizure, visual change, chest pain, sudden breathlessness or very little urine. Report fever, sudden illness, painful hands/feet and new bleeding promptly.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Your targeted medicine

Fruquintinib is a targeted capsule treatment to help control bowel cancer that has spread after previous treatments. The team checks which treatments you have received, whether they remain suitable, and your priorities.

Targeted therapy capsule

Fruquintinib

Fruzaqla
Blocks VEGF receptor signals

Fruquintinib blocks VEGF receptor signals that help tumours form blood vessels. Reducing these signals can slow cancer growth. Blood vessels in healthy tissues are also affected, which helps explain blood-pressure, bleeding, protein-in-urine and wound-healing risks.

VEGF signals↓Receptors blocked↓Blood-vessel growth reduced

What to watch for
Report painful hands/feet, bowel changes, bleeding or neurological symptoms; attend blood-pressure, urine and blood checks even if well.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Prescription and calendar

Take the prescribed fruquintinib dose once daily on Days 1–21, then take no fruquintinib on Days 22–28. Take it at about the same time each day, with or without food. Repeat only when your team authorises the next cycle.

STEP 2

Taking capsules

The usual starting dose is 5 mg once daily, but follow your own label. Swallow capsules whole with water, with or without food; do not chew, dissolve or open them. Tell the team before starting if swallowing capsules is difficult.

STEP 3

Missed or vomited dose

If a dose is less than 12 hours late, take it and then follow the usual schedule. If more than 12 hours late, skip it and take the next dose at the normal time. At exactly 12 hours, or if unsure, ask your pharmacist or team. Do not take a replacement capsule after vomiting; take the next dose at the usual time.

STEP 4

Tests and visits

Baseline and regular blood count, kidney/liver profile, thyroid function and urine protein. Blood pressure must be controlled before starting, then checked weekly for the first month and monthly thereafter, or more often if needed. For warfarin, INR weekly until stable, then before each cycle. Scans and markers follow the consultant plan.

STEP 5

Storage

Keep the original bottle tightly closed to protect from moisture and out of children’s sight and reach. No special temperature conditions are required. Follow the expiry date and ask the pharmacist how to return unused capsules; do not put them into household waste or wastewater.

STEP 6

Treatment changes

Your team must confirm dose changes and restarts. Bring the capsule pack, current calendar and medicine list to review.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Skin care

Use the unit’s skin-care plan and report redness, pain, swelling or blisters early. The team may prescribe creams or change treatment. Do not wait until walking or everyday hand use is difficult.

Sickness and bowels

This oral medicine has minimal-to-low emetogenic potential. Routine premedication is not usually needed; agree any anti-sickness and diarrhoea medicines with the team. Persistent vomiting, diarrhoea, poor intake or reduced urine needs prompt advice.

Blood pressure and medication review

Take prescribed blood-pressure medicines and keep monitoring visits. Tell the team about anticoagulants and every prescription, over-the-counter and herbal medicine.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Use highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least two weeks after the last dose if you could become pregnant. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for at least two weeks afterwards.

Treatment reference · 01

Why this treatment may be offered

Confirm the treatment history and personal goals.

Previously treated bowel cancer

Fruquintinib is a targeted capsule treatment to help control bowel cancer that has spread after previous treatments. The team checks which treatments you have received, whether they remain suitable, and your priorities. The team confirms the full treatment history, including chemotherapy, anti-VEGF and anti-EGFR treatment where applicable, and progression or intolerance on trifluridine/tipiracil or regorafenib.

Evidence and uncertainty

Median overall survival was 7.4 months with fruquintinib and 4.8 months with placebo. These group medians are not a prediction of one person’s lifespan or a guaranteed extra 2.6 months. The team should discuss the balance of benefit and side effects for you.

Treatment duration

There is no fixed total number of cycles. Continue while benefit and tolerability justify it; the team reviews symptoms, scans, tests and your preferences.

Treatment reference · 02

How fruquintinib works

It affects signals involved in blood-vessel growth.

VEGF receptor signals

Fruquintinib blocks VEGF receptor signals that help tumours form blood vessels. Reducing these signals can slow cancer growth. Blood vessels in healthy tissues are also affected, which helps explain blood-pressure, bleeding, protein-in-urine and wound-healing risks.

Safety checks

Blood-pressure and urine-protein changes can develop without symptoms. Tests and early symptom reporting help the team decide whether treatment can safely continue.

Treatment reference · 03

Your capsule and rest-day plan

Use your own prescription and calendar.

Days 1–21 and Days 22–28

Take the prescribed fruquintinib dose once daily on Days 1–21, then take no fruquintinib on Days 22–28. Take it at about the same time each day, with or without food. Repeat only when your team authorises the next cycle.

Swallow whole, with or without food

The usual starting dose is 5 mg once daily, but follow your own label. Swallow capsules whole with water, with or without food; do not chew, dissolve or open them. Tell the team before starting if swallowing capsules is difficult.

Missed or vomited dose

At exactly 12 hours, or if unsure, ask your pharmacist or team. Do not take a replacement capsule after vomiting; take the next dose at the usual time.

If too much is taken

Contact your doctor immediately if you take more than prescribed. You may need medical attention; take the pack and treatment calendar with you.

Treatment reference · 04

Checks and follow-up

Feeling well does not replace testing.

Before and during treatment

Baseline and regular blood count, kidney/liver profile, thyroid function and urine protein. Blood pressure must be controlled before starting, then checked weekly for the first month and monthly thereafter, or more often if needed. For warfarin, INR weekly until stable, then before each cycle. Scans and markers follow the consultant plan.

If a test is abnormal

The team may arrange extra blood or urine tests, treat hypertension, pause capsules, reduce the dose or stop treatment. Do not change the dose yourself.

Scans and tumour markers

The consultant decides the imaging and marker schedule for your situation. This regimen does not mandate a fixed computed tomography (CT) or carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) interval.

After a pause or stopping

Ask who follows persistent symptoms and abnormal tests, and when the next review is due. Do not restart without clear instructions.

Treatment reference · 05

Skin, mouth, eating and energy

Early support can help preserve daily activities.

Hands and feet

Use the unit’s skin-care plan and report redness, pain, swelling or blisters early. The team may prescribe creams or change treatment. Do not wait until walking or everyday hand use is difficult.

Bowels, fluids and sickness

This oral medicine has minimal-to-low emetogenic potential. Routine premedication is not usually needed; agree any anti-sickness and diarrhoea medicines with the team. Persistent vomiting, diarrhoea, poor intake or reduced urine needs prompt advice.

Mouth and appetite

Report mouth soreness, voice changes, poor appetite or weight loss. Ask for mouth care and nutritional support. Drink as advised for your health.

Fatigue and thyroid

New marked fatigue, feeling unusually cold, weight or skin changes may need thyroid assessment. Balance activity with rest and report symptoms affecting usual activities.

Driving

Do not drive or use machinery if tiredness or other symptoms affect concentration or reactions.

Treatment reference · 06

Symptoms needing urgent help

A rare effect may still need immediate care.

Bleeding and bowel problems

Seek urgent help for black or bloody stools, blood in urine, coughing/vomiting blood, severe or persistent abdominal pain. Heavy bleeding or collapse needs emergency care.

Neurological, heart or vascular symptoms

Severe headache with confusion, seizure or visual changes, chest pain, severe breathlessness, sudden weakness or sudden severe chest/back/abdominal pain requires emergency help.

Kidneys, skin and infection

Report much less urine or swelling, painful hands/feet, fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell. Symptoms may need assessment even during the capsule-free week.

Treatment reference · 07

Other medicines, wounds and procedures

Tell every treating team you take fruquintinib.

Medication review

Tell the pharmacist about anticoagulants, antibiotics, infection treatments, seizure medicines, herbal remedies and supplements. Rifampicin and other strong enzyme inducers can affect fruquintinib exposure. Ask before starting or stopping anything.

Wound healing and surgery

Report unhealed wounds and planned surgery. Product information recommends withholding fruquintinib for at least two weeks before surgery and not restarting for at least two weeks afterwards and until adequate wound healing. Your oncology and surgical teams must agree the exact plan; do not stop or restart it yourself.

Allergies

Tell the pharmacist about medicine or colouring-agent allergies. Capsule colours differ by strength and contain colouring agents that can cause allergic reactions.

Treatment reference · 08

Pregnancy precautions and safe storage

Keep the medicine in its labelled packaging.

Pregnancy and feeding

Use highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least two weeks after the last dose if you could become pregnant. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for at least two weeks afterwards.

Storage

Keep the original bottle tightly closed to protect from moisture and out of children’s sight and reach. No special temperature conditions are required. Follow the expiry date and ask the pharmacist how to return unused capsules; do not put them into household waste or wastewater.

Your treatment record

Carry the wallet card and current medicine list. Record the prescribed dose, any holds, symptoms, BP and upcoming tests. Replace all example telephone numbers with your real oncology contacts before using the card.

Evidence in previously treated colorectal cancer

FRESCO-2 compared fruquintinib plus best supportive care with placebo plus best supportive care after previous metastatic colorectal treatments.
