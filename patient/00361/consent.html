One-page consent aid
NCCP 00361 · version 4

Fulvestrant · injections every 4 weeks

NCCP schedule: 500 mg on Days 1 and 15 of the first 28-day cycle, then Day 1 of each 28-day cycle. This means Day 29 next, then every 28 days.

Purpose, setting and alternatives

The aim is to control advanced hormone-sensitive breast cancer and its symptoms, with response and tolerability reviewed before continuing. This NCCP monotherapy pathway covers postmenopausal women with ER-positive locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer that has relapsed or progressed on or after adjuvant anti-oestrogen treatment. Fulvestrant prescribed with capivasertib or a CDK4/6 medicine follows a separate combination regimen.

Material risks and support

  • Injection-site pain and uncommon nerve injury
  • Hot flushes, fatigue and nausea
  • Joint / muscle pain
  • Liver enzyme changes or liver injury
  • Bleeding / bruising risk with intramuscular injections
  • Blood clots and hypersensitivity reactions

Discuss the treatment aim, injection discomfort and possible nerve injury, bleeding or bruising, liver effects, flushes, joint symptoms and fatigue. Liver impairment or an existing modified dose requires an agreed prescriber and pharmacy plan. Where relevant, use effective contraception during treatment and for two years after the final dose under the EMA product information. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are excluded. Tell staff about blood thinners; do not stop them yourself.

Monitoring and understanding

Before treatment, check blood count, kidney and liver function, the menopausal and treatment history, pregnancy exclusion and bleeding risk. Before injections, tell staff about anticoagulants, bruising, bleeding or previous nerve symptoms. Repeat liver tests when indicated; scans follow the agreed cancer-control plan. Tell anyone ordering an oestrogen blood test that you receive fulvestrant because some tests can give a misleadingly high result.
