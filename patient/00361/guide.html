Your practical treatment plan

Know the next step.

Taking treatment

NCCP schedule: 500 mg on Days 1 and 15 of the first 28-day cycle, then Day 1 of each 28-day cycle. This means Day 29 next, then every 28 days.

Each usual 500 mg dose is given as two 5 mL injections, one in each buttock, slowly over one to two minutes per injection. This is not an intravenous infusion; no port or pump is needed for fulvestrant itself.

Checks and follow-up

FBC, renal and liver profile. Confirm the indication, menopausal state, liver category, pregnancy exclusion and any bleeding / anticoagulant risk before intramuscular injections. Liver profile as clinically indicated. At injection visits review symptoms, bleeding / bruising and injection-site or nerve symptoms; response imaging follows the cancer-control plan.

The whole-treatment map distinguishes early-breast follow-up from advanced-disease response assessment.

Support between reviews

Symptoms and daily life

Report injection pain with numbness or radiating leg pain, extensive bruising, jaundice or worsening symptoms.

Support medicines

No routine antiemetic, steroid premedication or G-CSF is required by this endocrine monotherapy. Ask for individual symptom support.

Tests and appointments

Ask who books each check, when it is due and who reviews it. Tell the team if an expected appointment has not arrived.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
