How this medicine supports your treatment

The aim is to control advanced hormone-sensitive breast cancer and its symptoms, with response and tolerability reviewed before continuing.

Hormone therapy · oestrogen-receptor degrader

Fulvestrant

Binds the oestrogen receptor, blocks its signalling and helps the cell remove the receptor
Oestrogen receptors can drive cancer-cell growth↓Fulvestrant binds and blocks the receptor↓The number of receptors falls, reducing growth signals

Fulvestrant is a selective oestrogen-receptor degrader. It blocks receptor activity and lowers the amount of receptor available in cancer cells. The slow-release injection keeps the medicine in the body between visits; the extra first-month dose helps establish treatment exposure.

Report severe injection pain, numbness, weakness or pain travelling down the leg, unusual bleeding or bruising, jaundice and symptoms that interfere with daily life.

NCCP 00361 v4 · official regimen

Medicine and schedule

NCCP schedule: 500 mg on Days 1 and 15 of the first 28-day cycle, then Day 1 of each 28-day cycle. This means Day 29 next, then every 28 days. Each usual 500 mg dose is given as two 5 mL injections, one in each buttock, slowly over one to two minutes per injection. This is not an intravenous infusion; no port or pump is needed for fulvestrant itself.

Review and duration

Each usual 500 mg dose is given as two 5 mL injections, one in each buttock, slowly over one to two minutes per injection. This is not an intravenous infusion; no port or pump is needed for fulvestrant itself. Symptoms can occur at any time; this is not a chemotherapy nadir or recovery curve.

NCCP 00361 v4 · official regimen

Individual risks and support

Discuss the treatment aim, injection discomfort and possible nerve injury, bleeding or bruising, liver effects, flushes, joint symptoms and fatigue. Liver impairment or an existing modified dose requires an agreed prescriber and pharmacy plan. Where relevant, use effective contraception during treatment and for two years after the final dose under the EMA product information. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are excluded. Tell staff about blood thinners; do not stop them yourself.

Your setting changes the pathway

Advanced-disease scans

For advanced breast cancer, response assessment is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease pace, sites, symptoms and treatment. Indolent disease may be reviewed less often; new symptoms can prompt earlier scans. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not a universal three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO metastatic breast cancer guidelines

Tumour markers

Tumour markers may supplement imaging and clinical assessment in advanced disease if they are informative, especially if elevated at baseline. They do not replace scans, and a marker change alone should not determine treatment failure. There is no universal fixed marker timetable.

ESO-ESMO ABC 5 consensus: tumour-marker interpretation

Menopause, pregnancy and contraception

Where relevant, effective contraception is needed during treatment and for two years after the final dose under the EMA product information. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are excluded.

Practical support

Make injection visits manageable

Discuss injection discomfort, flushes, joint or muscle symptoms, fatigue and nausea. Ask early for symptom help or practical support attending appointments.

Support medicines

Fulvestrant alone does not require routine steroid premedication, injections to raise white cells or a standard anti-sickness course. A combination medicine can add its own supportive-care needs.

Bleeding and nerve symptoms

Tell staff about blood thinners, unusual bruising or bleeding before an injection. Severe buttock pain, new numbness, weakness or pain down the leg needs prompt assessment.

Tests and appointments

Confirm the extra loading visit and later appointments, who books required blood tests and scans, and who reviews the results. Tell the laboratory team about fulvestrant if an oestrogen blood test is ordered.

Taking tablets / attending injections

Why fulvestrant is offered

Fulvestrant can help control advanced hormone-sensitive breast cancer. Studies support the usual treatment schedule, while the choice of treatment depends on previous endocrine therapy and the individual cancer.

A study comparing fulvestrant doses does not show that monotherapy is better than modern combination treatment. A separate study in people who had not received endocrine therapy found longer control of progression but no proven overall-survival advantage over anastrozole. These findings do not predict an individual outcome.

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.

Questions to ask

When are the injections given?

The usual schedule is Day 1, Day 15 and Day 29, then every 28 days. The first month includes an extra loading visit. Follow your clinic appointments; contact the team if a visit is missed.

When are bloods, ECGs or echoes needed?

Before treatment, check blood count, kidney and liver function, the menopausal and treatment history, pregnancy exclusion and bleeding risk. Before injections, tell staff about anticoagulants, bruising, bleeding or previous nerve symptoms. Repeat liver tests when indicated; scans follow the agreed cancer-control plan. Tell anyone ordering an oestrogen blood test that you receive fulvestrant because some tests can give a misleadingly high result. There is no routine ECG or echo schedule for fulvestrant alone; a companion medicine may need additional checks.

Which scans apply to me?

For advanced breast cancer, response assessment is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease pace, sites, symptoms and treatment. Indolent disease may be reviewed less often; new symptoms can prompt earlier scans. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not a universal three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

What is my bone-health plan?

Assess personal fracture and osteoporosis risk. Fulvestrant does not carry an automatic AI DXA timetable; agree testing and repeat intervals when indicated.

How long will treatment continue?

Continue while the treatment is beneficial and tolerated, with planned response reviews. There is no fixed adjuvant five-year course on this card.

Do I need a port or home pump?

No port or home infusion pump is needed for this medicine itself. Any separately prescribed medicine has its own access requirements.

Is this the same plan as capivasertib with fulvestrant?

No. The injections are part of both plans, but capivasertib adds its own tablet schedule, selection requirements, blood-sugar checks and side-effect care. Use the full combination plan if both are prescribed.

Why might an oestrogen test need checking?

Fulvestrant can interfere with some laboratory tests and make the result look higher than it is. Tell the team about treatment so they can check the test method before making a treatment decision.
