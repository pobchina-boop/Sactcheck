Gemcitabine alone · non-small-cell lung cancer

Gemcitabine aims to control lung cancer and its symptoms. Treatment is reviewed against side effects, your health and your priorities; benefit is not guaranteed.

This guide covers the lung indication only. The same NCCP number also covers pancreatic treatment after surgery with a separate six-cycle plan. Use the correct disease-specific plan.

My overall treatment

Gemcitabine is given on Days 1, 8 and 15. Week 4 has no planned dose. Each cycle lasts 28 days. For lung cancer there is no fixed six-cycle limit in this source: continue only while the team considers treatment helpful and tolerable.

  1. TreatmentDays 1, 8, 15; repeat every 28 days. Duration agreed with your team.
  2. ReviewYour team reviews benefit and side effects, then agrees whether to continue, pause or change treatment.

Between treatments

  • Tiredness, sickness, mouth soreness or a flu-like feeling may occur; follow your prescribed support plan.
  • Infection risk is not confined to treatment days. Fever or sudden illness needs immediate advice.

Bring your medicine list and report new symptoms before dosing. Confirm the prescription, test appointments and daytime / out-of-hours contact plan.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials a cycle when it is complete, after the final planned dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
