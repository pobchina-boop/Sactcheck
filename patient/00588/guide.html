Your practical treatment plan

Know the next step.

Taking treatment

Take olaparib tablets morning and evening every day. There is no planned week off. Blood-review appointments are usually every four weeks during this one-year breast course.

Swallow tablets whole with water, with or without food. Do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. If a dose is missed, take the next normal dose at its usual time; do not double it. Check tablet strength after any dose change.

Checks and follow-up

Confirm the germline BRCA result and breast eligibility. Review blood count, kidney and liver tests, recovery from previous treatment, medicines and supplements, and pregnancy testing where relevant. Confirm the surgery/radiotherapy sequence and the planned start. NCCP specifies FBC, kidney and liver tests every four weeks for the first twelve months. Reviews also assess nausea, appetite, energy, bruising, infections and breathing symptoms. Extra tests or an interruption may be needed if results or symptoms change.

Support between reviews

Nausea support matched to oral treatment

NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high emetic risk. Agree an anti-sickness plan under local policy, and seek review if oral intake falls. The SmPC describes early/intermittent symptoms; do not automatically copy an intravenous chemotherapy antiemetic cycle.

Check the antiemetic interaction

Aprepitant is a moderate CYP3A inhibitor in NCCP 00588 and can increase olaparib exposure. The prescriber/pharmacist should choose and reconcile supportive medicines; never add an antiemetic from another regimen without checking.

Blood and infection support

Anaemia or other low counts can lead to interruption, dose adjustment, transfusion or additional investigation. There is no universal routine G-CSF course for every person on this regimen.

Medicines and supplements

Review all prescriptions, over-the-counter drugs and herbal products. Strong/moderate CYP3A inhibitors or inducers can change exposure. Avoid grapefruit products and seek advice before starting anything new.

Stomach symptoms

NCCP allows consideration of a proton pump inhibitor under local policy. This is symptom- and patient-dependent, rather than a compulsory medication for everyone.
