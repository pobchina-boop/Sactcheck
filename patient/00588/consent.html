NCCP 00588 · version 5b

Olaparib · adjuvant breast treatment

Take olaparib tablets morning and evening every day. There is no planned week off. Blood-review appointments are usually every four weeks during this one-year breast course.

Purpose, setting and alternatives

Reduce the chance of invasive recurrence after standard chemotherapy and local treatment. It may be taken alongside prescribed endocrine therapy for hormone receptor-positive disease. NCCP 00588 h only: germline pathogenic BRCA1/2-associated HER2-negative high-risk early breast cancer after neoadjuvant/adjuvant chemotherapy. Do not route other 00588 indications into this breast suite.

Material risks and support

  • Nausea, vomiting, reduced appetite and indigestion
  • Fatigue and anaemia
  • Low neutrophils or platelets, with infection or bleeding risk
  • Diarrhoea or altered taste
  • Uncommon but serious pneumonitis or drug-induced liver injury
  • Rare serious marrow disorders, including MDS/AML
  • Venous thrombosis or pulmonary embolism; risk differs by treatment setting
  • Fetal harm; contraception and breastfeeding restrictions

This patient suite is only for breast adjuvant indication 00588 h. Kidney/liver impairment, previous dose reductions and interacting medicines can change the dose. New blood abnormalities can occasionally signal a serious marrow problem and must be investigated. Olaparib can harm an unborn baby. Discuss effective contraception before treatment; EMA recommends two reliable complementary methods for women during treatment and for six months afterwards. Men and their partners need contraception during treatment and for three months afterwards. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for one month after the last dose.

Monitoring and understanding

FBC, renal and liver profile every 4 weeks throughout the 12-month breast course; earlier/additional tests for symptoms or abnormal results. Review adherence, tablet supply, nausea, fatigue and all medicines. No routine olaparib-specific ECG, echo or DXA; follow separate previous/ongoing treatment risks. Breast surveillance continues after olaparib, without automatic asymptomatic CT or tumour-marker scheduling.
