Discuss fertility and pregnancy prevention before treatment. If you could become pregnant, use two reliable complementary contraception methods during treatment and for six months after the last dose, following your team’s advice. A non-hormonal method may be needed because olaparib can reduce the effect of some hormonal contraceptives; hormone-sensitive cancer also affects the choice. Pregnancy testing is needed before treatment and may be repeated. Do not breastfeed during olaparib or for one month after the last dose. Tell the team immediately if pregnancy is possible.

Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenBefore starting and every four weeks for the first 12 months, then as clinically indicated; earlier if necessary.

Check red cells, infection-fighting white cells and platelets; persistent unexplained changes may need specialist tests.

Kidney and liver profile

WhenBefore starting and every four weeks for the first 12 months, then as clinically indicated.

Check organ function and whether the prescription needs review.

BRCA1/2 result and prior-treatment review

WhenA validated qualifying result is needed before treatment. Discuss any inherited implications with the team.

The Irish monotherapy pathway requires a qualifying BRCA mutation, inherited or tumour-derived. Prior PARP treatment is an exclusion in this NCCP regimen.

If part of your plan
WhenThe team reviews symptoms, scans and any relevant CA-125 trend together. CA-125 is not a standalone instruction to change tablets. The consultant sets the response-assessment timetable and continuation decisions.

Assess cancer control in the whole clinical picture, separate from safety blood tests.

Pregnancy testing where relevant

If part of your plan

Avoid exposing an unborn baby to olaparib.

The whole treatment journey

Olaparib after treatment for relapsed ovarian cancer · the whole treatment journey

BRCA-mutated, platinum-sensitive relapsed ovarian, fallopian-tube or primary-peritoneal cancer

Take your prescribed olaparib dose morning and evening every day, without a planned rest week. Treatment continues while the cancer remains controlled and side effects remain acceptable. There is no automatic two-year endpoint for this relapsed-maintenance pathway; the first-line ovarian two-year rule does not apply.

What each check is for

1Baseline checksBefore

Confirm a qualifying BRCA result, response to the latest platinum, earlier platinum-treatment history and CA-125 trend. Check recovery, full blood count, kidney/liver tests, medicines and pregnancy testing where relevant.

Ask / act: Ask when this check is due, who arranges it and who reviews the result. New symptoms need advice before the next appointment.

Sources: NCCP · SMPC

2Safety blood tests and symptom reviewDuring

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests every four weeks for the first 12 months, then as clinically indicated. Review symptoms, food/fluid intake, medicines and tablet supply; abnormal results or new symptoms can require earlier checks.

Ask / act: Ask when this check is due, who arranges it and who reviews the result. New symptoms need advice before the next appointment.

Sources: NCCP · SMPC
