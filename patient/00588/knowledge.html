When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team urgently for fever, shaking chills, feeling suddenly unwell, unusual bruising or bleeding, marked weakness, new or worsening cough or breathlessness, a new painful swollen limb, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or inability to keep fluids down. Understand your treatment

How your medicine works

Reduce the chance of invasive recurrence after standard chemotherapy and local treatment. It may be taken alongside prescribed endocrine therapy for hormone receptor-positive disease.

Targeted therapy: PARP inhibitor

Olaparib

Lynparza
Olaparib is a PARP inhibitor: it blocks proteins that help repair damaged deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) and can trap them on the DNA. Cancer cells with a harmful BRCA1 or BRCA2 change already have difficulty using an important repair pathway. Blocking PARP can leave more damage than these cells can repair. Normal blood-forming cells can also be affected, which explains why blood counts and symptom checks remain important during targeted treatment.

What to watch for
Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

After chemotherapy and local treatment

Olaparib follows the chemotherapy and local-treatment plan. In OlympiA, surgery and any indicated radiotherapy were completed before olaparib, with at least a two-week gap after local treatment. NCCP advises starting ideally within twelve weeks of the last surgery, chemotherapy or radiation; your team sets the start.

STEP 2

Endocrine therapy may overlap

For hormone receptor-positive disease, the prescribed hormone treatment can continue alongside olaparib and often continues after the one-year olaparib course. Its injections, tablets and bone checks keep their own schedule.

STEP 3

A daily routine at home

Choose consistent morning and evening times and check the strength and number of tablets on each new prescription. Keep a sufficient supply before weekends or holidays. No infusion pump or port is needed for olaparib itself.

STEP 4

If treatment is paused

The oncology team decides when and at what dose to restart after blood recovery or symptom improvement. Do not calculate a catch-up dose or restart automatically because a calendar month has passed.

STEP 5

After the planned year

Agree the stop date and ongoing breast follow-up. Endocrine treatment and breast surveillance may continue. Report persistent unexplained fatigue, infections, bruising or breathlessness even after olaparib finishes.

STEP 6

Tablet strengths and missed doses

Swallow the prescribed tablets whole with or without food, once in the morning and once in the evening. Do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. The usual starting dose is two 150 mg tablets each time (300 mg twice daily); your own dose may be lower. Check both tablet strength and tablet count after every prescription change. If you miss a dose, take the next normal dose at its usual time; do not double up. Do not substitute olaparib capsules for tablets. Avoid grapefruit juice and check other grapefruit or bitter-orange products with your pharmacist.

STEP 7

Genetics and family questions

BRCA1 and BRCA2 help repair damaged DNA. A germline change is present in the body’s cells and may have implications for relatives. A tumour-only change does not by itself show that it is inherited. Ask which test you had, what the result means and whether genetic counselling is appropriate. A variant of uncertain significance is not automatically a treatment-qualifying result.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Nausea support matched to oral treatment

Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy.

Check the antiemetic interaction

Aprepitant is a moderate CYP3A inhibitor in NCCP 00588 and can increase olaparib exposure. The prescriber/pharmacist should choose and reconcile supportive medicines; never add an antiemetic from another regimen without checking.

Blood and infection support

Blood counts check red cells that carry oxygen, white cells that help fight infection and platelets that help stop bleeding. Low counts can lead to a pause, a lower prescribed dose, a transfusion or further tests. Persistent or unexplained changes sometimes need specialist bone-marrow tests to investigate myelodysplastic syndrome or acute myeloid leukaemia (MDS/AML). These are serious conditions; their risk differs by treatment setting and previous therapy. A treatment pause is not itself a diagnosis. There is no universal routine white-cell growth-factor course.

Medicines and supplements

Give the pharmacist a complete list of prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and herbal products. Some antibiotics, antifungals, seizure medicines and St John’s wort can change how much olaparib reaches your body. Even anti-sickness treatment needs checking: aprepitant can increase olaparib exposure. Do not start a borrowed support medicine, change the olaparib dose or restart a paused course yourself.

Stomach symptoms

NCCP allows consideration of a proton pump inhibitor under local policy. This is symptom- and patient-dependent, rather than a compulsory medication for everyone.

If tablets are paused

Only the treating team can confirm when and at what dose to restart. Do not take catch-up doses after a pause.

Pregnancy, fertility and contraception

Olaparib can harm an unborn baby. Discuss fertility and pregnancy prevention before treatment. If you could become pregnant, use two reliable complementary contraception methods during treatment and for six months after the last dose, following your team’s advice. A non-hormonal method may be needed because olaparib can reduce the effect of some hormonal contraceptives; hormone-sensitive cancer also affects the choice. Pregnancy testing is needed before treatment and may be repeated. Do not breastfeed during olaparib or for one month after the last dose. Tell the team immediately if pregnancy is possible. Use a condom during treatment and for three months after the last dose when having sex with a partner who is pregnant or could become pregnant. A partner who could become pregnant should also use highly effective contraception. Do not donate sperm during treatment or for three months afterwards; discuss fertility preservation before treatment if relevant.

Treatment reference · 01

The exact treatment being discussed

Reduce the chance of invasive recurrence after standard chemotherapy and local treatment. It may be taken alongside prescribed endocrine therapy for hormone receptor-positive disease.

Who this covers

Adults with a confirmed pathogenic germline BRCA1/2 mutation, HER2-negative high-risk early breast cancer and previous neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy. The high-risk definition depends on hormone receptors, prior chemotherapy setting, surgical pathology and, for one pathway, CPS+EG score.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Dose and administration

Swallow the prescribed tablets whole with or without food, once in the morning and once in the evening. Do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. The usual starting dose is two 150 mg tablets each time (300 mg twice daily); your own dose may be lower. Check both tablet strength and tablet count after every prescription change. If you miss a dose, take the next normal dose at its usual time; do not double up. Do not substitute olaparib capsules for tablets. Avoid grapefruit juice and check other grapefruit or bitter-orange products with your pharmacist.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Duration and decisions

For breast indication 00588 h: up to one year, or earlier if the cancer recurs or side effects make treatment unsuitable. This is different from olaparib durations in ovarian, prostate and pancreatic cancer.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Individual safety and source differences

This patient suite is only for breast adjuvant indication 00588 h. Kidney/liver impairment, previous dose reductions and interacting medicines can change the dose. New blood abnormalities can occasionally signal a serious marrow problem and must be investigated.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Treatment reference · 02

Checks throughout treatment

Blood tests assess treatment safety. Scans assess disease or investigate symptoms. Echo and ECG answer different questions.

Before treatment

Confirm the germline BRCA result and breast eligibility. Review blood count, kidney and liver tests, recovery from previous treatment, medicines and supplements, and pregnancy testing where relevant. Confirm the surgery/radiotherapy sequence and the planned start.

Sources: nccp · smpc

During treatment

NCCP specifies FBC, kidney and liver tests every four weeks for the first twelve months. Reviews also assess nausea, appetite, energy, bruising, infections and breathing symptoms. Extra tests or an interruption may be needed if results or symptoms change.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Heart checks

Olaparib alone has no routine electrocardiogram (ECG) or heart-ultrasound (echo) timetable in this NCCP regimen. Checks may still be needed because of previous treatment that can affect the heart, an existing condition or new symptoms.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Bone health

There is no olaparib-specific DXA schedule. If an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression is also prescribed, retain that treatment’s separate bone-density and bone-health plan.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Treatment reference · 03

Your wider treatment plan

Additional treatments depend on the confirmed setting and indication.

Early-breast follow-up

After early breast cancer treatment, annual mammography is generally used for remaining breast tissue: both breasts after conservation, or the opposite breast after unilateral mastectomy. Routine mammography of the mastectomy side is not required. Routine CT, bone scans and tumour markers are not recommended solely to look for recurrence in an asymptomatic person. Safety blood tests for ongoing olaparib are a separate requirement. New symptoms need assessment between appointments.

Treatment reference · 04

Evidence depends on the population

Different studies answer different questions; do not compare their results as if populations and follow-up were identical.

OlympiA: four-year survival analysis

The earlier survival update showed that the benefit extended beyond preventing invasive recurrence. These results apply to the selected high-risk inherited BRCA-associated early breast population, not every HER2-negative breast cancer.

Sources: trial-1

OlympiA: sustained benefit at six years

Longer follow-up confirms that reductions in invasive recurrence and improvements in survival persist after the planned year of treatment. This is follow-up after one year of olaparib, not a longer prescribed course; rare late marrow effects still need attention.

Sources: trial-2

Treatment reference · 05

Benefit, course and continuing checks

In people with the high-risk inherited BRCA-associated breast cancer studied, one year of olaparib reduced invasive recurrence and improved survival. Longer follow-up shows that the benefit persists after treatment ends. Eligibility is specific, and benefit for an individual remains uncertain.

Before and during treatment

FBC, renal and liver profile every 4 weeks throughout the 12-month breast course; earlier/additional tests for symptoms or abnormal results. Review adherence, tablet supply, nausea, fatigue and all medicines.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Heart checks and the full course

No routine olaparib-specific ECG, echo or DXA; follow separate previous/ongoing treatment risks. Breast surveillance continues after olaparib, without automatic asymptomatic CT or tumour-marker scheduling. High risk:TNBC residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy or node-positive/≥2 cm after adjuvant chemotherapy; HR-positive disease requires residual disease with CPS+EG≥3 after neoadjuvant therapy or≥4 positive nodes after adjuvant chemotherapy. Ideally start within 12 weeks of the last surgery, chemotherapy or radiation.

OlympiA: benefit after one year of adjuvant treatment

OlympiA compared one year of olaparib with placebo after chemotherapy and local treatment in 1,836 people with germline BRCA1/2-associated, high-risk HER2-negative early breast cancer.

At six years, estimated invasive disease-free survival was 79.6% with olaparib and 70.3% with placebo; estimated overall survival was 87.5% and 83.2%. Invasive disease-free survival counts specified invasive-cancer events or death, not just breast recurrence. These descriptive follow-up results support benefit after the planned one-year course; they do not mean six years of tablets or predict your personal outcome.

Heart checks may still be needed because of previous anthracycline/HER2 treatment, existing disease or new symptoms.

Which scans apply to me?

After early breast cancer treatment, annual mammography is generally used for remaining breast tissue: both breasts after conservation, or the opposite breast after unilateral mastectomy. Routine mammography of the mastectomy side is not required. Routine CT, bone scans and tumour markers are not recommended solely to look for recurrence in an asymptomatic person. Safety blood tests for ongoing olaparib are a separate requirement. New symptoms need assessment between appointments.

How long will treatment continue?

For breast indication 00588 h: up to one year, or earlier if the cancer recurs or side effects make treatment unsuitable. This is different from olaparib durations in ovarian, prostate and pancreatic cancer.

What should make me call today?

Call your oncology team urgently for fever, shaking chills, feeling suddenly unwell, unusual bruising or bleeding, marked weakness, new or worsening cough or breathlessness, a new painful swollen limb, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or inability to keep fluids down. Follow your own temperature and contact plan; do not wait for the next appointment. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse, seizures or stroke symptoms: call 112/999.

What is my bone-health plan?

There is no olaparib-specific DXA schedule. If an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression is also prescribed, retain that treatment’s separate bone-density and bone-health plan.

Does longer follow-up mean taking olaparib for longer?

No. The OlympiA follow-up followed people after a planned year of treatment. Your breast course remains up to one year unless the team stops earlier.

Can hormone treatment continue?

For hormone receptor-positive disease, the prescribed hormone treatment can continue alongside olaparib and often continues after the one-year olaparib course. Its injections, tablets and bone checks keep their own schedule.

Why does the inherited BRCA result matter?

BRCA1 and BRCA2 help repair damaged DNA. A germline change is present in the body’s cells and may have implications for relatives. A tumour-only change does not by itself show that it is inherited. Ask which test you had, what the result means and whether genetic counselling is appropriate. A variant of uncertain significance is not automatically a treatment-qualifying result.
