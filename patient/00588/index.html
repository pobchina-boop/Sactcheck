PARP inhibitor

Olaparib (Lynparza)

Disrupts DNA repair in susceptible cancer cells
BRCA-related repair is impaired↓Olaparib blocks and traps PARP↓DNA damage accumulates in cancer cells

BRCA1 and BRCA2 help cells repair breaks in DNA. Some cancers with these changes rely more heavily on other repair routes. Olaparib inhibits PARP activity and traps PARP on DNA, making it harder for those cancer cells to survive DNA damage. Normal blood-forming cells can also be affected, which is why regular blood counts matter.

Keep the four-weekly blood reviews and report persistent nausea, marked fatigue, infections, bleeding or new respiratory symptoms. Severe blood abnormalities need review even if you feel well.

What each check is for

1Blood testsConfirm the germline BRCA result and breast eligibility. Review blood count, kidney and liver tests, recovery from previous treatment, medicines and supplements, and pregnancy testing where relevant. Confirm the surgery/radiotherapy sequence and the planned start. NCCP specifies FBC, kidney and liver tests every four weeks for the first twelve months. Reviews also assess nausea, appetite, energy, bruising, infections and breathing symptoms. Extra tests or an interruption may be needed if results or symptoms change.

Checks safety before continuation.

3ImagingAfter early breast cancer treatment, annual mammography is generally used for remaining breast tissue: both breasts after conservation, or the opposite breast after unilateral mastectomy. Routine mammography of the mastectomy side is not required. Routine CT, bone scans and tumour markers are not recommended solely to look for recurrence in an asymptomatic person. Safety blood tests for ongoing olaparib are a separate requirement. New symptoms need assessment between appointments.

Support throughout treatment

Nausea support matched to oral treatment

NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high emetic risk. Agree an anti-sickness plan under local policy, and seek review if oral intake falls. The SmPC describes early/intermittent symptoms; do not automatically copy an intravenous chemotherapy antiemetic cycle.

Check the antiemetic interaction

Aprepitant is a moderate CYP3A inhibitor in NCCP 00588 and can increase olaparib exposure. The prescriber/pharmacist should choose and reconcile supportive medicines; never add an antiemetic from another regimen without checking.

Blood and infection support

Anaemia or other low counts can lead to interruption, dose adjustment, transfusion or additional investigation. There is no universal routine G-CSF course for every person on this regimen.

Medicines and supplements

Review all prescriptions, over-the-counter drugs and herbal products. Strong/moderate CYP3A inhibitors or inducers can change exposure. Avoid grapefruit products and seek advice before starting anything new.

Stomach symptoms

NCCP allows consideration of a proton pump inhibitor under local policy. This is symptom- and patient-dependent, rather than a compulsory medication for everyone.
