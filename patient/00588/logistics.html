Olaparib after treatment for early breast cancer

National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) 00588 · Source-reconciled feasibility draft; independent clinician, pharmacy and patient/PPI review pending.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

After chemotherapy and local treatment

Olaparib follows the chemotherapy and local-treatment plan. In OlympiA, surgery and any indicated radiotherapy were completed before olaparib, with at least a two-week gap after local treatment. NCCP advises starting ideally within twelve weeks of the last surgery, chemotherapy or radiation; your team sets the start.

STEP 2

Endocrine therapy may overlap

For hormone receptor-positive disease, the prescribed hormone treatment can continue alongside olaparib and often continues after the one-year olaparib course. Its injections, tablets and bone checks keep their own schedule.

STEP 3

A daily routine at home

Choose consistent morning and evening times and check the strength and number of tablets on each new prescription. Keep a sufficient supply before weekends or holidays. No infusion pump or port is needed for olaparib itself.

STEP 4

If treatment is paused

The oncology team decides when and at what dose to restart after blood recovery or symptom improvement. Do not calculate a catch-up dose or restart automatically because a calendar month has passed.

STEP 5

After the planned year

Agree the stop date and ongoing breast follow-up. Endocrine treatment and breast surveillance may continue. Report persistent unexplained fatigue, infections, bruising or breathlessness even after olaparib finishes.

STEP 6

Tablet strengths and missed doses

Swallow the prescribed tablets whole with or without food, once in the morning and once in the evening. Do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. The usual starting dose is two 150 mg tablets each time (300 mg twice daily); your own dose may be lower. Check both tablet strength and tablet count after every prescription change. If you miss a dose, take the next normal dose at its usual time; do not double up. Do not substitute olaparib capsules for tablets. Avoid grapefruit juice and check other grapefruit or bitter-orange products with your pharmacist.

STEP 7

Genetics and family questions

BRCA1 and BRCA2 help repair damaged deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA). A germline change is present in the body’s cells and may have implications for relatives. A tumour-only change does not by itself show that it is inherited. Ask which test you had, what the result means and whether genetic counselling is appropriate. A variant of uncertain significance is not automatically a treatment-qualifying result.
