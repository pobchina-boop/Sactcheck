When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team promptly for fever, shaking chills, feeling suddenly unwell, unusual bleeding, persistent vomiting/diarrhoea or new breathlessness. Follow your own temperature and contact plan. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse, seizures or stroke symptoms: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How your medicine works

Help control prostate cancer that has progressed despite hormone treatment, where a BRCA1 or BRCA2 mutation is confirmed.

Targeted therapy: PARP inhibitor

Olaparib

Lynparza
Olaparib blocks poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP), which helps repair damaged deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA). A cancer cell with a pathogenic BRCA1/2 change already has difficulty using another repair pathway. Blocking PARP can leave too much damage for the cell to survive. This is why a validated mutation result matters.

What to watch for
NCCP classes oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk. The team considers anti-sickness medicine and a proton pump inhibitor for stomach protection under local policy. Obtain a written prevention/rescue plan and report persistent nausea or inability to take tablets.

Individual planned course

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Preparation and symptom support

Confirm the pathogenic germline or tumour BRCA1/2 result and previous treatment. Continue prescribed androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) unless you have had surgical castration. NCCP classes oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk. The team considers anti-sickness medicine and a proton pump inhibitor for stomach protection under local policy. Obtain a written prevention/rescue plan and report persistent nausea or inability to take tablets.

Safe medicine use

Swallow tablets whole; do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. Tablets and capsules are not interchangeable. If a dose is missed, take the next normal dose at its usual time; never double up. Avoid grapefruit and Seville oranges, and check all medicines with pharmacy.

Fertility and contraception

Use a condom during treatment and for three months afterwards when having sex with a partner who is or could become pregnant. The partner should use highly effective contraception during that time. Do not donate sperm during treatment or for three months afterwards; discuss fertility preservation.

Your wider treatment

Continue ADT, which keeps testosterone low, unless you have had surgical castration. Olaparib targets DNA repair rather than replacing hormone suppression. Abiraterone and other androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPIs) act on hormone signalling; adding or switching them needs a separate evidence-based plan. Radiotherapy can treat selected sites or relieve symptoms, with timing agreed by the team.

Treatment reference · 04

Benefits, uncertainty and your choices

Evidence in this setting

In the BRCA1/2-mutated subgroup of PROfound, 160 patients received olaparib or the investigator’s choice of abiraterone or enzalutamide after progression on a new hormonal agent. Median radiological progression-free survival was 9.8 versus 3.0 months (hazard ratio 0.22; 95% confidence interval 0.15–0.32); median overall survival was 20.1 versus 14.4 months (hazard ratio 0.63; 0.42–0.95). These subgroup analyses were not controlled for multiple comparisons. They do not compare every modern alternative or establish the separate olaparib-plus-abiraterone regimen.

Sources: nccp · smpc
