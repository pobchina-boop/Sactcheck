Olaparib for prostate cancer

National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) 00588 · Source-reconciled feasibility draft; independent clinician, pharmacy and patient/PPI review pending.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Confirm the validated germline or tumour BRCA1/2 result, previous new hormonal treatment and ongoing testosterone suppression. Check full blood count, kidney and liver tests and recovery from previous treatment.

STEP 2

Dose and timing

The usual starting dose is 300 mg of olaparib tablets twice daily, with or without food. Treatment continues while the cancer remains controlled and side effects remain acceptable; there is no fixed one-year or two-year prostate course. Follow your own prescription if the dose changes.

STEP 3

Practical care

Swallow the prescribed tablets whole with or without food, once in the morning and once in the evening. Do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. The usual starting dose is two 150 mg tablets each time (300 mg twice daily); your own dose may be lower. Check both tablet strength and tablet count after every prescription change. If you miss a dose, take the next normal dose at its usual time; do not double up. Do not substitute olaparib capsules for tablets. Avoid grapefruit juice and check other grapefruit or bitter-orange products with your pharmacist.

STEP 4

Support medicines

Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy. Give the pharmacist a complete list of prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and herbal products. Some antibiotics, antifungals, seizure medicines and St John’s wort can change how much olaparib reaches your body. Even anti-sickness treatment needs checking: aprepitant can increase olaparib exposure. Do not start a borrowed support medicine, change the olaparib dose or restart a paused course yourself.

STEP 5

Before leaving

Confirm your next tests, dose, review date and verified daytime/out-of-hours contact numbers.

STEP 6

Blood-clot symptoms

A clot can develop in a vein or lung. Report a new painful swollen limb urgently. Chest pain, sudden severe breathlessness or collapse require emergency help. Clot risk is particularly relevant in this prostate setting; do not start an anticoagulant without the team’s assessment.

STEP 7

Genetic results and family questions

BRCA1 and BRCA2 help repair damaged deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA). A germline change is present in the body’s cells and may have implications for relatives. A tumour-only change does not by itself show that it is inherited. Ask which test you had, what the result means and whether genetic counselling is appropriate. A variant of uncertain significance is not automatically a treatment-qualifying result.
