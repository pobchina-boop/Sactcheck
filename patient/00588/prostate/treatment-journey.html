Medicines and practical careConfirm the pathogenic germline or tumour BRCA1/2 result and previous treatment. Continue prescribed androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) unless you have had surgical castration.Swallow the prescribed tablets whole with or without food, once in the morning and once in the evening. Do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. The usual starting dose is two 150 mg tablets each time (300 mg twice daily); your own dose may be lower. Check both tablet strength and tablet count after every prescription change. If you miss a dose, take the next normal dose at its usual time; do not double up. Do not substitute olaparib capsules for tablets. Avoid grapefruit juice and check other grapefruit or bitter-orange products with your pharmacist.Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy.Review lasting effects, support needs and safe return/disposal of unused tablets through the dispensing service.

Olaparib for prostate cancer · the whole treatment journey

BRCA1/2-mutated metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer after a new hormonal agent

Take your prescribed olaparib dose morning and evening every day, without a planned rest week. Treatment continues while the cancer remains controlled and side effects remain acceptable; there is no fixed one-year or two-year prostate course.

What each check is for

1Baseline checksBefore

Confirm the validated germline or tumour BRCA1/2 result, previous new hormonal treatment and ongoing testosterone suppression. Check full blood count, kidney and liver tests and recovery from previous treatment.

Ask / act: Ask when this check is due, who arranges it and who reviews the result. New symptoms need advice before the next appointment.

Sources: NCCP · SMPC

2Safety blood tests and symptom reviewDuring

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests every four weeks for the first 12 months, then as clinically indicated. Reviews also cover symptoms, tablets, support medicines and supply; extra tests may be needed between appointments.

Ask / act: Ask when this check is due, who arranges it and who reviews the result. New symptoms need advice before the next appointment.

Sources: NCCP · SMPC

3Cancer assessmentResponse

Your consultant reviews symptoms, prostate-specific antigen (PSA) and scans together. Testosterone testing can check continuing castration. The team sets the timetable rather than an automatic three-month scan interval.

Ask / act: Ask when this check is due, who arranges it and who reviews the result. New symptoms need advice before the next appointment.

Sources: NCCP · SMPC
