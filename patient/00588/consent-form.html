Treatment context
Reduce the chance of invasive recurrence after standard chemotherapy and local treatment. It may be taken alongside prescribed endocrine therapy for hormone receptor-positive disease.
Schedule
Take your prescribed olaparib dose morning and evening every day, without a planned rest week. This breast course lasts up to one year, or stops earlier if the cancer returns or side effects make treatment unsuitable. Prescribed endocrine therapy can continue on its own timetable.

General treatment discussion

Possible short-term, long-term, unexpected and unlisted effects; benefits are not guaranteed.
Severe complications can require hospital admission and can rarely be fatal.
Infection / sepsis, allergic or infusion reactions and leakage outside a vein (extravasation) should be discussed where applicable to the medicines and route.
Pregnancy, contraception, breastfeeding, sexual health and fertility preservation should be discussed where relevant.
Treatment can be delayed, changed or stopped if risks outweigh benefit. Consent is voluntary and can be withdrawn.

2. Regimen / agent-specific material risks

Olaparib

Targeted therapy: PARP inhibitor
Anaemia, sometimes requiring transfusion; low white cells or platelets with infection or bleeding risk
Nausea, vomiting, indigestion, diarrhoea, reduced appetite and changes in taste
Fatigue or weakness, headache, dizziness and mouth soreness
Serious persistent blood-count changes, including MDS/AML; the risk varies with the treatment setting and previous therapy
Pneumonitis: inflammation of the lungs that can be serious or fatal
Blood clots in veins or lungs; symptoms need urgent assessment
Liver injury and changes in kidney blood tests; the team may need to review treatment
Allergic reactions and harm to an unborn baby; discuss fertility, contraception and breastfeeding where relevant

Discuss the chance of reducing recurrence, the limits of trial averages, alternatives and your priorities. Review common effects and serious blood/marrow, lung, clotting, liver and allergic risks. Olaparib can harm an unborn baby. Discuss fertility and pregnancy prevention before treatment. If you could become pregnant, use two reliable complementary contraception methods during treatment and for six months after the last dose, following your team’s advice. A non-hormonal method may be needed because olaparib can reduce the effect of some hormonal contraceptives; hormone-sensitive cancer also affects the choice. Pregnancy testing is needed before treatment and may be repeated. Do not breastfeed during olaparib or for one month after the last dose. Tell the team immediately if pregnancy is possible. Use a condom during treatment and for three months after the last dose when having sex with a partner who is pregnant or could become pregnant. A partner who could become pregnant should also use highly effective contraception. Do not donate sperm during treatment or for three months afterwards; discuss fertility preservation before treatment if relevant.
