NCCP 00588 · version 5b

Olaparib · adjuvant breast treatment

Take olaparib tablets morning and evening every day. There is no planned week off. Blood-review appointments are usually every four weeks during this one-year breast course.

Selected material risks

□ Nausea, vomiting, reduced appetite and indigestion
□ Fatigue and anaemia
□ Low neutrophils or platelets, with infection or bleeding risk
□ Diarrhoea or altered taste
□ Uncommon but serious pneumonitis or drug-induced liver injury
□ Rare serious marrow disorders, including MDS/AML
□ Venous thrombosis or pulmonary embolism; risk differs by treatment setting
□ Fetal harm; contraception and breastfeeding restrictions

This patient suite is only for breast adjuvant indication 00588h. Kidney/liver impairment, previous dose reductions and interacting medicines can change the dose. New blood abnormalities can occasionally signal a serious marrow problem and must be investigated. Olaparib can harm an unborn baby. Discuss effective contraception before treatment; EMA recommends two reliable complementary methods for women during treatment and for six months afterwards. Men and their partners need contraception during treatment and for three months afterwards. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for one month after the last dose.

Individual benefit, alternatives and concerns

Agreed checks and next review

Confirm the germline BRCA result and breast eligibility. Review blood count, kidney and liver tests, recovery from previous treatment, medicines and supplements, and pregnancy testing where relevant. Confirm the surgery/radiotherapy sequence and the planned start. NCCP specifies FBC, kidney and liver tests every four weeks for the first twelve months. Reviews also assess nausea, appetite, energy, bruising, infections and breathing symptoms. Extra tests or an interruption may be needed if results or symptoms change.
