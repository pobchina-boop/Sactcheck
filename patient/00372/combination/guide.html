Nintedanib + docetaxel

To help control adenocarcinoma lung cancer after first-line chemotherapy; treatment benefit and side effects are reviewed together.

My overall treatment

Day 1: docetaxel drip, with no nintedanib. Days 2–21: nintedanib capsules twice daily. Repeat every 21 days for up to 6 cycles (about 18 weeks without delays); treatment may stop earlier. A later tablet-only phase needs a new team-confirmed plan.

  1. Combination phaseDay 1 docetaxel; Days 2–21 nintedanib twice daily. Up to 6 cycles.
  2. Possible tablet-only phaseOnly after at least 4 completed combination cycles and team approval: nintedanib every day in 28-day cycles. This change is not automatic.

Between treatments

  • Diarrhoea, nausea and fatigue may occur at home. Call early if drinking becomes difficult or symptoms persist.
  • Low blood counts can occur between visits. The team will set an extra blood-test date; there is no guaranteed safe day.

Bring your medicine list and report new symptoms before dosing. Confirm test appointments, the current phase and daytime / out-of-hours contact plan.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
