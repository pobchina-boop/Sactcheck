NCCP 00322 · version 4

Everolimus + exemestane

Both tablets are taken every day continuously. There is no planned week off. A four-week blood-test review is a monitoring interval, not a stop-and-restart treatment cycle.

Purpose, setting and alternatives

Exemestane lowers oestrogen production while everolimus blocks an intracellular growth pathway that can contribute to endocrine resistance. The aim in this setting is to control cancer and delay progression. NCCP 00322a: postmenopausal hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer without symptomatic visceral disease after recurrence or progression following a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor.

Material risks and support

  • Mouth ulcers/stomatitis
  • Infections, including opportunistic infection
  • Non-infectious pneumonitis
  • High glucose and abnormal lipids
  • Reduced blood counts
  • Rash, fatigue and diarrhoea
  • Renal impairment/proteinuria
  • Impaired wound healing and radiation-related toxicity
  • Joint and muscle pain
  • Hot flushes and sleep disturbance
  • Vaginal dryness/sexual symptoms
  • Bone thinning and fractures
  • Fatigue

If everolimus is interrupted or reduced for toxicity, NCCP says exemestane may continue; the treating team must confirm the plan for each medicine. Do not stop both automatically or restart everolimus yourself. Everolimus can increase toxicity around radiotherapy and impair wound healing: agree a specific plan before radiotherapy or surgery. This NCCP indication is postmenopausal. If pregnancy remains possible or menopause is uncertain, ask the team to confirm endocrine status and suitable contraception. Everolimus product information advises highly effective contraception during treatment and for eight weeks after it ends; pregnancy and breastfeeding are NCCP exclusions.

Monitoring and understanding

Before starting: FBC, renal and liver profile, urea/BUN, urinary protein, blood glucose, cholesterol and triglycerides. Confirm the endocrine indication, review lung symptoms and infections, and check all medicines. Assess bone/fracture risk for the exemestane component. FBC, renal/liver profile, cholesterol and triglycerides every four weeks or as clinically indicated. Check fasting glucose as clinically indicated, with closer monitoring if diabetes or another medicine increases risk. Ask about mouth soreness, infection, cough/breathlessness and daily function at reviews.
