Oral PARP inhibitor

Talazoparib

Disrupts DNA repair in BRCA-associated cancer
PARP inhibited / trapped↓DNA damage accumulates↓BRCA-deficient cells less able to repair

Talazoparib inhibits PARP and traps it on damaged DNA. Cancer cells with inherited BRCA1/2 changes may have less ability to repair this damage. Normal bone marrow can also be affected, so blood-count monitoring is essential.

Report fever, infections, unusual bruising, persistent fatigue or breathlessness.

What each check is for

1Before treatmentBefore the first dose

Germline BRCA1/2-mutated, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer. Check FBC, kidney and liver function before starting and every four weeks. More frequent blood review may be needed for low counts; prolonged abnormalities require specialist assessment.

Ask / act: Confirm eligibility, test results and medicine list.

Sources: NCCP 00605

2Other checksAt the specified review points

Confirm the germline BRCA result and previous treatment. Review pregnancy status and contraception, kidney function and interacting medicines, particularly P-glycoprotein inhibitors.

Ask / act: Confirm who arranges and reviews each check.

Sources: NCCP 00605

3Support and practical planAt each treatment or phase change

Blood monitoring, adherence, prompt infection/bleeding review and individually prescribed rescue antiemetics.

Ask / act: Choose the matching support plan; confirm active medicines.

Sources: NCCP 00605

4Response and follow-upAgreed review points

Agree the scan type and review interval for the known disease sites. Review response, symptoms and toxicity together; new or worsening symptoms may need an earlier assessment. Keep the agreed follow-up and report persistent or new symptoms between visits.

Ask / act: Review symptoms, results and patient priorities.

Sources: METASTATIC · MARKERS

Support throughout treatment

Blood counts

Keep four-weekly blood reviews and any additional tests. Fever or unusual bleeding needs prompt advice.

Taking capsules

Use the strength and number on your current prescription. Do not replace a missed or vomited dose.

Nausea

Use the prescribed rescue plan; call if vomiting affects fluids or tablets.

Medicines review

Check every new medicine or supplement with the oncology pharmacist.
