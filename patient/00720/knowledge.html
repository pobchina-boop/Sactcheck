How this medicine supports your treatment

Oral HER-family kinase inhibitor

Neratinib

Blocks growth signalling inside HER2-positive cancer cells
Enters the cell↓HER signalling inhibited↓Growth signals reduced

Neratinib binds irreversibly to signalling enzymes in the HER family, including HER2. It is used after trastuzumab-based adjuvant therapy in a specific hormone-receptor-positive, HER2-positive early-breast setting. Its effects on the bowel make early diarrhoea prevention important.

Start the prescribed diarrhoea-prevention plan with the first dose. Report diarrhoea early, especially poor intake, dizziness or reduced urine.

Questions to ask

Why was this treatment chosen for me?

Neratinib is an extended adjuvant option for selected hormone-receptor-positive, HER2-positive early breast cancer after trastuzumab-based treatment. Expected benefit, prior HER2 therapy, bowel and liver health, other medicines and the burden of daily treatment guide the discussion.

What happens if a dose is delayed or missed?

Do not replace a missed dose. Contact the team about diarrhoea or a treatment hold; restart and dose reductions require their instructions.

Who arranges the checks and explains the results?

Agree the due window, booking team, result reviewer and contact route if a test or appointment is missing. This page does not record completed checks.

Can I take other medicines or supplements?

Avoid grapefruit, pomegranate and proton-pump inhibitors. If an H2 blocker is prescribed, take neratinib at least two hours before or ten hours after it; separate antacids by at least three hours. Check CYP3A/P-glycoprotein interactions with the pharmacist.
