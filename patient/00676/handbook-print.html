My treatment handbook

Cisplatin chemoradiation, then paclitaxel and carboplatin

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Lower the chance of the cancer returning after surgery.

Cisplatin · Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. It damages DNA in dividing cells. It can also affect kidney function, hearing and nerves. Report ringing in the ears, hearing loss, reduced urine or new numbness.

Paclitaxel · Paclitaxel

Taxane chemotherapy. It disrupts cell division. Hair follicles, blood cells and nerves can also be affected. Report numbness and tell the nurse immediately if you flush or wheeze during treatment.

Carboplatin · Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. It damages DNA in dividing cells. Kidney function helps determine the prescribed amount. Report fever, bleeding, hearing changes or an infusion reaction.

What the research found

Adjuvant chemoradiotherapy improved survival outcomes compared with pelvic radiotherapy alone in the high-risk trial population.

Individual benefit depends on stage, pathology and molecular risk. Review the postoperative plan at the multidisciplinary meeting.

Your infusion schedule

During pelvic radiotherapy

Usually start 4–6 weeks after surgery. Cisplatin is given before radiation on two treatment days three weeks apart. Ring both teams if radiation is cancelled. Cisplatin is only given on a radiation day.

After radiotherapy

Within three weeks of finishing radiation, start paclitaxel then carboplatin AUC 5, every three weeks for four cycles. Bring your steroid premedication instructions.

Common effects and what to do

How you feel between visits

Tiredness, nausea or appetite changes can make everyday activities harder. Ask your team for help if symptoms interfere with eating or drinking.

Serious effects: when to call

Hearing changes · Cisplatin

Tell the team about ringing or new hearing loss before further cisplatin.

Kidney injury · Cisplatin

IV fluids help protect the kidneys. Report reduced urine or persistent vomiting urgently.

Numb hands or feet · Paclitaxel

Report tingling, difficulty fastening buttons or a change in balance before your next dose.

Hair loss · Paclitaxel

Discuss headwear or scalp cooling with the team before the first infusion.

Hearing · Carboplatin

Report ringing in your ears or a change in hearing.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shivering or sudden illness. Report persistent vomiting, reduced urine or severe diarrhoea promptly. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.
