When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever or suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, severe tummy pain, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or new cough or breathlessness. Do not wait for the next cycle.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Your chemotherapy

This treatment aims to slow or shrink extensive small-cell lung cancer when intravenous or combination chemotherapy is unsuitable. Benefit varies; your team explains the aim for you.

Topoisomerase-II inhibitor chemotherapy

Etoposide capsules

Supplied brand may vary
Disrupts DNA copying and repair

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme involved in DNA structure and repair. DNA damage can stop cancer cells dividing. Healthy blood-forming and other cells can also be affected. Absorption by mouth varies, so oral and intravenous doses are not interchangeable.

Call oncology immediately for fever or suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, severe tummy pain, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or new cough or breathlessness. Do not wait for the next cycle.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before treatment

Confirm capsule strength, number, dosing days, storage and contact numbers with the pharmacist.

STEP 2

Treatment visit or home course

Take etoposide capsules on an empty stomach using the exact prescribed dose and days. Do not open, chew or crush them. Capsule strengths, quantities and storage follow the supplied pharmacy label. Oral and intravenous doses are not interchangeable.

STEP 3

Bring to review

Bring your medicine list, dose record and questions; report new symptoms before starting another course.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Sickness and bowel symptoms

Use your prescribed prevention or rescue medicine. Report persistent vomiting, diarrhoea or inability to drink; fever with severe tummy pain needs urgent assessment.

Blood tests and review

Blood count, kidney and liver tests are checked before treatment and regularly on dates set by your team for the selected schedule.

Pregnancy, fertility and medicines

Discuss fertility and contraception before treatment. Tell the team about pregnancy, breastfeeding, all medicines, supplements and vaccines. Check before changing any medicine.

Missed or vomited capsules

Do not double a missed dose or take replacement capsules after vomiting without team advice. Record what happened, contact the oncology pharmacist/team, and follow the next scheduled dose in the agreed plan.

Safe handling

Take etoposide capsules on an empty stomach using the exact prescribed dose and days. Do not open, chew or crush them. Capsule strengths, quantities and storage follow the supplied pharmacy label. Oral and intravenous doses are not interchangeable. Keep away from children; ask the pharmacy how to return unused or damaged capsules.

My treatment

This treatment aims to slow or shrink extensive small-cell lung cancer when intravenous or combination chemotherapy is unsuitable. Benefit varies; your team explains the aim for you.

How it works

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme involved in DNA structure and repair. DNA damage can stop cancer cells dividing. Healthy blood-forming and other cells can also be affected. Absorption by mouth varies, so oral and intravenous doses are not interchangeable.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Evidence and limits

Older studies reported tumour responses with oral etoposide. The evidence is limited, and the different oral schedules cannot be assumed to have the same benefit or risks. A small uncontrolled study of a 21-day oral course does not establish a survival advantage or validate all three NCCP schedules. Its participants had recurrent cancer and substantial treatment toxicity; do not transfer its response percentage to an individual.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Why can treatment be delayed?

Blood results or symptoms may make a delay or dose change safer. Your team confirms when to restart.

Are capsules gentler than an infusion?

No. Oral chemotherapy can cause serious toxicity and needs blood tests and review. Oral and intravenous doses cannot be exchanged.

When will I have scans?

Your consultant sets the timing for your disease and treatment plan. No universal scan or tumour-marker interval is specified here.

Try explaining the plan back: Explain which days you take treatment, when your next blood tests are due and whom you will call tonight if unwell.
