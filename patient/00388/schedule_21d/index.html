Oral etoposide · Days 1–21 once daily

This treatment aims to slow or shrink extensive small-cell lung cancer when intravenous or combination chemotherapy is unsuitable. Benefit varies; your team explains the aim for you.

Extensive small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) · Days 1–21 once daily · NCCP 00388 v4 · Document revised 4 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

This guide covers only Days 1–21 once daily. Do not combine it with another etoposide schedule.

My overall treatment

Take your prescribed etoposide capsules once daily on Days 1–21. This source lists treatment on all 21 days; your team must explicitly confirm when to stop and restart. The source cycle is 21 days; continue only while helping and tolerable, after team review.

Between treatments

  • Blood counts can fall after dosing ends; fever, bleeding or feeling suddenly unwell needs an immediate call.
  • Recovery is checked before another cycle; no calendar day guarantees that your counts or symptoms have recovered.

Confirm capsule strength, number, dosing days, storage and contact numbers with the pharmacist. Have the planned blood tests and tell the team about symptoms and all medicines. Keep the written prescription and symptom record; start the next cycle only after team confirmation.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
