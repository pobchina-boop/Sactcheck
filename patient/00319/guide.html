Carboplatin + Oral Etoposide

This chemotherapy combination aims to control extensive-disease small-cell lung cancer, ease symptoms and help people live longer. Expected benefit depends on the cancer, general health and treatment choices. Ask what the team hopes it will achieve for you.

My overall treatment

Carboplatin is planned into a vein on Day 1, before oral etoposide. Etoposide capsules are prescribed twice daily on Days 1, 2 and 3 only, on an empty stomach. The cycle repeats every 21 days while treatment is helping and side effects remain acceptable. Your team decides when to delay, change or stop; the source does not set a fixed number of cycles.

  1. Day 1Carboplatin infusion, then oral etoposide.
  2. Days 2–3Oral etoposide twice daily.
  3. Days 4–21 and reviewNo planned chemotherapy; support and tests continue. Another cycle needs team confirmation.

A typical 21-day cycle

Day 1Infusion and first capsule day
Day 2–3Continue prescribed capsules
Day 4–5Capsules finish; support may continue
Day 6–20Recovery and monitoring
Day 21Review before another cycle

Timing may change after review. Side effects can occur on any day; there are no guaranteed safe days.

Between treatments

  • Nausea and tiredness may occur; follow one reconciled sickness plan.
  • Blood counts may fall after dosing ends. Fever or feeling unwell needs a call at any time.

Confirm capsule dose, twice-daily Days 1–3 and the Day 1 infusion appointment. Have the planned blood tests and report new symptoms before treatment. Keep the written support plan and contact numbers; do not double or extend capsule doses.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.

Swelling of the tongue/throat or trouble breathing can be an emergency. Report new tinnitus, hearing change, numbness or difficulty walking promptly.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials a cycle when it is complete, after the final planned dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
