When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your unit urgently for fever at its stated threshold, shaking chills, sudden illness, new or worsening breathlessness, chest pain, unusual or uncontrolled bleeding, severe diarrhoea, repeated vomiting, inability to drink, very little urine, or a spreading/blistering rash. Swelling of the tongue/throat or trouble breathing can be an emergency. Report new tinnitus, hearing change, numbness or difficulty walking promptly.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Your two chemotherapy medicines

This chemotherapy combination aims to control extensive-disease small-cell lung cancer, ease symptoms and help people live longer. Expected benefit depends on the cancer, general health and treatment choices. Ask what the team hopes it will achieve for you.

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Damages DNA through platinum links

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing, but healthy tissues may also be affected. The dose is tailored using kidney function because the kidneys clear carboplatin.

Platinum reaches cells↓DNA links form↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report ringing in the ears, hearing changes, numbness or loss of function. Report infection or unusual bruising/bleeding promptly and describe any symptoms during the infusion.

Sources: nccp · carboplatin_smpc

Topoisomerase-II inhibitor chemotherapy

Oral etoposide

Capsules; supplied brand may vary
Interferes with DNA repair and cell division

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme involved in DNA structure and repair. DNA damage can stop cancer cells dividing. Healthy blood-forming and other cells can also be affected. Absorption by mouth varies, so oral and intravenous doses are not interchangeable.

Capsules absorbed↓DNA repair disrupted↓Cell growth slowed

What to watch for
Follow the exact capsule schedule and record doses. Report missed/vomited doses, infection, bruising, mouth ulcers or severe bowel symptoms. Do not double or convert doses yourself.

Sources: nccp · smpc · leaflet

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Infusion and first capsule day

Carboplatin runs into a vein over about 30 minutes on Day 1. Etoposide is taken as capsules by mouth twice daily on Days 1–3, with the Day 1 sequence confirmed by the unit. The visit includes blood-result review, prevention medicines, preparation and observation. The pharmacy label gives your exact capsule strengths, number and times; do not calculate capsules from this guide. Confirm the capsule calendar, prevention plan and 24-hour number before leaving.

Day 2–3: Continue prescribed capsules

Follow the prescribed capsule count and twice-daily Days 1–3 schedule exactly, using the written calendar to record each dose. Take capsules on an empty stomach with water; ask the pharmacist to explain the timing around food. Do not chew, open or crush capsules. If swallowing is difficult, contact the team before taking them; do not substitute an intravenous preparation or change the dose yourself. Record each dose. Contact the team about a missed or vomited dose; do not take an extra dose yourself.

Day 4–5: Capsules finish; support may continue

Do not continue etoposide beyond the three prescribed days unless the treating team has changed the plan. Follow the written anti-sickness schedule; its medicines do not all stop together. Call if sickness prevents food, fluids or prescribed medicines staying down.

Day 6–20: Recovery and monitoring

Attend the blood tests requested by the team. Low counts, fatigue and mouth or bowel problems may develop between visits. Hearing and nerve symptoms can build across cycles. Use the emergency contact plan at any time; do not wait for the next infusion.

Day 21: Review before another cycle

The team checks blood counts, kidney/liver results, symptoms and treatment benefit before authorising another cycle. Do not start the next capsule course until your team confirms it. Bring the medicine list and dose record.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm blood-test arrangements, transport, capsule prescription and the 24-hour contact number. Tell the team if you are unwell before travelling.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Carboplatin runs into a vein over about 30 minutes on Day 1. Etoposide is taken as capsules by mouth twice daily on Days 1–3, with the Day 1 sequence confirmed by the unit. The visit includes blood-result review, prevention medicines, preparation and observation. The pharmacy label gives your exact capsule strengths, number and times; do not calculate capsules from this guide.

STEP 3

Access for the infusion

The team selects a cannula or appropriate existing central line for carboplatin. Etoposide is taken by mouth, so this regimen does not normally require a take-home infusion pump.

STEP 4

Taking capsules home

Follow the prescribed capsule count and twice-daily Days 1–3 schedule exactly, using the written calendar to record each dose. Take capsules on an empty stomach with water; ask the pharmacist to explain the timing around food. Do not chew, open or crush capsules. If swallowing is difficult, contact the team before taking them; do not substitute an intravenous preparation or change the dose yourself.

STEP 5

Home support

Keep capsules in their original pack, away from children and excess heat or damp, following the supplied label. The manufacturer leaflet advises gloves for handling and washing hands afterwards. Do not open a blister with a leaking capsule; ask the pharmacist what to do. Return unused capsules through the pharmacy; do not put them in household waste or drains.

STEP 6

Bring to review

Bring the capsule dose record, current medicines list and symptom notes. Ask for help if remembering doses or managing the pack is difficult.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Your capsule prescription

Follow the prescribed capsule count and twice-daily Days 1–3 schedule exactly, using the written calendar to record each dose. Take capsules on an empty stomach with water; ask the pharmacist to explain the timing around food. Do not chew, open or crush capsules. If swallowing is difficult, contact the team before taking them; do not substitute an intravenous preparation or change the dose yourself.

Missed or vomited doses

If a dose is missed, do not double the next dose or extend the course. The manufacturer leaflet advises taking the next dose when due; inform your oncology pharmacist/team and follow your written plan. If you vomit after a dose, contact the team for instructions rather than automatically taking replacement capsules. BC Cancer specifically advises no repeat dose after vomiting within one hour unless the healthcare team directs it. Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down needs urgent advice.

Safe handling and storage

Keep capsules in their original pack, away from children and excess heat or damp, following the supplied label. The manufacturer leaflet advises gloves for handling and washing hands afterwards. Do not open a blister with a leaking capsule; ask the pharmacist what to do. Return unused capsules through the pharmacy; do not put them in household waste or drains.

Sickness prevention across several days

Carboplatin Day 1 has high sickness risk; the oral etoposide-only days have a different risk. Your team provides one clear prevention and rescue plan covering treatment days and the next two days. Some medicines overlap; take only the reconciled prescription and do not repeat the full Day 1 plan on your own.

Fluids and symptom reporting

Ask for a drinking plan appropriate to your heart and kidney health. Report inability to drink or reduced urine promptly. Keep mouth-care advice and the emergency number nearby.

Other medicines and vaccines

Show the team all prescribed and non-prescribed medicines, supplements and vaccines. Etoposide has important interactions. Check before adding or stopping medicines; ask about contraception and fertility before treatment.

The precise regimen

This guide covers carboplatin plus oral etoposide for extensive-disease small-cell lung cancer in people unsuitable for cisplatin-based treatment. It does not cover an intravenous etoposide schedule or an added immunotherapy medicine. If the prescription differs, ask for its matching guide and consent discussion.

Sources: nccp

What the evidence can tell us

The studies cited by NCCP support platinum–etoposide treatment and provide background on using etoposide by mouth, but they did not test this exact carboplatin plus all-oral three-day schedule. The oral comparison used cisplatin and a mixture of intravenous and oral etoposide. A separate pooled analysis compared platinum choices across several trials. These results cannot give a precise survival estimate for this prescription or predict your individual benefit.

Sources: johnson · cocis

Treatment reference · 04

What to watch for

Tell the team early so symptoms can be assessed and treated.

Blood counts and infection

Both medicines can lower blood counts, increasing infection, bleeding or anaemia risk. Attend monitoring and report sudden illness, bruising or bleeding.

Sources: nccp · leaflet

Mouth, stomach and energy

Sickness, diarrhoea, constipation, mouth soreness, appetite loss, fatigue and hair loss may occur. Ask for help early; inability to drink or persistent vomiting needs urgent advice.

Sources: leaflet · hse

Hearing, nerves and kidneys

Tell the team about tinnitus, hearing change, numbness, weakness, balance problems or reduced urine. Some effects can persist after treatment.

Sources: nccp

Less common serious effects

Serious allergic or skin reactions can occur. Etoposide also carries a risk of secondary leukaemia; ask the team to explain serious and lasting risks in your situation.

Sources: leaflet · smpc

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Does having more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably show whether the cancer is responding. Report them so they can be treated. Your team assesses benefit using the tests and review plan appropriate to your cancer.

Why might a dose be delayed or reduced?

Your team may allow recovery, investigate a symptom or reduce the chance of lasting harm. Ask which drug changes, why, and when review is planned. A delay does not itself mean treatment has failed.

What should I say when calling?

Give the regimen name, last treatment date, symptoms and when they started. Explain whether you can eat, drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Give your temperature if available, but do not delay calling to collect details.

What belongs in the consent conversation?

The individual aim and likely benefit, uncertainty, reasonable alternatives including not proceeding, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and practical concerns. You can ask for an interpreter, more time or another explanation and can reconsider your decision.

How many cycles should I use this passport for?

The passport provides reusable recording pages, not a prescribed course length. Your oncology team sets the duration and can provide extra pages.

Can I use this page to decide if blood results are safe?

No. Treatment decisions require the clinical protocol, symptoms, previous toxicity, organ function and the prescriber’s review. The patient page explains the checks and does not authorise treatment.

Are oral capsules a weaker form of chemotherapy?

No. Etoposide capsules are chemotherapy and can cause serious effects. Their absorption differs from an infusion, so only the treating team can choose or change the dose.

What if I miss or vomit a dose?

If a dose is missed, do not double the next dose or extend the course. The manufacturer leaflet advises taking the next dose when due; inform your oncology pharmacist/team and follow your written plan. If you vomit after a dose, contact the team for instructions rather than automatically taking replacement capsules. BC Cancer specifically advises no repeat dose after vomiting within one hour unless the healthcare team directs it. Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down needs urgent advice.

Do I continue capsules every day until the next infusion?

No. This regimen prescribes twice-daily etoposide on Days 1–3 only. Use your own written calendar and wait for the team to confirm the next cycle.

Will I always have a fixed number of cycles?

The current NCCP source continues this regimen until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, with ongoing review. Your team agrees the number of cycles and may stop or change earlier.

Try explaining the plan back: Can you describe which medicine is given through a drip, which capsules you take twice daily on Days 1–3, what to do if a dose is missed or vomited, and whom to contact urgently?
