When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call the team urgently for jaundice, unusual bleeding, new cough or worsening breathlessness. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or a serious allergic reaction: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

Block the androgen receptor to help control advanced prostate cancer alongside testosterone suppression.

Androgen-receptor blocker

Bicalutamide 50 mg

Bicalutamide 50 mg
Bicalutamide binds to the androgen receptor, preventing androgens from switching on growth signals. The 50 mg NCCP service combines receptor blockade with reduced testosterone from an LHRH analogue or surgery.

Continuous oral treatment

Duration set by your treatment plan

Take bicalutamide 50 mg once daily continuously with an LHRH analogue, or after surgical castration. A 28-day display is a review window, not a treatment break.

This 50 mg service is an older anti-androgen added to testosterone suppression. It does not replace ADT and is not the same treatment as bicalutamide 150 mg alone or newer androgen-receptor pathway agents. A short flare-protection course has a separate prescribed stop date.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests and blood glucose. Review liver disease, heart-rhythm risks, medicines, fertility and contraception.

STEP 2

Taking or receiving treatment

Swallow the tablet whole at approximately the same time each day, with or without food. Do not chew, split or crush it. If a dose is missed or vomited, take only the next scheduled dose; do not take an extra tablet.

STEP 3

Tests and reviews

Repeat blood count, kidney and liver tests and glucose when clinically indicated. Periodic liver tests are particularly relevant in the first six months. Warfarin or other coumarin treatment needs close INR monitoring.

STEP 4

Specific instructions

Tell the team about yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, worsening breathlessness or unusual bleeding. Use effective contraception during treatment and for 130 days afterwards. Do not stop or alter blood thinners yourself.

STEP 5

Leaving with a clear plan

Confirm the exact dose, next dates, duration and verified daytime/out-of-hours contact numbers.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Sickness support

Minimal sickness risk. Routine preventive anti-sickness medication is not generally required; agree treatment if symptoms develop.

Bone, muscle and general health

Discuss fracture risk, calcium intake, vitamin D, suitable resistance and aerobic exercise and falls prevention. Supplements and bone medicines follow the individual plan.

Medicine review
