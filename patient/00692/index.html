My treatment

My overall treatment

A 14-day cycle with weekly cetuximab. Chemotherapy starts on Day 1 and continues on Day 2 through two separate 22-hour fluorouracil infusions. There is no fixed total number of cycles.

  1. Day 1 · combinationCetuximab, then oxaliplatin/folinic acid, fluorouracil bolus and first 22-hour infusion.
  2. Day 2–3 · fluorouracilDay 2 bolus and second 22-hour infusion; finish around Day 3. Team confirms other Day 2 medicines.
  3. Day 8 · cetuximabAntibody-only visit, including premedicines and observation. Next cycle after review.

More about my medicines

Cetuximab

Cetuximab attaches to EGFR on the outside of cells and blocks some growth messages. In this bowel-cancer plan, validated extended RAS testing helps identify whether treatment is suitable. Healthy skin also uses EGFR, so skin and nail effects need early care.

Oxaliplatin

Oxaliplatin makes links within DNA. These interfere with copying and repair, which can stop a cancer cell dividing. Healthy tissues can also be affected.

Folinic acid

Folinic acid helps fluorouracil remain bound to an enzyme needed to make DNA. In this regimen it increases fluorouracil activity and can also increase its unwanted effects. It is not a vitamin supplement to start yourself.

Fluorouracil

Fluorouracil interferes with the production and use of DNA and RNA, the instructions cells need to grow. This FOLFOX4 schedule gives a bolus followed by a 22-hour infusion on Day 1 and repeats that fluorouracil sequence on Day 2.

Getting ready for treatment visits

Consent discussion aid

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1: Treatment and checks

The team reviews blood tests, symptoms and your prescription before treatment. Premedicines and observation are part of cetuximab visits.

What to do: Know the contact number and the next planned visit.

Day 2–3: Day 2 treatment and completion

A second fluorouracil treatment is planned on Day 2; trained staff renew the infusion. The team confirms the setting and any other Day 2 medicines.

What to do: Call early for symptoms; do not wait for the next appointment.

Day 4–14: Recovery and the next review

Weekly cetuximab includes a Day 8 visit between chemotherapy cycles. The team uses symptoms, tests and your priorities to decide what happens next.

What to do: Bring questions and a symptom record if useful; this is optional.

Support at home

Skin, nails and minerals

Start the prescribed skin-care plan early, use moisturiser and sun protection, and report painful or infected skin or nails. Keep mineral blood-test appointments during treatment and for two months after cetuximab.

Bowel symptoms and fluids

Follow your written rescue plan. Fever, blood, severe pain, reduced urine or inability to drink needs urgent advice.

Line and pump plan

Arrange Day 2 infusion renewal and final disconnection around Day 3. This is two separate 22-hour infusions; do not use a 46-hour pump timetable.

Comfort and daily life

Avoid cold drinks and cold exposure as instructed after oxaliplatin; do not suck ice chips during fluorouracil in this regimen. Use prescribed anti-sickness medicines, pace activities and ask for nutritional, emotional or practical support.

The whole treatment plan

Ask how and when the team will assess benefit, which blood tests continue during a pause, and what happens after the final dose. Mineral checks continue monthly for two months after the last antibody treatment.

The cetuximab-only visit

Day 8 contains cetuximab only. Its premedicines and observation still matter, but the chemotherapy-day anti-sickness course is not automatically repeated.
