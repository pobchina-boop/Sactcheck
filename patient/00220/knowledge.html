How this medicine supports your treatment

Gefitinib blocks abnormal EGFR growth signals in selected lung cancers. Treatment aims to control advanced cancer and delay progression.

EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Gefitinib

Blocks abnormal EGFR growth signals
Activating EGFR mutation identified↓EGFR signalling blocked↓Cancer growth slowed

Gefitinib blocks the EGFR tyrosine kinase signal that can drive growth in cancers with activating EGFR mutations. Effects on normal skin and bowel cells also contribute to side effects.

Report diarrhoea, rash, breathing changes and eye symptoms promptly.

Your treatment aim

Gefitinib blocks abnormal EGFR growth signals in selected lung cancers. Treatment aims to control advanced cancer and delay progression.

Which setting?

NCCP 00220a covers adults with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with an activating EGFR mutation. This is continuous treatment for advanced disease, not a fixed adjuvant course.

NCCP 00220 v5 · official regimen · EMA Iressa product information

Duration

The usual dose is 250 mg by mouth once daily, continuously without a planned rest week. Use the dose on your prescription. Treatment continues while it helps and side effects remain acceptable; any change is agreed with your team.

Checks before and during treatment

Tests and symptoms guide safe treatment decisions.

Before starting

Confirm the activating EGFR result, exact indication, previous treatment and prescription. Check blood count, kidneys and liver. NCCP also specifies baseline chest X-ray and CT scan. Review lungs, bowel, skin, eyes, all medicines and pregnancy precautions.

NCCP 00220 v5 · official regimen · EMA Iressa product information

During treatment

NCCP specifies blood count, kidney and liver tests throughout treatment as clinically indicated. The team sets your appointments and scan plan. Report symptoms promptly; extra checks may be needed for diarrhoea, dehydration or liver problems.

Taking gefitinib

Follow your individual prescription.

Daily tablets

Take once daily with or without food, at about the same time. If missed, take when remembered unless less than 12 hours remain before the next dose; then skip it. Never double a dose. If swallowing is difficult, pharmacy can teach dispersion in non-carbonated water without crushing; drink within 60 minutes and rinse the glass. Ask your team what to do after vomiting; do not take an extra tablet without advice.

NCCP 00220 v5 · official regimen · EMA Iressa product information

Dose changes

For poorly tolerated bowel or skin effects, the source permits a brief interruption of up to 14 days and reinstatement of 250 mg daily. If treatment remains intolerable after interruption, an alternative is considered. Liver impairment requires a separate specialist plan; do not split tablets or calculate half a dose yourself.

Living with treatment

Report symptoms early and keep contact details available.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Keep the prescribed anti-diarrhoea plan available from the first dose. Report loose stools early and keep drinking if able. Persistent or severe diarrhoea, weakness, inability to drink or much less urine requires urgent advice. The team decides whether to hold treatment and when it is safe to restart.

NCCP 00220 v5 · official regimen · EMA Iressa product information

Skin, nails and mouth

Use the agreed skin, nail and mouth-care plan. Report rash, painful nails or mouth sores early. Emollients and sun protection may help; the team decides whether creams, antibiotics or other treatment are needed. Blistering or peeling skin needs urgent assessment.

NCCP 00220 v5 · official regimen · EMA Iressa product information

Nausea medicines

Gefitinib has minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Agree any as-needed anti-sickness medicine with the team. Routine infusion premedication is not required.

NCCP 00220 v5 · official regimen · NCCP SACT antiemetic guidance V6 (2025), oral tables · NCCP NCIS antiemetic medicines V8 (2025); parenteral scope

Medicine interactions

Give pharmacy all medicines and supplements. Strong CYP3A4 inducers can reduce exposure; inhibitors and some CYP2D6 interactions can increase risk. Medicines that keep stomach acid low may reduce absorption. Ask for an individual medicine plan; do not borrow timing from another EGFR medicine. Warfarin needs regular blood-clotting checks.

Pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding

Avoid pregnancy during treatment and discuss effective contraception and future pregnancy plans with the team. NCCP excludes pregnancy and breastfeeding; do not breastfeed during treatment. The cited product information does not specify a universal post-treatment contraception interval, so ask for an individual plan.

When to get urgent help

Do not wait for the next appointment.

Call urgently

Call urgently for new or worsening breathlessness, cough or fever; severe diarrhoea, inability to drink or much less urine; severe or persistent abdominal pain; blistering or peeling skin; or yellow skin/eyes. Eye pain, light sensitivity or blurred vision needs prompt assessment. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or swelling of the tongue/throat needs emergency help. Use your unit’s temperature and 24-hour contact instructions.

NCCP 00220 v5 · official regimen · EMA Iressa product information

When you call

Give the medicine name, last dose and symptoms. Tell the team if you can drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Do not delay calling while gathering information.

What studies tell us

In a study of previously untreated EGFR-mutated advanced lung cancer, gefitinib delayed progression compared with chemotherapy. The final survival comparison did not show a statistically significant difference. The study population and subsequent treatments matter; ask how the evidence applies to your exact setting.

Your own decision

Ask how the study population matches your situation and discuss alternatives, harms and remaining uncertainty.
