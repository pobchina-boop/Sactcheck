My treatment handbook

EMA/EP for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds.

Dactinomycin · Dactinomycin

Cytotoxic antibiotic. Interferes with the way tumour cells use DNA to make RNA. Tell the nurse immediately if the injection site burns or stings.

Etoposide · Etoposide

Topoisomerase inhibitor. Prevents tumour cells repairing DNA during division. Tell the nurse if you feel faint or breathless during the infusion.

Methotrexate · Methotrexate

Antifolate. Prevents rapidly dividing trophoblastic cells using folate to make new DNA. Check new medicines with oncology pharmacy and follow the timed rescue plan.

Folinic acid · Folinic acid

Rescue medicine. Supplies a usable form of folate to protect healthy cells after methotrexate. Ring the unit for any missed, late or vomited rescue dose.

Cisplatin · Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. Links strands of DNA together so tumour cells cannot divide normally. Attend for fluids and blood tests. Report hearing changes or reduced urine.

What the research found

A specialist series supports platinum-containing salvage treatment after EMA/CO failure.

Non-randomised series with differing prior treatments. Follow the Irish sequence for administration.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · EMA

Dactinomycin and etoposide are followed by a 12-hour methotrexate infusion. Record its start time. There is no second day of IV EMA in this regimen.

Days 2 and 3 · oral rescue

Start the first oral folinic acid tablet 24 hours after the methotrexate infusion STARTS. Take four doses 12 hours apart unless your team gives you a different written rescue plan. If a tablet is late, missed or vomited, phone the unit straight away.

Day 8 · EP

Etoposide is followed by cisplatin. Fluids are given before and after cisplatin to protect the kidneys. The complete cycle repeats every two weeks.

Common effects and what to do

How you feel between visits

Tiredness, nausea or appetite changes can make everyday activities harder. Ask your team for help if symptoms interfere with eating or drinking.

Serious effects: when to call

IV site injury · Dactinomycin

Report pain, swelling or blistering at the injection site immediately.

Sore mouth · Dactinomycin

Phone if mouth ulcers prevent you drinking.

Infection or bleeding · Dactinomycin

Contact the unit urgently for fever, chills or unusual bleeding.

Low blood counts · Etoposide

Phone urgently for fever, chills or unexplained bleeding.

Infusion reaction · Etoposide

Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, faintness or breathing problems.

Mouth ulcers · Methotrexate

Ask for advice if sores make eating or drinking difficult.

Slower clearance · Methotrexate

Attend kidney blood tests. Report vomiting or difficulty drinking.

Rescue tablets · Folinic acid

Check each time against your written schedule. Phone about a missed or vomited dose.

Hearing changes · Cisplatin

Report ringing in the ears or trouble hearing conversation.

Kidney injury · Cisplatin

Report reduced urine, vomiting or difficulty drinking.

Nerve changes · Cisplatin

Report numb fingers, tingling or difficulty with buttons.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Call promptly for reduced urine, ringing in the ears, new numbness, or missed rescue tablets. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Preparing for your oncology discussion

  • What happens at my next visit? EMA on day 1, folinic acid on days 2 and 3, then EP with fluids on day 8.
  • Which symptoms should I phone about? Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Call promptly for reduced urine, ringing in the ears, new numbness, or missed rescue tablets.
  • What should I plan before starting? Report hearing symptoms and medicines that may affect the kidneys.
  • How should I plan follow-up? After remission your specialist arranges hCG every two weeks for six months, then monthly for six months, then every two months for two years.
  • Why does treatment continue after my hCG is normal? Three further cycles over six weeks help treat disease too small to detect. Your consultant may extend this to four cycles over eight weeks when adverse features such as liver metastases are present.
  • What if I cannot keep fluids down after the day 8 infusion? Call your 24-hour oncology number promptly if vomiting prevents you from drinking or you are passing less urine. Cisplatin can strain the kidneys. Tell the team when you last drank and passed urine so they can assess whether you need fluids through a drip.
