Your treatment journey

EMA/EP for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · EMA

Dactinomycin and etoposide are followed by a 12-hour methotrexate infusion. Record its start time. There is no second day of IV EMA in this regimen.

Days 2 and 3 · oral rescue

Start the first oral folinic acid tablet 24 hours after the methotrexate infusion STARTS. Take four doses 12 hours apart unless your team gives you a different written rescue plan. If a tablet is late, missed or vomited, phone the unit straight away.

Day 8 · EP

Etoposide is followed by cisplatin. Fluids are given before and after cisplatin to protect the kidneys. The complete cycle repeats every two weeks.

At review visits

  • What happens at my next visit? EMA on day 1, folinic acid on days 2 and 3, then EP with fluids on day 8.
  • Which symptoms should I phone about? Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Call promptly for reduced urine, ringing in the ears, new numbness, or missed rescue tablets.
  • What should I plan before starting? Report hearing symptoms and medicines that may affect the kidneys.
  • How should I plan follow-up? After remission your specialist arranges hCG every two weeks for six months, then monthly for six months, then every two months for two years.
  • Why does treatment continue after my hCG is normal? Three further cycles over six weeks help treat disease too small to detect. Your consultant may extend this to four cycles over eight weeks when adverse features such as liver metastases are present.
  • What if I cannot keep fluids down after the day 8 infusion? Call your 24-hour oncology number promptly if vomiting prevents you from drinking or you are passing less urine. Cisplatin can strain the kidneys. Tell the team when you last drank and passed urine so they can assess whether you need fluids through a drip.
