EMA/EP for gestational trophoblastic neoplasia

Treat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds.

High-risk gestational trophoblastic neoplasia · NCCP 00264 v4 · Source checked 2026-11-26

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

My overall treatment

EMA on day 1, folinic acid on days 2 and 3, then EP with fluids on day 8.

  1. What your team is monitoringTreat the trophoblastic tumour with the aim of cure. Serial blood hCG measures how the disease responds.

My treatment schedule

Day 1 · EMADactinomycin and etoposide are followed by a 12-hour methotrexate infusion. Record its start time. There is no second day of IV EMA in this regimen.
Days 2 and 3 · oral rescueStart the first oral folinic acid tablet 24 hours after the methotrexate infusion STARTS. Take four doses 12 hours apart unless your team gives you a different written rescue plan. If a tablet is late, missed or vomited, phone the unit straight away.
Day 8 · EPEtoposide is followed by cisplatin. Fluids are given before and after cisplatin to protect the kidneys. The complete cycle repeats every two weeks.

Your team confirms the active phase and dates. Symptoms may occur during or after treatment; there are no guaranteed safe days.

My tests and checks

WhenWhat is checkedTiming / reason
Before treatmentCheck blood count, kidney and liver tests and serum hCG. Arrange hearing assessment and measured or calculated clearance as clinically indicated.NCCP 00264Report hearing symptoms and medicines that may affect the kidneys.
Before each cycleRepeat blood count, kidney and liver tests. Check serum hCG on day 1, more often if indicated.NCCP 00264Ask how your hCG trend affects the next treatment.
After treatmentContinue follow-up through your GTN service.NCCP 00264After remission your specialist arranges hCG every two weeks for six months, then monthly for six months, then every two months for two years.

Between treatments

  • Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills or sudden illness. Call promptly for reduced urine, ringing in the ears, new numbness, or missed rescue tablets.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
