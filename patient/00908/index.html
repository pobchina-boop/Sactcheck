Serplulimab, carboplatin and etoposide

This treatment aims to control extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer, relieve symptoms and help people live longer. The balance of benefit, side effects and daily life is individual; it is generally not offered with an expectation of cure.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

Induction lasts four planned 21-day cycles unless treatment needs to stop earlier. On Day 1 you receive serplulimab, carboplatin and etoposide by drip. With the standard intravenous (IV) schedule, etoposide is repeated on Days 2 and 3. After induction, carboplatin and etoposide stop. Serplulimab alone can continue every three weeks while it is helping and its risks remain acceptable. There is no fixed universal number of maintenance cycles in this National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) regimen. Your team may instead prescribe etoposide by drip on Day 1 and capsules twice daily on Days 2 and 3. This is a separate alternative, not extra treatment. Oral and IV doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Confirm which plan you have; take the capsules on an empty stomach as prescribed.

Tests and checks

Blood counts, kidney and liver function and glucose are checked before each cycle. Thyroid tests are repeated every 3–6 weeks. The medicine information also recommends pancreatic enzyme checks at baseline and periodically. Other tests and scans depend on symptoms and the consultant plan.

Between treatment visits

The main bands illustrate a 21-day induction cycle with treatment Days 1–3. Maintenance uses serplulimab on Day 1 only. The reference picture does not predict symptom timing or identify safe days.

Use the oncology emergency instructions for fever, shivers or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. New cough/breathlessness, severe diarrhoea, reduced urine, confusion or severe rash need prompt advice. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or major bleeding need emergency help. Immune symptoms may start after treatment stops.
