Serplulimab, carboplatin and etoposide

This treatment aims to control extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer, relieve symptoms and help people live longer. The balance of benefit, side effects and daily life is individual; it is generally not offered with an expectation of cure.

Induction lasts four planned 21-day cycles unless treatment needs to stop earlier. On Day 1 you receive serplulimab, carboplatin and etoposide by drip. With the standard IV schedule, etoposide is repeated on Days 2 and 3. After induction, carboplatin and etoposide stop. Serplulimab alone can continue every three weeks while it is helping and its risks remain acceptable. There is no fixed universal number of maintenance cycles in this NCCP regimen. Your team may instead prescribe etoposide by drip on Day 1 and capsules twice daily on Days 2 and 3. This is a separate alternative, not extra treatment. Oral and IV doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Confirm which plan you have; take the capsules on an empty stomach as prescribed.

How your medicines work

Immunotherapy

Serplulimab

Blocks the PD-1 immune brake
Immune cell→Brake blocked→Cancer cell

Serplulimab is an antibody that blocks PD-1, a control switch on immune T cells. Some cancers use PD-L1 or PD-L2 to engage this switch and reduce the immune response. Blocking it can help immune cells attack cancer. The same immune activation can inflame healthy organs, so new symptoms need assessment even after treatment stops.

The immune system can also inflame healthy organs. New cough, diarrhoea, unusual weakness, severe headache, thirst or a spreading rash need prompt advice. Effects can start during treatment or after it finishes.

Chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Damages cancer-cell DNA
DNA strands→Platinum links→Copying disrupted

Carboplatin is a platinum-containing chemotherapy. Inside cells, it forms chemical attachments to DNA, including links that stop DNA strands working normally. This disrupts copying and repair and can lead to cell death. Healthy cells can also be injured, especially blood-forming cells in the bone marrow. The kidneys help remove carboplatin, so kidney function is used to calculate the dose. AUC 5 describes a target drug exposure over time; it is not a fixed dose of 5 mg.

Low blood counts, nausea and vomiting can occur. An allergic reaction is also possible: tell the nurse immediately if you feel flushed, itchy, tight-chested or unwell during the drip.

Chemotherapy

Etoposide

Interferes with DNA repair during cell division
DNA strands→Repair disrupted→Cell division stops

Etoposide blocks topoisomerase II, an enzyme that helps untangle DNA. It can leave DNA breaks unrepaired so that dividing cancer cells die. Healthy dividing cells are affected too, which can lower blood counts, irritate the mouth and bowel and cause hair loss. Kidney function, liver tests and albumin help the team choose a safe dose. Capsules have different dosing instructions from the drip.

Report fever or sudden illness immediately. During a drip, tell the nurse about dizziness, flushing, breathing difficulty or pain at the cannula. If capsules are prescribed, do not take an extra dose after vomiting or a missed dose without advice.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Appointments

Serplulimab is given first over about 60 minutes at the first visit. If tolerated, later doses may take about 30 minutes. Carboplatin takes about 30 minutes and IV etoposide about 60 minutes. Assessment, preparation and observation add time. Days 2–3 need further etoposide visits unless the separate oral alternative has been prescribed.

STEP 2

Route and phases

Induction lasts four planned 21-day cycles unless treatment needs to stop earlier. On Day 1 you receive serplulimab, carboplatin and etoposide by drip. With the standard IV schedule, etoposide is repeated on Days 2 and 3. Your team may instead prescribe etoposide by drip on Day 1 and capsules twice daily on Days 2 and 3. This is a separate alternative, not extra treatment. Oral and IV doses are not interchangeable milligram for milligram. Confirm which plan you have; take the capsules on an empty stomach as prescribed.

STEP 3

Maintenance visits

After induction, carboplatin and etoposide stop. Serplulimab alone can continue every three weeks while it is helping and its risks remain acceptable. There is no fixed universal number of maintenance cycles in this NCCP regimen.

STEP 4

Medicines and urgent advice

Keep the written anti-sickness schedule, capsule instructions if relevant, thermometer, immunotherapy alert card and oncology numbers available. Ask early about transport or help at home.

  • Immune inflammation of lungs, bowel, liver, kidneys, skin and hormone glands
  • Immune effects can also involve the heart, pancreas, nervous system or blood cells

Carboplatin

  • Low blood counts, infection or bleeding
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Allergic reactions
  • Kidney, hearing and nerve effects

Etoposide

  • Low blood counts, infection and bleeding
  • Hair loss and mouth or bowel symptoms
  • Infusion reactions and low blood pressure
  • A delayed risk of treatment-related leukaemia

Support around treatment

Induction: plan each treatment day

Day 1 carboplatin has a stronger anti-sickness plan. Days 2–3 etoposide also need planned cover, with review for two days after the final chemotherapy dose. Ask for one reconciled schedule; do not add another steroid or anti-sickness course yourself.

Maintenance: a different prescription

Serplulimab alone usually does not need the routine chemotherapy anti-sickness course. Continue the alert card and immune symptom checks; new nausea or vomiting may need investigation, not just extra anti-sickness tablets.

Do not wait for the next appointment

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
