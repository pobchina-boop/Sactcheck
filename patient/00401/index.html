My schedule and checks

Know the plan before your next visit.

Alectinib monotherapy

Alectinib targets an abnormal ALK growth signal. After complete surgery the aim is to reduce the chance of cancer returning; in advanced cancer the aim is to control the cancer. Your team explains the individual aim, alternatives and uncertainty.

My schedule at a glance

Your treatment pattern

Continuous twice-daily capsules

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

Take alectinib twice daily with food, every day, with no planned rest week. After surgery, treatment lasts up to 24 months, stopping sooner for recurrence or unacceptable side effects. For advanced cancer, treatment continues until progression or unacceptable side effects under the oncology plan.

Tests and checks

Liver profile every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then every 4 weeks or as clinically indicated. full blood count (FBC) and renal profile every 4 weeks. creatine phosphokinase (CPK) every 2 weeks for the first month, then as clinically indicated, including muscle symptoms. Blood pressure and pulse as clinically indicated. National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) says electrocardiogram (ECG) prior to cycle 2, then as indicated; a continuous oral regimen has no defined cycle length, so agree the first repeat ECG appointment locally. Assess symptoms, interactions, adherence, current dose and treatment benefit; scans and disease monitoring follow the Consultant plan.

Between treatment visits

Relative timing only. Symptoms can occur at any time; no dates or personal information are required.

Keep your oncology emergency number available. A serious symptom needs review between scheduled visits.

Selected effects, not every possible effect or a prediction. Blood-count, skin and line markers are schematic. Number and text carry meaning; colour is decorative.
Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening cough or breathlessness, unexplained muscle pain or weakness, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, marked tiredness or pallor, dizziness or fainting, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or reduced urine. Sudden serious breathing difficulty, severe chest pain or collapse needs emergency help. Do not wait for the next blood test. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms: 112 / 999.
