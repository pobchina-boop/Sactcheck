NCCP 00401 · version 6

Alectinib monotherapy

Take alectinib twice daily with food, every day, with no planned rest week. After surgery, treatment lasts up to 24 months, stopping sooner for recurrence or unacceptable side effects. For advanced cancer, treatment continues until progression or unacceptable side effects under the oncology plan.

Purpose, setting and alternatives

Alectinib targets an abnormal ALK growth signal. After complete surgery the aim is to reduce the chance of cancer returning; in advanced cancer the aim is to control the cancer. Material risks and support

  • Muscle pain or weakness and raised CPK
  • Liver injury and raised liver tests or bilirubin
  • Anaemia, including destruction of red blood cells (haemolysis)
  • Slow heart rate, dizziness or fainting
  • Lung inflammation (ILD/pneumonitis), which can be serious
  • Constipation, nausea, diarrhoea and swelling
  • Skin rash and sensitivity to sunlight
  • Gastrointestinal perforation; urgent severe abdominal pain
  • Potential harm to a developing baby

Discuss the individual aim, the evidence for your setting, alternatives including not proceeding, uncertainty, material risks, pregnancy/fertility, daily capsules, monitoring and access. You can ask for an interpreter, more time or another explanation and can reconsider. This aid supports a consent conversation; it does not replace it.

Monitoring and understanding

Liver profile every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then every 4 weeks or as clinically indicated. FBC and renal profile every 4 weeks. CPK every 2 weeks for the first month, then as clinically indicated, including muscle symptoms. Blood pressure and pulse as clinically indicated. NCCP says ECG prior to cycle 2, then as indicated; a continuous oral regimen has no defined cycle length, so agree the first repeat ECG appointment locally. Assess symptoms, interactions, adherence, current dose and treatment benefit; scans and disease monitoring follow the Consultant plan.
