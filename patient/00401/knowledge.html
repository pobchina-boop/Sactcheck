How this medicine supports your treatment

Alectinib

Blocks an abnormal ALK growth signal
ALK signal drives cancer growth↓Alectinib blocks ALK activity↓Growth can slow or stop

Alectinib blocks the abnormal ALK protein that drives growth in ALK-positive cancer cells. It can act against cancer in the body and brain. It can also affect healthy tissues, so blood tests and symptom checks remain important.

Take with food. Keep blood tests and report muscle weakness, breathing changes, jaundice or dizziness promptly.

Understanding your treatment

This guide covers alectinib alone for ALK-positive lung cancer. Confirm whether it is adjuvant treatment after complete surgery, first treatment for advanced cancer, or treatment after crizotinib. These settings have different evidence and treatment lengths.

Your treatment aim

Alectinib targets an abnormal ALK growth signal. After complete surgery the aim is to reduce the chance of cancer returning; in advanced cancer the aim is to control the cancer. Your team explains the individual aim, alternatives and uncertainty.

NCCP 00401 v6 · official regimen · EMA Alecensa product information, updated 31 March 2026

How long treatment lasts

Take alectinib twice daily with food, every day, with no planned rest week. After surgery, treatment lasts up to 24 months, stopping sooner for recurrence or unacceptable side effects. For advanced cancer, treatment continues until progression or unacceptable side effects under the oncology plan.

NCCP 00401 v6 · official regimen · EMA Alecensa product information, updated 31 March 2026

Evidence for your setting

After complete surgery, ALINA found that alectinib delayed cancer recurrence compared with platinum chemotherapy; whether it improves overall survival remains uncertain. In untreated advanced cancer, ALEX showed better cancer control than crizotinib, but its final survival comparison did not establish a clear overall-survival difference. Separate studies showed tumour responses after crizotinib. These groups cannot predict your personal outcome or rank all current treatment choices.

ALINA primary analysis: resected ALK-positive NSCLC, 2024 · ALINA updated DFS, ESMO 2025 abstract 1787MO; cutoff 8 December 2024 · ALEX final OS analysis, 2025; cutoff 28 April 2025 · Ou et al: post-crizotinib single-arm phase II global study

Before the first capsules

Confirm a practical plan with your team.

Baseline checks

Confirm ALK-positive NSCLC by a validated test; establish indication, staging/resection and prior-treatment history. FBC, renal and liver profile, CPK, blood pressure and ECG before treatment. Review all medicines, supplements, muscle/lung/cardiac symptoms, pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding. For treatment after crizotinib allow a minimum 7-day washout. For adjuvant treatment confirm complete resection, high-risk criteria and ECOG 0–1.

Taking alectinib

The usual starting dose is 600 mg twice daily: four 150 mg capsules each time, taken with food. Use your own prescription, which may specify a lower dose. Swallow capsules whole; do not open or dissolve them.

Missed or vomited dose

If a dose is missed, take it only if the next dose is more than 6 hours away. If the next dose is due within 6 hours, skip the missed dose. Do not take two doses together. If you vomit after a dose, take the next dose at its usual time; do not take an extra dose.

Medicines and food

Check all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal products with pharmacy. Ask specifically about medicines that slow the pulse and grapefruit/Seville oranges. Do not stop another prescribed medicine on your own.

EMA Alecensa product information, updated 31 March 2026

Tests and symptom review

Blood-test results may change without symptoms.

Scheduled monitoring

Liver profile every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then every 4 weeks or as clinically indicated. FBC and renal profile every 4 weeks. CPK every 2 weeks for the first month, then as clinically indicated, including muscle symptoms. Blood pressure and pulse as clinically indicated. NCCP says ECG prior to cycle 2, then as indicated; a continuous oral regimen has no defined cycle length, so agree the first repeat ECG appointment locally. Assess symptoms, interactions, adherence, current dose and treatment benefit; scans and disease monitoring follow the Consultant plan.

Symptoms between visits

Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening cough or breathlessness, unexplained muscle pain or weakness, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, marked tiredness or pallor, dizziness or fainting, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or reduced urine. Sudden serious breathing difficulty, severe chest pain or collapse needs emergency help. Do not wait for the next blood test.

Daily living and planning

Raise practical concerns early.

Sun protection

Avoid prolonged sunlight during treatment and for at least 7 days after stopping. Use broad-spectrum UVA/UVB sunscreen and lip balm SPF 50 or higher.

Pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding

Women who could become pregnant need highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least 5 weeks afterwards. Men with female partners who could become pregnant need highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3 months afterwards. Oral contraceptive effectiveness may be reduced: ask for a suitable method. Do not breastfeed during treatment. Discuss fertility before treatment and contact the team promptly if pregnancy occurs.

Practical support

Anti-sickness medicines

Alectinib is minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Treat nausea with prescribed as-required medicine if needed. Routine preventive anti-sickness treatment and infusion premedication are not required; your team selects a suitable rescue medicine.

Capsules, food and missed doses

The usual starting dose is 600 mg twice daily: four 150 mg capsules each time, taken with food. Use your own prescription, which may specify a lower dose. Swallow capsules whole; do not open or dissolve them. If a dose is missed, take it only if the next dose is more than 6 hours away. If the next dose is due within 6 hours, skip the missed dose. Do not take two doses together. If you vomit after a dose, take the next dose at its usual time; do not take an extra dose.

Muscles, liver, blood and breathing

Keep liver, CPK and blood-count appointments. Muscle symptoms, jaundice, dark urine, unusual fatigue, cough or breathlessness need prompt review even between appointments.

Heart rate and other medicines

Tell the team about dizziness, fainting and medicines that slow the pulse. Check new prescriptions, supplements, grapefruit and Seville oranges with pharmacy; interactions may require closer monitoring rather than an automatic dose change.

Sun protection

Avoid prolonged sunlight during treatment and for at least 7 days after stopping. Use broad-spectrum UVA/UVB sunscreen and lip balm SPF 50 or higher.

Pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding

Women who could become pregnant need highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least 5 weeks afterwards. Men with female partners who could become pregnant need highly effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3 months afterwards. Oral contraceptive effectiveness may be reduced: ask for a suitable method. Do not breastfeed during treatment. Discuss fertility before treatment and contact the team promptly if pregnancy occurs.

Questions to ask

Does taking capsules mean fewer important risks?

Capsules are cancer treatment and can cause serious side effects. Keep the planned tests and use the oncology contact plan for new symptoms.

Does having side effects show it is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably indicate cancer response; your team assesses benefit using the relevant follow-up plan.

How long will I take alectinib?

Take alectinib twice daily with food, every day, with no planned rest week. After surgery, treatment lasts up to 24 months, stopping sooner for recurrence or unacceptable side effects. For advanced cancer, treatment continues until progression or unacceptable side effects under the oncology plan.

What if I miss a dose or vomit?

If a dose is missed, take it only if the next dose is more than 6 hours away. If the next dose is due within 6 hours, skip the missed dose. Do not take two doses together. If you vomit after a dose, take the next dose at its usual time; do not take an extra dose.

Which blood tests happen early?

Liver tests are planned every 2 weeks for 3 months. CPK is checked every 2 weeks for the first month. Blood count and kidney tests are checked every 4 weeks; symptoms may prompt extra tests.

When is my next ECG?

NCCP asks for an ECG before its second treatment cycle, but this medicine is taken continuously. Ask the team to confirm the first repeat ECG appointment and later checks.

Can I change or restart my dose myself?

No. If alectinib is held or reduced, ask what needs to recover, when review happens and who will authorise restarting.

What should I say when calling?

Give the medicine name, current dose, last dose, symptoms and when they began. Explain whether you can drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Do not delay calling to collect details.

Can I use this page to decide whether tests are safe?

No. Blood results, symptoms, current dose and treatment history need the prescriber’s assessment. This guide does not authorise treatment.

Do I need to enter dates?

No. This map shows general timing. Your hospital arranges actual appointments.
