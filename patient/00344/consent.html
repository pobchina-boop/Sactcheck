Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

FLOT · chemotherapy before and after surgery

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

Shrink the cancer before surgery and treat microscopic cancer cells that could cause recurrence afterwards. The aim is cure. For resectable stomach or gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma, cT2 or higher or node positive (NCCP 00344). ESOPEC also studied resectable oesophageal adenocarcinoma.

Treatment schedule. Four cycles before surgery, then four after recovery. Each cycle is 14 days. Cycle 5 usually starts 6–12 weeks after the operation.

Regimen-specific side effects

Docetaxel

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Blood counts, bowel and fluidFever, chills or abdominal pain with diarrhoea need an urgent call. Report ankle swelling. Hair and nail changes can occur.
Infusion reactionTell the nurse immediately about flushing, wheeze or dizziness. Take the prescribed steroid before infusion.

Oxaliplatin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Cold sensitivity and nerve injuryCold can trigger tingling or throat tightness. Numbness may persist and affect walking or finger movements. Report it before the next cycle.
Allergy or line painTell the nurse immediately about swelling, stinging, rash or trouble breathing.

Fluorouracil

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Mouth, gut, hands and feetReport ulcers, diarrhoea, painful palms or soles early. Severe toxicity can develop rapidly with low DPD activity.
Chest painFluorouracil can cause coronary spasm during or after the pump. Get urgent help for chest pain.

Folinic acid

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Interaction and allergyFolinic acid strengthens fluorouracil and can increase mouth and bowel symptoms. Alert the nurse for a new rash, swelling or wheeze.

Treatment discussion and decision

FLOT · chemotherapy before and after surgery

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

In FLOT4, median survival was 50 months with perioperative FLOT and 35 months with older chemotherapy. In ESOPEC, 57.4% were alive at three years with FLOT versus 50.7% with preoperative chemoradiotherapy. These trials compared whole treatment strategies. They cannot quantify the benefit of an individual postoperative cycle or predict one person’s outcome.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Phone oncology immediately for fever, chills, sudden illness, severe diarrhoea, or a leaking pump. Call 112 or 999 for severe chest pain, collapse or severe breathing difficulty. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
