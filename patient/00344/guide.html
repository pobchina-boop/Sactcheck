FLOT · chemotherapy before and after surgery

Shrink the cancer before surgery and treat microscopic cancer cells that could cause recurrence afterwards. The aim is cure.

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

My overall treatment

Four cycles → surgery → four cycles. Every 14 days during chemotherapy.

  1. Before surgeryFour treatments two weeks apart. On day 1 you receive infusions and leave with a 24-hour fluorouracil pump. Your team disconnects it on day 2.
  2. Surgery and recoveryThe surgeon and oncology team review healing, infection, swallowing, fluid and food intake, weight and strength. The operation sets the timing of the next phase.
  3. After surgeryCycle 5 usually starts 6–12 weeks after surgery, once you have recovered. The team confirms the restart date. Four further cycles are planned two weeks apart if tolerated.

What happens in each cycle

Day beforeStart prescribed steroid tablets. Contact the unit if you miss a dose.
Day 1Infusions in the day unit, then your 24-hour pump is connected.
Day 2 and recoveryThe team disconnects your pump. Between visits phone early for fever, diarrhoea or trouble drinking.

Your team confirms the active phase and dates. Symptoms may occur during or after treatment; there are no guaranteed safe days.

Between treatments

  • Day 1 infusions and pump. Day 2 pump disconnection. Contact oncology promptly if you feel unwell.

Take your prescribed steroids from the day before. Confirm your day 2 pump appointment.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
