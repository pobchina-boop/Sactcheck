My treatment passport

FLOT · chemotherapy before and after surgery

My treatment record

Shrink the cancer before surgery and treat microscopic cancer cells that could cause recurrence afterwards. The aim is cure.

PhaseWhenWhat changes
Before surgeryCycles 1–4Four treatments two weeks apart. On day 1 you receive infusions and leave with a 24-hour fluorouracil pump. Your team disconnects it on day 2.
Surgery and recoveryBetween cycles 4 and 5The surgeon and oncology team review healing, infection, swallowing, fluid and food intake, weight and strength. The operation sets the timing of the next phase.
After surgeryCycles 5–8Cycle 5 usually starts 6–12 weeks after surgery, once you have recovered. The team confirms the restart date. Four further cycles are planned two weeks apart if tolerated.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Phone oncology immediately for fever, chills, sudden illness, severe diarrhoea, or a leaking pump. Call 112 or 999 for severe chest pain, collapse or severe breathing difficulty. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Appointments, tests and results

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat is checkedWhat you can do
Before first cycleBlood counts, kidney and liver function and a dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) test. Low DPD activity can make fluorouracil dangerous.Tell us about earlier reactions and bring a medication list.
Before each cycleRepeat blood counts, kidney and liver tests. Report tingling or numbness. The team assesses nerves at least every two cycles and sooner if needed.Tell us about fever, sore mouth, diarrhoea or trouble using your hands.
Before cycle 5Surgical recovery, eating and drinking, weight, activity, blood results and lasting side effects.Contact the surgical or oncology team if swallowing worsens, feeds are difficult or you feel unwell.

Your team plans scans and follow-up according to your treatment and recovery.
