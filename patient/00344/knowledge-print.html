Your treatment reference

FLOT · chemotherapy before and after surgery

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Shrink the cancer before surgery and treat microscopic cancer cells that could cause recurrence afterwards. The aim is cure.

Docetaxel · Taxotere

Taxane chemotherapy. It holds the structures used in cell division in place so dividing cells cannot separate. Call urgently for fever or sudden illness. During the drip, report flushing, wheeze or dizziness.

Oxaliplatin · Eloxatin

Platinum chemotherapy. It links strands of DNA, making it harder for cells to copy them. Tell the team about numb fingers, falls or difficulty buttoning clothes. Report throat tightness or breathing difficulty immediately.

Fluorouracil · 5-FU

Fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy. It interferes with the building blocks cells need to make DNA and RNA. A pump gives it over 24 hours. Chest pain needs immediate assessment. Phone early for mouth ulcers, diarrhoea or painful hands and feet.

Folinic acid · Leucovorin

Chemotherapy modulator. It helps fluorouracil block a DNA-making enzyme for longer. It can increase the bowel and mouth effects of fluorouracil. Report a rash, swelling or wheeze during infusion.

Your infusion schedule

Before surgery

Four treatments two weeks apart. On day 1 you receive infusions and leave with a 24-hour fluorouracil pump. Your team disconnects it on day 2.

Surgery and recovery

The surgeon and oncology team review healing, infection, swallowing, fluid and food intake, weight and strength. The operation sets the timing of the next phase.

After surgery

Cycle 5 usually starts 6–12 weeks after surgery, once you have recovered. The team confirms the restart date. Four further cycles are planned two weeks apart if tolerated.

Common effects and what to do

Cold sensitivity and numbness

Cold drinks or cold air can trigger tingling after oxaliplatin. Use warm layers and room-temperature drinks. Report symptoms that persist between cycles.

Mouth, bowel and skin

Use the mouth care your unit recommends and report soreness, loose stools, painful palms or soles early. Phone if you cannot drink.

Fatigue and eating after surgery

Small frequent meals and drinks can be easier. Contact your dietitian if your weight falls or feeds are difficult. Ask about support for fatigue.

Serious effects: when to call

Fever or sudden illness

A low white cell count can allow infection to progress quickly. Call your 24-hour oncology number immediately for a fever at the threshold your unit gave you, chills or feeling suddenly unwell, even without a fever.

Chest pain or trouble breathing

Fluorouracil can affect the heart. During a drip alert the nurse immediately. At home seek urgent help. Call 112 or 999 for severe chest pain or breathing difficulty.

Severe early diarrhoea or mouth ulcers

Phone urgently if symptoms are severe, especially in the first cycle, or if you cannot drink. These may signal severe fluorouracil toxicity.

Pump or line trouble

Phone the unit promptly for swelling, pain, a wet dressing, leakage or a pump that finishes much earlier or later than planned. Follow your unit’s spill instructions.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Phone oncology immediately for fever, chills, sudden illness, severe diarrhoea, or a leaking pump. Call 112 or 999 for severe chest pain, collapse or severe breathing difficulty. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Docetaxel steroid tablets

Take prescribed dexamethasone beginning the day before each infusion. Bring the times and doses taken. If you miss a dose, ring before attending. The team reconciles this with anti-sickness steroids.

Nausea or vomiting

Follow your signed anti-sickness prescription. Contact the unit if vomiting continues or fluids will not stay down.

Infection prevention

After a fever with low white cells or a treatment delay from low white cells, your team may prescribe growth-factor injections. Confirm the dates with your team.

Fatigue and eating after surgery

Small frequent meals and drinks can be easier. Contact your dietitian if your weight falls or feeds are difficult. Ask about support for fatigue.

Planning your visits

Day before treatment

Check the steroid prescription and bring your medication list.

Connecting the pump

The day unit attaches your 24-hour fluorouracil pump to your line on day 1. Confirm where and when your trained team will disconnect it on day 2.

At home with the pump

Keep the line secure, dry and free of kinks. Follow your unit’s instructions for sleeping and washing. Leave disconnection and any change to the flow to your trained team.

Pump or line trouble

Phone the unit promptly for swelling, pain, a wet dressing, leakage or a pump that finishes much earlier or later than planned. Follow your unit’s spill instructions.

What the research found

In FLOT4, median survival was 50 months with perioperative FLOT and 35 months with older chemotherapy. In ESOPEC, 57.4% were alive at three years with FLOT versus 50.7% with preoperative chemoradiotherapy.

These trials compared whole treatment strategies. They cannot quantify the benefit of an individual postoperative cycle or predict one person’s outcome.
