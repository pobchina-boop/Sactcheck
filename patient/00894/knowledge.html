When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact your urology or oncology treatment team immediately for fever, chills, sudden illness, severe bladder pain, persistent bleeding or inability to pass urine. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or chest pain: call112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Treat selected high-risk bladder cancer when BCG has not worked, cannot be tolerated or is unsuitable.

Intravesical chemotherapy

Gemcitabine in the bladder

Gemcitabine in the bladder
Interferes with deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) copying in bladder-lining cells

Gemcitabine is placed directly in the bladder, held there and drained before docetaxel. Local treatment limits exposure elsewhere but does not guarantee that systemic effects cannot occur.

Treatment target↓Cancer-cell effect↓Healthy-tissue effects

What to watch for
Report fever, persistent bleeding, severe pain or inability to pass urine.

Sources: nccp

Intravesical chemotherapy

Docetaxel in the bladder

Docetaxel in the bladder
Disrupts the cell machinery needed for division

Docetaxel is instilled after gemcitabine has been drained. It acts on dividing cells in the bladder lining. Its dose and preparation are different from intravenous docetaxel.

Treatment target↓Cancer-cell effect↓Healthy-tissue effects

What to watch for
Do not substitute intravenous docetaxel instructions or steroid premedication for this bladder plan.

Individual prescribed course

Both medicines are given at each weekly induction visit for six weeks. Selected patients then receive treatment monthly for 18 months, beginning six weeks after the final induction dose. Maintenance depends on response and tolerability; it is not automatic.

Intravesical gemcitabine/docetaxel uses an unlicensed route and dose schedule in Ireland. The clinician must discuss this and follow hospital approval/consent policy.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the bicarbonate prescription and the fluid/diuretic plan. The source advises avoiding fluids for four hours before and until two hours after instillation, and avoiding alcohol/coffee around treatment. The team must adapt instructions for your medical conditions; do not change a prescribed diuretic yourself.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Gemcitabine1000 mg in50 mL is instilled through a urinary catheter and held for 60 minutes, then drained. Docetaxel40 mg in50 mL follows and is held for 60 minutes. The team may use90-minute dwell times. These are fixed bladder doses, not intravenous doses based on body size.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

A temporary urinary catheter is used; no routine intravenous line or take-home pump is needed. Obtain written instructions for passing urine, toilet hygiene, skin exposure and sexual/contraceptive precautions.

STEP 4

Home support

The source includes prescribed sodium bicarbonate1200 mg the night before and1200 mg one hour before treatment to reduce bladder irritation. Anti-sickness medicine may be considered if nausea followed a previous instillation. Pain or spasm medicines are individual prescriptions; ask before taking ibuprofen or other medicines.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Treatment reference · 04

Symptoms and help

Report new or worsening symptoms promptly.

Call now

Call your treatment team immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Sources: nccp

During catheter treatment

Tell the nurse immediately about severe pain, bleeding, catheter difficulty or an allergic reaction. The team may postpone instillation.

NCCP 00894 and sequential intravesical treatment evidence

Published multi-centre observational evidence supports sequential gemcitabine/docetaxel as a bladder-preserving salvage option after BCG. The NCCP regimen defines the Irish doses, sequence and selected maintenance plan.

Observational results do not establish equivalence to cystectomy or other options. Published schedules differ; follow the NCCP regimen and individual urology plan, with explicit discussion of unlicensed use.

Sources: trial · nccp

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Treat selected high-risk bladder cancer when BCG has not worked, cannot be tolerated or is unsuitable. This is a bladder-preserving option for selected patients. Cystoscopy remains necessary; high-grade recurrence or progression requires discussion of other options, including bladder removal where suitable.

When do I attend?

Both medicines are given at each weekly induction visit for six weeks. Selected patients then receive treatment monthly for 18 months, beginning six weeks after the final induction dose. Maintenance depends on response and tolerability; it is not automatic.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
