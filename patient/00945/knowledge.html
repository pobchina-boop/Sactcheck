When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Help control advanced urothelial cancer using a targeted chemotherapy-carrying antibody and immune treatment.

Antibody-drug conjugate (targeted chemotherapy)

Enfortumab vedotin

Padcev
Delivers a cell-division inhibitor to cells bearing nectin-4

An antibody attaches to a protein called nectin-4 and delivers a chemotherapy payload that disrupts cell division. Healthy skin, nerves and other tissues can also be affected. This is not a checkpoint immunotherapy medicine.

Nectin-4 attachment↓Payload delivered↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report any rash early, especially blistering, fever or mouth/eye/genital sores; also report thirst, frequent urination, numbness or new breathing symptoms.

Sources: nccp

Immunotherapy

Pembrolizumab

Keytruda
Releases an immune brake

PD-1 is a control switch on T cells, a type of immune cell. When it connects with PD-L1 or PD-L2, the T cell receives a signal to reduce its activity. Some cancers use this pathway to escape immune attack. Pembrolizumab is an antibody that blocks PD-1, helping T cells recognise and attack the cancer. It does not directly poison cancer cells like chemotherapy. Releasing this brake can also allow immune cells to attack healthy organs, which explains the different side-effect pattern and why symptoms can appear after treatment stops.

Immune cell↓Brake blocked↓Cancer cell

What to watch for
The immune system can also inflame healthy organs. New cough, diarrhoea, unusual weakness, severe headache, thirst or a spreading rash need prompt advice. Effects can start during treatment or after it finishes.

Individual prescribed course

Option 1: enfortumab on days 1 and8 plus pembrolizumab200 mg on day 1, repeating every 21 days. Option 2: enfortumab on days 1,8,22 and29 plus pembrolizumab400 mg on day 1, repeating every 42 days. Your team selects one option. The six-week reference shows two enfortumab blocks; pembrolizumab day 22 applies only to option 1.

This is the NCCP 00945 advanced-disease combination. It is distinct from28-day enfortumab monotherapy and from perioperative bladder treatment.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the chosen21-day or42-day schedule, glucose plan, skin/nerve baseline and both medicine alert cards.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Enfortumab is given first over 30 minutes; pembrolizumab follows over 30 minutes when both are due. Enfortumab starts at 1.25 mg/kg with a125 mg cap. Staff confirm your current dose and chosen pembrolizumab interval; pembrolizumab is not dose-reduced for toxicity.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

Suitable intravenous access is needed. There is no routine take-home pump. Report pain, redness or swelling at the infusion site immediately.

STEP 4

Home support

Follow the prescribed low-risk anti-sickness plan for enfortumab. Ask about moisturiser, skin care and artificial tears. After a prior enfortumab infusion reaction, the team may prescribe antihistamine, antipyretic and intravenous steroid before later infusions. Immune effects need assessment; do not treat new diarrhoea or breathlessness as routine without contacting the team.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

EV-302 randomised phase III study

In886 previously untreated patients, median overall survival was 31.5 months with the combination versus 16.1 months with platinum/gemcitabine (hazard ratio0.47;95% confidence interval0.38–0.58). Median progression-free survival was 12.5 versus 6.3 months.

These are results from the primary analysis, not a personal prognosis or a comparison with every possible treatment sequence. The current NCCP indication specifies eligibility for platinum-containing chemotherapy.

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Help control advanced urothelial cancer using a targeted chemotherapy-carrying antibody and immune treatment. Treatment continues while effective and tolerable. At24 months, stopping pembrolizumab may be considered for sustained response without progression, but this is not mandatory. Radiotherapy may help selected symptoms; the regimen does not automatically prescribe surgery or a perioperative pathway.

When do I attend?

Option 1: enfortumab on days 1 and8 plus pembrolizumab200 mg on day 1, repeating every 21 days. Option 2: enfortumab on days 1,8,22 and29 plus pembrolizumab400 mg on day 1, repeating every 42 days. Your team selects one option. The six-week reference shows two enfortumab blocks; pembrolizumab day 22 applies only to option 1.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
