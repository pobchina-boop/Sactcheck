When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new breathlessness, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea, dehydration, jaundice, unusual bleeding or sudden illness promptly. Do not wait for the next planned test.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

Control the cancer and symptoms, with repeated review of benefit and tolerance.

Oral HER2 / EGFR kinase inhibitor

Lapatinib

Blocks signals inside cells

Lapatinib inhibits HER2 and EGFR signalling inside cancer cells. It is used with capecitabine in selected previously treated HER2-positive advanced breast cancer.

Target in the cell↓Blocks signals inside cells↓Cancer growth disrupted

What to watch for
Report new or worsening symptoms promptly and follow the agreed contact plan.

Sources: nccp

Oral chemotherapy

Capecitabine

Xeloda
Becomes fluorouracil in the body

The body converts capecitabine into fluorouracil, which disrupts deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) and RNA processes needed for cell growth. Tablets are active chemotherapy; taking them at home still requires a clear monitoring and interruption plan.

Swallowed tablet↓Converted to fluorouracil↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Stop tablets and contact the team for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth ulcers, hand-foot soreness affecting activities, fever or dehydration. Chest pain needs urgent assessment. Do not restart until instructed.

Day 1–21: The current treatment cycle

Lapatinib once daily on Days 1–21, continuously. Capecitabine twice daily on Days 1–14, then no capecitabine on Days 15–21. Repeat every 21 days while beneficial and tolerated. The capecitabine rest week is not a lapatinib rest week. Your team confirms treatment dates and checks. Serious symptoms need advice on any day.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical tablet plan

Both anticancer medicines are taken by mouth. This regimen itself requires no infusion pump or port.

STEP 1

Before starting

Confirm DPD result, blood tests, cardiac assessment, medicines review and the two tablet schedules.

STEP 2

Collect and check the prescription

Confirm tablet strengths, doses, Days 1–14 for capecitabine and continuous lapatinib. Keep in the original packaging and out of reach of children.

STEP 3

Food and medicine timing

Take lapatinib at least one hour before or after food, consistently in the same relation to meals. Take capecitabine with food or within 30 minutes after eating. Swallow tablets whole. A pharmacist can help arrange the different food timings.

STEP 4

Before each new cycle

Keep blood and clinical-review appointments even though treatment is at home. Bring your medicine list and tell the team about missed or held doses.

Interactions

Check all prescription, over-the-counter and herbal medicines with the oncology pharmacist. Acid-reducing medicines can affect lapatinib absorption: NCCP specifies separation by at least two hours, while the product information cautions against acid-suppressing combinations. Do not start or stop a necessary acid medicine yourself; agree a plan. Avoid grapefruit and report any recent brivudine use.

Missed or vomited tablets

Do not make up or double a dose. Follow the written instructions and call if unsure.

If you already have a port

Ask the line team whether it is still needed and how it should be maintained or removed. Removal is a separate planned procedure.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

A clear tablet schedule

Take lapatinib at least one hour before or after food, consistently in the same relation to meals. Take capecitabine with food or within 30 minutes after eating. Swallow tablets whole. A pharmacist can help arrange the different food timings.

Nausea and bowel plan

These oral agents are classified as minimal-to-low emetic risk. Agree an individual symptom plan; a repeated intravenous (IV) chemotherapy antiemetic course is not automatically required.

Early symptoms matter

Call promptly for diarrhoea, dehydration, painful hands or feet, mouth ulcers or poor intake. New chest pain needs urgent assessment.

Before restarting a cycle

Review bloods, kidney and liver function, symptoms and the agreed doses. Do not restart tablets after a treatment hold without the team’s instructions.

Treatment reference · 01

Why this treatment?

This all-tablet combination may be considered when HER2-positive advanced cancer has progressed after previous treatment including an anthracycline, a taxane and trastuzumab in the metastatic setting. It blocks growth signals and uses chemotherapy through a different mechanism. Earlier medicines, cancer sites, kidney and liver health, tolerance and available alternatives affect the choice.

Heart ultrasound and ECG

NCCP requires baseline LVEF assessment in its cardiac precautions and echo/MUGA every 12 weeks (around cycle 5 if on time), or earlier for concern. EMA Tyverb specifies an ECG with QT before starting and again 1–2 weeks after starting, around Days 8–15 of cycle 1. Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Who arranges my tests?

Ask the team who books each test, who checks the result and who to contact if an appointment is overdue. This map does not book appointments.

What if I become unwell between visits?

Use the urgent contact plan for sudden illness, fever, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea or poor fluid intake. Do not wait for a scheduled blood test or scan.

Why was this treatment chosen?

This all-tablet combination may be considered when HER2-positive advanced cancer has progressed after previous treatment including an anthracycline, a taxane and trastuzumab in the metastatic setting. It blocks growth signals and uses chemotherapy through a different mechanism. Earlier medicines, cancer sites, kidney and liver health, tolerance and available alternatives affect the choice.

When is my next heart scan?

NCCP requires baseline LVEF assessment in its cardiac precautions and echo/MUGA every 12 weeks (around cycle 5 if on time), or earlier for concern. EMA Tyverb specifies an ECG with QT before starting and again 1–2 weeks after starting, around Days 8–15 of cycle 1. Correct low potassium or magnesium before treatment and arrange extra ECG checks for interacting medicines, symptoms or QT risk. This early ECG is separate from the 12-week echo.

How do other treatments fit?

Surgery or radiotherapy may be used for particular symptoms or disease sites. There is no routine surgery-then-radiation sequence for this advanced-cancer regimen. The oncology and radiation teams agree any treatment pause or overlap.

Do I need regular scans or markers?

Record baseline disease sites. Response imaging is generally reviewed every 2–4 months under international guidance, adapted to symptoms, disease pace and treatment. The team selects the scan; this is not an automatic CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order for every patient. Tumour markers may support assessment in selected cases, but do not alone decide progression or a treatment change.

Does the rest week apply to both medicines?

No. Days 15–21 are a capecitabine break; lapatinib continues each day unless your team tells you to hold it.
