TRAIN-2 with Phesgo

Six 21-day cycles before surgery: Phesgo and carboplatin on Day 1; paclitaxel on Days 1 and 8. There is no planned Day 15 paclitaxel. Surgery and pathology determine postoperative HER2 therapy.

Your treatment, at home.

Important medicine-specific effects

Paclitaxel

  • Low blood counts and infection
  • Numbness / tingling, sometimes lasting
  • Hair loss, muscle or joint pain
  • Infusion reaction
  • Mouth, bowel, skin and nail effects

Carboplatin

  • Low blood counts, infection and bleeding
  • Nausea, vomiting and fatigue
  • Allergic reaction, sometimes after previous exposure
  • Kidney or electrolyte changes

Pertuzumab

  • Diarrhoea and dehydration
  • Injection / allergic reaction
  • Heart pumping-function reduction
  • Rash and fatigue

Trastuzumab

  • Heart pumping-function reduction / heart failure
  • Injection or allergic reaction
  • Diarrhoea and fatigue
  • Rare serious lung effects

Make the plan practical

Antiemetic plan

Carboplatin Day 1 uses its high-risk antiemetic plan. Day 8 paclitaxel uses a separate low-risk plan.

Premedicines

First paclitaxel dose: steroid, antihistamine and H2 blocker. Later doses: steroid is not routine unless the consultant adds it; the other premedicines follow the reviewed plan.

Appointments and results

Echo before treatment and every 12 weeks (around cycle 5 if on time), including maintenance. Bloods before each cycle. Ask who books and reviews each test; report a missing appointment.

Phase transition

After surgery, your team reviews the pathology and chooses the next HER2 medicine. The usual plan aims to complete about one year of HER2 treatment from the first dose. Remaining invasive cancer can change the medicine offered. The selected treatment has its own blood tests and supportive medicines.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
