Your treatment reference

PLD for recurrent or advanced endometrial cancer

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin is being offered to slow the growth of recurrent or advanced endometrial cancer. Your team will review symptoms and scan results with you to decide whether to continue.

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin · PLD · Caelyx

Liposomal chemotherapy. Doxorubicin is carried inside tiny coated fat particles called liposomes. This changes how it circulates and its side-effect pattern. Heart injury remains possible; skin and mouth effects are especially important. Report painful palms / soles or mouth ulcers early. Keep heart-test appointments and report breathlessness, swelling or chest symptoms; tell staff immediately about an infusion reaction.

Common effects and what to do

Hands and feet

Tender red palms or soles can peel or blister. Ring before skin pain limits walking or everyday tasks.

Mouth and gut

Mouth ulcers, nausea and poor appetite can limit drinking. Contact the team early for pain relief and mouth care.

Serious effects: when to call

Blood counts and infection

Call immediately for fever, chills, bleeding or sudden illness.

Heart muscle

Report increasing breathlessness, swollen ankles or chest pain. Heart checks guide further doses.

Infusion and IV site

Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest tightness or burning around the cannula.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Fever, chills or sudden illness need an immediate call. Report new breathlessness, ankle swelling or chest pain promptly. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Before treatment

Bring your medicines list and details of earlier anthracycline treatment. Tell the team about heart disease or breathlessness.

Between visits

Avoid friction, heat and tight shoes if your palms or soles become tender. Call early for peeling skin or mouth pain that affects eating.

Urgent contact

Fever, chills or sudden illness need an immediate call. Report new breathlessness, ankle swelling or chest pain promptly.

Planning your visits

Appointments

One infusion on day 1 of each 28-day cycle. Six cycles are typical. Your consultant may continue treatment while it helps and side effects remain manageable.

At the day unit

The team uses the pegylated liposomal formulation and starts the first infusion slowly. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest tightness, back pain or breathing difficulty.

What to bring

Bring your medicines list and details of earlier anthracycline treatment. Tell the team about heart disease or breathlessness.

What the research found

Some participants with persistent or recurrent endometrial cancer had a partial response to PLD.

This small single-arm study cannot establish a survival advantage or predict an individual response.
