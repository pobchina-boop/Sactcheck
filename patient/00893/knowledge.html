When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology urgently for fever, sudden illness, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, persistent vomiting or inability to drink. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicine works

This treatment aims to slow cancer growth and help control symptoms. Your team explains the expected benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your situation.

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

Methotrexate

Intravenous chemotherapy
Blocks folate-dependent deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) production

Methotrexate interferes with folate-dependent reactions that cells need to make DNA. This can slow cancer-cell division, while also affecting blood-cell production and the lining of the mouth and bowel.

Folate pathway inhibited↓DNA building blocks reduced↓Cell division slowed

What to watch for
Tell the pharmacist about all painkillers, antibiotics, supplements and other medicines because interactions can increase methotrexate toxicity. Report new cough or breathlessness, mouth ulcers, unusual bleeding, jaundice, reduced urine or severe rash promptly.

Chemotherapy course

Repeat as prescribed

Methotrexate 40 mg/m² by intravenous bolus on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle, until progression or unacceptable toxicity. There is no scheduled Day-22 dose. The clinician may increase to 50 mg/m² only if there has been no mucositis.

The team reviews the cancer, side effects and your preferences. Scans follow your individual treatment plan; this regimen does not set one universal scan interval.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests, and chest X-ray. Review mouth symptoms, medicines, hydration and any substantial fluid collection around the lungs or abdomen.

STEP 2

Taking or receiving treatment

Methotrexate is given into a vein by the treatment team. This is an intermittent intravenous cancer regimen, not a daily tablet schedule. Check the written appointment calendar before each visit. Never take extra methotrexate yourself.

STEP 3

Tests and reviews

Repeat blood count, kidney and liver tests and urinalysis; examine the mouth and throat for mucosal changes during treatment. The team confirms the test timing before each planned dose and reviews recovery and previous dose delays.

STEP 4

Specific practical instructions

Tell the pharmacist about all painkillers, antibiotics, supplements and other medicines because interactions can increase methotrexate toxicity. Report new cough or breathlessness, mouth ulcers, unusual bleeding, jaundice, reduced urine or severe rash promptly.

STEP 5

Leaving with a clear plan

Confirm the next appointment, dose, support medicines and verified daytime and out-of-hours phone numbers.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Preventing and treating sickness

Methotrexate in this regimen is low sickness risk. Follow the prescribed prevention/rescue plan.

Medicine-specific support

Tell the pharmacist about all painkillers, antibiotics, supplements and other medicines because interactions can increase methotrexate toxicity. Report new cough or breathlessness, mouth ulcers, unusual bleeding, jaundice, reduced urine or severe rash promptly.

Mouth care, nutrition and daily life

Follow the unit’s mouth-care plan. Report pain, poor intake or weight loss early; ask for dietetic support. Pace activity, rest and work around symptoms.

Urgent symptoms

Call oncology urgently for fever, sudden illness, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, persistent vomiting or inability to drink. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure: call 112/999.

Medicine and interaction review

Bring all prescription, non-prescription and herbal medicines. Check with the oncology pharmacist before starting or stopping anything.
