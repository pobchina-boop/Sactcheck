Methotrexate: head and neck cancer

This treatment aims to slow cancer growth and help control symptoms. Your team explains the expected benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your situation.

Recurrent or advanced head and neck cancer · NCCP 00893 v1 · Document revised 14 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

Recurrent, advanced or metastatic head and neck squamous cell cancer in people with comorbidities or unsuitable for combination chemotherapy.

My overall treatment

intravenous (IV) on Days 1, 8 and 15 every 28 days

28day view

Treatment timing in this window

Methotrexate IVDay 1, 8, 15
  1. TreatmentMethotrexate 40 mg/m² by intravenous bolus on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle, until progression or unacceptable toxicity. There is no scheduled Day-22 dose. The clinician may increase to 50 mg/m² only if there has been no mucositis.
  2. ReviewThe team reviews the cancer, side effects and your preferences. Scans follow your individual treatment plan; this regimen does not set one universal scan interval.

Timing may change after review. Side effects can occur on any day; there are no guaranteed safe days.

My tests and checks

WhenWhat is checkedTiming / reason
BeforeBaseline preparationNCCPFull blood count, kidney and liver tests, and chest X-ray. Review mouth symptoms, medicines, hydration and any substantial fluid collection around the lungs or abdomen.
DuringSafety checksNCCPRepeat blood count, kidney and liver tests and urinalysis; examine the mouth and throat for mucosal changes during treatment. The team confirms the test timing before each planned dose and reviews recovery and previous dose delays.
ResponseCancer-specific checksIndividual specialist planBlood tests and urinalysis assess treatment safety. Scans and cancer-specific tests follow the consultant’s plan; no routine universal tumour-marker panel is specified.

Between treatments

  • Symptoms can occur on any day; use your written contact plan.
  • Tell the pharmacist about all painkillers, antibiotics, supplements and other medicines because interactions can increase methotrexate toxicity. Report new cough or breathlessness, mouth ulcers, unusual bleeding, jaundice, reduced urine or severe rash promptly.

Bring your medicines list, calendar and verified contacts.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
