IV vinorelbine · Days 1 and 8

Download this phase PDF · Open printable script

Active medicines: Pertuzumab / trastuzumab (Phesgo) + IV vinorelbine (HER2 treatment on Day 1 only, if still prescribed)

Emetogenic risk: Minimal · IV route

No routine prophylactic antiemetic order is populated for this phase. Agree an individual symptom-treatment plan where needed.

Additional phase-specific support

  • If HER2 therapy has stopped, the IV or oral vinorelbine plan may still apply, but remove the stopped antibodies from the prescription. Confirm the active components at every visit.
  • Confirm Days 1 and 8 only, active vinorelbine route, current counts and the actual prescribed dose. IV and oral doses are not interchangeable.
  • Assess constipation; agree prophylactic laxative or dietary support where appropriate. Severe pain, vomiting or no stool/wind requires urgent review.
  • Antibody premedication is usually unnecessary unless indicated by previous hypersensitivity; consider paracetamol/antihistamine per local policy.
  • No routine IV taxane steroid or carboplatin antiemetic course applies. An individual rescue plan may still be needed.
  • Clinical template for individual review, not an issued prescription. Confirm allergy, pregnancy precautions, interactions and local policy.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.

Oral vinorelbine · Days 1 and 8

Download this phase PDF · Open printable script

Active medicines: Pertuzumab / trastuzumab (Phesgo) + Oral vinorelbine (HER2 treatment on Day 1 only, if still prescribed)

Emetogenic risk: Oral moderate–high · NCCP oral classification

MedicineDose / routeWhen
Ondansetron (one 5-HT3 option)8–16 mg total orally per dayOn each prescribed oral vinorelbine treatment day; exact dose, timing and duration to be completedChoose ONE 5-HT3 regimen. Review QT, hepatic dosing, constipation and interactions; granisetron is an alternative, not an add-on.

Additional phase-specific support

  • If HER2 therapy has stopped, the IV or oral vinorelbine plan may still apply, but remove the stopped antibodies from the prescription. Confirm the active components at every visit.
  • Confirm Days 1 and 8 only, active vinorelbine route, current counts and the actual prescribed dose. IV and oral doses are not interchangeable.
  • Assess constipation; agree prophylactic laxative or dietary support where appropriate. Severe pain, vomiting or no stool/wind requires urgent review.
  • Antibody premedication is usually unnecessary unless indicated by previous hypersensitivity; consider paracetamol/antihistamine per local policy.
  • NCCP oral-agent guidance recommends a 5-HT3 antagonist: ondansetron 8–16 mg total daily OR granisetron 1–2 mg total daily. Confirm the intermittent Day 1/8 prescription; do not automatically give a daily 21-day course.
  • Do not copy the minimal IV vinorelbine classification to the oral route. Do not automatically copy a high-risk IV NK1/steroid schedule.
  • Swallow capsules whole with water and some food. No extra dose after vomiting; contact the team.
  • Source distinction: NCCP regimen supportive section labels vinorelbine minimal without separating routes; NCCP2025 nausea/vomiting classification Table8 places oral vinorelbine in moderate–high and Table5 gives oral prophylaxis.
  • Clinical template for individual review, not an issued prescription. Confirm allergy, pregnancy precautions, interactions and local policy.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
