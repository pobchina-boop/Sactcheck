How your medicines work

HER2-targeted antibody

Pertuzumab

Blocks HER2 pairing with other receptors
HER2 pairing→Pertuzumab blocks pairing→Growth signals reduced

Pertuzumab binds a different area of HER2 from trastuzumab. It limits receptor pairing, so the two antibodies act together at different points on the same growth pathway.

Keep heart-scan appointments. Report diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling or a treatment reaction.

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

Targets HER2 growth signals
HER2 on cell surface→Antibody binds→Growth signalling reduced

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

Keep heart-scan appointments. Report diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling or a treatment reaction.

Vinca-alkaloid chemotherapy

Vinorelbine

Disrupts cell division
Target in the cell→Disrupts cell division→Cancer growth disrupted

Vinorelbine interferes with the microtubules that cells use to divide. It can also affect blood cells, nerves and bowel movement. The IV and oral doses are different; your prescription specifies one route.

Report new or worsening symptoms promptly and follow the agreed contact plan.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Phesgo on Day 1; vinorelbine on Days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle. Vinorelbine is prescribed by IV OR by mouth, not both. There is no planned Day 15 vinorelbine. Continue while beneficial and tolerated; any antibody-only phase is separately prescribed.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm blood results, cardiac checks, active phase, previous dual HER2 dose and the consent discussion.

STEP 2

The thigh injection

Loading/reloading 1200 mg/600 mg over 8 minutes with 30 minutes observation; maintenance 600 mg/600 mg over 5 minutes with 15 minutes observation. Complete the observation before chemotherapy. Alternate thighs; avoid sore, bruised or damaged skin.

STEP 3

Chemotherapy and access

After Phesgo observation, receive the prescribed IV vinorelbine or follow the separately agreed oral dose plan. Phesgo itself needs no IV line; the oral vinorelbine option also avoids an infusion. IV vinorelbine still needs a cannula, PICC or port. No take-home infusion pump is routinely used.

STEP 4

Leaving the unit

Confirm supportive medicines, next visits and due tests. Injection time is shorter than the whole appointment; preparation, assessment and observation still take time.

Switching / a missed appointment

The team checks the last dual HER2 dose, including IV treatment: a gap of less than 6 weeks uses the maintenance dose; 6 weeks or longer needs reloading. A first Phesgo visit after IV treatment does not automatically require a loading dose.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Oral vinorelbine

Oral vinorelbine is taken only on the prescribed Days 1 and 8, with some food. Swallow capsules whole with water; do not chew, suck, open or dissolve them. Do not use a damaged capsule. Do not take an extra dose after vomiting; contact the team. Dose increases require a new prescription and blood-count review.

Support around treatment

Route-specific support

Oral vinorelbine needs a separate nausea plan; do not copy an IV-only minimal-risk template. Confirm anti-sickness treatment, constipation prevention and blood checks before each prescribed dose.

Tests before treatment

FBC, kidney and liver profile at baseline and before every 21-day cycle. The team reviews the necessary results before a planned dose. Confirm the blood-count check needed before each additional vinorelbine or paclitaxel dose; a Day 1 result does not automatically authorise a later dose. For oral vinorelbine, the product information requires blood counts on the day of each administration, including Day 8; the regimen source lists full blood/renal/liver checks before each cycle.

Keep heart checks on the plan

An echo every 12 weeks is separate from blood tests or cancer imaging. Ask who books and reviews it.

Plan for symptoms

Keep your agreed contact numbers and instructions. New symptoms can need assessment before a routine appointment.
