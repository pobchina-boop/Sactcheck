When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new breathlessness, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea, dehydration, jaundice, unusual bleeding or sudden illness promptly. Do not wait for the next planned test.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

What each medicine does

HER2-targeted antibody

Pertuzumab

HER2-targeted antibody
Blocks HER2 pairing with other receptors

Pertuzumab binds a different area of HER2 from trastuzumab. It limits receptor pairing, so the two antibodies act together at different points on the same growth pathway.

HER2 pairing↓Pertuzumab blocks pairing↓Growth signals reduced

What to watch for
Keep heart-scan appointments. Report diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling or a treatment reaction.

Sources: nccp

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

HER2-targeted antibody
Targets HER2 growth signals

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

HER2 on cell surface↓Antibody binds↓Growth signalling reduced

What to watch for
Keep heart-scan appointments. Report diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling or a treatment reaction.

Sources: nccp

Vinca-alkaloid chemotherapy

Vinorelbine

Disrupts cell division

Vinorelbine interferes with the microtubules that cells use to divide. It can also affect blood cells, nerves and bowel movement. The IV and oral doses are different; your prescription specifies one route.

Target in the cell↓Disrupts cell division↓Cancer growth disrupted

What to watch for
Report new or worsening symptoms promptly and follow the agreed contact plan.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Phesgo on Day 1; vinorelbine on Days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle. Vinorelbine is prescribed by IV OR by mouth, not both. There is no planned Day 15 vinorelbine. Continue while beneficial and tolerated; any antibody-only phase is separately prescribed.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm blood results, cardiac checks, active phase, previous dual HER2 dose and the consent discussion.

STEP 2

The thigh injection

Loading/reloading 1200 mg/600 mg over 8 minutes with 30 minutes observation; maintenance 600 mg/600 mg over 5 minutes with 15 minutes observation. Complete the observation before chemotherapy. Alternate thighs; avoid sore, bruised or damaged skin.

STEP 3

Chemotherapy and access

After Phesgo observation, receive the prescribed IV vinorelbine or follow the separately agreed oral dose plan. Phesgo itself needs no IV line; the oral vinorelbine option also avoids an infusion. IV vinorelbine still needs a cannula, PICC or port. No take-home infusion pump is routinely used.

STEP 4

Leaving the unit

Confirm supportive medicines, next visits and due tests. Injection time is shorter than the whole appointment; preparation, assessment and observation still take time.

Switching / a missed appointment

The team checks the last dual HER2 dose, including IV treatment: a gap of less than 6 weeks uses the maintenance dose; 6 weeks or longer needs reloading. A first Phesgo visit after IV treatment does not automatically require a loading dose.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Oral vinorelbine

Oral vinorelbine is taken only on the prescribed Days 1 and 8, with some food. Swallow capsules whole with water; do not chew, suck, open or dissolve them. Do not use a damaged capsule. Do not take an extra dose after vomiting; contact the team. Dose increases require a new prescription and blood-count review.

Treatment reference · 01

Why this treatment?

This NCCP option is for HER2-positive metastatic or unresectable locally recurrent cancer when taxane chemotherapy is clinically unsuitable and no chemotherapy or HER2 treatment has yet been given for metastatic disease. Phesgo combines two HER2 antibodies in one thigh injection. Vinorelbine provides a different chemotherapy option; its route and side effects need a separate discussion.

IV or oral vinorelbine: know your route

The two routes use different doses. Your team chooses one; never substitute milligram for milligram.

Oral vinorelbine

IV vinorelbine

Staff give this through a checked IV line. Tell them immediately about pain, burning or swelling. It must not be self-injected.

A bowel plan

Vinorelbine may slow bowel movement. Discuss prevention, any prescribed laxative and when to call; severe pain with vomiting or no stool/wind needs urgent assessment.

When scans, heart checks and bloods happen

Blood tests

Sources: nccp · vinorelbine-oral

Heart ultrasound and ECG

Check heart pumping function before starting HER2 treatment. Repeat echo or MUGA every 12 weeks: around cycle 5, then cycle 9 if 21-day cycles stay on time. Earlier checks are needed for symptoms or abnormal findings. Count elapsed time from the first HER2 dose; changing phase or route does not restart the clock. After the first year, ESC guidance allows six-monthly echo in selected asymptomatic low- or moderate-risk metastatic patients with normal assessments, only under an agreed team plan. Entering maintenance alone does not justify reducing checks.

Sources: nccp · herceptin · esc

Scans and tumour markers

Record baseline disease sites. Response imaging is generally reviewed every 2–4 months under international guidance, adapted to symptoms, disease pace and treatment. Tumour markers may support assessment in selected cases, but do not alone decide progression or a treatment change.

Bone health and endocrine treatment

If the cancer is also hormone-receptor positive, endocrine treatment may form part of the wider plan. Bone-density testing is linked to aromatase inhibitors, ovarian suppression and fracture risk; it is not automatically required because a HER2 medicine is prescribed. Questions worth asking

Who arranges my tests?

Ask the team who books each test, who checks the result and who to contact if an appointment is overdue. This map does not book appointments.

What if I become unwell between visits?

Use the urgent contact plan for sudden illness, fever, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea or poor fluid intake. Do not wait for a scheduled blood test or scan.

Why was this treatment chosen?

This NCCP option is for HER2-positive metastatic or unresectable locally recurrent cancer when taxane chemotherapy is clinically unsuitable and no chemotherapy or HER2 treatment has yet been given for metastatic disease. Phesgo combines two HER2 antibodies in one thigh injection. Vinorelbine provides a different chemotherapy option; its route and side effects need a separate discussion.

When is my next heart scan?

Check heart pumping function before starting HER2 treatment. Repeat echo or MUGA every 12 weeks: around cycle 5, then cycle 9 if 21-day cycles stay on time. Earlier checks are needed for symptoms or abnormal findings. Count elapsed time from the first HER2 dose; changing phase or route does not restart the clock. How do other treatments fit?

Surgery or radiotherapy may be used for particular symptoms or disease sites. There is no routine surgery-then-radiation sequence for this advanced-cancer regimen. The oncology and radiation teams agree any treatment pause or overlap.

Do I need regular scans or markers?

Record baseline disease sites. Response imaging is generally reviewed every 2–4 months under international guidance, adapted to symptoms, disease pace and treatment. The team selects the scan; this is not an automatic CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order for every patient. Tumour markers may support assessment in selected cases, but do not alone decide progression or a treatment change.

Is vinorelbine a daily tablet?

No. This regimen uses vinorelbine on Days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle only, whether IV or oral. Oral vinorelbine is taken only on the prescribed Days 1 and 8, with some food. Swallow capsules whole with water; do not chew, suck, open or dissolve them. Do not use a damaged capsule. Do not take an extra dose after vomiting; contact the team.
