Your practical treatment plan

Know the next step.

Taking treatment

The usual starting dose is 80 mg by mouth once daily, every day without a planned rest week. Use the dose on your prescription. Advanced treatment continues while helping and tolerated; adjuvant treatment is planned for up to three years, with earlier stopping if cancer returns or side effects become unacceptable.

Take at the same time each day, with or without food. Swallow whole with water; do not crush, split or chew. If you forget a dose, take it when remembered unless less than 12 hours remain before the next dose, when it should be skipped. Confirm the instruction with your pharmacist if the next dose is exactly 12 hours away. Do not take an extra dose without advice if you vomit. Ask for the approved water-dispersion instructions if swallowing is difficult.

Checks and follow-up

Confirm the relevant EGFR test, treatment setting and prescription. Adjuvant treatment needs complete resection and the specific stage/mutation criteria. Blood count, kidney, liver and bone profile; ECG before treatment. Consider an echocardiogram or MUGA LVEF assessment for cardiac risks or symptoms; review all medicines, pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding. Repeat blood count, kidney, liver and bone profile at the agreed reviews; extra checks may be needed for symptoms or abnormal results. NCCP specifies ECG at baseline and cycle 2, then cycle 3 onwards if clinically indicated. The team translates cycle 2 into a booked review; the tablets remain daily, without a treatment break. LVEF assessment if clinically indicated; ECG and electrolytes with cardiac/QT risks. Scans and disease checks follow the Consultant plan.

Support between reviews

Skin and nails

Agree an EGFR-inhibitor skin-care plan from the start and report rash or painful nail changes early. Blistering, peeling or mucosal sores need urgent assessment.

Bowel symptoms and hydration

Report diarrhoea early, follow the prescribed symptom plan and maintain fluids if able. Severe diarrhoea, weakness or reduced urine needs urgent advice.

Nausea medicines

Osimertinib has minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Routine infusion premedication is not usually needed. Agree rescue medicines individually, including QT and interaction review; do not copy a chemotherapy antiemetic course.

Medicine interactions

Tell the team about all medicines and supplements. Do not take St John’s wort. Strong enzyme-inducing medicines and medicines that affect heart rhythm require pharmacy review before changes.

Pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding

Avoid pregnancy during treatment. Effective contraception continues for at least two months after the final dose for women and four months for men. Osimertinib may reduce hormonal contraceptive effectiveness; discuss a suitable method. Do not breastfeed during treatment. Discuss fertility and plans for pregnancy before starting.
