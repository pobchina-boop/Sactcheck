NCCP 00353 · version 5

Osimertinib monotherapy

The usual starting dose is 80 mg by mouth once daily, every day without a planned rest week. Use the dose on your prescription. Advanced treatment continues while helping and tolerated; adjuvant treatment is planned for up to three years, with earlier stopping if cancer returns or side effects become unacceptable.

Purpose, setting and alternatives

Osimertinib blocks abnormal EGFR growth signals in selected lung cancers. After complete surgery, treatment aims to lower the risk of cancer returning. In advanced cancer, it aims to control cancer and delay progression. Confirm which aim applies to you. This guide covers NCCP 00353 osimertinib on its own: T790M-positive advanced disease, first-line activating-EGFR advanced disease, or completely resected stage IB–IIIA Ex19del/L858R adjuvant treatment. The newer licensed post-chemoradiation setting and osimertinib with platinum/pemetrexed need their own applicable plan; their study results and support medicines are not added to this monotherapy suite.

Material risks and support

  • Diarrhoea and mouth soreness
  • Rash, dry skin and nail changes
  • Low blood counts
  • Serious lung inflammation
  • Heart rhythm changes and reduced heart function
  • Rare serious skin reactions and aplastic anaemia
  • Eye inflammation

Discuss adjuvant versus advanced intent, expected benefit and uncertainty, alternatives including not proceeding, common skin/bowel effects, serious lung/heart/skin/blood problems, fertility and pregnancy, daily tablets, monitoring and your right to reconsider.

Monitoring and understanding

Confirm the relevant EGFR test, treatment setting and prescription. Adjuvant treatment needs complete resection and the specific stage/mutation criteria. Blood count, kidney, liver and bone profile; ECG before treatment. Consider an echocardiogram or MUGA LVEF assessment for cardiac risks or symptoms; review all medicines, pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding. Repeat blood count, kidney, liver and bone profile at the agreed reviews; extra checks may be needed for symptoms or abnormal results. NCCP specifies ECG at baseline and cycle 2, then cycle 3 onwards if clinically indicated. The team translates cycle 2 into a booked review; the tablets remain daily, without a treatment break. LVEF assessment if clinically indicated; ECG and electrolytes with cardiac/QT risks. Scans and disease checks follow the Consultant plan.
