Understand the treatment. Keep the plan manageable.

This oral chemotherapy aims to control colorectal cancer that has spread after earlier treatments, or when those treatments are unsuitable. It combines two ingredients in one medicine. Your team discusses the likely benefit, uncertainty, alternatives and your priorities.

Oral cytotoxic chemotherapy

Trifluridine/tipiracil

Damages cancer-cell DNA while slowing breakdown of the active drug
Trifluridine enters DNA↓Tipiracil slows drug breakdown↓Cancer-cell growth is disrupted

Trifluridine is incorporated into DNA and interferes with cell growth. Tipiracil slows its breakdown so more trifluridine can reach the bloodstream. Healthy cells can also be affected; blood counts and symptoms need monitoring.

Check the exact dosing and rest days. Report fever, sudden illness, vomiting, diarrhoea, bleeding or reduced urine promptly.

Support throughout treatment

A written tablet calendar

Swallow the prescribed tablets with water within one hour after finishing breakfast and the evening meal on dosing days. Doses are based on body size and previous side effects; two tablet strengths may be combined. Some prescriptions have different morning and evening tablet quantities. Use the pharmacy calendar and do not calculate doses yourself.

Prevent and treat sickness

This oral medicine has moderate-to-high sickness risk. Agree a preventive and rescue anti-sickness plan with the team. If you cannot keep fluids or medicines down, call; do not add or repeat doses yourself.

Food, fluids and bowel symptoms

Take tablets after meals as instructed. Drink as your team advises and report diarrhoea, repeated vomiting, poor intake or reduced urine early. A prescribed bowel treatment plan does not replace urgent assessment.

Blood tests and infection

Blood count, kidney/liver tests and urine-protein checks occur before treatment and each cycle; extra tests may be needed, especially with kidney impairment. Report fever or illness even on rest days.

Pregnancy prevention

Discuss fertility before treatment. Women need highly effective contraception during treatment and for six months after; add a barrier method to hormonal contraception. Men with a partner who could become pregnant need effective contraception during treatment and for six months afterwards. Do not breastfeed while taking this medicine.

Your practical treatment guide

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Call your oncology team immediately for a temperature of 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell. Infection can occur without a fever. Call urgently for bleeding, severe diarrhoea, repeated vomiting, inability to drink, very little urine or new breathlessness. Severe breathing difficulty, heavy bleeding or collapse needs 112 / 999.
