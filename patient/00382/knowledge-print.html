When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team immediately for a temperature of 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell. Infection can occur without a fever. Call urgently for bleeding, severe diarrhoea, repeated vomiting, inability to drink, very little urine or new breathlessness. Severe breathing difficulty, heavy bleeding or collapse needs 112 / 999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Two ingredients in one chemotherapy tablet

This oral chemotherapy aims to control colorectal cancer that has spread after earlier treatments, or when those treatments are unsuitable. It combines two ingredients in one medicine. Your team discusses the likely benefit, uncertainty, alternatives and your priorities.

Oral cytotoxic chemotherapy

Trifluridine/tipiracil

Lonsurf
Damages cancer-cell deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) while slowing breakdown of the active drug

Trifluridine is incorporated into DNA and interferes with cell growth. Tipiracil slows its breakdown so more trifluridine can reach the bloodstream. Healthy cells can also be affected; blood counts and symptoms need monitoring.

What to watch for
Check the exact dosing and rest days. Report fever, sudden illness, vomiting, diarrhoea, bleeding or reduced urine promptly.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Prescription and supply

Before collecting another course, confirm the dose, tablet strengths, calendar, blood-test date and authorisation. Bring the current packs and medicines list.

STEP 2

Meal routine

Swallow the prescribed tablets with water within one hour after finishing breakfast and the evening meal on dosing days. Doses are based on body size and previous side effects; two tablet strengths may be combined. Some prescriptions have different morning and evening tablet quantities. Use the pharmacy calendar and do not calculate doses yourself.

STEP 3

Missed or vomited doses

Do not make up a missed, held or vomited dose and do not double the next dose. Tell your team and follow the next scheduled dose on the agreed calendar. Repeated vomiting needs advice because of dehydration and missed medicines.

STEP 4

Appointments

Ask who books extra tests, who reviews results and whom to call if the plan is unclear. There is no infusion visit or take-home pump in monotherapy.

STEP 5

Swallowing and handling

Swallow tablets whole with water and wash your hands after handling them. If you cannot swallow tablets whole, ask the pharmacist before changing them. Keep them out of children’s reach and ask the pharmacist about safe disposal.

STEP 6

Accidental extra tablets

If you take more Lonsurf than prescribed, contact a doctor or go to hospital straight away. Take the packs and current calendar with you.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

A written tablet calendar

Swallow the prescribed tablets with water within one hour after finishing breakfast and the evening meal on dosing days. Doses are based on body size and previous side effects; two tablet strengths may be combined. Some prescriptions have different morning and evening tablet quantities. Use the pharmacy calendar and do not calculate doses yourself.

Prevent and treat sickness

This oral medicine has moderate-to-high sickness risk. Agree a preventive and rescue anti-sickness plan with the team. If you cannot keep fluids or medicines down, call; do not add or repeat doses yourself.

Food, fluids and bowel symptoms

Take tablets after meals as instructed. Drink as your team advises and report diarrhoea, repeated vomiting, poor intake or reduced urine early. A prescribed bowel treatment plan does not replace urgent assessment.

Blood tests and infection

Blood count, kidney/liver tests and urine-protein checks occur before treatment and each cycle; extra tests may be needed, especially with kidney impairment. Report fever or illness even on rest days.

Pregnancy prevention

Discuss fertility before treatment. Women need highly effective contraception during treatment and for six months after; add a barrier method to hormonal contraception. Men with a partner who could become pregnant need effective contraception during treatment and for six months afterwards. Do not breastfeed while taking this medicine.

What this treatment is for

Check the treatment aim and exact regimen.

Colorectal monotherapy

This oral chemotherapy aims to control colorectal cancer that has spread after earlier treatments, or when those treatments are unsuitable. It combines two ingredients in one medicine. Your team discusses the likely benefit, uncertainty, alternatives and your priorities.

Separate licensed uses

This guide covers Lonsurf on its own for previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer under NCCP 00382a. It does not cover gastric cancer or Lonsurf with bevacizumab. Confirm the exact regimen on your prescription.

How long it continues

Cycles continue while treatment is helping and side effects are acceptable. There is no fixed total number of cycles. Blood results, symptoms, scans and your preferences guide continuation, delays or stopping.

Treatment reference · 03

Tests and dose review

The team authorises each new cycle.

Baseline and each cycle

Blood count, kidney and liver tests and urine-protein screening are required. Additional blood counts may be needed during treatment, especially with kidney impairment.

Why a new cycle may wait

The blood-count requirements for starting another cycle are different from the thresholds for stopping tablets during a cycle. Symptoms, prior severe low counts, time to recover and the current prescription all matter.

Dose changes

Your team may delay treatment or use a lower dose after toxicity. Do not restart from a diary or increase the dose after a reduction. Tablet quantities are checked by pharmacy.

Treatment reference · 05

Safe handling and individual needs

Share any difficulties before the next prescription.

Swallowing and handling

Swallow tablets whole with water and wash your hands after handling them. If you cannot swallow tablets whole, ask the pharmacist before changing them. Keep them out of children’s reach and ask the pharmacist about safe disposal.

Accidental extra tablets

If you take more Lonsurf than prescribed, contact a doctor or go to hospital straight away. Take the packs and current calendar with you.

Lactose and rare inherited conditions

Lonsurf contains lactose. Tell the prescriber about rare inherited galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency or glucose-galactose malabsorption before treatment; these conditions affect suitability. If you have been told you cannot tolerate some sugars, ask the pharmacist.

Additional blood tests

Extra blood-count tests may be needed during a cycle, particularly with kidney impairment or symptoms. The NCCP regimen requires tests before every cycle or as indicated; it does not specify a mandatory Day 15 test for everyone. Follow your team’s individual appointment schedule.

After stopping treatment

Ask when unresolved symptoms or abnormal blood tests will be checked again, who remains your contact and what the next treatment or supportive-care plan is. Continue the prescribed contraception precautions after the last dose.

Evidence for colorectal monotherapy

RECOURSE compared Lonsurf plus supportive care with placebo plus supportive care in previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer.

The trial found longer survival with Lonsurf than placebo in this selected population. A group result cannot predict an individual benefit. Gastric treatment and Lonsurf with bevacizumab are different evidence contexts.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Does having more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably show whether the cancer is responding. Report them so they can be treated. Your team assesses benefit using the tests and review plan appropriate to your cancer.

Why might a dose be delayed or reduced?

Your team may allow recovery, investigate a symptom or reduce the chance of lasting harm. Ask which drug changes, why, and when review is planned. A delay does not itself mean treatment has failed.

What should I say when calling?

Give the regimen name, last treatment date, symptoms and when they started. Explain whether you can eat, drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Give your temperature if available, but do not delay calling to collect details.

What belongs in the consent conversation?

The individual aim and likely benefit, uncertainty, reasonable alternatives including not proceeding, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and practical concerns. You can ask for an interpreter, more time or another explanation and can reconsider your decision.

How many cycles should I use this passport for?

The passport provides reusable recording pages, not a prescribed course length. Your oncology team sets the duration and can provide extra pages.

Can I use this page to decide if blood results are safe?

No. Treatment decisions require the clinical protocol, symptoms, previous toxicity, organ function and the prescriber’s review. The patient page explains the checks and does not authorise treatment.

What if I miss a dose or vomit?

Do not make up a missed, held or vomited dose and do not double the next dose. Tell your team and follow the next scheduled dose on the agreed calendar. Repeated vomiting needs advice because of dehydration and missed medicines.

Are tablet-free days safe days?

No. The rest period allows recovery, but low blood counts and other problems can occur then too. Use the same urgent contact plan throughout the cycle.

Can my morning and evening tablet numbers differ?

Yes. The pharmacy may prescribe different morning and evening quantities to match the correct total daily dose. Do not assume the same number or substitute tablet strengths; follow the written label and calendar.

Is the bevacizumab combination evidence the same?

No. A study of Lonsurf plus bevacizumab tests an additional medicine and its extra risks. Its results should not be presented as the expected benefit of Lonsurf on its own.

Try explaining the plan back: Can you show the two five-day dosing blocks and the rest days, explain how you take tablets with meals, what you do after a missed or vomited dose, and how you get urgent help?
