My treatment handbook

Weekly paclitaxel with three-weekly carboplatin

Your treatment and how the medicines work

The team chooses this weekly schedule to reduce recurrence risk after surgery or to treat advanced disease.

Paclitaxel · Paclitaxel

Taxane chemotherapy. It disrupts cell division. Hair follicles, blood cells and nerves can also be affected. Report numbness and tell the nurse immediately if you flush or wheeze during treatment.

Carboplatin · Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. It damages DNA in dividing cells. Kidney function helps determine the prescribed amount. Report fever, bleeding, hearing changes or an infusion reaction.

What the research found

In the US randomised study, weekly paclitaxel did not significantly prolong time before cancer progression in the overall study population compared with three-weekly paclitaxel.

Bevacizumab use varied. This result does not predict which schedule will suit an individual.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1

Paclitaxel and carboplatin together.

Days 8 and 15

Paclitaxel alone on both visits. Repeat the cycle every 21 days. The planned course is 3–6 cycles after surgery or six in advanced disease, if suitable.

Common effects and what to do

Hair and scalp · Paclitaxel

Hair loss is likely. Ask the unit about scalp cooling or wig support before treatment.

Nerves · Paclitaxel

Report tingling, numb toes, poor balance or difficulty with buttons before the next dose.

Nausea and vomiting · Chemotherapy

Use your prescribed anti-sickness plan. Call if vomiting continues or you cannot drink.

Muscles and joints · Paclitaxel

Tell the team about aches that limit walking or interrupt sleep.

Serious effects: when to call

Infusion reaction · Infused medicines

Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, wheeze or chest tightness during treatment.

Nerves · Paclitaxel

Report tingling, numb toes, poor balance or difficulty with buttons before the next dose.

Infection or bleeding · Chemotherapy

Fever, shivering, unexplained bleeding or suddenly feeling unwell? Call the oncology team now.

Kidneys · Carboplatin

Tell the team if you pass much less urine or cannot keep fluids down. Kidney checks guide treatment.

Nausea and vomiting · Chemotherapy

Use your prescribed anti-sickness plan. Call if vomiting continues or you cannot drink.

Hearing · Carboplatin

Report new ringing in your ears or reduced hearing.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Fever, shaking chills, unusual bleeding or sudden illness? Call the oncology unit now. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Planning your visits

At the day unit

Paclitaxel runs over about an hour each week. Carboplatin follows it on day 1 only. You still attend on days 8 and 15.

If a visit changes

Your team will tell you if a visit moves after a delay. Do not work out a new date yourself.

Before starting treatment

Discuss fertility, contraception and breastfeeding with your team. Bring your current medicines and details of previous infusion reactions.

Preparing for your oncology discussion

  • Which visits involve paclitaxel alone, and when should you report numbness or fever? Talk through this with your nurse and write the answer on your appointment sheet.
  • Who can I contact outside clinic hours? Ask the team to fill in your wallet card with local numbers.
