Active medicines: Cyclophosphamide tablets Days 1–14 + Methotrexate and fluorouracil IV Days 1 and 8

Emetogenic risk: Moderate–high oral cyclophosphamide; IV agents low

MedicineDose / routeWhen
Ondansetron8–16 mg total orally per day; prescriber selects exact doseStart before SACT and continue daily through the oral-treatment course; confirm stop dayNCCP oral Table 5. Review QT, constipation and hepatic dosing. Reconcile the same daily total on IV Days 1 and 8; do not add a duplicate IV-day order.

Additional phase-specific support

  • Confirm allergy/interaction review, blood tests and the source-specific renal/hepatic assessment before prescribing.
  • Confirm DPD status before first fluorouracil and review partial deficiency with the consultant.
  • Confirm an individual hydration and bladder-symptom plan; adapt fluid advice to cardiac/renal restrictions.
  • Review pleural effusion or ascites because methotrexate can accumulate in third-space fluid.
  • NCCP oral guidance uses a daily 5-HT3 antagonist. Do not automatically apply the single-day parenteral high-risk/AC steroid schedule to this 14-day oral course.
  • Specify rescue medicine and duration, and reconcile prophylaxis for two days after multiday SACT under the local plan.
  • Cyclophosphamide tablets are Days 1–14 only; give clear missed/vomited-dose instructions. Methotrexate is IV in this regimen.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
