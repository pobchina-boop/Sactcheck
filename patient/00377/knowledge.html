When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology promptly for fever, shivering, unusual bleeding, severe mouth or bowel symptoms, inability to drink, reduced urine, new breathlessness or a painful/leaking line. Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

After surgery: reduce recurrence risk. In metastatic disease: control cancer and symptoms. Your team confirms which setting applies.

Alkylating chemotherapy

Cyclophosphamide

Tablets on Days 1–14 only
Damages DNA needed for growth

Cyclophosphamide is activated in the body and damages DNA. This helps stop dividing cancer cells but can also affect blood-cell production and reproductive tissues. In this regimen it is the only take-home chemotherapy tablet; methotrexate and fluorouracil are given in the unit.

Drug activated↓DNA damage↓Cell division affected

What to watch for
Use the prescribed anti-sickness and hydration plan. Report fever, painful urination or blood in the urine.

Sources: nccp

Antifolate chemotherapy

Methotrexate

IV injection in CMF
Interferes with folate-dependent DNA production

Methotrexate reduces the supply of building blocks needed to make DNA. Kidney function affects its clearance, and fluid collections can prolong exposure. In CMF it is given intravenously by the unit; it is not a take-home weekly tablet.

Folate pathway blocked↓DNA building blocks reduced↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Report mouth ulcers, diarrhoea, poor intake, reduced urine, new cough or breathlessness. Ask before starting medicines or supplements.

Sources: nccp

Antimetabolite chemotherapy

Fluorouracil (5-FU)

5-FU by IV injection on the specified CMF days
Disrupts DNA and RNA production

Fluorouracil interferes with DNA and RNA production. In CMF it is given by injection in the unit. DPD testing helps identify increased toxicity risk; it does not exclude all serious reactions.

Cell building blocks disrupted↓DNA / RNA affected↓Growth reduced

What to watch for
Report severe diarrhoea, mouth ulcers or inability to drink promptly. New chest pain needs urgent assessment.

Sources: nccp

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Cyclophosphamide tablets on Days 1–14, with IV methotrexate and fluorouracil on Days 1 and 8 of each 28-day cycle. Six cycles for adjuvant treatment; metastatic treatment continues while beneficial and tolerated.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Blood tests, medicines review and the consent discussion come first. Confirm DPD testing before fluorouracil and review methotrexate kidney clearance.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Staff check the line, give prescribed premedicines and treatment, and observe for reactions. Tell them immediately about burning, pain, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

Home and next review

Take the prescribed cyclophosphamide tablets only on Days 1–14. Keep them safely and follow the pharmacy handling advice. Keep the Day 8 IV appointment and confirm the next cycle before restarting tablets.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit time and transport

Allow time for blood review, preparation, treatment and observation. Your unit confirms the full visit duration. Arrange transport or an escort where advised.

Phase-specific visit pattern

Cyclophosphamide tablets on Days 1–14, with IV methotrexate and fluorouracil on Days 1 and 8 of each 28-day cycle. Six cycles for adjuvant treatment; metastatic treatment continues while beneficial and tolerated. No take-home infusion pump is part of this schedule. Port de-accessing after a visit is different from removal of the implanted port.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Tablet days and safe handling

Cyclophosphamide is taken on Days 1–14 only. Swallow tablets whole, one hour before food or on an empty stomach, as prescribed. Ask the unit what to do after a missed or vomited dose; do not double a dose. Methotrexate is given by the unit, not taken at home.

Anti-sickness plan

Follow the prevention and rescue instructions prescribed for this schedule. Call if vomiting prevents drinking.

Infection and bleeding

Know the local urgent-contact number. Fever, shivering, sudden illness or unusual bleeding needs prompt advice even between visits.

Medicine review

Show the oncology pharmacist all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines. Ask before starting anything new.

Hydration and symptoms

Agree fluid intake with the team, especially with heart or kidney fluid restrictions. Report reduced urine, swelling, mouth ulcers or diarrhoea.

DPD result and methotrexate clearance

Confirm DPD testing before the first fluorouracil dose. Kidney impairment can affect interpretation of uracil-based testing; ask the oncology team to review the result and testing method. Tell the team about fluid around the lungs or in the abdomen, which can prolong methotrexate exposure.

Treatment reference · 01

Why this treatment?

CMF is an anthracycline-free chemotherapy option. After surgery it may be considered when an anthracycline is unsuitable; it also has a metastatic indication. The team considers cancer biology, prior treatment, fitness, kidney function and your ability to follow the tablet schedule.

Treatment reference · 03

The wider treatment plan

For adjuvant CMF, surgery has usually already happened; agree any later radiotherapy and endocrine treatment separately. In metastatic disease, surgery or radiotherapy is considered for selected clinical needs, not automatically after a fixed number of cycles.

CMF is an anthracycline-free chemotherapy option. After surgery it may be considered when an anthracycline is unsuitable; it also has a metastatic indication. The team considers cancer biology, prior treatment, fitness, kidney function and your ability to follow the tablet schedule.

Which days are treatment days?

Who arranges the next tests?

Your treating service arranges and reviews tests. If instructions or appointments are missing, contact the team; the map does not book tests.

What if a dose is missed or I feel unwell?

Contact the unit for a revised plan. Do not double tablets, shift treatment dates or assume that an omitted dose should be made up.
