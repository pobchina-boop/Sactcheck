When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact the oncology emergency service immediately for fever or shaking chills, sudden illness, bleeding, new breathlessness, chest pain or severe allergy. Report reduced urine, hearing changes and new numbness promptly. Do not wait for the next visit.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

How the medicines work

Aim to slow or shrink metastatic breast cancer and help symptoms, balancing possible benefit, side effects and treatment burden.

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

IV infusion
Damages DNA through cross-links

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with DNA copying and repair. This can stop cancer cells dividing. Normal blood-forming cells can also be affected, and kidney function determines how the medicine is cleared.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Carboplatin is infused on Day 1 of each 21-day cycle. The team selects the dose using the prescribed AUC and kidney function. Treatment continues while helpful and tolerated; there is no fixed breast course length.

STEP 1

Visits and tests

One infusion visit per 21-day cycle plus pre-cycle blood tests and clinical review. Additional tests depend on symptoms and the individual plan.

STEP 2

Treatment preparation

Your team chooses venous access. A port is optional, requires a separate insertion procedure and later removal; it is not removed after every infusion.

STEP 3

At home

Allow time for blood review, preparation, anti-sickness treatment, the infusion and observation. No take-home infusion pump is part of this regimen.

STEP 4

Contacts / follow-up

Keep emergency contacts, medicine lists and the next appointment plan. Report line redness, swelling, leakage, pain or fever promptly.

Individual instructions

Carboplatin is given through a vein over about 30 minutes after blood and symptom review and prescribed anti-sickness medicines. The complete appointment takes longer. A cannula or existing line may be used; a port is not automatically required.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Preventing sickness

Take the prescribed anti-sickness medicines before treatment and on the following days, even if you feel well. Follow your own prescription and contact the team if nausea or vomiting is not controlled.

Blood counts

Keep blood tests and use the emergency contact for fever, chills or bleeding. Preventive growth-factor injections depend on individual risk.

Kidneys and other medicines

Tell the team about every medicine or supplement, especially treatments that affect kidneys, hearing or bleeding. Follow individual drinking advice.

Steroid and rescue plan

The anti-sickness plan may affect sleep, glucose and bowel symptoms. Confirm the exact doses and last day of each medicine; contact the team if symptoms persist.

Checks before and during treatment

Every dose depends on the reviewed treatment plan and results.

Blood and symptom checks

Blood count, kidney and liver tests are checked before each cycle. The team confirms the kidney-function method, current AUC and previous dose changes. Report numbness, hearing changes, infection and bleeding. Additional tests follow symptoms and the individual plan.

Sources: nccp

Heart and response checks

Kidney dosing, hearing and nerve review are central. Scans follow the consultant plan; no universal heart-scan schedule is specified.

Sources: nccp

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment if relevant. Carboplatin may affect periods and fertility, sometimes permanently. Do not breastfeed during treatment. Discuss reliable contraception and contact the team immediately if pregnancy is possible. The linked Irish SmPC advises effective contraception for women during treatment and at least 6 months afterwards; men should avoid fathering a child during treatment and at least 3 months afterwards. Confirm the actual product/local advice and any longer requirement from other medicines. Discuss low blood counts and sepsis, bleeding, nausea/vomiting, fatigue, kidney, hearing and nerve effects, and infusion allergy. Benefit is uncertain and alternatives depend on your cancer and priorities.

Why this treatment may be chosen

In the TNT study of advanced triple-negative or inherited BRCA1/2-associated breast cancer, carboplatin was active, but did not outperform docetaxel in the full study population. The inherited BRCA1/2 subgroup responded more often to carboplatin. These findings help inform a treatment discussion; they do not predict an individual result.

The team considers previous treatment, cancer features, health and your priorities. A response in a trial cannot guarantee benefit for an individual.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Is everyone given the same dose?

No. The consultant selects an AUC target within the source range and the team uses verified kidney function and previous effects to calculate the dose. Do not calculate or change it yourself.

How many cycles will I have?

The breast source continues until cancer progression or unacceptable toxicity. The team agrees response reviews and continuation; the fixed course of a particular trial or another cancer type does not set your course.

Do I need heart scans?

These two carboplatin monotherapy protocols do not mandate routine heart scans. Tests are arranged when symptoms, other medicines or your individual risks make them appropriate.

Try explaining the plan back: Which treatment days and doses are prescribed, which blood tests must happen first, how do you take the support medicines, and which symptoms need urgent help?
