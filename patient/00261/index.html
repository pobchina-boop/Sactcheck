How your medicines work

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Damages DNA through cross-links
Platinum reaches cells→DNA cross-links form→Copying and repair are disrupted

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with DNA copying and repair. This can stop cancer cells dividing. Normal blood-forming cells can also be affected, and kidney function determines how the medicine is cleared.

Contact the oncology emergency service immediately for fever or shaking chills, sudden illness, bleeding, new breathlessness, chest pain or severe allergy. Report reduced urine, hearing changes and new numbness promptly. Do not wait for the next visit.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Carboplatin is infused on Day 1 of each 21-day cycle. The team selects the dose using the prescribed AUC and kidney function. Treatment continues while helpful and tolerated; there is no fixed breast course length.

STEP 1

Visits and tests

One infusion visit per 21-day cycle plus pre-cycle blood tests and clinical review. Additional tests depend on symptoms and the individual plan.

STEP 2

Treatment preparation

Your team chooses venous access. A port is optional, requires a separate insertion procedure and later removal; it is not removed after every infusion.

STEP 3

At home

Allow time for blood review, preparation, anti-sickness treatment, the infusion and observation. No take-home infusion pump is part of this regimen.

STEP 4

Contacts / follow-up

Keep emergency contacts, medicine lists and the next appointment plan. Report line redness, swelling, leakage, pain or fever promptly.

Individual instructions

Carboplatin is given through a vein over about 30 minutes after blood and symptom review and prescribed anti-sickness medicines. The complete appointment takes longer. A cannula or existing line may be used; a port is not automatically required.

Carboplatin

  • Low blood counts, infection and bleeding
  • Nausea, vomiting and fatigue
  • Kidney, hearing and nerve effects
  • Infusion allergy, particularly with repeat platinum exposure
  • Fertility effects and harm to an unborn baby

Support around treatment

Preventing sickness

Take the prescribed anti-sickness medicines before treatment and on the following days, even if you feel well. Follow your own prescription and contact the team if nausea or vomiting is not controlled.

Blood counts

Keep blood tests and use the emergency contact for fever, chills or bleeding. Preventive growth-factor injections depend on individual risk.

Kidneys and other medicines

Tell the team about every medicine or supplement, especially treatments that affect kidneys, hearing or bleeding. Follow individual drinking advice.

Steroid and rescue plan

The anti-sickness plan may affect sleep, glucose and bowel symptoms. Confirm the exact doses and last day of each medicine; contact the team if symptoms persist.
