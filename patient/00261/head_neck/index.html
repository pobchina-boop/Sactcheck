Carboplatin every 3 weeks with head and neck radiotherapy

Carboplatin is given with radiotherapy to treat locally advanced head and neck cancer when cisplatin is unsuitable. Your team explains the intended chance of cure or long-term control for your diagnosis.

Locally advanced stage III–intravenous (IV) head and neck squamous cell carcinoma when cisplatin is not appropriate. · NCCP 00261 v7 · Document revised 9 November 2026

My overall treatment

Carboplatin every 3 weeks × 3 with radiotherapy.

21day view

Treatment timing in this window

Carboplatin treatmentDay 1
  1. Carboplatin with radiotherapyCarboplatin is given through a vein over about 30 minutes on Day 1 every 21 days. The head and neck prescription uses AUC 5, a target used with your checked kidney function to calculate the dose. The whole visit includes safety checks, anti-sickness medicines, preparation and observation. Three carboplatin cycles are planned with concurrent radiotherapy. Radiation has a separate appointment calendar; the team coordinates any interruptions or changes.
  2. Recovery and follow-upContinue mouth, swallowing, dental, skin and nutrition care after radiation. Blood tests and hearing or nerve review follow any persisting problem; scans and examinations follow your individual oncology and radiation plan.

Between treatments

  • Radiation has its own calendar. Mouth, skin and swallowing effects can build across weeks and continue afterwards.
  • Take your prescribed anti-sickness medicines and seek help early for poor intake, fever or bleeding.

Bring your medicine list, test dates, treatment calendar and actual urgent contacts.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
