1. Treatment and purpose

Carboplatin every 3 weeks with head and neck radiotherapy · NCCP 00261 v7

Carboplatin is given through a vein over about 30 minutes on Day 1 every 21 days. The head and neck prescription uses AUC 5, a target used with your checked kidney function to calculate the dose. The whole visit includes safety checks, anti-sickness medicines, preparation and observation. Three carboplatin cycles are planned with concurrent radiotherapy. Radiation has a separate appointment calendar; the team coordinates any interruptions or changes. Continue mouth, swallowing, dental, skin and nutrition care after radiation. Blood tests and hearing or nerve review follow any persisting problem; scans and examinations follow your individual oncology and radiation plan.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Carboplatin · Platinum chemotherapy

Contact the oncology emergency service immediately for fever or shaking chills, sudden illness, bleeding, new breathlessness, chest pain or severe allergy. Report reduced urine, hearing changes and new numbness promptly. Do not wait for the next visit.

Radiotherapy · Local treatment

Report difficulty swallowing, weight loss, mouth pain, skin breakdown or problems managing saliva.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss your diagnosis, intent, alternatives, expected benefit and uncertainty, treatment burden, serious or lasting harms, fertility and pregnancy precautions and your priorities. Include blood-count suppression, serious infection and bleeding, nausea/vomiting, allergy, renal dose calculation, hearing and nerve effects, and the separate radiotherapy consent covering mouth, saliva, skin, dental, jaw and swallowing effects. Confirm actual urgent contacts and use teach-back. This draft does not authorise treatment.
