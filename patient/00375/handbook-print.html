My treatment handbook

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin and trabectedin

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Control relapsed disease and delay further progression.

Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin · Caelyx

Liposomal anthracycline. A coated carrier delivers doxorubicin, which damages DNA. Prior anthracycline exposure matters for heart safety. Report sore palms or soles, mouth ulcers, ankle swelling or breathlessness.

Trabectedin · Yondelis

DNA-binding chemotherapy. It binds to DNA and disrupts processes that tumour cells need to grow. Severe muscle pain, weakness or dark urine needs urgent assessment.

What the research found

Adding trabectedin to liposomal doxorubicin delayed progression compared with liposomal doxorubicin alone.

The trial does not compare this combination directly with retreatment using platinum.

Your infusion schedule

Day 1 · two infusions

You receive a steroid before pegylated liposomal doxorubicin, followed by trabectedin over three hours. Your team normally recommends a central line. Treatment repeats every 21 days while it helps and remains tolerable.

Common effects and what to do

How you feel between visits

Tiredness, nausea or appetite changes can make everyday activities harder. Ask your team for help if symptoms interfere with eating or drinking.

Serious effects: when to call

Hand and foot soreness · Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin

Report painful redness, peeling or blisters before the next cycle.

Heart problems · Pegylated liposomal doxorubicin

Report new breathlessness, ankle swelling or difficulty lying flat.

Muscle injury · Trabectedin

Report severe muscle pain, weakness or dark urine immediately.

Liver injury · Trabectedin

Report jaundice, dark urine or marked fatigue urgently.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call your oncology unit urgently for fever, shivering or sudden illness. Call urgently for severe muscle pain, weakness or dark urine. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.
