When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Confusion, unusual sleepiness, hallucinations, seizures, blood in urine or very little urine requires immediate assessment. New breathlessness or swelling also needs urgent advice.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Your team may choose a combination when reducing tumour size is an important goal and you are fit for more intensive treatment. For advanced disease, treatment generally aims at control and symptom relief; a combination is not a guarantee of longer survival.

Anthracycline chemotherapy

Doxorubicin

Damages deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) and interferes with repair

Doxorubicin interacts with DNA and topoisomerase II, disrupting copying and repair. Its heart and tissue effects require particular care.

DNA interaction↓Repair disrupted↓Division reduced

What to watch for
Report breathlessness, chest pain, palpitations or ankle swelling promptly. Tell the nurse immediately about pain or burning at the infusion site. Red urine can occur briefly after the dose; painful urination, clots or persistent redness need assessment.

Sources: nccp · doxorubicin-pi

Chemotherapy

Ifosfamide

Ifosfamide
Damages DNA needed for cell division

Ifosfamide damages cancer-cell DNA. Its breakdown products can also affect the bladder, kidneys and brain. Mesna protects the bladder but does not prevent all kidney or brain effects.

DNA cross-links form↓Cell division is disrupted↓Bladder, kidney and brain checks

What to watch for
Report confusion, sleepiness, blood in urine or reduced urine immediately.

Sources: nccp · ifosfamide-pi

Individual prescribed course

NCCP 00391: doxorubicin 20 mg/m² and ifosfamide 3000 mg/m² on each of days 1–3, repeated every 21 days for up to 6 cycles. Per-cycle totals are doxorubicin 60 mg/m² and ifosfamide 9000 mg/m². Mesna, hydration and other support continue for their prescribed periods.

This is NCCP 00391: doxorubicin 60 mg/m² and ifosfamide 9 g/m² per cycle. The two NCCP combination regimens have different doxorubicin doses and eligibility details. They must not be substituted for each other or for a published 10 g/m² ifosfamide regimen.

Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenBefore first treatment and before each cycle. Extra counts may be needed for symptoms or abnormal results.

Check white cells for infection risk, haemoglobin for anaemia and platelets for bleeding risk.

Kidney and liver tests

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle; repeat sooner for symptoms, abnormal results or clinical concerns.

Check safe medicine handling and identify kidney or liver effects.

Heart assessment and lifetime exposure

If part of your plan
Whenelectrocardiogram (ECG) before treatment. echocardiogram (heart ultrasound) (ECHO) or multigated acquisition (heart pumping) scan (MUGA) if over 65 or clinically indicated; the source baseline left ventricular ejection fraction (heart pumping measurement) (LVEF) criterion is above 50%. Review cardiac symptoms and cumulative anthracycline exposure during treatment; further heart tests follow clinical risk or symptoms.

Assess anthracycline-related heart risk and account for previous doxorubicin or related treatment.

Neurological checks

WhenBefore each ifosfamide dose; assess immediately for new confusion, unusual sleepiness, hallucinations or seizures.

Detect ifosfamide-related brain effects early.

Urine, fluid balance and electrolytes

WhenCheck for blood in urine before each ifosfamide dose and every 8 hours on treatment days. Urine output, fluid balance and electrolytes follow the individual ifosfamide and hydration prescription.

Detect bladder bleeding, kidney injury or fluid/electrolyte problems despite mesna.

Medicines and symptoms

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1–3: Doxorubicin + Ifosfamide

Doxorubicin 20 mg/m² each day, total 60 mg/m² per cycle. Ifosfamide 3000 mg/m² each day over 3 hours. Mesna, fluids and safety checks accompany treatment. Follow your confirmed appointment and support plan.

Day 4–21: Between scheduled chemotherapy doses

Continue the prescribed support and monitoring plan. Side effects can occur between visits and late in the cycle. Contact oncology promptly if unwell. Follow your confirmed appointment and support plan.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the full three-day treatment and post-treatment mesna/fluid plan, granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (white-cell growth support) (G-CSF) injections, stomach protection and anti-sickness medicines before starting. Tell the team about sedating medicines or new confusion. Do not change fluid intake or medicines without the team’s advice.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Each treatment day includes intravenous doxorubicin followed by a 3-hour ifosfamide infusion, with prescribed mesna and fluids. Mesna includes a prolonged infusion and hydration continues after chemotherapy; the service confirms admission, pump arrangements and the actual finish time.

STEP 3

Intravenous access and line care

The team chooses suitable intravenous access and explains line care. Tell the nurse immediately about burning, pain, redness or swelling during an injection or drip. Do not wait for the visit to finish. If you have a port or peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC), agree dressing, flushing and removal arrangements. Confirm who manages the mesna pump and when it is disconnected; do not alter pump settings yourself.

STEP 4

Home support

Mesna and prescribed hydration are essential bladder protection. NCCP requires G-CSF support and proton-pump inhibitor prophylaxis for this regimen; the team specifies product, dose and timing. Multi-day anti-sickness treatment needs a single reconciled prescription. Aprepitant may increase ifosfamide-related brain toxicity, so pharmacy must check the chosen plan.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Treatment reference · 05

Your consent discussion

Use the general treatment risks and the separate medicine-specific risks together.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste change, diarrhoea or constipation; tiredness, hair and skin changes; infusion reactions and intravenous-line complications; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. Risks differ between medicines and people. The medicine-specific section explains the main additional risks for this regimen.

Sources: nccp · doxorubicin-pi · ifosfamide-pi

This regimen and your priorities

Discuss the treatment goal, alternatives including supportive care, uncertainty of benefit, common and serious risks, lasting effects and individual priorities. Record questions and the agreed plan. EORTC 62012: doxorubicin with or without intensified ifosfamide

In a first-line advanced soft tissue sarcoma trial, adding intensified ifosfamide increased tumour response and delayed progression, but did not demonstrate a statistically significant overall-survival advantage and caused substantially more toxicity. This helps explain why a combination may be considered when tumour shrinkage is especially important.

The trial used doxorubicin 75 mg/m² with ifosfamide 10 g/m² over four days. NCCP 00391 uses doxorubicin 60 mg/m² with ifosfamide 9 g/m² over three days. Trial response or survival figures are not validated estimates for this exact schedule. The advanced-disease trial does not establish neoadjuvant benefit.

Sources: trial · nccp

When do I attend?

NCCP 00391: doxorubicin 20 mg/m² and ifosfamide 3000 mg/m² on each of days 1–3, repeated every 21 days for up to 6 cycles. Per-cycle totals are doxorubicin 60 mg/m² and ifosfamide 9000 mg/m². Mesna, hydration and other support continue for their prescribed periods.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Are blood tests the same as checking whether treatment works?

No. Blood counts and organ tests mainly check safety. Your sarcoma team uses the clinical review and planned imaging to assess cancer control.
