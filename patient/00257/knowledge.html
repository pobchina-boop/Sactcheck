When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team immediately for fever, shaking chills, feeling suddenly unwell, unusual bleeding or persistent vomiting/diarrhoea. Follow your own temperature and contact plan. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or new leg weakness with bladder/bowel changes: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

Treat prostate cancer in affected bones, help control symptoms and delay complications in the appropriate disease setting.

Radiopharmaceutical

Radium-223

Radium-223
Radium behaves like calcium and concentrates where bone is being formed around metastases. It emits short-range alpha particles that damage nearby cells. It does not treat visceral metastases and can suppress blood-cell production.

Radium course

Up to six injections

Radium-223 is given as a slow injection into a vein every 28 days, for a maximum of six injections. It is administered by a specialist service authorised to use radioactive medicines.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Confirm symptoms, bone-metastatic disease without visceral metastases, previous treatments, blood count, kidney/liver tests, bone scan/CT and bone health.

STEP 2

Your treatment visit

Radium-223 is given as a slow injection into a vein every 28 days, for a maximum of six injections. It is administered by a specialist service authorised to use radioactive medicines. No routine premedication is usually required. The team prescribes sickness or other symptom medicines if needed.

STEP 3

intravenous (IV) access

Treatment is given through a cannula or suitable established access. No take-home pump is part of this regimen. Report pain, burning, leakage or swelling immediately.

STEP 4

Between visits

Do not combine with abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone. Bone-protective treatment is assessed before starting. Obtain the nuclear-medicine team’s written toilet, body-fluid, laundry and travel instructions; your treating service will explain which precautions to follow at home and for how long.

STEP 5

Leaving with a clear plan

Confirm the next blood-test and treatment dates, support medicines and verified daytime/out-of-hours numbers.

How it works

Radium behaves like calcium and concentrates where bone is being formed around metastases. It emits short-range alpha particles that damage nearby cells. It does not treat visceral metastases and can suppress blood-cell production.

Sources: nccp · smpc

How it fits with other treatment

Continue prescribed androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), unless both testes have been surgically removed. ADT lowers testosterone; chemotherapy or a bone-targeted radiopharmaceutical has a different action. Additional androgen-pathway agents and radiotherapy need their own disease-specific plan.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Radiotherapy

External-beam radiotherapy may be used for local disease control or symptoms according to the disease setting. Radium-223 is a systemic bone-targeted radiopharmaceutical and is not interchangeable with external-beam treatment.

Treatment reference · 04

Benefits, uncertainty and your choices

Evidence in this setting

ALSYMPCA studied radium-223 plus best standard care against placebo plus best standard care in symptomatic bone-metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer without known visceral metastases. In the updated descriptive analysis of 921 participants, median overall survival was 14.9 versus 11.3 months; hazard ratio 0.695 (95% CI 0.581–0.832). Later safety restrictions and current treatment-selection criteria remain essential; these results do not establish safety with abiraterone or chemotherapy.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Consent discussion

Discuss low blood counts, infection/bleeding, diarrhoea/vomiting/dehydration, fractures, radiation precautions, fertility and potential late radiation effects. This is a radiopharmaceutical, not conventional chemotherapy. Discuss alternatives, likely benefit and uncertainty, possible lasting or fatal effects, fertility, contraception and your priorities.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Discuss fertility preservation before starting. Men should use effective contraception during treatment and for six months afterwards, according to the current product information.
