1. Treatment and purpose

Methotrexate, doxorubicin and cisplatin for osteosarcoma · NCCP 00463 v4

Doxorubicin and cisplatin on days 1–2 of weeks 1 and 6; methotrexate in weeks 4, 5, 9 and 10. Actual timing depends on the operation and recovery. Additional preoperative methotrexate is only a separate consultant decision. Doxorubicin in weeks 12, 17, 22 and 26; cisplatin in weeks 12 and 17; methotrexate in weeks 15, 16, 20, 21, 24, 25, 28 and 29.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

High-dose methotrexate · Chemotherapy

Report mouth ulcers, severe diarrhoea, reduced urine, dry cough, breathlessness or new neurological symptoms urgently. Do not stop rescue medicine or start other medicines yourself.

Doxorubicin · Chemotherapy

Report fever, mouth ulcers, breathlessness, ankle swelling, palpitations or pain at the drip immediately.

Cisplatin · Chemotherapy

Report reduced urine, persistent vomiting, ringing ears, hearing changes, numbness or weakness before further treatment.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss the individual aim, realistic benefit and uncertainty, treatment burden, alternatives, serious and lasting risks, fertility, personal priorities and the option to decline or reconsider. This course is limited to the stated age, site and complete-resection context. Up to two additional preoperative methotrexate doses may be prescribed if surgery is delayed. These are not automatic. Poor response on surgical pathology does not automatically justify adding ifosfamide/etoposide.
