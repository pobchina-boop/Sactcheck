Dose-dense AC → fortnightly paclitaxel + weekly trastuzumab

Four AC cycles every 14 days, then four fortnightly paclitaxel treatments with trastuzumab weekly on Days 1 and 8 of each 14-day block (eight weekly antibody doses). Trastuzumab then continues every 21 days to complete one year total; it is not given with AC.

Your treatment, at home.

Important medicine-specific effects

Doxorubicin

  • Low counts, infection and mouth ulcers
  • Nausea, hair loss and fatigue
  • Heart damage, sometimes delayed
  • Severe tissue injury if the infusion leaks
  • Rare later blood cancers

Cyclophosphamide

  • Low counts and infection
  • Nausea, vomiting and hair loss
  • Bladder irritation / bleeding
  • Fertility effects and menopause
  • Rare later blood cancers

Paclitaxel

  • Low blood counts and infection
  • Numbness / tingling, sometimes lasting
  • Hair loss, muscle or joint pain
  • Infusion reaction
  • Mouth, bowel, skin and nail effects

Trastuzumab

  • Heart pumping-function reduction / heart failure
  • Infusion or allergic reaction
  • Diarrhoea and fatigue
  • Rare serious lung effects

Make the plan practical

The correct phase prescription

AC is high emetogenic risk; a separate low-risk taxane script replaces it.

Premedicines and growth factors

Paclitaxel premedicines differ between weekly and fortnightly dosing. Use the phase-specific prescription.

Tests and appointments

The team should name the booking owner, due window and person reviewing each required test. Tell them if an expected appointment has not arrived.

After chemotherapy

HER2-only continuation has its own minimal-emetic-risk plan; do not automatically continue chemotherapy steroids, antiemetics or growth factors.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
