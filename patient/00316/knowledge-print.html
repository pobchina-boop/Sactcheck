When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology promptly for fever, shivering, unusual bleeding, severe mouth or bowel symptoms, inability to drink, reduced urine, new breathlessness or a painful/leaking line. Severe chest pain, collapse or severe breathing difficulty needs emergency help.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Before surgery: treat the cancer and support the agreed surgical plan. After surgery: reduce recurrence risk. Confirm which source indication and sequence apply.

Anthracycline chemotherapy

Doxorubicin

Interferes with deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) handling

Doxorubicin interferes with enzymes and DNA processes needed for cell division. It can also affect heart muscle, so the team considers previous exposure and cardiac risk.

DNA handling disrupted↓Cell damage↓Division reduced

What to watch for
Report pain or burning at the infusion site immediately. New breathlessness or chest symptoms need prompt review. Red urine for a short time can be a drug-colour effect; pain, clots or persistent bleeding still need advice.

Sources: nccp

Alkylating chemotherapy

Cyclophosphamide

Damages DNA needed for growth

Cyclophosphamide is activated in the body and damages DNA. This helps stop dividing cancer cells but can also affect blood-cell production and reproductive tissues.

Drug activated↓DNA damage↓Cell division affected

What to watch for
Use the prescribed anti-sickness and hydration plan. Report fever, painful urination or blood in the urine.

Sources: nccp

Taxane chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Taxol (conventional paclitaxel)
Locks the cell division machinery

Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues.

Microtubules form↓Structures remain locked↓Division is interrupted

What to watch for
Report tingling or numbness before the next weekly dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Sources: nccp

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

Targets HER2 growth signals

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

HER2 on cell surface↓Antibody binds↓Growth signalling reduced

What to watch for
Keep echo appointments throughout HER2 treatment. Report breathlessness, swelling, palpitations or a reaction; chemotherapy side effects are tracked separately.

Sources: nccp

What each check is for

1Baseline reviewBefore treatment

HER2-positive neoadjuvant or adjuvant high-risk breast cancer. Review lifetime anthracycline exposure and baseline cardiac risk before AC; reassess before starting trastuzumab. Assess cardiac function before trastuzumab and every 12 weeks during treatment. Keep the same monitoring clock when paclitaxel ends or trastuzumab changes schedule; arrange earlier review for symptoms or falling function. EMA product information recommends cardiac checks every 6 months after treatment until 24 months; following anthracyclines in early disease, yearly up to 5 years from the last trastuzumab dose or longer if function falls. Your team individualises this plan.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1–14: Your current reference cycle

Four AC cycles every 14 days, then four fortnightly paclitaxel treatments with trastuzumab weekly on Days 1 and 8 of each 14-day block (eight weekly antibody doses). Trastuzumab then continues every 21 days to complete one year total; it is not given with AC. Follow the team’s appointment and medicine instructions; contact them about missed doses or new symptoms.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Four AC cycles every 14 days, then four fortnightly paclitaxel treatments with trastuzumab weekly on Days 1 and 8 of each 14-day block (eight weekly antibody doses). Trastuzumab then continues every 21 days to complete one year total; it is not given with AC.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Blood tests, medicines review and the consent discussion come first. Confirm baseline and planned ongoing cardiac assessment.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Staff check the line, give prescribed premedicines and treatment, and observe for reactions. Tell them immediately about burning, pain, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit time and transport

First visits can take longer because of loading doses, premedicines and observation. Ask the unit about expected time, transport and any escort needed after sedation or line insertion.

Phase-specific visit pattern

Four AC cycles every 14 days, then four fortnightly paclitaxel treatments with trastuzumab weekly on Days 1 and 8 of each 14-day block (eight weekly antibody doses). Trastuzumab then continues every 21 days to complete one year total; it is not given with AC. No take-home infusion pump is part of this schedule. Port de-accessing after a visit is different from removal of the implanted port.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Anti-sickness plan

Follow the prevention and rescue instructions prescribed for this schedule. Call if vomiting prevents drinking.

Infection and bleeding

Know the local urgent-contact number. Fever, shivering, sudden illness or unusual bleeding needs prompt advice even between visits.

Medicine review

Show the oncology pharmacist all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines. Ask before starting anything new.

Premedicines belong to the active phase

Paclitaxel reaction medicines and AC anti-sickness medicines have different purposes. They are not automatically continued during trastuzumab-only treatment.

Treatment reference · 03

The wider treatment plan

For neoadjuvant treatment, agree response review and surgery planning; for adjuvant treatment, surgery has usually happened. Follow the agreed early-breast pathway. Trastuzumab may continue after chemotherapy and can overlap radiotherapy when approved by the treating team. Endocrine therapy depends on hormone receptors. Do not give trastuzumab concurrently with the AC phase.

When treatment changes

Reconcile active cancer medicines, supportive medicines and remaining symptoms before every transition.

Questions worth asking

Why is this schedule being recommended?

This dose-dense AC–taxane sequence adds trastuzumab for HER2-positive high-risk early breast cancer. Trastuzumab starts after AC. Fitness, recurrence risk, heart health, nerve symptoms and available HER2-directed alternatives affect selection.

Which days are treatment days?

Four AC cycles every 14 days, then four fortnightly paclitaxel treatments with trastuzumab weekly on Days 1 and 8 of each 14-day block (eight weekly antibody doses). Trastuzumab then continues every 21 days to complete one year total; it is not given with AC.

Who arranges the next tests?

Your treating service arranges and reviews tests. If instructions or appointments are missing, contact the team; the map does not book tests.

What if a dose is missed or I feel unwell?

Contact the unit for a revised plan. Do not double tablets, shift treatment dates or assume that an omitted dose should be made up.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are due on which days? What symptoms mean you should call today? Which tests come next and who arranges them?
