1. Treatment and purpose

Alternating ifosfamide/etoposide and vincristine/doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide · every 2 weeks · NCCP 00675 v2

Ifosfamide and etoposide: cycles 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13. Repeats at the prescribed interval only after safety review. Vincristine, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide: cycles 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. Repeats at the prescribed interval only after safety review. Vincristine and cyclophosphamide: cycles 12 and 14. Repeats at the prescribed interval only after safety review.

2. Risks and supportive care

Ifosfamide · Alkylating chemotherapy

Report confusion, unusual sleepiness, hallucinations, marked weakness or seizures immediately. Blood or pain when passing urine, reduced urine, fever or new breathing problems needs urgent review. Continue prescribed mesna and hydration until the team confirms completion.

Etoposide · Chemotherapy

Tell the nurse immediately about dizziness, flushing, rash, chest tightness, breathlessness, faintness or infusion-site pain. Contact oncology promptly for fever, bleeding, severe mouth symptoms or a blistering rash.

Vincristine · Vinca alkaloid chemotherapy

Report new numbness, weakness, difficulty walking, jaw pain or hearing/vision changes before another dose. Constipation with pain, vomiting or abdominal swelling, difficulty passing urine, confusion or seizures needs urgent advice. Report infusion-site pain immediately.

Doxorubicin · Anthracycline chemotherapy

Report fever, new breathlessness, chest symptoms or palpitations urgently. Tell staff immediately about infusion-site burning or pain. Temporary red urine may reflect the medicine's colour, but visible blood, clots, painful urination or persistent red urine needs assessment.

Cyclophosphamide · Alkylating chemotherapy

Report blood or pain when passing urine, reduced urine, fever, new breathlessness, chest symptoms, confusion or severe weakness urgently. Follow the individual fluid plan; do not force large fluid volumes if you have a fluid restriction.

Dactinomycin · only if selected for cycles 12 and 14 · Chemotherapy

Report fever, bleeding, severe mouth sores or diarrhoea, jaundice, increasing abdominal pain/swelling or rapid weight gain urgently. Tell staff immediately about infusion-site pain or burning. Report a new painful rash, especially in a previously irradiated area.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss realistic benefit and uncertainty, alternatives including clinical trials or symptom-focused care where appropriate, common and serious risks, lasting effects, personal priorities and the option to decline or reconsider treatment. This is the 14-day national schedule for adolescents and young adults. The regimen guidance does not specify a numerical age or fitness-score limit. The treatment starts with ifosfamide/etoposide. The final two even-numbered administrations omit doxorubicin, and dactinomycin remains optional. Trial schedules and other age-group protocols are not interchangeable.
