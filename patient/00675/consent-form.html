Alternating ifosfamide/etoposide and vincristine/doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide · every 2 weeks

Adolescents and young adults with newly diagnosed Ewing-family sarcoma, or the separately listed rhabdomyosarcoma/desmoplastic intra-abdominal small round blue cell tumour indication · NCCP 00675 · version 2 · 14-day cycle

Treatment context
Treat cancer throughout the body as part of a specialist plan that also addresses local tumour control. The intended benefit depends on the exact tumour, disease extent and treatment aim.
Schedule
There are 14 alternating administrations, planned every 14 days after recovery, not 14 pairs. Ifosfamide and etoposide are given on days 1–5 in cycles 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13. Vincristine, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide are given on day 1 in cycles 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. Cycles 12 and 14 contain vincristine and cyclophosphamide with no doxorubicin; dactinomycin may be added only if the consultant selects it. Surgery, radiotherapy, toxicity or delayed recovery may change the sequence or dates.

Complete general risk discussion

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines, doses and people. Read this general section together with the risks of every medicine actually included in the agreed treatment phase.

General treatment discussion

Possible short-term, long-term, unexpected and unlisted effects; benefits are not guaranteed.
Severe complications can require hospital admission and can rarely be fatal.
Infection / sepsis, allergic or infusion reactions and leakage outside a vein (extravasation) should be discussed where applicable to the medicines and route.
Pregnancy, contraception, breastfeeding, sexual health and fertility preservation should be discussed where relevant.
Treatment can be delayed, changed or stopped if risks outweigh benefit. Consent is voluntary and can be withdrawn.

2. Regimen / agent-specific material risks

Ifosfamide

Alkylating chemotherapy
Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
Nausea, vomiting, mouth/bowel effects, hair loss and fatigue
Brain effects causing confusion, drowsiness, hallucinations or seizures; recovery is not guaranteed
Bladder inflammation or bleeding, kidney filtering/tubular injury and electrolyte losses; kidney damage can persist or appear later
Serious heart or lung injury; allergic reactions
Potentially permanent infertility, later cancers and pregnancy harm

Etoposide

Chemotherapy
Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
Nausea, vomiting, appetite loss, mouth soreness and bowel changes
Hair loss and fatigue
Allergic or severe infusion reactions and low blood pressure, particularly with rapid administration
Inflammation or tissue injury at the infusion site, liver effects and severe skin reactions
Later blood cancers, fertility effects and pregnancy harm

Vincristine

Vinca alkaloid chemotherapy
Peripheral nerve injury causing tingling, pain, weakness or impaired walking/hand function; effects may persist
Autonomic nerve injury causing severe constipation or bowel paralysis, urinary retention and blood-pressure changes
Low sodium from abnormal water retention, confusion or seizures; hearing, vision or cranial-nerve effects
Hair loss, mouth/bowel symptoms and jaw or muscle pain; blood-count effects can occur in the combination
Allergic reactions, breathing reactions and tissue injury after leakage outside the vein
Fertility and pregnancy harm; wrong-route administration can be fatal

Doxorubicin

Anthracycline chemotherapy
Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
Nausea, vomiting, mouth ulcers, appetite/bowel changes, hair loss and fatigue
Cumulative heart-muscle damage and heart failure, including effects that can appear after treatment; heart-rhythm disturbances
Severe tissue damage if the medicine leaks outside the vein; allergic reactions
Later blood cancers and potentially lasting fertility effects; pregnancy harm

Cyclophosphamide

Alkylating chemotherapy
Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
Bladder inflammation or bleeding and kidney/tubular injury
Low blood sodium and excessive water retention, which can cause confusion or seizures
Heart injury, lung inflammation or scarring, and severe liver blood-flow injury
Nausea, vomiting, mouth/bowel effects, hair loss, fatigue and impaired wound healing
Potentially permanent infertility, later cancers and pregnancy harm; allergic reactions

Dactinomycin · only if selected for cycles 12 and 14

Chemotherapy
Low blood counts, serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding
Mouth and gut ulceration, nausea, vomiting and bowel changes
Severe liver injury, including obstruction of blood flow through small liver vessels
Severe tissue damage if the medicine leaks outside the vein
Increased radiation effects or renewed inflammation in previously irradiated tissue, called radiation recall
Severe skin or allergic reactions, later cancers, fertility effects and pregnancy harm

Discuss realistic benefit and uncertainty, alternatives including clinical trials or symptom-focused care where appropriate, common and serious risks, lasting effects, personal priorities and the option to decline or reconsider treatment. This is the 14-day national schedule for adolescents and young adults. The regimen guidance does not specify a numerical age or fitness-score limit. The treatment starts with ifosfamide/etoposide. The final two even-numbered administrations omit doxorubicin, and dactinomycin remains optional. Trial schedules and other age-group protocols are not interchangeable.
