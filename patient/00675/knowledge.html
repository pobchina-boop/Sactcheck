When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Confusion or unusual drowsiness during ifosfamide, blood in urine, reduced urine, severe constipation, new weakness or heart symptoms needs urgent assessment. Doxorubicin can colour urine red for one or two days; do not dismiss painful urination, clots or suspected blood as harmless colour.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Treat cancer throughout the body as part of a specialist plan that also addresses local tumour control. The intended benefit depends on the exact tumour, disease extent and treatment aim.

Alkylating chemotherapy

Ifosfamide

Links genetic strands so cells cannot divide normally

The liver activates ifosfamide into substances that damage deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material. Some breakdown products can injure the urinary tract. Mesna and prescribed fluids reduce bladder injury, but do not remove kidney, brain or other chemotherapy risks.

Activated medicine damages genetic material↓Mesna helps protect the bladder↓Brain, kidney, urine and blood checks continue

What to watch for
Report confusion, unusual sleepiness, hallucinations, marked weakness or seizures immediately. Blood or pain when passing urine, reduced urine, fever or new breathing problems needs urgent review. Continue prescribed mesna and hydration until the team confirms completion.

Sources: nccp · ifosfamide-pi

Chemotherapy

Etoposide

Interferes with an enzyme that manages breaks in genetic material

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme that manages breaks in deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material. Damaged cancer cells cannot divide normally. Blood production and other healthy tissues can also be affected.

Genetic-material repair is disrupted↓Dividing cells are damaged↓Slow infusion and blood-count checks protect safety

What to watch for
Tell the nurse immediately about dizziness, flushing, rash, chest tightness, breathlessness, faintness or infusion-site pain. Contact oncology promptly for fever, bleeding, severe mouth symptoms or a blistering rash.

Sources: nccp · etoposide-pi

Vinca alkaloid chemotherapy

Vincristine

Disrupts the structures needed for cell division

Vincristine interferes with microtubules, structures needed for cells to divide and for nerves to work normally. This helps explain numbness, weakness and bowel slowing. It is given only into a vein by trained staff; administration into spinal fluid can be fatal.

Microtubules are disrupted↓Cell division and nerves are affected↓Bowel and nerve symptoms need early review

What to watch for
Report new numbness, weakness, difficulty walking, jaw pain or hearing/vision changes before another dose. Constipation with pain, vomiting or abdominal swelling, difficulty passing urine, confusion or seizures needs urgent advice. Report infusion-site pain immediately.

Sources: nccp · vincristine-pi

Anthracycline chemotherapy

Doxorubicin

Damages genetic material and disrupts cell division

Doxorubicin interferes with deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material, and the enzymes that manage it. Cancer cells can be damaged, but blood production, the mouth and the heart can also be affected. Previous anthracycline treatment contributes to the heart-risk assessment.

Genetic material is damaged↓Cell division is disrupted↓Blood counts and heart function are checked

What to watch for
Report fever, new breathlessness, chest symptoms or palpitations urgently. Tell staff immediately about infusion-site burning or pain. Temporary red urine may reflect the medicine's colour, but visible blood, clots, painful urination or persistent red urine needs assessment.

Sources: nccp · doxorubicin-pi

Alkylating chemotherapy

Cyclophosphamide

Damages genetic material after activation in the body

Cyclophosphamide is activated into substances that damage deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material. Blood and reproductive cells can also be affected. Urinary breakdown products can injure the bladder, so the prescribed fluid and any mesna plan must be followed.

Activated medicine damages genetic material↓Urinary metabolites can irritate the bladder↓Blood, urine and fluid checks support safety

What to watch for
Report blood or pain when passing urine, reduced urine, fever, new breathlessness, chest symptoms, confusion or severe weakness urgently. Follow the individual fluid plan; do not force large fluid volumes if you have a fluid restriction.

Sources: nccp · cyclophosphamide-pi

Chemotherapy

Dactinomycin · only if selected for cycles 12 and 14

Actinomycin D
Interferes with reading genetic instructions

This medicine is optional in the final two even-numbered administrations and is included in your consent only if selected. Dactinomycin binds to deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), the cell's genetic material, and disrupts production of ribonucleic acid (RNA), which carries instructions for cell activity. Cancer cells and healthy blood, mouth, gut and liver tissues can be affected.

Medicine binds genetic material↓Genetic instructions cannot be read normally↓Blood, liver and infusion-site checks matter

What to watch for
Report fever, bleeding, severe mouth sores or diarrhoea, jaundice, increasing abdominal pain/swelling or rapid weight gain urgently. Tell staff immediately about infusion-site pain or burning. Report a new painful rash, especially in a previously irradiated area.

Sources: nccp · dactinomycin-pi

Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenBefore treatment and each cycle; further samples are directed by symptoms, recovery or the prescribed admission plan.

Check infection-fighting cells, anaemia and bleeding risk.

Kidney, liver and electrolytes

WhenKidney and liver tests at baseline and before every cycle. Sodium, potassium and phosphate at baseline and before each ifosfamide/etoposide cycle; repeat when clinically indicated.

Detect organ injury and electrolyte loss that affect treatment safety.

Neurological function

WhenAssess alertness and neurological function before every ifosfamide dose. Report numbness, weakness, bowel slowing or walking changes before further vincristine.

Detect ifosfamide-related changes in alertness and vincristine nerve toxicity.

Urine, fluid balance and weight

WhenCheck urine for blood before each ifosfamide dose and every 8 hours on treatment days. Staff monitor urine output and fluid balance and weigh daily during the prescribed hydration period.

Detect bladder bleeding, kidney effects and fluid retention despite protective treatment.

Heart pumping and anthracycline exposure

If part of your plan
WhenBefore treatment if clinically indicated; repeat when indicated. Heart ultrasound or a pumping scan measures left ventricular ejection fraction; the national source requires greater than 50% for doxorubicin. Review symptoms, previous anthracyclines and radiation exposure before further dosing.

Assess cardiac safety before further doxorubicin.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1–14: Use the phase chart for your numbered administration

There are 14 alternating administrations, planned every 14 days after recovery, not 14 pairs. Ifosfamide and etoposide are given on days 1–5 in cycles 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13. Vincristine, doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide are given on day 1 in cycles 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. Cycles 12 and 14 contain vincristine and cyclophosphamide with no doxorubicin; dactinomycin may be added only if the consultant selects it. Surgery, radiotherapy, toxicity or delayed recovery may change the sequence or dates. Confirm the phase and actual prescribed treatment days before every administration.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the current numbered administration and which drugs it contains. Review constipation prevention, mouth care, medicines and infection contacts. If pelvic radiotherapy is planned, the team prescribes additional mesna with cyclophosphamide. Confirm the timing of chemotherapy around radiation.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

During ifosfamide/etoposide cycles, each medicine is infused over 60 minutes on each of days 1–5. During the alternating day-1 cycles, vincristine is given by an intravenous minibag over 15 minutes, conventional doxorubicin by slow intravenous bolus, and cyclophosphamide over 30 minutes. Optional final-phase dactinomycin is given by trained staff. Mesna, fluids, tests and observation add substantially to attendance time.

STEP 3

Intravenous access and practical care

The treatment service chooses suitable intravenous access. Report burning, pain, swelling or redness during administration immediately. If a port or central catheter is used, agree dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements. Do not alter infusion or mesna-pump settings yourself.

STEP 4

Home support

Check the support section and your prescription for current protective medicines, timing and contact instructions.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Anti-sickness medicines

Follow one written prevention, home and rescue plan. The team reconciles all steroids and interacting medicines; no generic reference is an additional prescription.

Mesna and bladder protection

Ifosfamide cycles: mesna 360 mg/m² intravenously before each dose and at 4 and 8 hours from the START of that dose. It binds bladder-damaging metabolites; it does not prevent every kidney or neurological effect.

Hydration and fluid balance

Hospital-prescribed fluids support urine flow and clearance. The team adjusts them to urine output, blood tests, weight and heart/kidney status; continue the prescribed protection after chemotherapy finishes. Follow individual home fluid advice.

White-cell growth factor

Required after every administration. After ifosfamide/etoposide: daily injections on days 8–13 (source 0.5 million units/kg, maximum 48 million units/day for primary prevention). After vincristine/cyclophosphamide-containing treatment: pegylated injection 24–48 hours later. These support recovery of infection-fighting cells; fever still needs urgent assessment.

Mouth and bowel care

Use the prescribed mouth-care and constipation-prevention plan. Vincristine slows bowel movement through nerve effects; report painful constipation, vomiting or abdominal swelling promptly. Mouth care helps detect and manage damaged lining before drinking becomes difficult.

Radiotherapy and protective care

If pelvic radiotherapy is planned, additional mesna is prescribed with cyclophosphamide: 240 mg/m² intravenously before it, then 240 mg/m² intravenously or 480 mg/m² orally 4 and 8 hours from its start. Doxorubicin interruption depends on the radiation field; it is not reintroduced until at least three weeks after radiation ends. Confirm actual dates with both teams.

Access and practical care

The treatment service chooses suitable intravenous access. Report burning, pain, swelling or redness during administration immediately. If a port or central catheter is used, agree dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements. Do not alter infusion or mesna-pump settings yourself.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility preservation before treatment; these combinations can permanently affect fertility. Obtain a written pregnancy-prevention, contraception and breastfeeding plan covering every medicine and the longest relevant precaution period. Current product instructions differ from the older general six-month regimen paragraph. Do not assume one interval applies to everyone, and contact oncology promptly if pregnancy is possible.

Your wider treatment plan

For Ewing sarcoma, systemic chemotherapy is combined with surgery and/or radiotherapy for local tumour control. For rhabdomyosarcoma, the specialist combines systemic and local treatment according to site, risk and feasibility. These decisions require the relevant multidisciplinary team; Ewing trial percentages cannot be used as estimates for the other listed tumour types. Rehabilitation, symptom control and psychological support can accompany cancer treatment.

Your treatment decision

Read the general chemotherapy risks together with each medicine-specific section.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines, doses and people. Read this general section together with the risks of every medicine actually included in the agreed treatment phase.

Interval-compressed versus three-weekly chemotherapy in localised Ewing sarcoma

In a randomised trial of predominantly children and adolescents with localised Ewing sarcoma, giving alternating chemotherapy every two weeks improved event-free survival compared with every three weeks. Longer follow-up of the same study supported sustained benefit. This evidence informs interval choice but does not make the individual national calendars identical to the study.

The trial population was mostly young and had localised Ewing sarcoma. It began with vincristine/doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide, whereas these national schedules begin with ifosfamide/etoposide; some doses and recovery thresholds also differ. Do not transfer Ewing outcomes to rhabdomyosarcoma, desmoplastic tumours, metastatic disease or every adult. Long-term follow-up is the same cohort, not an independent trial.

Sources: trial · nccp

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Can I restart after symptoms improve?

Only after the treating team confirms the new prescription. A normal test or recovered symptom does not erase previous severe toxicity or a permanent-stop decision.
