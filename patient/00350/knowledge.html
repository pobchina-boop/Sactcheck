When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new breathlessness, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea, dehydration, jaundice, unusual bleeding or sudden illness promptly. Do not wait for the next planned test.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

What each medicine does

Treat HER2-positive early breast cancer as part of a plan intended to reduce the risk of it returning.

HER2-targeted antibody

Pertuzumab

HER2-targeted antibody
Blocks HER2 pairing with other receptors

Pertuzumab binds a different area of HER2 from trastuzumab. It limits receptor pairing, so the two antibodies act together at different points on the same growth pathway.

HER2 pairing↓Pertuzumab blocks pairing↓Growth signals reduced

What to watch for
Keep heart-scan appointments. Report diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling or a treatment reaction.

Sources: nccp

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

HER2-targeted antibody
Targets HER2 growth signals

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

HER2 on cell surface↓Antibody binds↓Growth signalling reduced

What to watch for
Keep heart-scan appointments. Report diarrhoea, new breathlessness, swelling or a treatment reaction.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

IV pertuzumab and IV trastuzumab every 21 days. Before surgery: 3–6 cycles alongside the selected chemotherapy. After surgery: up to 18 cycles overall under the agreed adjuvant plan. If an anthracycline is used first, finish it before starting these antibodies with the taxane phase. Confirm the exact companion regimen and local access.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the active regimen, blood results, cardiac checks, medicines review, route and consent discussion.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Staff check the line, give prescribed premedicines and treatment, and observe for reactions. Tell them immediately about burning, pain, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit time and transport

First visits can take longer because of loading doses, premedicines and observation. Ask the unit about expected time, transport and any escort needed after sedation or line insertion.

Phase-specific visit pattern

Infusion and observation times differ by drug and first versus later doses; the unit confirms the full visit duration, including blood review and preparation.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Choose the companion chemotherapy

Confirm the exact chemotherapy regimen before issuing its anti-sickness or other supportive prescription. The minimal emetic risk of the two antibodies does not describe the whole combination.

Tests before treatment

FBC, kidney and liver profile at baseline and before every 21-day cycle. The team reviews the necessary results before a planned dose. The selected chemotherapy can require extra or more frequent checks.

Keep heart checks on the plan

An echo every 12 weeks is separate from blood tests or cancer imaging. Ask who books and reviews it.

Plan for symptoms

Keep your agreed contact numbers and instructions. New symptoms can need assessment before a routine appointment.

Treatment reference · 01

Why this treatment?

Two HER2-targeted antibodies may be combined with selected chemotherapy for higher-risk HER2-positive early breast cancer. Cancer extent, lymph nodes, hormone receptors, heart function and the chosen chemotherapy influence the plan. This card explains the antibody component; the companion chemotherapy needs its own regimen guide and assessment.

When scans, heart checks and bloods happen

Agree who books each test, who reviews the result and what happens if a check is overdue.

Blood tests

FBC, kidney and liver profile at baseline and before every 21-day cycle. The team reviews the necessary results before a planned dose. The selected chemotherapy can require extra or more frequent checks.

Sources: nccp

Heart ultrasound and ECG

Check heart pumping function before starting HER2 treatment. Repeat echo or MUGA every 12 weeks: around cycle 5, then cycle 9 if 21-day cycles stay on time. Earlier checks are needed for symptoms or abnormal findings. Count elapsed time from the first HER2 dose; changing phase or route does not restart the clock.

Sources: nccp · herceptin · esc

Scans and tumour markers

Baseline breast and nodal imaging, with staging tests appropriate to the diagnosis. Review response during preoperative treatment and before surgery. After local treatment, arrange breast surveillance; routine CT, PET or tumour-marker testing is not the usual screening plan for someone without symptoms.

Sources: early

Bone health and endocrine treatment

If the cancer is also hormone-receptor positive, endocrine treatment may form part of the wider plan. Treatment reference · 04

How surgery and radiotherapy fit

The selected indication sets the order: neoadjuvant treatment → surgery → selected postoperative treatment; adjuvant treatment starts after surgery. Finish any anthracycline phase before starting these antibodies with the taxane. Radiation timing is agreed with the treating teams.

Questions worth asking

Who arranges my tests?

Ask the team who books each test, who checks the result and who to contact if an appointment is overdue. This map does not book appointments.

What if I become unwell between visits?

Use the urgent contact plan for sudden illness, fever, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea or poor fluid intake. Do not wait for a scheduled blood test or scan.
