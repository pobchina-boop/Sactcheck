How this medicine supports your treatment

To control the cancer, relieve or delay symptoms, and help people live longer. This advanced-disease regimen does not promise a cure or an individual duration of benefit.

Trop-2-directed antibody–drug conjugate

Sacituzumab govitecan (Trodelvy)

Carries the topoisomerase-I inhibitor SN-38 using an antibody that recognises Trop-2
Antibody recognises Trop-2↓SN-38 payload is released↓deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) copying is disrupted

The antibody component recognises Trop-2 on cancer cells. Its linked SN-38 payload interferes with topoisomerase I, an enzyme involved in managing DNA during copying, which can damage dividing cells. Healthy tissues can also be affected, particularly blood-forming cells and the gut; being antibody-directed does not mean it is free of chemotherapy-type effects.

The most important early problems to act on are fever or infection with low neutrophils, diarrhoea and dehydration, persistent nausea/vomiting, and infusion reactions. Report symptoms early rather than waiting for a planned scan or clinic.

The exact treatment being discussed

To control the cancer, relieve or delay symptoms, and help people live longer. This advanced-disease regimen does not promise a cure or an individual duration of benefit.

Who this covers

NCCP00794a covers adults with unresectable or metastatic triple-negative breast cancer after at least two prior systemic therapies, including at least one for advanced disease. The NCCP entry specifies ECOG 0–1. Broader European licensed indications must be checked separately against the applicable regimen and reimbursement pathway.

NCCP 00794 v3 · official regimen · Trodelvy EU Summary of Product Characteristics, sections 4.2–4.6 and 5.1; checked 20 October 2026 · retrieved 25 October 2026

Dose and administration

10 mg/kg intravenously on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle; prescribed reductions may apply. The first infusion takes about three hours; later infusions usually take one to two hours if the earlier infusion was tolerated. Premedicines, checks and at least 30 minutes of observation after every infusion add to the visit. No take-home infusion pump is part of this regimen.

NCCP 00794 v3 · official regimen · Trodelvy EU Summary of Product Characteristics, sections 4.2–4.6 and 5.1; checked 20 October 2026 · retrieved 25 October 2026

Duration and decisions

Treatment continues while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable. There is no fixed number of cycles; the oncology team reviews benefit, scans and tolerability.

Individual safety and source differences

Blood-count rules differ between Day 1 and Day 8. Known reduced UGT1A1 activity may increase side effects, but routine genotype testing is not required by the EU product information. Important differences between the NCCP and current EU dose-modification wording are flagged for Consultant and oncology-pharmacy review; patients should never change the dose themselves.

Your wider treatment plan

Additional treatments depend on the confirmed setting and indication.

Advanced-disease scans

Response assessment in advanced breast cancer is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease dynamics, sites, symptoms and treatment. Less frequent monitoring may suit indolent disease. New symptoms or suspected progression need earlier assessment. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not an automatic three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO metastatic breast cancer guidelines

Tumour markers

If informative, particularly when elevated at baseline or disease is not readily measurable, tumour markers may supplement clinical assessment and imaging. A marker rise alone should not trigger a treatment change. There is no universal fixed marker timetable.

ESO-ESMO ABC 5 consensus: tumour-marker interpretation

Pregnancy, fertility and contraception

Discuss fertility before treatment. Effective contraception is needed during treatment and for six months after the final dose for women who could become pregnant, and for three months for male patients with female partners who could become pregnant. Do not breastfeed during treatment; the EU product information also advises against breastfeeding for one month after the last dose.

Practical support

Prevent nausea before both doses

This is a high-emetic-risk regimen. Preventive antiemetics are needed for both Day 1 and Day 8, with take-home medicines and clear instructions. The steroid used as an infusion premedicine also provides antiemetic cover, so the team must reconcile the total dose.

A written diarrhoea plan

Report diarrhoea early. The team assesses infection, dehydration and electrolyte loss, and supplies an appropriate antidiarrhoeal prescription. Follow the written oncology instructions; do not improvise repeated high doses of loperamide.

Blood-count support

G-CSF injections may be considered from cycle 1 if the risk of febrile neutropenia is increased, or after significant neutropenia. Product and timing depend on the two-infusion schedule and the prescribing plan.

Infusion-reaction prevention

The unit gives premedicines to reduce reaction risk and observes you during and after each infusion. Tell staff immediately if you feel different or unwell.

Early cramping or diarrhoea during an infusion

This can be an acute cholinergic effect and needs staff assessment. Atropine may be used for later infusions when appropriate; tell the team if you have glaucoma.

Taking tablets / attending treatment

ASCENT evidence in previously treated triple-negative breast cancer

This explains the study setting and its meaning. Ask the team how the evidence applies to you.

The primary analysis excluded baseline brain metastases; the overall-population estimates differ. Open-label design, selected trial fitness and previous-treatment requirements limit extrapolation. Group medians do not predict an individual patient's benefit. This trial does not establish eligibility for every broader current EU indication.
