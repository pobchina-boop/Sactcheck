Sacituzumab govitecan (Trodelvy)

Two treatment visits in each three-week cycle: Day 1, then Day 8. There is no planned infusion on Day 15. The next cycle starts after Day 21 if the team confirms it is appropriate.

Make treatment fit into daily life

The first infusion takes about three hours; later infusions usually take one to two hours if the earlier infusion was tolerated. Premedicines, checks and at least 30 minutes of observation after every infusion add to the visit. No take-home infusion pump is part of this regimen.

Plan two visits per cycle

Day 1 and Day 8 are treatment days. Allow time for blood review, premedicines, the infusion and at least 30 minutes of observation afterwards. The first infusion is longer than later ones.

Cannula or existing venous access

The team chooses suitable intravenous access. A port is not automatically required and this regimen has no take-home pump. If you already have a port, staff access and flush it according to local policy; report pain, redness or swelling.

Before leaving the unit

Confirm the Day 8 appointment, blood-test arrangements, nausea medicines, written diarrhoea instructions and the number to call if unwell. If G-CSF is prescribed, ask exactly which days to take it around both treatment visits.

If treatment is delayed

A low count or significant side effect can mean a delay or dose change. The team will give the revised plan; do not calculate a replacement infusion from this diagram.

If radiotherapy is also planned

Radiotherapy is not an automatic phase of this advanced-disease regimen. If needed for local symptoms or another reason, the oncology and radiotherapy teams agree its timing and any treatment interruption.

Who arranges each check?

Agree the due window, booking team, result reviewer and whom to contact if an appointment or result is missing. This reference page does not book or record a test.
