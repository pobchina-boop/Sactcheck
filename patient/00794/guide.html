Your practical treatment plan

Know the next step.

Taking treatment

Two treatment visits in each three-week cycle: Day 1, then Day 8. There is no planned infusion on Day 15. The next cycle starts after Day 21 if the team confirms it is appropriate.

The first infusion takes about three hours; later infusions usually take one to two hours if the earlier infusion was tolerated. Premedicines, checks and at least 30 minutes of observation after every infusion add to the visit. No take-home infusion pump is part of this regimen.

Checks and follow-up

Before the first treatment: full blood count, kidney and liver blood tests, glucose and electrolytes including magnesium, calcium and phosphate. Pregnancy testing is needed where relevant. ECG is added if clinically indicated. The team checks infection, medicines, previous low blood counts and whether preventive G-CSF injections are appropriate. Blood count, kidney and liver tests are checked before each cycle; the neutrophil count must also meet the separate Day 8 threshold before that infusion. Glucose and the listed electrolytes are checked every second cycle. Pregnancy testing is repeated before each cycle where relevant. Review diarrhoea, hydration, nausea, oral intake, fever and infection before each infusion. Scans follow the individual response-assessment plan.

Support between reviews

Prevent nausea before both doses

This is a high-emetic-risk regimen. Preventive antiemetics are needed for both Day 1 and Day 8, with take-home medicines and clear instructions. The steroid used as an infusion premedicine also provides antiemetic cover, so the team must reconcile the total dose.

A written diarrhoea plan

Report diarrhoea early. The team assesses infection, dehydration and electrolyte loss, and supplies an appropriate antidiarrhoeal prescription. Follow the written oncology instructions; do not improvise repeated high doses of loperamide.

Blood-count support

G-CSF injections may be considered from cycle 1 if the risk of febrile neutropenia is increased, or after significant neutropenia. Product and timing depend on the two-infusion schedule and the prescribing plan.

Infusion-reaction prevention

The unit gives premedicines to reduce reaction risk and observes you during and after each infusion. Tell staff immediately if you feel different or unwell.

Early cramping or diarrhoea during an infusion

This can be an acute cholinergic effect and needs staff assessment. Atropine may be used for later infusions when appropriate; tell the team if you have glaucoma.
