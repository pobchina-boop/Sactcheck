Active medicines: Sacituzumab govitecan (Trodelvy)

Emetogenic risk: High · sacituzumab govitecan

MedicineDose / routeWhen
Aprepitant125 mg orallyDay 1 before treatment
Ondansetron16 mg orallyDay 1 before treatmentReview QT risk, hepatic impairment, constipation and interacting medicines.
Dexamethasone12 mg orallyDay 1 before treatmentReconcile all steroid premedication; do not duplicate.
Olanzapine5 mg orally once dailyDays 1–4Review sedation, falls, glucose and QT risk. Avoid routine combination with metoclopramide because of additive extrapyramidal effects; select rescue individually.
Aprepitant80 mg orally once dailyDays 2–3
Dexamethasone8 mg orally once dailyDays 2–4Confirm suitability and total steroid exposure.
Metoclopramide10 mg orally up to three times dailyRescue use / duration to be completed by prescriberReview renal function and neurological contraindications; avoid automatic prolonged use.

Additional phase-specific support

  • This is a high-emetic-risk regimen. Preventive antiemetics are needed for both Day 1 and Day 8, with take-home medicines and clear instructions. The steroid used as an infusion premedicine also provides antiemetic cover, so the team must reconcile the total dose.
  • Report diarrhoea early. The team assesses infection, dehydration and electrolyte loss, and supplies an appropriate antidiarrhoeal prescription. Follow the written oncology instructions; do not improvise repeated high doses of loperamide.
  • G-CSF injections may be considered from cycle 1 if the risk of febrile neutropenia is increased, or after significant neutropenia. Product and timing depend on the two-infusion schedule and the prescribing plan.
  • The unit gives premedicines to reduce reaction risk and observes you during and after each infusion. Tell staff immediately if you feel different or unwell.
  • This can be an acute cholinergic effect and needs staff assessment. Atropine may be used for later infusions when appropriate; tell the team if you have glaucoma.
  • This is the high-risk national antiemetic template anchored to this infusion day. Day8 repeats prophylaxis only if treatment proceeds; do not print both scripts as simultaneous orders. Antihistamine/antipyretic infusion premedication and G-CSF are separate regimen-specific decisions.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
