Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + cisplatin

This combination aims to control metastatic non-squamous lung cancer, help symptoms and extend life. Your team weighs likely benefit, risks and what matters to you.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

Induction: all three medicines on Day 1 every 21 days for up to four cycles. Maintenance: cisplatin stops; pembrolizumab and pemetrexed may continue every 21 days while appropriate. The team agrees duration and review points.

Tests and checks

Confirm first-line metastatic non-squamous nsclc without egfr or alk alterations, ECOG 0–1 and adequate organ function. full blood count (FBC), renal/liver function, glucose, thyroid, hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg), hepatitis B core antibody and hepatitis C at baseline. Review autoimmune disease, immunosuppression, infection, previous pneumonitis and active/unstable central nervous system (CNS) disease. Record baseline breathing, bowel, skin and endocrine symptoms. Assess creatinine clearance (CrCl) and hearing/neuropathy; pre-existing neuropathy ≥2 or significant hearing impairment/tinnitus excludes this cisplatin combination. Check folate/B12 lead-in and hydration plan. FBC, renal/liver function and glucose before every cycle, including maintenance. Thyroid function every 3–6 weeks; immune-toxicity history and symptom review at every visit. Assess hearing and sensory neuropathy before each platinum-containing cycle; confirm active phase and stop cisplatin after induction. Response imaging follows the consultant plan; no fixed universal scan interval is specified in these National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) regimens. Immune toxicity can arise after the final pembrolizumab dose. Continue symptom vigilance, immunotherapy alert card and the team’s follow-up plan. Continue folic acid during pemetrexed and for 21 days after its last dose; B12 every three cycles while pemetrexed remains active.

Between treatment visits

The treatment bands show a reference 21-day cycle. Induction and maintenance contain different medicines. Symptom bands are broad educational illustrations, not measured daily risks or a prediction of when you will feel well; immune-related effects can occur after treatment ends.

Use the oncology emergency instructions for fever, shivers or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. New cough/breathlessness, severe diarrhoea, reduced urine, confusion or severe rash need prompt advice. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or major bleeding need emergency help. Immune symptoms may start after treatment stops.
