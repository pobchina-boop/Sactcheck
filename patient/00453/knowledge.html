When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Aim for lasting cancer control or cure in selected relapsed germ-cell cancer, using high-dose chemotherapy with your own stored stem cells.

Chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Damages cancer-cell deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)

Carboplatin is a platinum-containing chemotherapy. Inside cells, it forms chemical attachments to DNA, including links that stop DNA strands working normally. This disrupts copying and repair and can lead to cell death. Healthy cells can also be injured, especially blood-forming cells in the bone marrow. The kidneys help remove carboplatin, so kidney function is used to calculate the dose. AUC 5 describes a target drug exposure over time; it is not a fixed dose of 5 mg.

DNA strands↓Platinum links↓Copying disrupted

What to watch for
Low blood counts, nausea and vomiting can occur. An allergic reaction is also possible: tell the nurse immediately if you feel flushed, itchy, tight-chested or unwell during the drip.

Sources: nccp

Chemotherapy

Etoposide

Topoisomerase II chemotherapy
Interferes with DNA repair

Etoposide interferes with an enzyme used to copy and repair DNA. This can stop cancer cells growing and can also lower healthy blood counts.

DNA copying↓Repair interrupted↓Cell growth reduced

What to watch for
Report fever, bleeding, a sore mouth or feeling faint during an infusion.

Individual prescribed course

Carboplatin AUC7 is followed by etoposide 750 mg/m² on days −5, −4 and −3. Your stored stem cells are infused on day 0. Growth-factor injections start on day +1 and continue until the team confirms neutrophil recovery. These day numbers are relative to the stem-cell infusion.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day -5–-3: High-dose chemotherapy

Carboplatin AUC7 is followed by etoposide 750 mg/m² on days −5, −4 and −3. Your stored stem cells are infused on day 0. Growth-factor injections start on day +1 and continue until the team confirms neutrophil recovery. These day numbers are relative to the stem-cell infusion. Follow the transplant-team plan.

Day -2–-1: Between conditioning and rescue

Continue prescribed support and daily tests. This is not a period free of treatment risks. Report symptoms to the transplant team.

Day 0: Your stored stem cells are infused

The transplant service gives the infusion and monitors you. Tell staff immediately about any reaction.

Day 1–8: Blood-count recovery

Growth factor starts on day+1. Recovery may extend beyond this illustrated interval. Daily monitoring and infection precautions continue until the team changes the plan.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm admission, stored stem-cell availability, fertility discussion and the complete transplant-support prescription before conditioning.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Chemotherapy and stem-cell rescue take place in a specialist transplant service. Carboplatin runs over 30 minutes and etoposide over two hours on each treatment day. Preparation, fluid management and monitoring add time. Staff give stem-cell infusion premedication under the local plan.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

The transplant team arranges suitable intravenous access and line care. Record that you require irradiated cellular blood components and tell every treating service.

STEP 4

Home support

Prescribed anti-sickness treatment is needed across the high-dose days. The local transplant plan includes antiviral and antifungal prevention, mouth care, a proton-pump inhibitor and Pneumocystis pneumonia prevention. Co-trimoxazole starts only after engraftment; the team specifies the stop date. Irradiated cellular blood components are required from one week before conditioning until 12 months after stem-cell infusion.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

High-dose chemotherapy and stem-cell rescue · Einhorn 2007

In a retrospective series of 184 previously treated patients, 116 remained in complete remission without relapse during a median follow-up of 48 months. Three treatment-related deaths and three later cases of acute leukaemia were reported.

This was not a randomised comparison. It used carboplatin 700 mg/m², whereas NCCP 00453 uses AUC7. The study supports the treatment approach but its dose and outcomes must not be presented as identical to this protocol.

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Aim for lasting cancer control or cure in selected relapsed germ-cell cancer, using high-dose chemotherapy with your own stored stem cells. Your stem cells help restore blood-cell production after intensive chemotherapy; they are not donor cells. A second high-dose course may be considered after recovery, at least 28 days later, depending on response and toxicity. Later oral etoposide is only considered in selected responders after two courses and is not automatic.

When do I attend?

Carboplatin AUC7 is followed by etoposide 750 mg/m² on days −5, −4 and −3. Your stored stem cells are infused on day 0. Growth-factor injections start on day +1 and continue until the team confirms neutrophil recovery. These day numbers are relative to the stem-cell infusion.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
