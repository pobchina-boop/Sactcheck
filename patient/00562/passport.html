Brigatinib monotherapy · ALK-positive advanced NSCLC Brigatinib on its own for adults with ALK-positive advanced non-small-cell lung cancer: either after crizotinib (00562a) or before any ALK inhibitor (00562b). This is not an adjuvant course or a general pathway after every ALK inhibitor.
NCCP 00562 · version 4

Brigatinib Therapy

The usual starting plan is 90 mg once daily for seven days, then 180 mg once daily if your team confirms the increase. Some people need a different dose. Treatment continues while helping and tolerable, with regular reviews and no fixed number of cycles.

Your treatment and review passport

Bring this to reviews. Record symptoms, treatment changes and questions when helpful. You do not need to complete a daily diary or calculate a toxicity grade.

The four sheets are reusable review periods, not a prescribed number of cycles. Follow the treatment and rest days agreed with your team; do not calculate a restart from this sheet.

These patient-language prompts are not a validated questionnaire or a formal CTCAE assessment. Urgent symptoms override the form.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Contact your oncology team immediately for new or worsening breathlessness, cough or fever, especially during the first week but also later. Do not wait for a booked check. Urgent advice is also needed for fainting, a very slow heartbeat, chest pain, severe headache or vision changes, marked muscle pain/weakness, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, unusual thirst or frequent urination, jaundice or severe diarrhoea. Major breathing difficulty, collapse or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
