Brigatinib Therapy

Brigatinib is a targeted medicine intended to control ALK-positive advanced lung cancer. The choice depends on the cancer’s ALK result, previous treatment, your health and your priorities.

My schedule at a glance

Your treatment pattern

Continuous oral treatment with a seven-day lead-in

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

The usual starting plan is 90 mg once daily for seven days, then 180 mg once daily if your team confirms the increase. Some people need a different dose. Treatment continues while helping and tolerable, with regular reviews and no fixed number of cycles.

Tests and checks

Before starting: ALK result, blood count, kidney/liver tests, breathing-function assessment, electrocardiogram (ECG), blood pressure, fasting glucose, muscle enzyme creatine phosphokinase (CPK) and pancreatic enzymes. Review breathing symptoms on days 8 and 15, then monthly or sooner if needed. ECG and blood pressure are checked every two weeks initially, then monthly. Liver tests are every two weeks for the first three months, then as advised. Blood count, kidneys, fasting glucose, CPK and pancreatic enzymes are usually checked monthly. Scans follow your Consultant’s plan.

Between treatment visits

Relative treatment milestones, not equal time intervals or booked appointments. New symptoms can occur at any time.

Report breathing changes immediately. A normal recent blood test does not make a new symptom safe to ignore.

Contact your oncology team immediately for new or worsening breathlessness, cough or fever, especially during the first week but also later. Do not wait for a booked check. Urgent advice is also needed for fainting, a very slow heartbeat, chest pain, severe headache or vision changes, marked muscle pain/weakness, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, unusual thirst or frequent urination, jaundice or severe diarrhoea. Major breathing difficulty, collapse or severe chest pain needs emergency help. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms: 112 / 999.

Carry your treatment details

Brigatinib monotherapy · ALK-positive advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) Brigatinib on its own for adults with ALK-positive advanced non-small-cell lung cancer: either after crizotinib (00562a) or before any ALK inhibitor (00562b). This is not an adjuvant course or a general pathway after every ALK inhibitor.

Print, cut out and fold. Follow your treating hospital’s contact plan.
