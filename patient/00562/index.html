Brigatinib Therapy

Understand the treatment. Keep the plan manageable.

Brigatinib is a targeted medicine intended to control ALK-positive advanced lung cancer. The choice depends on the cancer’s ALK result, previous treatment, your health and your priorities.

Oral ALK-targeted therapy

Brigatinib

Blocks an abnormal ALK growth signal
ALK alteration drives growth↓Brigatinib blocks ALK signalling↓Cancer growth may slow

An altered ALK gene can drive cancer-cell growth. Brigatinib blocks the activity of this signal. Testing confirms whether this target is present; the medicine can also affect healthy tissues, so regular checks and early symptom reporting matter.

Report breathing changes immediately, especially in the first week. Follow the exact tablet strength and lead-in plan; check all new medicines with your pharmacist.

Hold / restart / stopReason-specific medical review; do not restart or increase yourself
Do not restart or increase brigatinib yourself. A break of 14 days or more for a reason other than a side effect normally needs a new seven-day lead-in before the previously tolerated dose. A break because of a side effect follows a different recovery plan. Early lung inflammation can prevent the usual increase to 180 mg.

Support throughout treatment

One written tablet plan

Keep the current dose, tablet strengths and next review clear. Ask the pharmacist about safe storage, handling and returning unused tablets. Do not share medicines.

Anti-sickness medicines only if needed

Brigatinib has minimal-to-low oral sickness risk. The oral NCCP guidance uses anti-sickness medicines as required; your team chooses a suitable medicine, dose and duration. Persistent vomiting still needs advice.

Food and other medicines

Take with or without food; avoid grapefruit. Check every prescription, herbal or non-prescription product, including St John’s wort, before starting or stopping it.

Skin and sunshine

Avoid prolonged sun exposure during treatment and for at least five days afterwards. Use protective clothing, a hat, broad-spectrum sun protection factor (SPF) 30 or higher sunscreen and lip balm. Report severe rash or sun reaction.

Symptoms and practical support

Keep the 24-hour oncology contact available. Ask early for help with symptoms, eating, tablet supply, work, travel or emotional wellbeing.

Your practical treatment guide

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Contact your oncology team immediately for new or worsening breathlessness, cough or fever, especially during the first week but also later. Do not wait for a booked check. Urgent advice is also needed for fainting, a very slow heartbeat, chest pain, severe headache or vision changes, marked muscle pain/weakness, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, unusual thirst or frequent urination, jaundice or severe diarrhoea. Major breathing difficulty, collapse or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
