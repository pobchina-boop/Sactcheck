How this medicine supports your treatment

Brigatinib is a targeted medicine intended to control ALK-positive advanced lung cancer. The choice depends on the cancer’s ALK result, previous treatment, your health and your priorities.

Oral ALK-targeted therapy

Brigatinib

Blocks an abnormal ALK growth signal
ALK alteration drives growth↓Brigatinib blocks ALK signalling↓Cancer growth may slow

An altered ALK gene can drive cancer-cell growth. Brigatinib blocks the activity of this signal. Testing confirms whether this target is present; the medicine can also affect healthy tissues, so regular checks and early symptom reporting matter.

Report breathing changes immediately, especially in the first week. Follow the exact tablet strength and lead-in plan; check all new medicines with your pharmacist.

Taking treatment at home

Your prescription is the individual plan.

Taking the tablets

Swallow the tablets whole with water, with or without food. Do not crush, chew or split them. Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit juice. If a dose is missed or you vomit after it, do not take an extra dose; take the next dose at its usual time. Contact the team for ongoing vomiting or difficulty keeping fluids or medicine down.

Missed or vomited dose

Checks before and during treatment

Some important reactions appear before the next appointment.

Baseline and ongoing checks

Before starting: ALK result, blood count, kidney/liver tests, breathing-function assessment, ECG, blood pressure, fasting glucose, muscle enzyme CPK and pancreatic enzymes. Review breathing symptoms on days 8 and 15, then monthly or sooner if needed. ECG and blood pressure are checked every two weeks initially, then monthly. Liver tests are every two weeks for the first three months, then as advised. Blood count, kidneys, fasting glucose, CPK and pancreatic enzymes are usually checked monthly. Scans follow your Consultant’s plan.

NCCP 00562 v4 · official regimen

Breathing needs particular attention

Most early pulmonary reactions happen in the first seven days, but lung inflammation can occur later. Report new cough, breathlessness or fever straight away; the team may pause treatment while checking the cause.

Other medicines, skin and family planning

Ask before changing other medicines or the brigatinib dose.

Interaction review

Some antibiotics, antifungals, antivirals, seizure medicines and herbal products can alter treatment. Your oncology pharmacist must review the complete medicine list. Do not change existing medicines yourself.

Sun protection

Avoid prolonged sun exposure during treatment and for at least five days afterwards. Use protective clothing, a hat, broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher sunscreen and lip balm. Report severe rash or sun reaction.

EMA Alunbrig product information and patient leaflet, current retrieved snapshot

Pregnancy, breastfeeding and fertility

Discuss fertility before treatment. Effective non-hormonal contraception is advised for women during treatment and for at least four months after the last dose; men with partners who could become pregnant need effective contraception during treatment and for at least three months afterwards. Tell the team promptly about possible pregnancy. Breastfeeding must stop during treatment.

Driving and daily tasks

Blurred vision, dizziness or fatigue can affect driving and machinery. Avoid these activities when affected and discuss persistent symptoms.

When to seek help

Follow your treating hospital’s urgent-contact instructions.

Call promptly

Contact your oncology team immediately for new or worsening breathlessness, cough or fever, especially during the first week but also later. Do not wait for a booked check. Urgent advice is also needed for fainting, a very slow heartbeat, chest pain, severe headache or vision changes, marked muscle pain/weakness, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, unusual thirst or frequent urination, jaundice or severe diarrhoea. Major breathing difficulty, collapse or severe chest pain needs emergency help.

When you call

Give the medicine, current dose, when symptoms started and when the last dose was taken. Say whether you can drink, pass urine and manage daily activities; do not delay calling to collect details.

Practical support

One written tablet plan

Keep the current dose, tablet strengths and next review clear. Ask the pharmacist about safe storage, handling and returning unused tablets. Do not share medicines.

Anti-sickness medicines only if needed

Brigatinib has minimal-to-low oral sickness risk. The oral NCCP guidance uses anti-sickness medicines as required; your team chooses a suitable medicine, dose and duration. Persistent vomiting still needs advice.

Food and other medicines

Take with or without food; avoid grapefruit. Check every prescription, herbal or non-prescription product, including St John’s wort, before starting or stopping it.

Skin and sunshine

Avoid prolonged sun exposure during treatment and for at least five days afterwards. Use protective clothing, a hat, broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher sunscreen and lip balm. Report severe rash or sun reaction.

Symptoms and practical support

Keep the 24-hour oncology contact available. Ask early for help with symptoms, eating, tablet supply, work, travel or emotional wellbeing.

Why this ALK-targeted medicine may be chosen

Clinical studies support brigatinib in selected ALK-positive advanced lung cancers before any ALK inhibitor and after crizotinib. The studies involved different treatment settings.

Trial averages cannot predict your outcome or establish which modern ALK inhibitor is best for you. Discuss your treatment choices and priorities.

Questions to ask

Why does the dose start lower?

The initial seven-day lead-in is part of the brigatinib schedule and helps reduce early pulmonary reactions. Only increase when the team confirms it is appropriate.

What if I miss or vomit a dose?

Do not take an extra dose. Take the next scheduled dose at the usual time. Contact the team for ongoing vomiting or inability to keep fluids or medicine down.

Can I restart my usual tablets after a break?

Do not restart or increase brigatinib yourself. A break of 14 days or more for a reason other than a side effect normally needs a new seven-day lead-in before the previously tolerated dose. A break because of a side effect follows a different recovery plan. Early lung inflammation can prevent the usual increase to 180 mg.

Does the first-week breathing warning stop after day seven?

No. Early reactions are especially important, but lung inflammation can also happen later. Report new or worsening breathing symptoms immediately.

How long will I take brigatinib?

There is no fixed number of cycles. Your team reviews benefit, side effects, scans and your priorities and agrees whether to continue, change or stop.

Can I use this page to decide whether tests are safe for treatment?

No. The clinical team combines the protocol, symptoms, previous dose changes and organ function before prescribing.

What belongs in the consent discussion?

Your treatment aim, expected benefit and uncertainty, other options including not proceeding, serious and common harms, lasting effects, fertility and practical concerns. You can ask for more time or explanation and reconsider the decision.

Explain the plan back: Can you explain the seven-day lead-in, who authorises a dose increase or restart, what to do after a missed or vomited dose, which checks are due and how to get urgent help?
