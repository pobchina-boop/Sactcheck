Your practical treatment plan

Know the next step.

Taking treatment

The usual starting dose is 450 mg once daily with food, every day without a planned rest week. Follow your own prescription. Treatment continues while it helps and side effects remain acceptable; there is no fixed course length.

Take at the same time daily with food. Swallow capsules whole with water; do not chew or crush. Take a missed dose unless the next is due within 12 hours; ask your pharmacist if timing is unclear. Never double a dose. If you vomit, do not replace the dose; take the next at its usual time. If illness prevents you eating with the dose, contact the team. Only the prescriber can arrange a different fasting dose. Do not alternate between fed and fasting schedules or change the dose yourself.

Checks and follow-up

Before starting: ALK test, blood count, kidney and liver tests, pancreatic enzymes, fasting glucose, ECG and symptom/medicine review. Blood count, kidneys and fasting glucose every 4 weeks; liver tests every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly. ECG after 1 month, then as needed. Extra heart, electrolyte, glucose or pancreatic tests may be needed for risks, symptoms or abnormal results; scans follow the consultant plan.

Support between reviews

Prevent and treat nausea

Ceritinib has moderate-to-high oral sickness risk. Ask for daily prevention and rescue instructions. Your team must check heart-rhythm and medicine interactions when choosing anti-sickness treatment; do not add medicines or a steroid course yourself.

Food and daily doses

If illness prevents you eating with the dose, contact the team. Only the prescriber can arrange a different fasting dose. Do not alternate between fed and fasting schedules or change the dose yourself.

Check interactions

Avoid grapefruit or grapefruit juice and St John’s wort. Check prescription, pharmacy and herbal medicines before use, including heart medicines, antibiotics, antifungals, seizure treatments and acid-reducing medicines. Do not stop a prescribed medicine without advice.

Pregnancy, fertility and feeding

Discuss pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding before treatment. Women who could become pregnant need highly effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months afterwards; hormonal contraception may be less effective, so agree a suitable method. Do not breastfeed during this treatment. Fertility effects are uncertain; ask about preservation if relevant.

Diarrhoea, fluids and daily life

Use the agreed diarrhoea treatment promptly. Report persistent symptoms, poor intake or dehydration. Ask for help with appetite, fatigue and practical support; avoid driving or machinery if fatigue or visual symptoms affect safety.
