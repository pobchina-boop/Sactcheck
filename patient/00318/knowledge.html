When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call urgently for fever or shaking chills, sudden illness, new or worsening breathlessness, chest pain, uncontrolled bleeding, severe or persistent diarrhoea, inability to drink, very little urine, a spreading or blistering rash, or swelling/wheeze during an infusion. Tell the team promptly about new tinnitus, hearing change, numbness, weakness or difficulty walking.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Your two chemotherapy medicines

Pemetrexed + Carboplatin is chemotherapy used for selected people with unresectable pleural mesothelioma or non-squamous non-small-cell lung cancer. The aim may be disease control or, within a separate combined preoperative plan, support for surgery. Ask your team to explain the expected benefit for your own diagnosis.

Antifolate chemotherapy

Pemetrexed

Alimta
Interrupts DNA building-block production

Pemetrexed blocks several enzymes that use folate to make the building blocks of DNA and RNA. This can slow or stop cancer-cell growth. Healthy cells can also be affected. Prescribed folic acid and vitamin B12 reduce important toxicities and are part of treatment.

Folate-dependent enzymes blocked↓DNA building blocks reduced↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Take the prescribed folic acid and attend for B12. Report mouth sores, diarrhoea, a rash, breathlessness or reduced urine; check before taking ibuprofen or other non-prescription medicines.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Damages DNA through platinum links

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing, but healthy tissues may also be affected. The dose is tailored using kidney function because the kidneys clear carboplatin.

Platinum reaches cells↓DNA links form↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report ringing in the ears, hearing changes, numbness or loss of function. Report infection or unusual bruising/bleeding promptly and describe any symptoms during the infusion.

Sources: nccp

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Treatment day

Pemetrexed is given into a vein over about 10 minutes, followed by carboplatin over about 30 minutes. Carboplatin dosing uses a verified kidney-function measure; the dose may change when kidney function changes. The whole visit also includes blood-result review, prevention medicines, preparation and observation. Tell the nurse immediately about rash, swelling, chest tightness, dizziness, pain or stinging near the drip.

Day 2–4: Follow your home prevention plan

Continue folic acid and the prescribed anti-sickness medicines. Your team combines the pemetrexed rash steroid and anti-sickness steroid into one clear schedule. Do not double up dexamethasone courses. Call if vomiting prevents medicines or fluids staying down.

Day 5–20: Recovery and symptom monitoring

Fatigue, bowel or mouth symptoms and low blood counts may develop between visits. Nerve or hearing symptoms can build across cycles. Use the emergency contact plan at any time; do not wait for the next treatment day.

Day 21: Review before the next cycle

The team checks blood counts, kidney and liver results and symptoms before deciding whether to continue, delay or change a dose. Bring your medicine list and symptom notes; confirm the next B12 injection and whether the treatment phase changes.

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm blood-test arrangements, transport, prescriptions, B12 booking and the 24-hour contact number. Tell the team if you are ill before travelling.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

The whole visit also includes blood-result review, prevention medicines, preparation and observation.

STEP 3

Access for treatment

Treatment may use a small cannula or an existing suitable central line. The team chooses access for your veins and treatment plan. There is no routine take-home infusion pump in this regimen; ask for line-care instructions if you have a line.

STEP 4

Home medicines

Keep your vitamin, steroid, anti-sickness and rescue plans together. Ask the pharmacist to resolve duplicate dexamethasone directions before taking them.

STEP 5

Support and review

Arrange help with travel and daily tasks if needed. Bring symptom notes, a medicines list and questions to review. Request more passport pages rather than treating the printed page count as a course limit.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Folic acid every day

The source uses 350–1000 micrograms daily: at least five doses during the seven days before the first pemetrexed, throughout treatment and for 21 days after the last pemetrexed. Follow your own prescription and tell the team about missed doses.

Vitamin B12 injection

The source uses 1000 micrograms into a muscle during the week before the first pemetrexed, then every three cycles. Later injections can be on treatment day. Confirm who books them.

One steroid schedule

Pemetrexed rash prevention uses dexamethasone-equivalent 4 mg twice daily on the day before, day of and day after treatment. The anti-sickness plan also uses steroids. Your prescriber/pharmacist must provide one reconciled schedule; do not add two courses together.

Preventing and treating sickness

This carboplatin combination has high sickness risk. Take prevention medicines as prescribed even when you feel well, and keep the team’s rescue plan available. Carboplatin AUC5 has a dedicated anti-sickness plan; do not automatically copy another high-risk chemotherapy plan.

Fluids and kidney protection

Ask for an individual drinking plan, especially with heart or kidney problems. Report inability to drink or reduced urine. Kidney tests help the team set the carboplatin dose. Extra tests may be needed after dehydration or a change in health.

Medicines and self-care

Check all prescriptions, painkillers, supplements and vaccines with the oncology team. Some anti-inflammatory medicines can affect pemetrexed clearance; do not start or stop them on this guide’s authority. Follow mouth-care and infection advice.

This guide’s scope

This guide covers pemetrexed and carboplatin chemotherapy. It does not include an immunotherapy medicine. If nivolumab, pembrolizumab or another medicine is also prescribed, obtain its separate regimen, consent, monitoring and emergency instructions. Mesothelioma and NSCLC have different aims and evidence; ask which indication is on your prescription.

How long treatment lasts

For pleural mesothelioma, the source describes up to 4–6 cycles. For advanced non-squamous NSCLC, it describes 4–6 combination cycles followed, when appropriate, by a separate pemetrexed maintenance plan (NCCP00222). The selected preoperative nivolumab combination uses three cycles and a separate immunotherapy plan (NCCP00849). Your team confirms which course applies and may stop or change it earlier.

Looking after yourself between visits

Ask for help early with symptoms and practical difficulties.

Symptoms and daily life

Rest when needed while keeping activity manageable. Ask for nutrition, fatigue, work, transport or financial support. Keep a short symptom record for review.

Sources: hse

Mouth and bowel care

Follow the team’s mouth-care plan. Report painful swallowing, persistent diarrhoea, vomiting or difficulty maintaining fluids; severe symptoms need urgent advice.

Sources: smpc · hse

Hearing and nerves

Report tinnitus, hearing change, numbness or difficulty with everyday tasks before further treatment. These effects can persist after treatment ends.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility preservation, contraception and pregnancy plans before treatment. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are exclusions in the regimen. Ask the team for the medicine-specific contraception period.

Evidence depends on the cancer and the exact medicines

Ask which study and treatment aim match your situation.

In advanced NSCLC, a randomised study found similar reported quality of life and survival with pemetrexed/carboplatin and gemcitabine/carboplatin, with less blood-count toxicity in the pemetrexed group. Mesothelioma evidence includes a smaller study without a randomised comparator. Cisplatin trial survival figures cannot be assigned to carboplatin. Your team should explain which evidence matches your diagnosis and alternatives.

Sources: gronberg · ceresoli

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

Does having more side effects mean treatment is working?

No. Side effects do not reliably show whether the cancer is responding. Report them so they can be treated. Your team assesses benefit using the tests and review plan appropriate to your cancer.

Why might a dose be delayed or reduced?

Your team may allow recovery, investigate a symptom or reduce the chance of lasting harm. Ask which drug changes, why, and when review is planned. A delay does not itself mean treatment has failed.

What should I say when calling?

Give the regimen name, last treatment date, symptoms and when they started. Explain whether you can eat, drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Give your temperature if available, but do not delay calling to collect details.

What belongs in the consent conversation?

The individual aim and likely benefit, uncertainty, reasonable alternatives including not proceeding, common and serious risks, lasting effects, fertility and practical concerns. You can ask for an interpreter, more time or another explanation and can reconsider your decision.

How many cycles should I use this passport for?

The passport provides reusable recording pages, not a prescribed course length. Your oncology team sets the duration and can provide extra pages.

Can I use this page to decide if blood results are safe?

No. Treatment decisions require the clinical protocol, symptoms, previous toxicity, organ function and the prescriber’s review. The patient page explains the checks and does not authorise treatment.

Is immunotherapy part of these two medicines?

This guide covers pemetrexed and carboplatin chemotherapy. It does not include an immunotherapy medicine. If nivolumab, pembrolizumab or another medicine is also prescribed, obtain its separate regimen, consent, monitoring and emergency instructions. Mesothelioma and NSCLC have different aims and evidence; ask which indication is on your prescription.

Will I always have four cycles?

For pleural mesothelioma, the source describes up to 4–6 cycles. For advanced non-squamous NSCLC, it describes 4–6 combination cycles followed, when appropriate, by a separate pemetrexed maintenance plan (NCCP00222). The selected preoperative nivolumab combination uses three cycles and a separate immunotherapy plan (NCCP00849). Your team confirms which course applies and may stop or change it earlier.

Why take vitamins during chemotherapy?

Folic acid and vitamin B12 lower important pemetrexed side effects. They are prescribed parts of this regimen. Tell the team promptly if they were missed.

What happens when the platinum chemotherapy stops?

The next step depends on your disease and treatment aim. Selected advanced NSCLC plans may move to pemetrexed maintenance under a separate regimen. Preoperative treatment leads to a surgical-team review; mesothelioma follow-up is agreed with the oncology team.
