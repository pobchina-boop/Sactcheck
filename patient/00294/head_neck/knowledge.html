When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology urgently for fever, sudden illness, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, persistent vomiting or inability to drink. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicine works

This treatment aims to slow cancer growth and help control symptoms. Your team explains the expected benefit, uncertainty and alternatives for your situation.

Kinase inhibitor

Sorafenib

Oral targeted treatment
Blocks growth and blood-vessel signalling

Sorafenib blocks several enzymes involved in signals that help cancer cells grow and new blood vessels form. It can also affect healthy blood vessels, skin and thyroid hormone control.

Growth signals blocked↓Blood-vessel signals reduced↓Cancer growth slowed

Day 1

Every day, including weekends

Take with water, without food or with a low- or moderate-fat meal. For a high-fat meal, take sorafenib at least 1 hour before or 2 hours afterwards. Follow the prescribed morning and evening doses; never change the dose yourself.

Your next step: Follow your prescription. A hold or restart must be agreed with the team.

Treatment continues daily across review dates unless your team tells you to hold or stop. No automatic rest week is scheduled.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Preventing and treating sickness

This is minimal-to-low oral sickness risk; the team agrees rescue medicine if needed.

Medicine-specific support

Tell the team before surgery or dental procedures. Report painful palms or soles early; skin care and early dose review can help prevent severe hand-foot reactions.

Mouth care, nutrition and daily life

Follow the unit’s mouth-care plan. Report pain, poor intake or weight loss early; ask for dietetic support. Pace activity, rest and work around symptoms.

Urgent symptoms

Call oncology urgently for fever, sudden illness, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, persistent vomiting or inability to drink. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure: call 112/999.

Medicine and interaction review

Bring all prescription, non-prescription and herbal medicines. Check with the oncology pharmacist before starting or stopping anything.

What the evidence can tell you

Research supports a treatment discussion; it cannot predict one person’s outcome.

DECISION, a randomised placebo-controlled study in radioactive-iodine-refractory differentiated thyroid cancer, reported median progression-free survival of 10.8 versus 5.8 months (hazard ratio 0.587; 95% CI 0.454–0.758). The cited overall-survival analysis did not show a statistically significant difference. Progression-free survival measures time without progression or death, not a predicted lifespan.
