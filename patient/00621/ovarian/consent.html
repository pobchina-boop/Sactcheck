Treatment and purpose

After platinum chemotherapy has stopped controlling metastatic ovarian cancer, weekly paclitaxel can shrink or slow the cancer and help relieve symptoms. 00621c covers second-line metastatic ovarian cancer after failure of platinum-containing treatment. This weekly schedule is outside the Irish product licence.

Treatment schedule. Paclitaxel over one hour on days 1, 8 and 15 of a 28-day cycle. Day 22 is a planned week off. The next cycle begins on day 29 if your team confirms it.

Treatment discussion and decision

Weekly paclitaxel for ovarian cancer · three visits per cycle

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

Some participants with previously treated ovarian cancer had tumour shrinkage with paclitaxel. This was a small, single-arm study. It cannot show a survival advantage or establish the best dose or schedule for you.

The prescriber will discuss the medicines on your individual plan. Discuss the possibility of persistent nerve symptoms, infection, hair loss and a severe infusion reaction.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call the 24-hour oncology number immediately for fever, chills, bleeding or feeling suddenly unwell. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, breathlessness, dizziness, throat tightness or pain at the infusion site. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
