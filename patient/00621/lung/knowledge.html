When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call the oncology contact number immediately for fever, shivers or sudden illness, severe or new breathing difficulty, chest pain, fainting, severe abdominal pain or persistent diarrhoea. Tell staff immediately about infusion symptoms. Report new numbness, weakness, painful skin changes, swelling or visual changes promptly; do not wait for the next cycle.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Your medicines and what they do

Treatment aims to control small-cell lung cancer after previous therapy and may help symptoms. The evidence is limited, so discuss likely benefit, burdens and alternatives with the team.

Taxane chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Taxol (conventional paclitaxel)
Locks the cell division machinery

Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues.

What to watch for
Report tingling or numbness before the next weekly dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: First visit of this cycle

Review symptoms, blood results and premedication before the infusion. Tell the nurse straight away about itching, flushing, throat or chest tightness, back pain, dizziness or breathing change. Check transport if premedication makes you drowsy and confirm the next weekly visit.

Day 2–7: Between weekly doses

Aches, fatigue, nausea or bowel symptoms may occur. Tingling may be mild at first but matters before the next dose. Record what symptoms stop you doing. Contact the team for new or worsening numbness, balance trouble, marked weakness or uncontrolled symptoms. Fever or sudden illness needs immediate contact.

Day 8: Second weekly review

This dose requires its own symptom and blood-test review. Describe the worst symptoms since the previous visit, not only how you feel today. Report sensory changes before the infusion and confirm your premedication instructions.

Day 9–14: Keep tracking function

Repeated treatment can increase nerve symptoms and fatigue. Check practical tasks such as buttons, writing and walking. Use the symptom passport to show changes. Seek early advice rather than waiting until numbness is severe.

Day 15: Third weekly review

The team decides whether treatment can proceed using the current assessment. Confirm your next review and the planned treatment-free fourth week.

Day 16–21: After the third visit

Symptoms may settle or continue. An interval between appointments does not rule out infection or a treatment complication. Use the urgent contact plan if unwell and bring your record to the next review.

Day 22: Planned week off

There is no Day 22 infusion in NCCP 00621. This interval does not guarantee that blood counts or symptoms have recovered. Continue your urgent contact plan and confirm the next cycle appointment.

Day 23–28: Complete the cycle record

Record symptoms and functional changes, including those that improve before the next appointment. Treatment continuation depends on benefit, toxicity and your priorities. Check the next cycle date and review plan. Do not treat a gap in visits as a guarantee of recovery.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Paclitaxel is given on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle. Day 22 is a planned week off, not a fourth infusion. Treatment continues while helping and tolerated; there is no fixed cycle limit.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm the next appointment on Days 1, 8 or 15. There is no Day 22 dose. Blood results and symptom review are needed before every dose.

STEP 2

At the unit

Paclitaxel is given through a cannula or suitable existing IV line over about one hour. The appointment also includes checks, preparation, premedication and observation and is longer than the infusion itself. This regimen has no routine take-home pump.

STEP 3

At home

Keep the contact number, medicine list and symptom record available. Ask who will arrange your next blood test, scan and review. Sedating medicines may affect driving.

Planned course

Paclitaxel is given on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28-day cycle. Day 22 is a planned week off, not a fourth infusion. Treatment continues while helping and tolerated; there is no fixed cycle limit.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Premedication

Premedication reduces infusion-reaction risk. The first-dose and later-dose plans may differ under the NCCP weekly pathway. The team must reconcile your exact product and reaction history; do not omit premedication yourself.

Nausea, bowel symptoms and pain

Use your individual anti-sickness and pain plan. Contact the team if symptoms are uncontrolled or you cannot drink. Antihistamines and other support medicines may cause drowsiness; arrange transport if advised.

Nerves and daily activities

Report tingling, numbness or weakness early, including effects on buttons, writing, walking or balance. Protect hands and feet and take care with hot surfaces if sensation changes.

Infection and blood counts

Each treatment needs its own blood and symptom review. A growth-factor injection may be prescribed after individual risk assessment; follow its separate instructions. Fever or sudden illness needs immediate contact on any day.

What the evidence can tell us

A small uncontrolled study found some tumour responses to weekly paclitaxel in previously treated SCLC. The study used six weekly doses followed by two weeks without treatment, which differs from this NCCP schedule. It cannot establish a survival advantage over other treatment or supportive care.

Treatment reference · 02

Checks through treatment

Your team arranges tests, reviews results and confirms each dose.

Blood tests and symptoms

Blood count, kidney and liver tests at baseline and before each treatment. Review symptoms, function, neuropathy and infusion reactions before every dose. Scans follow the consultant’s individual plan.

Sources: nccp

Heart and breathing

Check observations during infusion, especially early in the infusion. Significant conduction problems require a specialist cardiac-monitoring plan. Routine serial echocardiograms are not imposed for this monotherapy.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Response review

The consultant agrees when scans and other disease checks are due. New or worsening symptoms may need assessment sooner. There is no automatic fixed scan interval in this regimen.

Sources: nccp
