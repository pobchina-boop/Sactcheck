01 / treatment identity

What this treatment is doing

First-line treatment for metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer without EGFR or ALK-positive mutations, when this regimen is appropriate.

Pembrolizumab

Helps immune cells stay active against cancer by blocking an immune stop signal.

Pemetrexed

Disrupts the building blocks cells need to copy DNA.

Carboplatin

Damages DNA, making it harder for cancer cells to grow and divide.

Regimen-specific point
Immune-related side effects can begin during treatment or after it stops. Do not wait for the next cycle if a new symptom is worrying.
02 / treatment journey

How the schedule fits together

Before cycle 1

Blood tests, kidney function, glucose and thyroid testing; folic acid and vitamin B12 support for pemetrexed.

Cycles 1-4 · day 1

Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + carboplatin every 21 days.

Between cycles

Watch for immune symptoms and chemotherapy effects; they can occur at any time.

After cycle 4

Carboplatin stops if the plan is followed and disease/toxicity allow.

Maintenance

Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed every 21 days until progression or unacceptable toxicity, according to the treatment plan.

A delay, dose change or omitted medicine can be appropriate. Your team should explain what changed and why.

04 / monitoring and support

What helps make treatment safer

Before and during treatment

  • Take folic acid exactly as prescribed while on pemetrexed and for the required period after it stops.
  • Vitamin B12 is given before pemetrexed and then at protocol-defined intervals.
  • Blood count, kidney/liver profile and glucose are checked before each cycle; thyroid tests are repeated during treatment.

Bring to each review

  • Your current medicines, including non-prescription products.
  • A note of new symptoms, when they started and what they stopped you doing.
  • Any temperature, blood-pressure or bowel-count readings your team asked you to record.
  • Questions about the purpose of treatment, scan/review timing or changes to the plan.
05 / when to call

Do not wait for the next appointment

Use your oncology SOS instructionsCall oncology urgently for fever, new breathlessness, persistent diarrhoea, jaundice, severe rash, marked weakness, confusion or any rapidly worsening symptom. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call 112/999.

If your team has given you different local thresholds or emergency instructions, follow those instructions.

06 / treatment passport

One page for each cycle

The Treatment Passport is a simple cycle-by-cycle record to help you describe what you actually experienced between treatments. Each cycle has its own page. Each active medicine has its own symptom table, with rows for patient-experienced symptoms and columns that translate CTCAE severity into plain language.

You do not need to diagnose the cause or decide a formal CTCAE grade. Tick the description that best matches the worst the symptom became, bring the passport to review, and use the red-flag instructions immediately if you become acutely unwell.
