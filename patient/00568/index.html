01 · your treatment at a glance

One regimen, explained in context

This NCCP regimen is used first-line for metastatic non-squamous non-small-cell lung cancer without EGFR or ALK alterations, when it is appropriate for the individual patient.

RegimenNCCP 00568 v5
CycleEvery 21 days
Cycles 1–4Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + carboplatin
After inductionPembrolizumab + pemetrexed may continue
Treatment settingSystemic anti-cancer therapy
MonitoringBlood counts, kidney/liver tests and treatment-specific review
Why these medicines are used together

The phase III KEYNOTE-189 study compared pemetrexed + platinum chemotherapy with or without pembrolizumab in previously untreated metastatic non-squamous NSCLC. Adding pembrolizumab improved overall survival and progression-free survival.

Evidence does not predict an individual patient’s outcome. Your oncologist can explain what benefit is realistic in your situation.

02 · how the medicines work

Three medicines. Different jobs.

The medicines are given together because they attack the cancer in different ways. The diagrams are simplified explanations rather than anatomical pictures.

Immunotherapy

Pembrolizumab

Keytruda
Releases an immune brake

Cancer can use a stop signal to quieten immune cells. Pembrolizumab blocks PD-1, helping immune cells stay active against the cancer.

Immune cell→PD-1 brake blocked→Immune attack can continue

Important: activating the immune system can also inflame healthy organs. These effects can start during treatment or after treatment has stopped.

Chemotherapy

Pemetrexed

Alimta
Disrupts DNA building

Cells need chemical building blocks to copy DNA. Pemetrexed blocks enzymes involved in making those building blocks, making it harder for cancer cells to divide.

DNA building blocks→Supply disrupted→Cell division slows

Support matters: folic acid, vitamin B12 and prescribed steroid support are part of reducing particular pemetrexed toxicities.

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

AUC 5 in this regimen
Damages cancer-cell DNA

Carboplatin binds to DNA and damages it, making it harder for cancer cells to copy or repair their genetic instructions.

DNA strands→Platinum damage→Copying is disrupted

Dose: AUC dosing is calculated by the treating team using kidney function and other required treatment information.

03 · what treatment looks like

What to expect — without pretending every cycle follows a clock

The regimen has a 21-day cycle, but symptoms and blood-count changes do not reliably happen on a particular day. This is the treatment pathway rather than a prediction of your personal side effects.

Before cycle 1

Blood tests and treatment review. Pemetrexed support includes folic acid and vitamin B12; prescribed steroids may also be used.

Cycles 1–4 · Day 1

Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + carboplatin are given on Day 1 of a 21-day cycle.

Between treatments

Take supportive medicines as directed. Report new symptoms rather than waiting for the next visit.

After cycle 4

Carboplatin usually stops if the treatment plan proceeds as intended and treatment remains appropriate.

Maintenance

Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed may continue every 21 days according to response, toxicity and the agreed plan.

Patterns you may notice

Treatment day

Infusion reactions are uncommon but can happen while treatment is being given. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, itching, chest tightness or feeling suddenly unwell.

Early after treatment

Nausea, appetite change, tiredness or bowel symptoms can occur. Use the anti-sickness and other supportive medicines you were prescribed.

Between visits

Blood counts can fall between treatments. You cannot judge your count from the date or from how well you feel.

At any time

Immune-related side effects can occur on any day and may begin after pembrolizumab has stopped. New symptoms deserve attention.

There are no guaranteed “safe days”. Fever, infection and immune toxicity should be acted on because of the symptom, not because of where you are in the cycle.

05 · monitoring and supportive care

Small supportive steps are part of the regimen

Folic acid + vitamin B12

These are part of pemetrexed supportive care. Take folic acid exactly as prescribed and attend for vitamin B12 when arranged.

Steroids + anti-sickness treatment

Follow your written prescription. Steroids can help reduce pemetrexed rash risk and antiemetics are used to prevent or treat nausea and vomiting.

Blood and organ-function tests

Blood counts and kidney/liver tests help the team decide whether treatment is safe to give. Treatment-specific endocrine and other monitoring is added when relevant.

Bring symptoms to the review

Use your Treatment Passport or your own notes. Record what happened, when it started and what it stopped you doing rather than trying to diagnose the cause yourself.

08 · questions patients often ask

Useful questions for the next conversation

How will we know whether treatment is helping?

Ask what the treatment is aiming to achieve for you, when scans or other reviews are planned and what would lead to continuing, changing or stopping treatment. How you feel does not reliably tell you whether the cancer is responding.

Why might a cycle be delayed?

A delay may give time to recover from toxicity, investigate a new symptom or allow blood results to improve. Ask what needs to recover and when the next review will happen.

What happens after the first four cycles?

In the standard regimen pathway, carboplatin stops after four induction cycles and pembrolizumab + pemetrexed may continue as maintenance if appropriate. Your actual plan is confirmed by your oncology team.

Does having more side effects mean the treatment is working better?

No. Side-effect severity is not a reliable measure of treatment benefit. Report symptoms so they can be assessed and treated appropriately.

Can immune side effects happen later?

Yes. Immune-related effects can begin during treatment and can also occur after pembrolizumab has stopped. Tell healthcare professionals that you have received immunotherapy when seeking care for a new symptom.
