SACTCHECK · PATIENT TREATMENT PASSPORT

Pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + carboplatin

Lung · NCCP 00568 · version 5

What this passport is

This is a cycle-by-cycle record of the symptoms you actually experience while receiving treatment. It is designed to help you describe patterns clearly when you speak to the day ward, clinic, GP or emergency team.

Each treatment cycle has its own page. The medicines being given in that phase each have their own table so you can see which symptoms are particularly relevant to that agent.

How to use it

  • Write the cycle date at the top of the page.
  • For each symptom, tick or circle the description that best matches the worst it was during that cycle.
  • If a symptom appears in more than one medicine table, it is fine to mark it more than once.
  • Bring the passport to your next treatment / review.
Why the tables look different from a standard CTCAE chart

CTCAE is a clinician grading system. This passport keeps the CTCAE severity boundaries as a reference, but rewrites them in patient language and uses patient-experienced symptoms rather than organ names. Your oncology team makes the formal grade.

What belongs in the tables

The cycle pages prioritise common, patient-observable toxicities linked to each active medicine. Less common but important immune toxicities and laboratory abnormalities are still covered in the treatment guide / portal and in the urgent red-flag section.

Red flags override the grading table

Do not wait until the end of the cycle or try to decide a grade if you develop fever / shivers, severe or rapidly worsening breathlessness, chest pain / fainting, jaundice / dark urine, very reduced urine / new swelling, severe headache / confusion / seizure, or another rapidly worsening symptom. Contact oncology urgently. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call 112/999.
