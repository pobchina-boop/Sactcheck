Vemurafenib alone — advanced melanoma

To control advanced BRAF V600-mutated melanoma with a daily oral targeted medicine; benefit and tolerability are reviewed for the individual.

Advanced melanoma · NCCP 00102 v6 · Document revised 6 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

This is vemurafenib monotherapy. It is not the cobimetinib combination and has no cobimetinib seven-day break. The team must plan any radiotherapy because BRAF treatment can increase radiation-related reactions.

My overall treatment

Vemurafenib twice daily, about 12 hours apart, every day. A 28-day review cycle does not mean a planned tablet-free week.

  1. Treatment courseDaily tablets; no planned week off.

A 28-day reference calendar

Day 1–28At home each day

Timing may change after review. Side effects can occur on any day; there are no guaranteed safe days.

Between treatments

  • Use sun protection; report new skin lesions, eye changes, fainting or severe rash. Missed dose: only take if at least four hours before next; no extra after vomiting.

Bring medicine list, test dates and actual oncology/out-of-hours numbers. Example numbers must be replaced.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Vemurafenib

The electrical rhythm can change; ECG and blood chemistry checks help detect risk. Palpitations or fainting need prompt assessment.

Vemurafenib

Selected effects, not every possible effect or a prediction. Blood-count, skin and line markers are schematic. Number and text carry meaning; colour is decorative.

Contact the oncology team promptly for fainting or palpitations, eye pain or blurred vision, a blistering or rapidly spreading rash, severe sunburn, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, reduced urine or yellow skin or eyes. Report new or changing skin growths even when the melanoma is responding.
