Your treatment reference

Mirvetuximab soravtansine for FRα-positive ovarian, fallopian tube or peritoneal cancer

Your treatment and how the medicines work

The treatment aims to shrink or slow the cancer and help with symptoms. A test on the tumour shows whether enough cells have the target for this medicine.

Mirvetuximab soravtansine · Elahere

Antibody-drug conjugate. An antibody recognises folate receptor alpha on some cancer cells and carries a medicine inside them that interferes with cell division. Call the team the same day about blurred vision, painful or light-sensitive eyes. New breathlessness or cough also needs urgent assessment.

Your infusion schedule

First infusion · cycle 1 day 1

After blood and eye checks, receive four premedicines. The nurse starts the drip slowly and monitors for reactions.

Later infusions · day 1 every 21 days

Tell the team about eye or nerve symptoms before each dose. Premedicines continue. The infusion can run at the highest previously tolerated rate.

Common effects and what to do

What you may notice

Dry eyes, blurred vision, tiredness, diarrhoea and nausea can affect usual activities. Contact the team early about any change in sight, including between infusions.

Serious effects: when to call

Corneal or vision changes · Mirvetuximab soravtansine

Call the same day for blurred vision, painful or very dry eyes, or sensitivity to light. Seek urgent help for sudden loss of vision.

Lung inflammation · Mirvetuximab soravtansine

Report new breathlessness or cough immediately. Severe breathing difficulty needs emergency help.

Nerve injury · Mirvetuximab soravtansine

Tell the team about tingling, numbness, pain or trouble buttoning clothes or walking.

Nausea and bowel changes · Mirvetuximab soravtansine

Use your prescribed sickness medicines. Phone if vomiting or diarrhoea stops you drinking.

Infusion reaction · Mirvetuximab soravtansine

Tell the nurse at once about flushing, wheeze, chest tightness or faintness during the drip.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Contact oncology promptly for new eye pain, light sensitivity or blurred vision, new cough, breathlessness, fever or shaking chills. Sudden visual loss or severe breathing difficulty needs emergency assessment. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Planning your visits

Appointments

One infusion on day 1 of each 21-day cycle. The team reviews benefit and side effects each cycle. Treatment continues until the cancer progresses or side effects become unacceptable.

On the day

You receive medicines to reduce sickness and infusion reactions before the drip. The first infusion starts slowly and may speed up in steps while the nurse checks you. The dose is based on height and weight. Subsequent infusions may use the rate you previously tolerated.

Between visits

Continue your prescribed lubricating drops. Avoid contact lenses and call about new eye symptoms before your next infusion.

What the research found

In this study about 42 in 100 people on mirvetuximab had tumour shrinkage, compared with about 16 in 100 on the chemotherapy choices. People receiving mirvetuximab lived longer as a group.

Randomised open-label trial in a biomarker-selected population. These group estimates cannot predict individual benefit or be applied to FRα-low, platinum-sensitive or differently pretreated disease.
