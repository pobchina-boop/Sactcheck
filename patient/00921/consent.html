Mirvetuximab soravtansine for FRα-positive ovarian, fallopian tube or peritoneal cancer

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

The treatment aims to shrink or slow the cancer and help with symptoms. A test on the tumour shows whether enough cells have the target for this medicine. For adults whose high-grade serous epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer resists platinum, expresses folate receptor alpha at the required level and has had one to three prior systemic regimens.

Treatment schedule. One infusion on day 1 of each 21-day cycle. The team reviews benefit and side effects each cycle. Treatment continues until the cancer progresses or side effects become unacceptable.

Treatment discussion and decision

Mirvetuximab soravtansine for FRα-positive ovarian, fallopian tube or peritoneal cancer

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

In this study about 42 in 100 people on mirvetuximab had tumour shrinkage, compared with about 16 in 100 on the chemotherapy choices. People receiving mirvetuximab lived longer as a group. Randomised open-label trial in a biomarker-selected population. These group estimates cannot predict individual benefit or be applied to FRα-low, platinum-sensitive or differently pretreated disease.

Discuss tumour receptor testing, the expected benefit and alternatives, injury to the eye surface, lung inflammation, nerve damage and infusion reactions. Ask about fertility and effective contraception during treatment and for 7 months after the last dose.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Contact oncology promptly for new eye pain, light sensitivity or blurred vision, new cough, breathlessness, fever or shaking chills. Sudden visual loss or severe breathing difficulty needs emergency assessment. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
