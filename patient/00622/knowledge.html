When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Help control advanced urothelial cancer and its symptoms.

Chemotherapy

Gemcitabine

Antimetabolite chemotherapy
Interferes with deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) building

Gemcitabine interferes with making DNA, which dividing cancer cells need. Healthy blood-forming cells and other tissues can also be affected.

DNA building↓Replication disrupted↓Dividing cells affected

What to watch for
Report fever, unusual bleeding, breathlessness, rash, swelling or reduced urine promptly.

Sources: nccp

Platinum chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Damages DNA through platinum links

Cisplatin forms links in DNA that interfere with copying and repair. This helps stop cancer cells dividing, but healthy tissues may also be affected. Kidney protection with fluids and monitoring is central to cisplatin treatment.

Platinum reaches cells↓DNA links form↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report ringing in the ears, hearing changes, numbness or loss of function. Tell the team if you cannot drink, are passing less urine or become swollen or breathless during fluids.

Sources: nccp

Advanced urothelial cancer · split-dose schedule

Both medicines on days 1 and 8 · every 21 days

Individual prescribed course

Gemcitabine and cisplatin are both given on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle, usually for four to six cycles. Cisplatin is split into two 35 mg/m² doses; do not use another GemCis calendar.

NCCP 00622 uses cisplatin 35 mg/m² on both days 1 and 8. Its impaired-renal-function indication and creatinine clearance (CrCl) <60 exclusion need clinical reconciliation; a split dose does not itself prove cisplatin is safe.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm two cisplatin visits per cycle and how the home anti-sickness courses fit each visit.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

At each treatment visit gemcitabine runs into a vein over 30 minutes and cisplatin over 60 minutes, with prescribed fluids and support medicines. Your unit confirms visit length.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

Suitable intravenous access is needed. This is not a routine take-home infusion-pump schedule.

STEP 4

Home support

Use one written anti-sickness plan, including medicines before treatment, tablets for home and rescue treatment. Your team reconciles steroids, interactions and any stomach-protection medicine from your local prescription. Cisplatin needs prescribed intravenous fluids and electrolyte monitoring/replacement. Follow your own fluid advice; tell the team about heart failure or fluid restriction. Review kidney function, hearing, nerves and other medicines before treatment. Both day 1 and day 8 contain cisplatin and need their own prescribed support course.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Evidence context for GemCis

Gemcitabine and cisplatin have randomised evidence in advanced urothelial cancer. The pivotal 2000 trial used a 28-day schedule with cisplatin 70 mg/m² on one day; its results provide background but do not directly establish the benefit or safety of this split-dose 21-day schedule.

No response or survival percentage from the different pivotal schedule is presented as an estimate for this split-dose regimen. Treatment suitability remains an individual clinical decision.

Sources: trial · nccp

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Help control advanced urothelial cancer and its symptoms. Treatment choice depends on kidney function, hearing, nerves, fitness and previous treatment. The source contains inconsistent renal eligibility wording, so the treating team must resolve suitability before prescribing.

When do I attend?

Gemcitabine and cisplatin are both given on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle, usually for four to six cycles. Cisplatin is split into two 35 mg/m² doses; do not use another GemCis calendar.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
