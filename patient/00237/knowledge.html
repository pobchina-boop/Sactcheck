When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. If your temperature is 38°C or higher, stop dabrafenib and contact the oncology team immediately under the current product information; do not restart on your own. Also call for shaking chills, dizziness, reduced urine, severe abdominal pain, eye pain or changed vision, or a blistering or spreading rash. Infection and medicine-related fever both need consideration.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

BRAF targeted treatment

Dabrafenib

Tafinlar
Blocks an altered BRAF signal

Dabrafenib inhibits the altered BRAF V600 protein involved in melanoma growth. This guide describes dabrafenib alone for advanced melanoma. It does not include a second targeted medicine or a postoperative one-year treatment schedule.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1–28: At home each day

The usual starting dose is 150 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart. Take capsules at least one hour before or at least two hours after food. Swallow whole with water. Use the prescribed strength and dose, and check the label again after any interruption or dose change. Skip a missed dose if the next dose is due in less than six hours. Do not take two doses together to make up for it. If you vomit after a dose, do not take a replacement dose; take the next dose when due. The team must give instructions for restarting after fever or another treatment hold.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Taking or receiving treatment

The usual starting dose is 150 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart. Take capsules at least one hour before or at least two hours after food. Swallow whole with water. Use the prescribed strength and dose, and check the label again after any interruption or dose change.

STEP 2

Missed, vomited or held treatment

Skip a missed dose if the next dose is due in less than six hours. Do not take two doses together to make up for it. If you vomit after a dose, do not take a replacement dose; take the next dose when due. The team must give instructions for restarting after fever or another treatment hold.

STEP 3

Appointments and supply

Before each visit, check four things: the blood-test booking, how you will get there, your medicine supply and any help you need. Ask who will review the results and tell you whether the next dose can go ahead.

STEP 4

Paper copies and contacts

Keep the handbook, short guide and treatment card in one place. Ask your team to fill in the real daytime and emergency numbers and print a replacement when the plan changes. A phone is optional.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Supportive medicines and symptom plan

NCCP classifies dabrafenib as minimal-to-low emetogenic risk; anti-sickness treatment follows local and individual need. Agree a written fever plan and keep a thermometer and actual urgent-contact number. The team may prescribe fever treatment and hydration advice after assessment; fever medicine is not a substitute for contacting them. Anti-sickness treatment is selected individually.

Skin and daily care

Inspect skin and report new lumps, persistent warty growths, sores or changing moles. Report painful thickening or redness of palms and soles before it limits walking or hand use. Protect skin from the sun and attend monthly skin reviews.

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Use effective contraception during dabrafenib alone and for two weeks after the last dose. Hormonal contraception may be less reliable; agree an effective alternative. A longer interval applies if another medicine requires it, so use your actual prescribed plan. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding before treatment.

Treatment reference · 01

Understanding your diagnosis and treatment goal

Use your own pathology, stage and agreed plan alongside this guide.

Your diagnosis and stage

Record where the melanoma began, its pathology result and the sites involved now. This pathway treats melanoma that cannot be fully removed or has spread. Treatment after complete removal has a different aim and a separate plan.

The tumour test

The laboratory checks a sample of the melanoma for a BRAF V600 change. The result can identify a target for certain medicines. A change found in the tumour does not, by itself, establish an inherited condition.

Scans and local treatments

Add planned scans, any operation and any radiotherapy to the same calendar. Ask what each part is intended to achieve and which team arranges it.

New changes still matter

Tell your team about a changing mole, a persistent sore, a new lump or an unexplained symptom. Improvement in a known tumour does not explain a new problem elsewhere.

Treatment reference · 02

Your medicines and overall plan

To control advanced BRAF V600-mutated melanoma using dabrafenib alone, with regular review of benefit, fever and other side effects.

The schedule

Dabrafenib twice daily, about 12 hours apart, every day. Take it on an empty stomach; there is no planned tablet-free week.

How treatment works

Dabrafenib inhibits the altered BRAF V600 protein involved in melanoma growth. This guide describes dabrafenib alone for advanced melanoma. It does not include a second targeted medicine or a postoperative one-year treatment schedule.

Important boundaries

This is dabrafenib monotherapy for advanced melanoma. Do not import the food, heart-testing, fertility or duration instructions of a different BRAF/MEK combination. The NCCP fever table states 38.5°C, while current EMA product information states interruption at 38°C for monotherapy; this guide uses the earlier current-product safety trigger and the team must reconcile the written plan.

Evidence and choices

Median progression-free survival was 5.1 versus 2.7 months; hazard ratio 0.30, 95% CI 0.18–0.51. These are group results from that study, not a prediction for you. Advanced-disease monotherapy evidence, not postoperative dabrafenib/trametinib evidence. The comparator and treatment era do not establish superiority over current combinations.

Treatment reference · 03

Taking or receiving treatment safely

Keep the written prescription and actual treatment record together.

Administration

The usual starting dose is 150 mg twice daily, about 12 hours apart. Take capsules at least one hour before or at least two hours after food. Swallow whole with water. Use the prescribed strength and dose, and check the label again after any interruption or dose change.

Missed or held treatment

Skip a missed dose if the next dose is due in less than six hours. Do not take two doses together to make up for it. If you vomit after a dose, do not take a replacement dose; take the next dose when due. The team must give instructions for restarting after fever or another treatment hold.

Other medicines

Give the team a complete list of prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines, supplements and allergies. Ask before adding a new medicine; treatment interactions and symptom-masking can matter.

Practical arrangements

Write down where blood samples are taken, who receives the results and how you obtain the next appointment or medicine supply. Request a printed plan if that is easier to use.

Treatment reference · 04

Tests, results and treatment decisions

Safety tests and scans answer different questions.

Before treatment

Confirm BRAF V600 and check blood count, kidney and liver function, ECG, medicines and skin. Record existing skin lesions and relevant head-and-neck or other cancer assessments so later changes can be compared.

During treatment

Monthly blood count, kidney and liver tests, and monthly skin review. Repeat ECG when clinically indicated. NCCP also describes head-and-neck review every three months and other surveillance according to the individual assessment; this is separate from the cancer-response scan plan.

What a result means

Ask which result is abnormal, whether the finding needs a repeat test and whether treatment is held, changed or stopped. Do not use a reference range or one normal result to decide independently that a dose is safe.

Response and follow-up

Continue skin surveillance for up to six months after stopping, as specified by NCCP. Keep the agreed review for other new lesions and the individual response-imaging plan. No fixed tumour-marker schedule is assumed.

What to expect between visits

A quiet treatment day does not rule out later problems.

Act on new symptoms

If your temperature is 38°C or higher, stop dabrafenib and contact the oncology team immediately under the current product information; do not restart on your own. Also call for shaking chills, dizziness, reduced urine, severe abdominal pain, eye pain or changed vision, or a blistering or spreading rash. Infection and medicine-related fever both need consideration.

Supportive medicines

NCCP classifies dabrafenib as minimal-to-low emetogenic risk; anti-sickness treatment follows local and individual need. Agree a written fever plan and keep a thermometer and actual urgent-contact number. The team may prescribe fever treatment and hydration advice after assessment; fever medicine is not a substitute for contacting them. Anti-sickness treatment is selected individually.

A useful daily record

Record symptoms, temperature when unwell, bowel changes, fluid intake when reduced, missed doses and any medicines used for relief. Note how symptoms affect eating, walking, sleep and normal activities; this helps the team judge severity.

When life is affected

Bring up difficulties with eating, sleep, pain, energy, work or caring for someone. The team can arrange symptom support while cancer treatment continues.

Treatment reference · 06

Skin, body changes and daily care

Use the detailed body map to locate symptoms, not to diagnose them.

Skin and sun

Inspect skin and report new lumps, persistent warty growths, sores or changing moles. Report painful thickening or redness of palms and soles before it limits walking or hand use. Protect skin from the sun and attend monthly skin reviews.

The body map

The organ anchors show possible locations and warning symptoms. They do not show how common each effect is, and a symptom may have another cause. New symptoms should be assessed in their clinical context.

Food, fluids and activity

Choose food, fluids and movement that you can manage, within any personal restrictions. Ask for help early if you are losing weight, drinking less or finding everyday activity harder.

Keep checking the plan

A new medicine, fever, hospital admission, procedure or radiotherapy may change treatment timing. Tell the oncology team and take the wallet card or medicine list to other appointments.

Treatment reference · 07

Fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding

Discuss these issues before the first dose whenever possible.

Contraception and pregnancy

Use effective contraception during dabrafenib alone and for two weeks after the last dose. Hormonal contraception may be less reliable; agree an effective alternative. A longer interval applies if another medicine requires it, so use your actual prescribed plan. Discuss fertility and breastfeeding before treatment.

Fertility preservation

Ask whether treatment or the cancer may affect fertility and whether fertility preservation is appropriate before treatment. A change in periods or sexual function does not reliably establish infertility.

Written dates and methods

Record the agreed method, last treatment date and how long precautions continue. Different medicines have different intervals, so ask for clarification after a switch or combination change.

Sexual health

You can request a private discussion about intimacy, sexual symptoms or relationships. Ask for help if the agreed pregnancy precautions are difficult to follow.

Treatment reference · 08

Decisions, support and useful records

Your priorities belong in the treatment discussion.

Benefits and alternatives

Ask what the treatment aims to achieve, what alternatives are suitable, how likely important benefits are in your situation and how treatment burden or uncertainty affects that choice. A trial average cannot promise an individual outcome.

Support alongside treatment

Supportive care addresses symptoms and daily life during cancer treatment. Palliative care can be part of that support; accepting it does not mean you must stop anticancer treatment.

A person you trust

Choose whether to involve a relative or supporter and what may be shared with them. Ask for an interpreter or a different information format. Check that you can explain the next dose, tests and urgent-contact plan in your own words.

Your document set

Keep the current treatment summary, medicine list, test plan and contact card together. Use the printed instructions when you need them; the QR code is an optional link to further information.

Treatment reference · 09

After a treatment hold, change or completion

The next step should be written down, not assumed.

Follow-up and late effects

Continue skin surveillance for up to six months after stopping, as specified by NCCP. Keep the agreed review for other new lesions and the individual response-imaging plan. No fixed tumour-marker schedule is assumed.

Restarting or changing treatment

Skip a missed dose if the next dose is due in less than six hours. Do not take two doses together to make up for it. If you vomit after a dose, do not take a replacement dose; take the next dose when due. The team must give instructions for restarting after fever or another treatment hold.

A treatment summary

Ask for a treatment summary showing what you received, important side effects, admissions, the last dose and the follow-up team. Share the current version with your GP and other clinicians.

Your next review

Before leaving, confirm the next test and review, how you will receive results and whom to call sooner. Keep those numbers available after treatment ends.

What the treatment evidence can and cannot tell you

Evidence needs to match the medicine, dose combination, disease stage and previous treatment.
