Which treatment this guide covers National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) 00219a is first-line EGFR-mutated advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC); 00219b is switch-maintenance after stable disease following first-line chemotherapy; 00219c is after failure of at least one previous chemotherapy regimen. Global NCCP eligibility requires a validated activating EGFR mutation in each setting. These are advanced-disease settings, not adjuvant treatment.

How this medicine supports your treatment

Erlotinib blocks abnormal EGFR growth signals in selected lung cancers. Treatment aims to control advanced cancer and delay progression.

EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Erlotinib

Blocks abnormal EGFR growth signals
Activating EGFR mutation identified↓EGFR signalling blocked↓Cancer growth slowed

Erlotinib blocks the EGFR tyrosine kinase signal that can drive growth in cancers with activating EGFR mutations. Effects on normal skin and bowel cells also contribute to side effects.

Report diarrhoea, rash, breathing changes and eye symptoms promptly.

Checks before and during treatment

Tests and symptoms guide safe treatment decisions.

Before starting

Confirm the activating EGFR result, exact indication, previous treatment and prescription. Check blood count, kidneys and liver. Review lungs, bowel, skin, eyes, all medicines and pregnancy precautions.

NCCP 00219 v6 · official regimen · EMA Tarceva product information

During treatment

NCCP specifies blood count, kidney and liver tests every 60 days. Diarrhoea or dehydration needs extra kidney and electrolyte tests, including potassium. Liver problems or interacting medicines can require closer tests. Scans follow the Consultant’s plan.

Taking erlotinib

Follow your individual prescription.

Daily tablets

Take at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after food. If a dose is missed, do not replace it; take the usual next dose. If you vomit within a few hours after taking it, do not repeat the dose. Ask pharmacy before changing smoking habits or adding medicines for stomach acid.

NCCP 00219 v6 · official regimen · EMA Tarceva product information

Dose changes

Dose reductions use 150 → 100 → 50 mg daily. Recovery and restart depend on the specific side effect; only the team may authorise a restart. Do not increase the dose to compensate for smoking or acid-suppressing medicines.

Living with treatment

Report symptoms early and keep contact details available.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Keep the prescribed anti-diarrhoea plan available from the first dose. Report loose stools early and keep drinking if able. Persistent or severe diarrhoea, weakness, inability to drink or much less urine requires urgent advice. The team decides whether to hold treatment and when it is safe to restart.

NCCP 00219 v6 · official regimen · EMA Tarceva product information

Skin, nails and mouth

Use the agreed skin, nail and mouth-care plan. Report rash, painful nails or mouth sores early. Emollients and sun protection may help; the team decides whether creams, antibiotics or other treatment are needed. Blistering or peeling skin needs urgent assessment.

NCCP 00219 v6 · official regimen · EMA Tarceva product information

Nausea medicines

Erlotinib has minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Agree any as-needed anti-sickness medicine with the team. Routine infusion premedication is not required.

NCCP 00219 v6 · official regimen · NCCP systemic anticancer therapy (SACT) antiemetic guidance V6 (2025), oral tables · NCCP NCIS antiemetic medicines V8 (2025); parenteral scope

Medicine interactions

Give pharmacy all medicines and supplements. Strong CYP3A4 inducers or inhibitors, grapefruit and medicines for stomach acid can change exposure. Avoid proton pump inhibitor combinations unless the specialist resolves the plan. Necessary antacids require source-specific separation; ask for written timing. Warfarin needs extra blood-clotting checks. Smoking reduces exposure; get cessation support and do not change your cancer dose yourself.

Pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding

Avoid pregnancy. Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the final dose. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for at least 2 weeks after the final dose. Discuss fertility and future pregnancy plans before starting.

When to get urgent help

Do not wait for the next appointment.

Call urgently

Call urgently for new or worsening breathlessness, cough or fever; severe diarrhoea, inability to drink or much less urine; severe or persistent abdominal pain; blistering or peeling skin; or yellow skin/eyes. Eye pain, light sensitivity or blurred vision needs prompt assessment. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or swelling of the tongue/throat needs emergency help. Use your unit’s temperature and 24-hour contact instructions.

When you call

Give the medicine name, last dose and symptoms. Tell the team if you can drink, pass urine and manage usual activities. Do not delay calling while gathering information.

What studies tell us

In a study of previously untreated EGFR-mutated advanced lung cancer, erlotinib delayed progression compared with chemotherapy. The final survival comparison did not show a statistically significant difference. The study population and subsequent treatments matter; ask how the evidence applies to your exact setting.

Your own decision

Ask how the study population matches your situation and discuss alternatives, harms and remaining uncertainty.
