Your treatment reference

Weekly carboplatin with radiotherapy for cervical cancer

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Carboplatin helps radiation treat cervical cancer when cisplatin cannot be used or was not tolerated.

Carboplatin · Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy. Damages DNA in cancer cells. Here it helps radiotherapy treat the cervix and nearby disease. Phone promptly about fever, bleeding or bruising. Tell the nurse immediately about itching, chest tightness or dizziness during the infusion.

Your infusion schedule

Weekly carboplatin with pelvic radiotherapy

The team calculates your dose from kidney function. Carboplatin runs into a vein over about 30 minutes on the scheduled chemotherapy day.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat your team checksWhat you can do

Your team books scans and any extra blood tests according to your symptoms and treatment plan.

Common effects and what to do

What you may notice

Tiredness, nausea and appetite changes can affect activity and meals. Ask for help if symptoms interfere with eating or drinking.

Serious effects: when to call

Low blood counts · Carboplatin

Phone the 24-hour number for fever or shaking chills. Report unexplained bruising or bleeding.

Bowel irritation · Pelvic radiation

Report increasing diarrhoea, urgency, bleeding or trouble keeping up with fluids.

Bladder or vaginal irritation · Pelvic radiation

Tell the radiation team about pain on passing urine, troublesome discharge, bleeding or soreness.

Infusion allergy · Carboplatin

Tell the nurse at once about flushing, tightness in the chest, wheezing or dizziness.

Appetite or nausea · Carboplatin

Tell the team if nausea prevents you eating, drinking or taking medicine.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Contact oncology urgently for fever, heavy bleeding, uncontrolled diarrhoea, very little urine or vomiting that prevents drinking. Tell both teams if you miss an appointment. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Carboplatin

Phone promptly about fever, bleeding or bruising. Tell the nurse immediately about itching, chest tightness or dizziness during the infusion.

Anti-sickness medicines

Use the signed anti-sickness prescription for this week’s infusion. Call if you cannot keep fluids down.

Radiotherapy appointments

Keep the chemotherapy and radiation calendars together. If either appointment changes, contact both teams.

Pelvic care

Ask about bladder, bowel, vaginal and sexual health symptoms. A specialist nurse can discuss dryness, pain, menopause and pelvic rehabilitation.

Planning your visits

Appointments

One carboplatin infusion each week during radiotherapy for up to six cycles. The team stops carboplatin if the radiation course ends early.

At the unit

The team calculates your dose from kidney function. Carboplatin runs into a vein over about 30 minutes on the scheduled chemotherapy day.

After this part of treatment

If radiation is interrupted, ask both teams to confirm the next infusion. There is no routine follow-on chemotherapy in this cervical protocol.

What the research found

Studies found that carboplatin can help control cancer alongside radiation when cisplatin is unsuitable. Tumours disappeared completely less often in the carboplatin groups.

Baseline differences between groups limit the comparison. Similar survival or equivalence to cisplatin has not been established.
