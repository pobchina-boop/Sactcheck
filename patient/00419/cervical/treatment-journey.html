Your treatment journey

Weekly carboplatin with radiotherapy for cervical cancer

Carboplatin helps radiation treat cervical cancer when cisplatin cannot be used or was not tolerated.

Your infusion schedule

Weekly carboplatin with pelvic radiotherapy

The team calculates your dose from kidney function. Carboplatin runs into a vein over about 30 minutes on the scheduled chemotherapy day.

Your tests and checks

CheckWhat your team checksWhat you can do
Before startingBlood count, kidney and liver tests. A measured kidney filtration test is preferred for dosing; the team can use an estimate when appropriate.Discuss the reason cisplatin is unsuitable. Hearing and nerve tests are considered when indicated.
Before each weekly infusionBlood count and kidney and liver function are checked. Kidney function can affect the calculated dose.Report unusual bruising, bleeding, numbness or vomiting. Verify that radiotherapy is still proceeding.
After treatmentThe radiotherapy team arranges follow-up and checks recovery, including bowel, bladder and vaginal symptoms.Ask for your follow-up date, a contact for new bleeding and advice on sexual health or menopause symptoms.

Your team books scans and any extra blood tests according to your symptoms and treatment plan.

At review visits

  • If my radiation appointment changes, should I still attend for the drip? Ring both teams. These treatments work together, so the team must confirm whether to give the weekly infusion and issue your next dates.
  • How will the team decide what happens next? If radiation is interrupted, ask both teams to confirm the next infusion. There is no routine follow-on chemotherapy in this cervical protocol.
