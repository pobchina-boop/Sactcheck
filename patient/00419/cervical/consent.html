Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

Weekly carboplatin with radiotherapy for cervical cancer

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

Carboplatin helps radiation treat cervical cancer when cisplatin cannot be used or was not tolerated. Concurrent treatment for locally advanced cervical cancer with a documented cisplatin contraindication or intolerance. Keep the systemic schedule linked to the radiation course.

Treatment schedule. One carboplatin infusion each week during radiotherapy for up to six cycles. The team stops carboplatin if the radiation course ends early.

Regimen-specific side effects

Carboplatin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Low blood counts and infectionCall urgently for fever, shivering or unexpected bleeding. Red cells and platelets can fall as well as white cells.
Nausea and vomitingTake the prescribed anti-sickness medicines. Call if vomiting continues or you cannot keep fluids down.
Allergy, kidneys and hearingTell the nurse immediately about an infusion reaction. Kidney function guides dosing. Report new ringing in the ears or hearing changes.

Pelvic radiotherapy alongside chemotherapy

Discuss bowel and bladder irritation, vaginal changes and the effects on sexual health, ovarian function and fertility that apply to you. Your radiation oncology team will discuss and document your radiotherapy plan separately.

Treatment discussion and decision

Weekly carboplatin with radiotherapy for cervical cancer

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

Studies found that carboplatin can help control cancer alongside radiation when cisplatin is unsuitable. Tumours disappeared completely less often in the carboplatin groups. Baseline differences between groups limit the comparison. Similar survival or equivalence to cisplatin has not been established.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Contact oncology urgently for fever, heavy bleeding, uncontrolled diarrhoea, very little urine or vomiting that prevents drinking. Tell both teams if you miss an appointment. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
