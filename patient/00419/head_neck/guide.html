Carboplatin AUC 2 with radiotherapy

Carboplatin and radiotherapy are used together as part of the treatment plan for head and neck cancer. The team explains the treatment aim, expected benefit and why this medicine and schedule suit your situation.

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

This guide covers NCCP 00419 for head and neck cancer. It does not prescribe the radiation dose or number of radiation sessions. The 6 planned weekly carboplatin doses are a separate count. Any earlier or later chemotherapy needs its own plan.

My overall treatment

Once every 7 days for 6 planned weekly doses, during radiotherapy.

Between treatments

  • Radiation appointments follow their own calendar. If a session changes, contact both teams before the next carboplatin dose.
  • Symptoms may build up and persist after treatment. Report fever, sudden illness, bleeding or inability to drink promptly.

Bring your medicines, blood-test dates, radiation calendar and verified urgent contacts.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

What to watch for

You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

A picture of side effects

Match each symptom box to its numbered body location. Tell your team about new symptoms.

1Mouth, saliva and teeth
Head and neck radiotherapy

Soreness (mucositis), dry mouth and taste change may affect intake. Dental risk may persist; keep the oral-care and dental plan.

3Skin in the radiation area
Radiotherapy

Redness, soreness or skin breakdown may occur in the treated area. Use the radiation team’s skin-care plan and report worsening pain.

5Stomach and intake
Carboplatin + radiation effects

Nausea, vomiting and poor intake can cause dehydration. Take prescribed anti-sickness medicines and call if you cannot drink.

7Hearing and ears
Platinum chemotherapy

Hearing change or ringing may be new or persist from earlier treatment. Report it before the next dose.

9Whole-body infusion reaction
Carboplatin

Rash, flushing, chest tightness, breathing difficulty or faintness may signal an allergic reaction. Tell infusion staff immediately.

2Throat, voice and swallowing
Head and neck radiotherapy

Pain or difficulty swallowing can build up and may last. Report choking, food sticking or inability to drink; ask for swallowing support.

4Blood and infection
Carboplatin

Low white cells can cause serious infection; low platelets can cause bleeding; anaemia can cause fatigue. Fever, sudden illness or unusual bleeding needs urgent contact.

6Kidneys and fluid balance
Carboplatin

Kidney function determines the dose. Report reduced urine, vomiting or poor intake; do not calculate or change the dose yourself.

8Hands, feet and nerves
Platinum chemotherapy

Numbness or tingling (peripheral neuropathy) may affect walking or fine tasks. Report changes and any earlier persistent symptoms.

Selected effects, not every possible effect or a prediction. Blood-count, skin and line markers are schematic. Number and text carry meaning; colour is decorative.

Call oncology immediately for fever, shivering, sudden illness, unusual bruising or bleeding, persistent vomiting, severe mouth or throat pain, inability to drink or much less urine. Report new hearing changes, numbness, worsening swallowing, choking or skin breakdown promptly. During an infusion, tell staff immediately about rash, flushing, chest tightness, breathing difficulty or faintness. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure is an emergency: call 112 or 999.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
