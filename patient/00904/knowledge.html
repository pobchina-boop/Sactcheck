Your complete treatment reference

Rucaparib (Rubraca) maintenance tablets

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Chemotherapy has reduced the cancer. These tablets aim to keep it under control for longer. Your team will explain how your response and tumour test results inform your options.

Rucaparib · Rubraca

Targeted tablet. Blocks enzymes called PARP that help repair DNA. Some ovarian cancer cells depend on these repairs to survive. Call about persistent fatigue, breathlessness, infections or unusual bruising. Regular blood tests can detect problems before you feel unwell.

Your tablet schedule

Every day · morning and evening

Check the strength and number of tablets against the latest dispensing label. A dose reduction can change both. Mark morning and evening doses separately.

Common effects and what to do

What you may notice

Tiredness, changes in appetite and nausea can affect meals and daily activity. Tell your team early if you are eating or drinking less.

At home

Tell the team if constipation, diarrhoea or changes in taste persist.

Rare marrow disorders

Low counts that persist despite a treatment pause may need specialist tests. Rarely, a marrow disorder or leukaemia develops.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call the oncology unit urgently for fever, shaking chills, unexpected bleeding, worsening breathlessness or vomiting that prevents drinking. Report unusual bruising or persistent fatigue promptly. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Call about persistent fatigue, breathlessness, infections or unusual bruising. Regular blood tests can detect problems before you feel unwell.

At home

Use the morning and evening tablet plan. Ask about any new medicine, including over-the-counter medicines, before starting it. Protect exposed skin from sunlight.

Your anti-sickness plan

Rucaparib can cause nausea. Your team may prescribe a daily anti-sickness tablet and separate rescue treatment. Ask which one to take and when.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Use effective contraception during treatment and for six months after the last dose if you could become pregnant. Avoid breastfeeding during treatment and for two weeks afterwards.

Planning your visits

Your appointments

Take the prescribed dose twice daily, about 12 hours apart, every day. There is no planned week off.

Taking treatment

Swallow the prescribed tablets whole with a large glass of water, with or without food. Leave a missed or vomited dose out and take only your next scheduled dose. Never crush the tablets or take extra to catch up.

Planning the next step

Start within eight weeks of the final platinum dose. Treatment lasts up to two years and may stop earlier if the cancer grows or side effects make continued treatment unsuitable.

What the research found

In the ATHENA-MONO trial, the time before the cancer grew or a participant died was about 20 months with rucaparib and 9 months with placebo at the middle of each group. Some people had shorter or longer periods.

The study has not established whether people live longer overall. Ask how your tumour results and the side effects you wish to avoid affect your choice.
