NCCP 00259 · version 6

Vinorelbine capsules · non-small-cell lung cancer

ONCE WEEKLY. First 3 administrations 60 mg/m², total maximum 120 mg. From administration 4, 80 mg/m² (maximum 160 mg) only after the team reviews nadirs and toxicity. 60 or 50 mg/m²/lower may be needed. Continue weekly while helpful and tolerated; no fixed total number of weeks.

Purpose, setting and alternatives

Vinorelbine chemotherapy can help control non-small-cell lung cancer and its symptoms. The team weighs possible benefit against side effects, previous treatments and your priorities, and reviews whether to continue. NCCP 00259b: non-small-cell lung cancer. This guide covers vinorelbine monotherapy. Platinum combinations, adjuvant treatment and radiotherapy combinations need their own complete plans; this guide does not authorise them.

Material risks and support

  • Low blood counts, infection and bleeding
  • Nausea, vomiting and fatigue
  • Constipation or bowel obstruction
  • Nerve effects and uncommon lung/heart toxicity
  • Incorrect weekly dosing or damaged capsules

Discuss the disease-control aim, likely benefit and uncertainty, other lung-cancer treatments or symptom-focused care, and the burden of visits and tests. Review low blood counts and infection, nausea/vomiting, fatigue, constipation/ileus, nerve symptoms, hair changes and uncommon serious lung/heart/allergic effects. Discuss once-weekly capsule safety, food, anti-sickness treatment, handling, dose escalation and no repeat dose after vomiting. Discuss fertility preservation and pregnancy planning. Do not breastfeed. Confirm the exact product contraception advice: HPRA recommends contraception during treatment and for 7 months afterwards for women, and 4 months for men.

Monitoring and understanding

Blood counts are checked on the day of each weekly dose. Kidney and liver tests follow the weekly regimen and prescribed plan. The team reviews the lowest counts after earlier doses before any increase, alongside bowel, nerve, infection and medicine checks.
