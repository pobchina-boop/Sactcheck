Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

More about my medicines

Lutetium-177 oxodotreotide

Lutathera joins a receptor-binding molecule to the radioactive element lutetium-177. After infusion, it attaches to somatostatin receptors on suitable NET cells and delivers radiation nearby. The receptor scan helps select patients. Kidneys and blood-forming marrow can also receive radiation, which is why kidney protection and long-term blood monitoring matter.

Lysine / arginine amino acids

The amino-acid infusion reduces the amount of radioactive medicine retained in the kidneys. It is an essential part of kidney protection. It can cause nausea and affect potassium or fluid balance, so anti-sickness treatment, blood tests and observation are needed.

Getting ready for treatment visits

Consent discussion aid

Between treatments

These are possible patterns, not guaranteed symptom dates.

Day 1–2: Treatment and early observation

Plan a long hospital visit; the first treatment generally involves an overnight stay. Staff give anti-sickness medicine and kidney-protective amino acids. Hormone reactions are especially important in the first 48 hours.

What to do: Before discharge, obtain written emergency contacts, SSA timing and radiation precautions.

Day 3–21: Recovery and individual precautions

Energy, appetite and bowel symptoms may change. Radiation precautions may last two to three weeks for some activities or contacts; exact restrictions come from the medical physicist.

What to do: Attend blood tests every two weeks. Call for fever, bleeding, reduced urine, severe pain or sudden hormone symptoms.

Day 22–56: Tests and next-treatment decision

Continue fortnightly bloods and attend the PRRT review. A post-infusion scan checks radiation distribution; response scans follow a separate specialist plan.

What to do: The team confirms whether and when the next administration is suitable. Do not treat an eight-week date as automatic clearance.

Support at home

Kidney protection

A lysine/arginine infusion is required for kidney protection and begins before Lutathera. Follow the service’s fluid and toileting advice. Tell staff about heart or kidney problems; do not use a generic fluid target.

Nausea prevention

PRRT has moderate emetogenic potential. A 5-HT3 anti-sickness medicine is given at least 30 minutes before the amino-acid infusion, with additional treatment if needed. Tell staff promptly if nausea starts.

Somatostatin analogue coordination

Long-acting SSA injections can interfere with receptor targeting. NCCP uses a 30-day interval before PRRT and short-acting injections are withheld for 24 hours. The referring NET consultant decides your exact stop and restart plan, balancing hormone control. Never stop injections on your own.

Radiation safety and practical planning

The medical physicist gives individual written and verbal advice. Contact with young children or pregnant people may need restriction for up to three weeks, and close-contact work may require up to two weeks away. Arrange transport, sleeping, toileting, laundry and caring responsibilities before admission.

Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenBefore treatment; once 2–4 weeks before and shortly before each administration; every 2 weeks between treatments.

Checks haemoglobin, white cells with differential, and platelets.

After the last administration: every 4 weeks for at least 3 months, then every 3 months for the following year. Your written plan may include extra early checks.

Kidney function

WhenWith the blood-test schedule above; monitor for at least the first year after treatment.

Serum creatinine and calculated creatinine clearance assess kidney safety.

The team calculates clearance using the required method. A normal symptom check does not replace these tests.

Liver function

WhenWith the blood-test schedule above.

Tests include alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), albumin, bilirubin and international normalised ratio (blood-clotting test) (INR) to assess liver function and protein production.

Potassium

WhenBefore the kidney-protective amino-acid infusion; further checks if indicated.

Detects potassium disturbances that the amino-acid infusion can worsen.

Heart pumping assessment

WhenBefore starting, as specified in the regimen.

An echocardiogram or MUGA scan assesses heart pumping function.

5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (5-HIAA)

If part of your plan
WhenOnly if requested for your hormone syndrome; the team sets the dates.

Measures a breakdown product of serotonin to help assess selected neuroendocrine tumours.

Often a timed urine collection. Follow the laboratory’s food and medicine instructions.

Chromogranin A

If part of your plan
WhenOnly when the team considers it informative for your tumour.

A selected tumour-marker result, interpreted with symptoms and scans.

Acid-suppressing medicines and other conditions can alter the result. It is not a routine test before every treatment; do not stop medicines yourself.

Selected pancreatic hormones

If part of your plan
WhenOnly for the hormone question identified by your specialist.

Examples include gastrin, insulin or glucagon when the diagnosis or symptoms call for them.
