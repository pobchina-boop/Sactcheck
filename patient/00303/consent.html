Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

Paclitaxel and carboplatin every three weeks

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

After surgery the aim may be to reduce the risk of recurrence. With advanced or recurrent disease the aim may be to shrink or control the cancer. The NCCP lists high-risk early or advanced ovarian cancer, fallopian tube and primary peritoneal cancers, and selected advanced or recurrent endometrial, cervical or unknown-primary cancers. Ask which indication applies to you.

Treatment schedule. Both infusions every 21 days, for six planned cycles unless treatment ends earlier.

Regimen-specific side effects

Paclitaxel

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Numbness or weaknessTell the team about tingling, falls, trouble walking or fastening buttons before the next infusion. Nerve damage can persist.
Infusion allergyTell the nurse immediately about flushing, wheeze, chest tightness or dizziness during the infusion.
Hair loss, aches and low blood countsHair loss and muscle or joint pain can occur. Fever, shivering or sudden illness may signal infection and need an urgent call.

Carboplatin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Low blood counts and infectionCall urgently for fever, shivering or unexpected bleeding. Red cells and platelets can fall as well as white cells.
Nausea and vomitingTake the prescribed anti-sickness medicines. Call if vomiting continues or you cannot keep fluids down.
Allergy, kidneys and hearingTell the nurse immediately about an infusion reaction. Kidney function guides dosing. Report new ringing in the ears or hearing changes.

Treatment discussion and decision

Paclitaxel and carboplatin every three weeks

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

In patients with optimally resected stage III ovarian cancer, the carboplatin combination was easier to tolerate and achieved comparable cancer control to cisplatin with paclitaxel. The trial population was specific to ovarian cancer. Other indications have their own evidence and treatment goals.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Fever, shaking chills, unusual bleeding or sudden illness? Call the oncology unit now. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
