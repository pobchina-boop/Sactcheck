My treatment handbook

Dostarlimab (Jemperli) alone

Your treatment and how the medicines work

The aim is to help your immune system recognise and control the cancer.

Dostarlimab · Jemperli

PD-1 immunotherapy. It removes a brake on immune cells so they can recognise and attack cancer. It can also cause inflammation in healthy organs. Report new cough, diarrhoea, severe fatigue or rash even after the final dose.

What the research found

In the GARNET trial, measurable tumour shrinkage was seen in about 46 in 100 evaluable people in the mismatch repair deficient or MSI-high endometrial cancer group.

This was a single-arm trial. A response rate does not tell us how long someone will live or how the medicine compares directly with another treatment.

Your infusion schedule

First four doses

One infusion every three weeks for four doses.

From dose 5

Dose 5 is three weeks after dose 4. After dose 5 the interval is six weeks, while treatment remains suitable.

Common effects and what to do

Hormone glands · Dostarlimab

Report severe tiredness, headache, unusual thirst or dizziness.

Bowel · Dostarlimab

Call urgently for new diarrhoea, pain or blood in stools.

Skin · Dostarlimab

Blistering, peeling skin or sores around your mouth or eyes need urgent assessment.

Serious effects: when to call

Lungs · Dostarlimab

Call urgently for new cough or breathlessness.

Hormone glands · Dostarlimab

Report severe tiredness, headache, unusual thirst or dizziness.

Heart or muscles · Dostarlimab

New weakness needs urgent advice. Severe chest pain or collapse needs 112 or 999.

Bowel · Dostarlimab

Call urgently for new diarrhoea, pain or blood in stools.

Liver · Dostarlimab

Call for yellow eyes or dark urine. Blood tests can also detect inflammation.

Skin · Dostarlimab

Blistering, peeling skin or sores around your mouth or eyes need urgent assessment.

Kidneys · Dostarlimab

Contact the team for reduced urine or new swelling. Blood tests may show changes before symptoms start.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call urgently for new cough, breathlessness, diarrhoea, severe weakness or a blistering rash. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

Immune alert card

Show the card to any clinician treating you, even months after your last infusion. Include the date of your last dose.

Between infusions

Routine sickness risk is minimal. Report new diarrhoea or vomiting to oncology before treating it yourself because it can reflect immune inflammation.

Planning your visits

At the day unit

Dostarlimab goes into a vein over about 30 minutes. Only dostarlimab is used on this pathway. Keep the immunotherapy alert card with you after treatment too.

If a visit changes

Your team will tell you if a visit moves after a delay. Do not work out a new date yourself.

Before starting treatment

Discuss fertility, contraception and breastfeeding with your team. Bring your current medicines and details of previous infusion reactions.

Preparing for your oncology discussion

  • When is dose 5 due, and which symptoms would you report even after your last dose? Talk through this with your nurse and write the answer on your appointment sheet.
  • Who can I contact outside clinic hours? Ask the team to fill in your wallet card with local numbers.
