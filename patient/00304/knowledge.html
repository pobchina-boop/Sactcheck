When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team immediately for fever, shivering, sudden illness, new breathing difficulty, chest pain, unusual bleeding, severe abdominal pain or inability to drink. Understand your treatment

Three medicines. Different jobs.

After surgery, treatment aims to reduce the chance of lung cancer returning. For advanced lung cancer, it aims to control cancer and help symptoms. Your team confirms which aim applies to you.

Chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Taxane chemotherapy
Disrupts microtubules and cell division

Paclitaxel stabilises microtubules, structures cells use during division. It can slow cancer growth but also harm healthy nerves, blood-forming cells and hair follicles. Both medicines are planned on Day 1 of each of four cycles.

Microtubules↓Division disrupted↓Cancer cells affected

What to watch for
Tell the nurse immediately about infusion symptoms. Report numbness, tingling, fever, painful muscles, mouth sores or diarrhoea.

Sources: nccp

Chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Damages DNA

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that hinder copying and repair. Healthy tissues can also be harmed. Its dose depends on target exposure (AUC) and measured or estimated kidney function; AUC is not a fixed number of milligrams. Both medicines are planned on Day 1 of each of four cycles.

DNA↓Platinum links↓Copying disrupted

What to watch for
Report fever, bleeding, nausea, hearing changes or numbness. Tell the nurse immediately about possible allergy during the infusion.

Sources: nccp

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Treatment day

Paclitaxel is given into a vein over three hours, followed by carboplatin over 30 minutes. Blood review, preparation, prevention medicines and observation make the visit longer. Tell staff immediately about flushing, rash, chest or throat tightness, breathing difficulty or feeling faint.

Day 2–7: Early days at home

Follow your prescribed anti-sickness plan. Aches, nausea, tiredness and bowel changes can occur. Call if symptoms are uncontrolled or you cannot drink; fever or sudden illness needs urgent advice.

Day 8–14: Keep watching for symptoms

Low blood counts can increase infection or bleeding risk. The timing varies between people. Call urgently for fever, shivering, sudden illness or unusual bleeding; do not wait for blood tests.

Day 15–21: Review before the next cycle

Symptoms may improve or persist. Describe the worst symptoms since treatment, not just how you feel today. Confirm blood tests and the next appointment. Report numbness, weakness or hearing changes before treatment.

Treatment reference · 01

Your treatment and its aim

After surgery, treatment aims to reduce the chance of lung cancer returning. For advanced lung cancer, it aims to control cancer and help symptoms. Your team confirms which aim applies to you.

Which setting?

NCCP 00304 a/b: selected adjuvant or locally advanced, recurrent or metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer. These indications are off-label in Ireland and require the local consent and approval process. Nivolumab combinations use a separate guide.

Sources: nccp

Evidence and choices

The adjuvant CALGB 9633 study did not show a statistically significant survival benefit for the whole stage IB group in its mature analysis. An exploratory large-tumour subgroup suggested benefit. Advanced-disease trials support platinum-doublet activity but do not turn adjuvant results into an advanced-cancer prognosis.

Sources: trial · advanced

Other options

Discuss suitable alternative treatments, clinical trials, symptom support and the option of not proceeding. Choices depend on stage, previous treatment and your priorities.

Benefit depends on the cancer stage, treatment aim, fitness and alternatives. Trial averages are not a prediction for you.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the aim in my case?

After surgery, treatment aims to reduce the chance of lung cancer returning. For advanced lung cancer, it aims to control cancer and help symptoms. Your team confirms which aim applies to you.

How often do I attend?

Both medicines on Day 1, repeated every 21 days for four planned cycles, unless cancer progresses or side effects require a change. This standalone plan does not include nivolumab or radiotherapy.

Does this include immunotherapy or radiotherapy?

No. If either is prescribed, ask for the matching combination plan. A four-cycle chemotherapy-only guide cannot replace the three-cycle nivolumab plan before surgery.

When are my scans?

Your consultant agrees scan timing for your cancer and treatment aim; the NCCP regimen does not prescribe a fixed scan interval.

Who do I ring if unwell?

Keep the unit’s daytime and out-of-hours numbers on your treatment summary and card. Call immediately for fever or sudden illness, even between appointments.
