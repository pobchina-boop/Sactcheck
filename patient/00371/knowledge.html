How this medicine supports your treatment

After early breast cancer treatment, endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence. In advanced disease, the aim is to control cancer and symptoms. Your setting determines the duration and follow-up.

Hormone therapy · aromatase inhibitor

Letrozole

Reduces the oestrogen available to stimulate cancer cells
Aromatase normally makes oestrogen↓The medicine blocks aromatase↓Less oestrogen reaches cancer-cell receptors

After menopause, much of the body’s oestrogen is made when aromatase converts other hormones in fat and other tissues. Blocking that enzyme lowers oestrogen signalling in hormone-sensitive breast cancer. It does not reliably switch off functioning ovaries: ovarian suppression, if needed, must be prescribed and checked separately.

Tell the team if joint pain, hot flushes, vaginal dryness or sleep disturbance affects daily life. Bone thinning may cause no early symptoms, so keep the agreed bone-health reviews.

The exact treatment being discussed

After early breast cancer treatment, endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence. In advanced disease, the aim is to control cancer and symptoms. Your setting determines the duration and follow-up.

Who this card covers

Postmenopausal hormone-sensitive breast cancer in the specific NCCP early, extended, advanced or neoadjuvant indications. Neoadjuvant use has additional HER2-negative and treatment-suitability criteria; it is not inferred for every ER-positive cancer.

NCCP 00371 v4 · official regimen

Dose and administration

2.5 mg orally once daily. Take with or without food. Take a forgotten dose when remembered unless the next dose is within about two to three hours; then skip it. Do not take two doses together.

NCCP 00371 v4 · official regimen

Duration and decision points

For the NCCP neoadjuvant pathway, reassess response during a four-to-eight-month course and discuss surgery or alternatives if response is inadequate. Adjuvant / extended duration and advanced-disease continuation follow separate plans.

NCCP 00371 v4 · official regimen

Individual safety

NCCP Child-Pugh C dosing is 2.5 mg every other day; this is not the same hepatic advice as anastrozole. The daily schedule shown is the usual dose and must be checked against the prescription.

NCCP 00371 v4 · official regimen

Checks throughout treatment

Baseline, ongoing safety checks and cancer scans have different purposes.

Before treatment

FBC, renal and liver profile; confirm menopause with hormone tests when uncertain. Assess fracture risk and baseline bone density, particularly for adjuvant AI treatment. The NICE adjuvant baseline-DXA recommendation has an exception for patients already receiving adjuvant bisphosphonates.

NCCP 00371 v4 · official regimen

During treatment

FBC, renal / liver profile and lipids when clinically indicated. Review bone density during treatment and after treatment according to bone risk. Neoadjuvant response and surgical planning need explicit review milestones.

NCCP 00371 v4 · official regimen

Heart checks

There is no routine ECG or echo schedule for letrozole alone. Assess cardiovascular risk factors and new symptoms as clinically indicated.

NCCP 00371 v4 · official regimen · HPRA Letrozole SmPC cited by NCCP

Bone health

Offer baseline DXA when starting an adjuvant aromatase inhibitor if not already receiving adjuvant bisphosphonates; assess fracture risk and relevant previous results. Under the 2025 joint guidance, patients with a T-score above −2.0 and no additional risk factors generally have risk and bone density reassessed every one to two years. Patients on bone-protective treatment generally have BMD reassessed about every two years. Fracture, substantial bone loss or a change in risk can require earlier review. The clinician selects the interval and any bone treatment; these are not automatic yearly scans for everyone.

NICE NG 101: early and locally advanced breast cancer · IOF/CABS/ECTS/IEG/ESCEO/IMS/SIOG updated joint statement on aromatase-inhibitor-associated bone loss

Your setting changes the pathway

Choose the pathway that your team has confirmed; these are not all extra treatments you must have.

Early-breast follow-up

After curative treatment, annual mammography is generally used for remaining breast tissue: both breasts after conservation or the opposite breast after unilateral mastectomy. The mastectomy side is not routinely mammographed. Routine CT, bone scans and tumour markers are not recommended for asymptomatic early-breast surveillance. New symptoms need assessment between appointments.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO early breast cancer guidelines · NICE NG 101: early and locally advanced breast cancer

Surgery, chemotherapy and radiotherapy

In an early-breast pathway, chemotherapy, if indicated, precedes endocrine treatment. Surgery and its pathology guide adjuvant choices. Postoperative radiotherapy is usually delivered after recovery and any adjuvant chemotherapy; aromatase inhibitors may overlap radiotherapy under the agreed plan. Tamoxifen timing around radiotherapy is agreed by the treating teams. None of these additional treatments is automatically required by an endocrine prescription.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO early breast cancer guidelines · eviQ 3650 v 6: adjuvant whole-breast radiotherapy

Advanced-disease scans

For advanced breast cancer, response assessment is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease pace, sites, symptoms and treatment. Indolent disease may be reviewed less often; new symptoms can prompt earlier scans. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not a universal three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

Pan-Asian adapted ESMO metastatic breast cancer guidelines

Tumour markers

Tumour markers may supplement imaging and clinical assessment in advanced disease if they are informative, especially if elevated at baseline. They do not replace scans, and a marker change alone should not determine treatment failure. There is no universal fixed marker timetable.

ESO-ESMO ABC 5 consensus: tumour-marker interpretation

Menopause, pregnancy and contraception

AIs alone require a confirmed postmenopausal endocrine state. Ovarian suppression is a separate treatment plan and must not be assumed from this card.

NCCP 00371 v4 · official regimen · HPRA Letrozole SmPC cited by NCCP

Evidence for a different decision

Each study applies to its own population and treatment setting.

MA.17: extended adjuvant letrozole after tamoxifen

5,187 postmenopausal women after 5 years of tamoxifen; planned 5 years letrozole versus placebo. At 30 months median follow-up, DFS HR 0.58 (95% CI 0.45–0.76); distant DFS HR 0.60 (0.43–0.84). Overall OS was not significantly improved (HR 0.82,0.57–1.19). Stopped early for benefit. Supports a specific post-tamoxifen extension strategy, not indefinite treatment or automatic extension after every previous AI course.

MA.17: letrozole following five years of tamoxifen

P 024: neoadjuvant letrozole

337 postmenopausal women with untreated ER/PgR-positive primary breast cancer, initially unsuitable for breast-conserving surgery; 4 months letrozole versus tamoxifen. Clinical response 55% versus 36%; breast-conserving surgery 45% versus 35%. Short preoperative response study, not a survival trial. Clinical reassessment and surgical planning remain essential; the trial duration must not override NCCP's individualized 4–8 month neoadjuvant window.

P 024: preoperative letrozole versus tamoxifen

What the evidence can tell us

BIG 1-98: letrozole as adjuvant therapy: population, findings and limits

8,010 postmenopausal women with hormone-receptor-positive early breast cancer; monotherapy comparison 4,922, letrozole versus tamoxifen for 5 years.

Intention-to-treat DFS HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.78–0.96); OS HR 0.87 (0.77–0.999). Sequential schedules did not significantly improve outcomes over letrozole alone.

619 tamoxifen participants crossed to letrozole; crossover-adjusted estimates differ from intention-to-treat and must not be mixed. This is adjuvant evidence, not a benefit estimate for neoadjuvant or metastatic treatment.
