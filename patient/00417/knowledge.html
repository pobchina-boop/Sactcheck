When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shivering, sudden illness, persistent vomiting, diarrhoea, severe mouth pain or inability to drink. Chest pain during or after fluorouracil, or unusually early severe symptoms, needs urgent assessment even if dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (a medicine-processing enzyme) (DPD) testing was normal. Report breathing difficulty, swelling, faintness, spreading skin infection or painful eyes promptly. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure is an emergency.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How your medicines work

Cetuximab with cisplatin and fluorouracil is used to treat squamous head and neck cancer. Discuss the individual aim, likely benefit, symptom control and alternatives with your oncology team.

EGFR-targeted antibody

Cetuximab

Erbitux
Blocks a growth signal on cells

Cetuximab attaches to the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) on cells and blocks growth signals. It is combined with chemotherapy in this plan. Healthy skin and nails also depend on this pathway, explaining some of the characteristic rash, dryness and nail problems. This is a different treatment from checkpoint immunotherapy.

EGFR blocked↓Growth messages reduced↓Cancer growth restrained

What to watch for
Tell staff immediately about itching, flushing, swelling, wheeze or faintness. Report skin infection, eye pain, new breathlessness or marked cramps.

Sources: nccp · erbitux

Platinum chemotherapy

Cisplatin

Cisplatin
Damages cancer-cell deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)

Cisplatin links strands of DNA, the instructions inside cells, making it harder for cancer cells to repair damage and divide. Healthy cells may also be affected. Kidney function, hearing, blood counts and nerve symptoms help the team decide whether the next dose is safe.

DNA damage↓Cell repair disrupted↓Cancer growth reduced

What to watch for
Report reduced urine, persistent vomiting, tinnitus, hearing change or new tingling.

Sources: nccp

Antimetabolite chemotherapy

Fluorouracil

5-FU
Disrupts production of DNA and RNA

Fluorouracil, often shortened to 5-FU, interferes with the building blocks used to make DNA and RNA, which cells need to grow. It runs through a vein over the prescribed hours. Dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) is an enzyme that breaks it down. Testing for DPD deficiency before treatment reduces risk but cannot rule out every severe reaction.

Cell building blocks disrupted↓Cells cannot divide normally↓Pump provides prescribed exposure

What to watch for
Report severe diarrhoea, mouth sores, chest pain, sudden confusion, sore palms or soles, or unexpectedly early severe symptoms.

Sources: nccp

Every 3 weeks

Combination treatment

Every 21 days for up to 6 combination cycles; cetuximab is weekly.

Cisplatin is given on Day 1, fluorouracil on Days 1–4, and cetuximab on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 21-day cycle, for up to 6 combination cycles. The team may then consider weekly cetuximab alone under a separate maintenance plan (NCCP 00207). This is not an automatic continuation.

Weekly cetuximab visit

Days 8 and 15 within the same combination cycle

Days 8 and 15 of each 21-day combination cycle

These cetuximab-only visits remain part of the same combination cycle. Continue the skin, allergy and symptom checks; use the prescribed visit-specific support plan.

Separate weekly plan

Possible cetuximab-only maintenance, if separately agreed

Only after an explicit review and maintenance prescription

The source allows consideration of cetuximab alone after the combination course, under NCCP 00207. Ask for a separate calendar and support/monitoring plan. The six combination stamps do not record maintenance.

Visits within a cycle are not separate courses. The team records delays, omitted doses and any change of plan. A stamp records completed treatment; it does not authorise the next dose.

Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle

Checks infection-fighting cells, oxygen-carrying cells and platelets.

Kidney and liver tests

WhenAt baseline and before each cycle

Guide safe treatment and identify organ problems.

DPD assessment

WhenBefore the first fluorouracil treatment unless already tested

Checks for reduced ability to break down fluorouracil; results affect treatment choice or dose. Testing cannot exclude every severe reaction. Kidney impairment can affect one blood-based test, so the team chooses the appropriate method.

Hearing assessment

If part of your plan
WhenAt baseline when clinically indicated; review new hearing or balance symptoms during treatment

Investigates hearing loss or tinnitus and helps judge platinum-related risk.

Electrocardiogram (ECG)

If part of your plan
WhenBefore treatment if heart function is compromised; additional assessment if symptoms warrant

Records the heart’s electrical activity. New chest symptoms require assessment even after a reassuring earlier test.

Magnesium and other electrolytes

WhenMagnesium at baseline and before each cycle; magnesium, calcium and electrolytes monthly for 2 months after final cetuximab

Detects mineral changes caused by cetuximab; low levels may cause cramps, weakness or heart-rhythm problems.

Imaging and tumour-specific tests

If part of your plan
WhenAs individually arranged for the disease and symptoms; no universal marker panel or imaging interval

Assesses the cancer rather than whether a treatment-day blood count is safe.

Day 1–4: Combination and fluorouracil days

Daily 22-hour fluorouracil on Days 1–4, or 2,000 mg/m² over 46–48 hours twice as prescribed; confirm changes and completion. Check the pump appointments and contact the unit for problems.

Day 5–15: Recovery plus weekly cetuximab

Cetuximab is due on Days 8 and 15 after review. Other days are not automatically free of side effects. Report diarrhoea, mouth pain, rash, allergy symptoms or poor drinking before each visit.

Continue symptom monitoring and arrange the blood tests required for the next cycle. Ask for revised dates after any hold or delay.

Your treatment, step by step

Your port and treatment pump

From preparing the line to coming home after disconnection. Your team arranges the appointments and shows you what to do.

1 · Before treatment

Confirm access and training

Your team selects the access device and teaches its care, including a port or peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) if used.

2 · Before Day 1

Write down the infusion plan

Daily 22-hour fluorouracil on Days 1–4, or 2,000 mg/m² over 46–48 hours twice as prescribed; confirm changes and completion.

3 · Treatment days

Staff connect and change infusions

Only trained staff connect, program or change the infusion. Do not calculate or alter the rate yourself.

4 · While connected

Watch the line and device

Call for leakage, pain, swelling, damage, disconnection, unexpected completion or a pump that appears not to run. Follow the unit’s device-specific emergency instructions.

5 · At prescribed completion

Attend for disconnection

Completion depends on the actual start time and chosen infusion duration. Do not disconnect or reconnect the line yourself.

6 · Between cycles

Keep the access-care plan

A line may remain between treatments. Keep dressing, flushing and review appointments and report redness, pain, fever or swelling.

Port or PICC: what is the difference?

A port is a small chamber beneath the skin, usually on the chest, attached to a tube ending in a large vein. A special needle goes through the skin into the port when it is used. A PICC enters a vein in the upper arm; a short section remains outside under a dressing. Both can deliver a home infusion. Your team helps choose the device and teaches its care.

Before insertion and during recovery

Tell the team about allergies, blood thinners, infections and any other cancer medicines. Follow the instructions about eating, drinking and transport; do not stop medicines yourself. Port placement uses local anaesthetic, sometimes with sedation or general anaesthetic. The team explains the procedure and risks, including infection, bleeding, clots and insertion complications. Ask how to manage discomfort, protect the wound and resume activity. Report increasing pain, redness, swelling, discharge or fever.

Connection: what the nurse does

The nurse checks the line and treatment, secures the connection and dressing, and starts the pump. Before leaving, you should have the disconnection location and time, contact numbers including out of hours, and the written spill instructions. Ask to be shown what normal pump progress looks like. You do not need to calculate a rate.

Carrying it, sleeping and washing

Use the supplied carrier and the position recommended for your pump. Keep tubing free from pulling and kinks. Ask how to place the pump safely for sleep and how to protect the line and dressing when washing. Do not immerse it or swim. Avoid direct heat and cold. An elastomeric pump has a balloon that shrinks slowly and may have no alarm; an electronic pump has different instructions. Follow the instructions for the device you actually receive.

Disconnection is not port removal

Keep the appointment even if the pump looks empty, unless the team changes the plan. Staff check the pump and line and arrange safe disposal. After flushing and other line care as required, the port needle may be removed and a small dressing applied. The implanted port stays in place until a separate removal procedure is arranged. A PICC remains in place and needs scheduled dressing and flushing care.

Leakage, disconnection or unexpected pump progress

Call the team immediately if the pump leaks, is damaged, finishes unexpectedly early, appears not to run, or the line disconnects. Follow the emergency or spill steps you have been specifically taught, including use of a clamp if instructed. Do not reconnect, change settings or disconnect the pump yourself. Keep other people away from leaked medicine. If medicine touches skin, rinse it off and wash with soap and water, then seek advice.

Pain, swelling or feeling unwell

Contact oncology immediately for pain or swelling around the line, a wet/loose dressing, redness, fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell. A painful swollen arm or neck needs urgent advice. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty or collapse: call 112 / 999. Do not wait for pump disconnection or the next clinic visit.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

One written support-medicine plan

Ask for one list separating prevention medicines, rescue treatment and regular medicines. The schedule must match this treatment and visit. Record what you take and whether it helps; do not double a dose, restart an old prescription or add medicines without checking.

Preventing sickness

This platinum-containing combination needs an agreed anti-sickness plan. Take prevention medicines at the stated times and keep rescue instructions available. Contact the team for persistent vomiting or inability to drink.

Cetuximab premedicines and delayed reactions

An antihistamine and corticosteroid are given before cetuximab to reduce reaction risk. They do not prevent every reaction. Report itching, flushing, swelling, wheeze, dizziness or faintness immediately, including symptoms that start after leaving the unit.

Skin, nails, eyes and minerals

Use the skin-care plan from the team, including moisturising and sun protection. Report painful cracks, inflamed nails, pus or spreading redness. Eye pain or visual change needs prompt review. Keep prescribed blood tests for magnesium and other minerals; supplements must follow clinical advice.

Hydration around cisplatin

Cisplatin requires prescribed intravenous fluids and mineral replacement when indicated. Follow the team’s drinking advice and report much less urine, persistent vomiting or difficulty drinking. Do not alter fluids or water tablets yourself.

Food, fluids and weight

Tell the team early about reduced intake or weight loss. A dietitian can adapt food texture, supplements and intake goals. Follow an individual fluid plan, especially with kidney or heart problems.

Mouth and swallowing care

Follow the mouth-care and pain-relief plan. Report ulcers, thrush or swallowing difficulty early. Ask for a swallowing assessment if food or drinks make you cough or choke; do not assume a general food texture is safe for you.

Fertility, pregnancy and medicine checks

Before treatment, discuss fertility, contraception, pregnancy and breastfeeding, including medicine-specific precautions after treatment. Bring all prescription, over-the-counter and herbal medicines to review. The team must check interactions and allergies.

Understanding the evidence

The EXTREME study tested adding cetuximab to platinum and fluorouracil for recurrent or metastatic squamous head and neck cancer.

The study supports this approach in an appropriate setting. The reported results pooled the platinum options; they are not a separate randomised survival estimate for this particular platinum or for a curative treatment plan. Population: Recurrent or metastatic disease; the study used either cisplatin or carboplatin. Comparison: Platinum and fluorouracil with or without cetuximab. Study populations and individual circumstances differ. The study does not establish that cisplatin and carboplatin give identical outcomes. The current regimen and an individually agreed plan govern treatment duration.

Sources: nccp · extreme

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the aim of this treatment for me, and what are the alternatives?

Cetuximab with cisplatin and fluorouracil is used to treat squamous head and neck cancer. Discuss the individual aim, likely benefit, symptom control and alternatives with your oncology team. Ask your team to write down the individual aim and explain reasonable alternatives, their burdens and the uncertainties before you decide.

Which medicines and tests are due at each visit?

Cisplatin is given on Day 1, fluorouracil on Days 1–4, and cetuximab on Days 1, 8 and 15 of each 21-day cycle, for up to 6 combination cycles. The team may then consider weekly cetuximab alone under a separate maintenance plan (NCCP 00207). This is not an automatic continuation. Full blood count, kidney and liver tests and magnesium before each cycle. Check skin, eyes, bowel symptoms and infusion reactions. Ask about hearing, tinnitus, tingling and hydration at each cycle. Report symptoms before every weekly cetuximab visit, even when chemotherapy is not scheduled. Confirm the actual dates with your unit.

Which symptoms mean I should call immediately?

Call oncology immediately for fever, shivering, sudden illness, persistent vomiting, diarrhoea, severe mouth pain or inability to drink. Chest pain during or after fluorouracil, or unusually early severe symptoms, needs urgent assessment even if DPD testing was normal. Report breathing difficulty, swelling, faintness, spreading skin infection or painful eyes promptly. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure is an emergency. Have the team replace the example numbers with verified daytime, out-of-hours and backup contacts before using the card.

How will we decide whether to continue, change or stop?

Your team reviews symptoms, test results, treatment benefit and your priorities. Ask when cancer-response assessment is planned and what would lead to a change. A delay or omitted dose must be recorded and does not count as treatment received.

What support can help me manage treatment?

Ask for a written support-medicine plan and early help with mouth pain, eating, drinking, swallowing, skin or nerve symptoms. Tell the team about travel, work, caring and communication needs. Dietitian, speech and language, nursing and other support can be arranged according to your needs.

Try explaining the plan back: Explain which medicines you receive on each visit. Name the symptoms that mean you should contact oncology immediately. Show the actual contact numbers and say which tests and appointments are next.
