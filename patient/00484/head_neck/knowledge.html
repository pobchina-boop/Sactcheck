When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology immediately for new breathlessness, chest pain, diarrhoea, severe tummy pain, yellow skin or eyes, much less urine, severe headache, vision changes, marked weakness, confusion, unusual thirst or a spreading or blistering rash. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or seizure is an emergency.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Your treatment medicines and modalities

Nivolumab aims to control head and neck cancer that has returned or spread, relieve symptoms and help some people live longer. Benefit and side effects are reviewed for you.

Immunotherapy

Nivolumab

Opdivo
Blocks PD-1 on immune cells

Nivolumab blocks PD-1 on immune cells, helping them recognise and attack cancer. It can also cause inflammation in healthy organs.

Immune cell↓PD-1 blocked↓Cancer attacked

What to watch for
Report new breathing, bowel, skin, hormone, heart or nerve symptoms promptly; these may be immune inflammation.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Immune alert card and treatment holds

Carry your immune alert card during and after nivolumab. Tell any clinician that you have received immunotherapy. Do not restart a held treatment or change prescribed steroids or hormone replacement yourself.

Eating and weight

Tell the team early if swallowing, appetite, pain or taste changes reduce intake. A dietitian can agree practical food, drink or feeding support with you. Do not wait for substantial weight loss.

Mouth and dental care

Agree a mouth-care plan and tell your dentist about previous or current cancer treatment. Arrange oncology advice before dental procedures, particularly after head and neck radiation.

Swallowing and communication

Ask for speech and language therapy if swallowing or speech is difficult. Use the food textures, exercises and communication support advised for you; a general guide cannot establish safe swallowing.

Medicines and fertility

Bring prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines and supplements for review. Discuss fertility preservation and breastfeeding before treatment. Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least five months after the final dose. Tell the team promptly about a possible pregnancy. Do not breastfeed without specific advice from the team.

Length of the course

Sources: nccp

A pause or change

Your team may pause or stop treatment to assess side effects. Record the reason, required tests and who can authorise restarting. A new cycle does not erase earlier severe toxicity.

Sources: nccp

Changing nivolumab schedule

When switching from two-week to four-week dosing, the first four-week dose is given two weeks after the last two-week dose. In the reverse direction, the first two-week dose is given four weeks after the last four-week dose. Your team confirms the actual switch date and formulation.

Medicines, precautions and visit preparation

Access and observation

Intravenous treatment needs suitable venous access. Under-the-skin nivolumab is given into the thigh or abdomen, rotating sites and avoiding damaged skin, scars and moles. It is not given into a vein. Tell staff about local pain or swelling.

Evidence and further information

Use evidence to support a personal treatment discussion.

What studies can tell us

CheckMate 141 found longer overall survival with nivolumab than the selected single-agent alternatives after platinum failure. The trial used weight-based intravenous nivolumab every two weeks. It did not separately randomise the current fixed-dose, four-week or under-the-skin schedules.

Sources: nccp · trial

Your individual decision

Group results cannot predict what will happen for you. Ask which benefits matter for your situation and how the team will decide whether treatment remains worthwhile.
