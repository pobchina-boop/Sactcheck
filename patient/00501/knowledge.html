When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. New breathlessness, reduced urine, severe abdominal pain or diarrhoea, widespread blistering rash, rapid swelling or new visual symptoms needs urgent assessment. Fever is never assumed to be only a flu-like drug effect.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

The treatment aims to control advanced sarcoma and help cancer-related symptoms. The choice depends on sarcoma subtype, previous treatment, fitness and your priorities; your consultant explains why this combination is being considered.

Chemotherapy

Gemcitabine

Antimetabolite chemotherapy
Interferes with deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) building

Gemcitabine interferes with making DNA, which dividing cancer cells need. Healthy blood-forming cells and other tissues can also be affected.

DNA building↓Replication disrupted↓Dividing cells affected

What to watch for
Report fever, breathlessness, new widespread rash, rapid swelling, reduced urine, unusual bruising or severe headache with visual change or confusion urgently.

Sources: nccp · gemcitabine-pi

Taxane chemotherapy

Docetaxel

Disrupts the cell’s division machinery

Docetaxel stabilises tiny structures called microtubules. Cells need these to separate their chromosomes during division; disrupting the process can stop cancer cells multiplying. Healthy dividing cells are also affected.

Microtubules stabilised↓Division disrupted↓Cell growth affected

What to watch for
Take the prescribed dexamethasone before docetaxel. Report fever, severe abdominal pain or diarrhoea, breathlessness, swelling, persistent numbness or new blurred vision promptly. Tell staff immediately about any infusion reaction.

Sources: nccp · docetaxel-pi

Individual prescribed course

NCCP 00501 gives gemcitabine 675 mg/m² on days 1 and 8, with docetaxel 75 mg/m² on day 8. Repeat every 21 days for up to 6 cycles if suitable. Day 8 has its own safety review and can be delayed or omitted on clinical advice.

This is the gemcitabine 675 mg/m², 90-minute sarcoma schedule. It must not be replaced by a 1000 mg/m² or 30-minute gemcitabine schedule from another regimen. The sarcoma use described by this source is off-label and requires a documented discussion and consent under local policy.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Get the day 7–9 dexamethasone prescription before the day 8 visit. Contact the team if doses were missed or vomited; do not silently double doses. Review diabetes control, infection symptoms and all medicines. Reconcile steroid premedication with any anti-sickness steroids.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Gemcitabine is infused over 90 minutes on both days 1 and 8. On day 8 it is followed by docetaxel over 60 minutes. Allow additional time for blood tests, review, line care and observation.

STEP 3

Intravenous access and line care

The team chooses suitable intravenous access and explains line care. Tell the nurse immediately about burning, pain, redness or swelling during an injection or drip. Do not wait for the visit to finish. If you have a port or peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC), agree dressing, flushing and removal arrangements.

STEP 4

Home support

Take dexamethasone 8 mg by mouth twice daily for 3 days, starting day 7, unless your team prescribes an explicitly revised plan; NCCP requires at least 3 doses before docetaxel. This reduces allergic reactions and fluid retention. Both drugs have low emetogenic classification in this source. granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (white-cell growth support) (G-CSF) may be used based on the prescribed risk plan; it is not mandatory for every patient in NCCP 00501.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Treatment reference · 05

Your consent discussion

Use the general treatment risks and the separate medicine-specific risks together.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste change, diarrhoea or constipation; tiredness, hair and skin changes; infusion reactions and intravenous-line complications; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. Risks differ between medicines and people. The medicine-specific section explains the main additional risks for this regimen.

Sources: nccp · gemcitabine-pi · docetaxel-pi

GeDDiS: gemcitabine/docetaxel versus doxorubicin

GeDDiS tested these gemcitabine/docetaxel doses and infusion durations against doxorubicin 75 mg/m² in previously untreated advanced soft tissue sarcoma. The combination did not improve progression-free or overall survival and was harder to deliver. The study did not show a clear superior effect in a particular histological subgroup.

A first-line comparison does not establish the best later-line choice for every sarcoma subtype. Individual suitability and prior treatment matter. Do not import growth-factor schedules or supportive orders from the trial into the NCCP regimen automatically.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Are blood tests the same as checking whether treatment works?

No. Blood counts and organ tests mainly check safety. Your sarcoma team uses the clinical review and planned imaging to assess cancer control.
