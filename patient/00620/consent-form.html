Consent to systemic anti-cancer treatment

Bevacizumab 7.5 mg/kg with carboplatin and paclitaxel

Patient nameDate of birthHospital / MRN

Treatment and purpose

Help control the cancer after surgery and delay its return or growth. First-line treatment after surgery for stage III epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer with at least 1 cm residual disease, or stage IV disease.

Treatment schedule. Paclitaxel, carboplatin and bevacizumab 7.5 mg/kg every 21 days for up to six cycles. Omit bevacizumab in cycle 1 if starting within four weeks of surgery. Bevacizumab 7.5 mg/kg alone every 21 days. Up to 18 bevacizumab doses in total, counting combination doses.

Regimen-specific side effects

Paclitaxel

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Numbness or weaknessTell the team about tingling, falls, trouble walking or fastening buttons before the next infusion. Nerve damage can persist.
Infusion allergyTell the nurse immediately about flushing, wheeze, chest tightness or dizziness during the infusion.
Hair loss, aches and low blood countsHair loss and muscle or joint pain can occur. Fever, shivering or sudden illness may signal infection and need an urgent call.

Carboplatin

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Low blood counts and infectionCall urgently for fever, shivering or unexpected bleeding. Red cells and platelets can fall as well as white cells.
Nausea and vomitingTake the prescribed anti-sickness medicines. Call if vomiting continues or you cannot keep fluids down.
Allergy, kidneys and hearingTell the nurse immediately about an infusion reaction. Kidney function guides dosing. Report new ringing in the ears or hearing changes.

Bevacizumab

Effect to discussWhat to look for and when to get help
Blood pressure and urine proteinHigh blood pressure and protein leaking into urine can develop without symptoms. Attend blood pressure and urine checks.
Bleeding, clots and wound healingReport bleeding or a wound that opens. Tell the team before surgery or invasive procedures. Sudden chest pain, weakness or severe breathlessness needs emergency help.
Bowel perforation or fistulaA tear in the bowel or an abnormal connection can be life-threatening. Phone urgently for severe abdominal pain, fever or new leakage of urine or stool.
Brain and visionA severe headache, seizure, confusion or sudden visual change needs emergency assessment.

Treatment discussion and decision

Bevacizumab 7.5 mg/kg with carboplatin and paclitaxel

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

Adding bevacizumab delayed progression in the trial. Overall survival was similar across the whole study population. The suggested survival benefit in the poor-prognosis subgroup was exploratory.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call the oncology unit now for fever, chills, severe abdominal pain, unexpected bleeding or new breathlessness. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
