When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shivering, sudden illness, unusual bruising or bleeding, persistent vomiting, severe mouth or throat pain, inability to drink or much less urine. Report new hearing changes, numbness, worsening swallowing, choking or skin breakdown promptly. During an infusion, tell staff immediately about rash, flushing, chest tightness, breathing difficulty or faintness. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure is an emergency: call 112 or 999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

What each part of treatment does

Carboplatin and radiotherapy are used together as part of the treatment plan for head and neck cancer. The team explains the treatment aim, expected benefit and why this medicine and schedule suit your situation.

Platinum chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Carboplatin
Damages cancer-cell deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA)

Carboplatin interferes with DNA repair and cell division. Its dose is calculated from kidney function. Blood counts can fall, including platelets that help stop bleeding. It is a different prescription from cisplatin. AUC is a prescribing target for drug exposure, not the number of milligrams in your bag; your clinical team calculates the dose using kidney function.

DNA damage↓Cell division disrupted↓Cancer growth reduced

What to watch for
Report fever, unusual bruising or bleeding, marked tiredness or an infusion reaction.

Sources: nccp

Local treatment

Radiotherapy

Radiotherapy
Damages cancer cells in the planned treatment area

Radiotherapy directs radiation at a carefully planned area. It is delivered on its own appointment calendar. Effects depend on which tissues receive radiation and can build up during the course or continue after it finishes.

Treatment area planned↓Repeated local treatment↓Recovery and late-effect review

What to watch for
Report difficulty swallowing, weight loss, mouth pain, skin breakdown or problems managing saliva.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Weekly medicine visit

Blood-test results, symptoms and the radiation plan are reviewed before carboplatin. The infusion takes about 30 minutes, but the whole appointment also includes assessment, preparation and any prescribed support medicines. Tell staff about new symptoms or any radiation cancellation before the infusion.

Day 2–7: Radiation appointments and symptom care

Attend radiation on its own calendar, follow the mouth, skin, nutrition and swallowing plan, and record symptoms. The weekly chemotherapy cycle does not imply seven daily radiation appointments. Call if eating, drinking, swallowing or pain control becomes difficult, or if you are unwell. Keep the next blood-test appointment.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the exact regimen, treatment aim, baseline tests, transport, support medicines and verified urgent contact numbers. Bring a full medicine list and any hearing, nerve or swallowing concerns.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Carboplatin AUC 1.5 is infused through a vein over 30 minutes. The dose in milligrams is calculated by the treating team using kidney function. Allow extra time for assessment, preparation and prescribed anti-sickness treatment, as well as separate radiation appointments. This weekly carboplatin regimen does not automatically require the cisplatin hydration or docetaxel steroid schedule from an earlier phase.

STEP 3

Keeping appointments together

Keep one calendar for blood tests, treatment and any radiation, dental, nutrition or swallowing appointments. If an appointment is cancelled, ask the relevant teams to agree the revised plan.

STEP 4

Practical help

Tell the team about travel, cost, work, caring responsibilities, communication or reading needs. Ask whether a support person or interpreter can attend and how treatment changes will be explained.

STEP 5

Leaving the unit

Check the next blood-test and treatment dates, medicine instructions and whom to call if unwell. Have the service confirm the daytime, out-of-hours and backup contact routes.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

A new support plan for this phase

Have one written list of preventive, rescue and regular medicines. Do not automatically continue steroid premedication, growth factor, antibiotics or cisplatin fluids from an earlier regimen; each requires a current prescription.

Anti-sickness treatment

The team prescribes anti-sickness medicines according to this carboplatin schedule and local policy. Ask which medicines prevent symptoms, which are rescue doses and when persistent vomiting requires a call.

Mouth and dental care

Arrange dental preparation before head and neck radiation. Follow the prescribed brushing, fluoride, rinse and pain-relief plan. Report ulcers, infection, bleeding or difficulty opening the mouth. Tell future dentists about radiation before invasive treatment.

Nutrition and hydration

A dietitian can adapt food, supplements or feeding support to your needs. Ask for a practical fluid plan that accounts for swallowing and heart or kidney problems. Call early for weight loss, poor intake or inability to drink.

Swallowing and communication

Follow advice from your speech and language therapist on safe swallowing, exercises and textures. Tell the team about choking, a wet voice after swallowing, food sticking, hearing difficulty or communication needs.

Skin and symptom relief

Use the radiation team’s skin and pain plan. Symptoms may increase during the course or persist after it ends; ask for review when care is no longer controlling them.

Fertility, pregnancy and other medicines

Discuss fertility, pregnancy, breastfeeding and the medicine-specific contraception period. Bring medicines, supplements and allergies and check before starting new products.

Treatment reference · 05

Mouth, teeth and jaw care

Dental preparation and gentle daily care reduce avoidable problems.

Before treatment

Arrange dental assessment before head and neck radiation. The dental and oncology teams decide what treatment is needed and how much healing time is required; do not schedule extractions independently.

Sources: oral

During treatment

Follow the prescribed brushing, fluoride, rinse and pain-relief advice. Report ulcers, infection, new bleeding, difficulty wearing dentures or worsening jaw opening. Avoid starting a new mouthwash without checking if the mouth is sore.

Sources: oral

After head and neck radiation

Dry mouth can increase dental problems and some radiation effects last. Keep regular dental care and tell any future dentist that you have had head and neck radiation before invasive treatment. Your radiation/dental team plans any procedure.

Sources: oral

Treatment reference · 06

Eating, drinking and maintaining strength

The aim is adequate intake and comfort during treatment.

Recognising an intake problem

Record weight and report food or drink becoming harder to manage. Pain, taste change, dry mouth or nausea can each need a different response; a dietitian can adapt the plan to you.

Sources: nutrition · hse

If ordinary food is not enough

The team may suggest food fortification, supplements or tube feeding when intake or swallowing safety requires it. A tube is a support option, not a judgement about how well you have tried to eat. Training and follow-up are needed.

Fluid needs are individual

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

How many weekly carboplatin doses are planned?

This source plans 7 weekly AUC 1.5 doses during radiotherapy, with each dose subject to safety review. Radiation has its own number of sessions and appointment calendar.

What does AUC mean?

It is a target drug exposure used by the clinical team to calculate your carboplatin dose from kidney function. It is not a fixed milligram dose and is not a calculation you need to perform.

What if radiation is cancelled or stopped?

Contact both teams before the next carboplatin dose. This regimen is only given with radiation, and carboplatin must be discontinued if the radiation course is cut short. The teams confirm the revised plan.

Do I keep medicines from my previous chemotherapy?

Only if your team includes them in the new prescription. Induction steroids, growth factors, antibiotics and cisplatin fluids do not automatically carry forward.

When does this phase start?

This phase follows induction chemotherapy. The source uses different reference points for the 3–8 week transition interval, so the oncology and radiation teams must confirm the actual start date after reviewing response and recovery.

Will more chemotherapy be given afterwards?

After the planned chemoradiation course, the team reviews recovery and response and decides whether surgery or other treatment is appropriate. Induction and this weekly phase have separate prescriptions; later treatment is not automatic.

When will my mouth and swallowing recover?

Recovery varies and effects can continue after radiation finishes. Keep pain, dental, nutrition and swallowing support and report worsening symptoms; the last session does not mean support should stop.
