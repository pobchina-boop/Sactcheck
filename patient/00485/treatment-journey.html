The whole treatment journey

Dose-dense AC → weekly paclitaxel: treatment and checks

Adjuvant or neoadjuvant high-risk node-negative or node-positive breast cancer

Four AC treatments (doxorubicin 60 mg/m² and cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m²) 14 days apart, followed 14 days after the last AC by 12 weekly paclitaxel 80 mg/m² doses, subject to recovery. The planned sequence spans about 20 weeks.

What each check is for

1Before treatmentBefore the first dose

Adjuvant or neoadjuvant high-risk node-negative or node-positive breast cancer. FBC, kidney and liver tests at baseline and before each cycle, with the unit confirming weekly paclitaxel test requirements. Review current illness and past dose delays separately.

Ask / act: Confirm eligibility, test results and medicine list.

Sources: NCCP 00485

2Other checksAt the specified review points

Review lifetime anthracycline exposure and cardiac risk. Echo/MUGA is indicated above age 65 or when clinically indicated; the source requires LVEF above 50% for doxorubicin. Reassess cardiac symptoms and neuropathy as indicated; no trastuzumab is included.

Ask / act: Confirm who arranges and reviews each check.

Sources: NCCP 00485

3Support and practical planAt each treatment or phase change

G-CSF is required for every dose-dense AC cycle. Prescribe product, dose and timing separately under the approved local plan. First paclitaxel dose: dexamethasone 8 mg intravenous (IV), chlorphenamine 10 mg IV and famotidine 20 mg IV, each 30 minutes before treatment (NCCP suggested table).

Ask / act: Choose the matching support plan; confirm active medicines.

Sources: NCCP 00485

4Response and follow-upAgreed review points

After early-breast treatment, breast imaging and clinical review follow the agreed plan. Routine computed tomography (CT)/positron emission tomography (PET) scans or blood tumour markers are not used to search for distant recurrence in someone without symptoms or clinical concerns. Keep the agreed follow-up and report persistent or new symptoms between visits.

Ask / act: Review symptoms, results and patient priorities.

Sources: EARLY
