How your medicines work

Anthracycline chemotherapy

Doxorubicin

Interferes with DNA handling
DNA handling disrupted→Cell damage→Division reduced

Doxorubicin interferes with enzymes and DNA processes needed for cell division. It can also affect heart muscle, so the team considers previous exposure and cardiac risk.

Report pain or burning at the infusion site immediately. New breathlessness or chest symptoms need prompt review. Red urine for a short time can be a drug-colour effect; pain, clots or persistent bleeding still need advice.

Alkylating chemotherapy

Cyclophosphamide

Damages DNA needed for growth
Drug activated→DNA damage→Cell division affected

Cyclophosphamide is activated in the body and damages DNA. This helps stop dividing cancer cells but can also affect blood-cell production and reproductive tissues.

Use the prescribed anti-sickness and hydration plan. Report fever, painful urination or blood in the urine.

Taxane chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Locks the cell division machinery
Microtubules form→Structures remain locked→Division is interrupted

Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues.

Report tingling or numbness before the next weekly dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Sources: NCCP 00485

2Other checksAt the specified review points

Review lifetime anthracycline exposure and cardiac risk. Echo/MUGA is indicated above age 65 or when clinically indicated; the source requires LVEF above 50% for doxorubicin. Reassess cardiac symptoms and neuropathy as indicated; no trastuzumab is included.

Ask / act: Confirm who arranges and reviews each check.

Sources: NCCP 00485

3Support and practical planAt each treatment or phase change

G-CSF is required for every dose-dense AC cycle. Prescribe product, dose and timing separately under the approved local plan. First paclitaxel dose: dexamethasone 8 mg IV, chlorphenamine 10 mg IV and famotidine 20 mg IV, each 30 minutes before treatment (NCCP suggested table).

Ask / act: Choose the matching support plan; confirm active medicines.

Sources: NCCP 00485

4Response and follow-upAgreed review points

After early-breast treatment, breast imaging and clinical review follow the agreed plan. Routine CT/PET scans or blood tumour markers are not used to search for distant recurrence in someone without symptoms or clinical concerns. Keep the agreed follow-up and report persistent or new symptoms between visits.

Ask / act: Review symptoms, results and patient priorities.

Practical treatment guide

Your treatment and access plan

Four AC treatments (doxorubicin 60 mg/m² and cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m²) 14 days apart, followed 14 days after the last AC by 12 weekly paclitaxel 80 mg/m² doses, subject to recovery. The planned sequence spans about 20 weeks.

STEP 1

Before treatment

FBC, kidney and liver tests at baseline and before each cycle, with the unit confirming weekly paclitaxel test requirements. Review current illness and past dose delays separately.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. A port is not automatically required; insertion and removal are separate procedures with individual instructions.

STEP 3

On the treatment day

Confirm the medicines due that day, any premedicines and the reviewed blood results. Tell staff immediately about pain, burning, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

After the visit

No routine take-home infusion pump is required for this regimen. Keep line-care, urgent-contact and next-review instructions.

Visit length

AC and weekly paclitaxel have different visit and supportive-medicine plans. The unit confirms the complete appointment duration.

Line care

A PICC or port has its own care plan. Report fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or line damage; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

Treatment changes

A delayed, omitted or changed dose needs an updated plan from the treating team. Do not move doses or restart on your own.

Support for this visit

G-CSF is required for every dose-dense AC cycle. Prescribe product, dose and timing separately under the approved local plan. First paclitaxel dose: dexamethasone 8 mg IV, chlorphenamine 10 mg IV and famotidine 20 mg IV, each 30 minutes before treatment (NCCP suggested table).

Selected effects, not every possible effect or a prediction. Blood-count, skin and line markers are schematic. Number and text carry meaning; colour is decorative.

Doxorubicin

  • Low counts, infection and mouth ulcers
  • Nausea, hair loss and fatigue
  • Heart damage, sometimes delayed
  • Severe tissue injury if the infusion leaks
  • Rare later blood cancers

Cyclophosphamide

  • Low counts and infection
  • Nausea, vomiting and hair loss
  • Bladder irritation / bleeding
  • Fertility effects and menopause
  • Rare later blood cancers

Paclitaxel

  • Low blood counts and infection
  • Numbness / tingling, sometimes lasting
  • Hair loss, muscle or joint pain
  • Infusion reaction
  • Mouth, bowel, skin and nail effects
