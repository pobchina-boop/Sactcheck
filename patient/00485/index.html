When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

My treatment

My overall treatment

Four AC treatments every 14 days, then 12 weekly paclitaxel doses starting 14 days after the last AC. About 20 weeks without delays.

  1. AC phaseFour treatments, 14 days apart; prescribed G-CSF support.
  2. Paclitaxel phaseTwelve weekly doses; the premedicines change.

Phase changePaclitaxel starts 14 days after the last AC
Paclitaxel ×12One dose every 7 days

Your team confirms the active phase and dates. Symptoms may occur during or after treatment; there are no guaranteed safe days.

More about my medicines

Doxorubicin

Doxorubicin interferes with enzymes and DNA processes needed for cell division. It can also affect heart muscle, so the team considers previous exposure and cardiac risk.

Cyclophosphamide

Cyclophosphamide is activated in the body and damages DNA. This helps stop dividing cancer cells but can also affect blood-cell production and reproductive tissues.

Paclitaxel

Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues.

Day 1–14: Reference cycle

Four AC treatments (doxorubicin 60 mg/m² and cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m²) 14 days apart, followed 14 days after the last AC by 12 weekly paclitaxel 80 mg/m² doses, subject to recovery. The planned sequence spans about 20 weeks.

What to do: Follow your written appointment and medicine plan. A calendar does not authorise a dose.
