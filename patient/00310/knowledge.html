When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team immediately for fever or suddenly feeling unwell, shaking chills, new breathlessness, chest pain, unusual bleeding, very little urine, severe rash or blistering, or vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down. Use your unit’s temperature threshold and emergency instructions. Do not wait until the next visit.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Three medicines. Different jobs.

These two chemotherapy medicines aim to slow or shrink advanced non-small-cell lung cancer and help control symptoms. Benefit is not guaranteed. Your team explains the aim for your own situation.

Chemotherapy

Gemcitabine

Antimetabolite chemotherapy
Interferes with deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) building

Gemcitabine interferes with making DNA, which dividing cancer cells need. Healthy blood-forming cells and other tissues can also be affected.

DNA building↓Replication disrupted↓Dividing cells affected

What to watch for
Report fever, unusual bleeding, breathlessness, rash, swelling or reduced urine promptly.

Sources: nccp

Chemotherapy

Carboplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Damages DNA

Carboplatin forms links in DNA that hinder copying and repair. Healthy tissues can also be harmed. Its dose depends on target exposure (AUC) and measured or estimated kidney function; AUC is not a fixed number of milligrams. There are two planned doses in this regimen.

DNA↓Platinum links↓Copying disrupted

What to watch for
Report fever, bleeding, nausea, hearing changes or numbness. Tell the nurse immediately about possible allergy during the infusion.

Sources: nccp

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm blood tests, transport, medicine instructions and contact numbers.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

Gemcitabine runs through a drip over 30 minutes on Days 1 and 8. Carboplatin runs over 30 minutes on Day 1. Its dose is calculated using your kidney function. Blood tests, pharmacy preparation, support medicines and review add to the visit time.

STEP 3

Access

Suitable intravenous (IV) access is needed. This plan does not use a routine take-home infusion pump.

STEP 4

Home support

Follow one written anti-sickness prescription, including any tablets for home and rescue treatment. Day 8 gemcitabine has a separate lower-risk anti-sickness plan.Carboplatin needs a reliable kidney-function result for dosing. Report unusual bruising or bleeding promptly.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next Day 1 or Day 8 visit and any response scan before leaving.

Treatment reference · 01

Your treatment

These two chemotherapy medicines aim to slow or shrink advanced non-small-cell lung cancer and help control symptoms. Benefit is not guaranteed. Your team explains the aim for your own situation.

Which plan is this?

This guide covers standalone chemotherapy for advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC). If nivolumab is part of your treatment before surgery, use the separate 00849 guide for gemcitabine/carboplatin; that is a different three-cycle plan. The urothelial cancer indication is separate and is not explained by this lung guide. The standalone lung indication is off-label in Ireland; your team should discuss this and follow local approval and consent policy.

Sources: nccp

How the medicines work

Gemcitabine interferes with the DNA building blocks a dividing cell needs. Platinum makes links within DNA that disrupt copying and repair. Healthy blood-forming cells and other tissues can also be affected.

Sources: nccp

Benefits and alternatives

A randomized study in previously untreated advanced lung cancer compared gemcitabine with cisplatin against gemcitabine with carboplatin. Both approaches showed anticancer activity. Treatment choice also depends on kidney function, hearing, nerves, blood counts and your preferences. Ask about alternative systemic treatments, clinical trials, symptom-focused care and the choice not to proceed.

Sources: nccp · trial

Treatment reference · 04

Side effects and action

Effects differ between people; report new symptoms early.

When to call now

Call your oncology team immediately for fever or suddenly feeling unwell, shaking chills, new breathlessness, chest pain, unusual bleeding, very little urine, severe rash or blistering, or vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down. Use your unit’s temperature threshold and emergency instructions. Do not wait until the next visit.

Sources: nccp

During a drip

Tell the nurse immediately about rash, flushing, chills, breathlessness, chest discomfort, faintness or pain/swelling around the cannula.

Sources: nccp

Blood and organ checks

Tests can detect changes before you notice symptoms. Normal results at the last visit do not rule out a problem now.

Why this combination may be offered

A randomized study in previously untreated advanced lung cancer compared gemcitabine with cisplatin against gemcitabine with carboplatin. Both approaches showed anticancer activity. Treatment choice also depends on kidney function, hearing, nerves, blood counts and your preferences.

This older study used different doses from the current NCCP schedules and predates modern biomarker-directed and immune treatments. Its averages cannot predict your outcome or establish an individual survival benefit.

Sources: trial · nccp

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

How often do I attend?

Usually Day 1 for both medicines and Day 8 for gemcitabine in each 21-day cycle. Your unit confirms actual appointments and blood tests.

Does a delayed dose mean treatment failed?

No. Blood counts, kidney results or symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Will I also receive immunotherapy, radiotherapy or surgery?

This guide covers standalone chemotherapy for advanced NSCLC. If nivolumab is part of your treatment before surgery, use the separate 00849 guide for gemcitabine/carboplatin; that is a different three-cycle plan. The urothelial cancer indication is separate and is not explained by this lung guide. The standalone lung indication is off-label in Ireland; your team should discuss this and follow local approval and consent policy.

Who do I call if I feel unwell?

Use the oncology number on your treatment summary or SACT card. Ask for a daytime and out-of-hours number before leaving. Do not use the QR website as an emergency contact service.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are you having now? What changes after induction? What would make you call today? Ask your team to fill any gaps.
