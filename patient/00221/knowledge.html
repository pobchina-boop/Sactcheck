How this medicine supports your treatment

Afatinib blocks abnormal EGFR-family growth signals in selected lung cancers. Treatment aims to control advanced cancer and delay progression.

EGFR-family targeted therapy

Afatinib

Blocks abnormal growth signals
Activating EGFR mutation identified↓Growth signalling blocked↓Cancer growth slowed

Afatinib is an irreversible EGFR-family tyrosine kinase inhibitor. It interrupts signals involved in cancer-cell growth; the tumour mutation test and treatment setting determine whether it is suitable.

Checks before and during treatment

Tests and symptoms guide safe treatment decisions.

Before starting

Confirm the activating EGFR test, previous treatments and prescription. Check blood count, kidneys and liver. A heart-pumping test (ECHO or MUGA) is used if clinically indicated. Review lungs, bowel, eyes, skin, all medicines and pregnancy precautions.

NCCP 00221 v5 · official regimen · EMA Giotrif product information (updated 11 May 2026)

During treatment

NCCP specifies blood count, kidney and liver tests monthly. Heart-function tests are repeated if clinically indicated. Report diarrhoea and skin changes early, especially in the first weeks. Scans and cancer reviews follow your Consultant’s plan.

Taking afatinib

Follow your individual prescription.

Daily tablets

Take at the same time daily without food: leave at least 3 hours after food before the dose and wait at least 1 hour afterwards before eating. Swallow with water. If missed, take it the same day unless the next dose is due within 8 hours; then skip. Never double a dose or repeat a vomited dose. Ask pharmacy for the approved water-dispersion method if swallowing is difficult.

NCCP 00221 v5 · official regimen · EMA Giotrif product information (updated 11 May 2026)

Dose changes

Afatinib dose changes are usually in 10 mg steps. Only the team may consider 50 mg after the initial 3 weeks if conditions are met; previous reductions rule out escalation. If 20 mg cannot be tolerated, a different treatment plan may be needed.

Living with treatment

Report symptoms early and keep contact details available.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Keep prescribed anti-diarrhoea medicine available from the first dose. Start your agreed plan at the first loose stool, keep drinking if able and report symptoms early. Your team may advise continuing symptom medicine until loose stools have stopped for 12 hours. Severe diarrhoea, weakness or reduced urine needs urgent advice.

Skin, nails and mouth

Use the agreed EGFR-inhibitor skin-care and mouth-care plan. Emollients and sun protection may help; report rash, painful nails or mouth sores early. The team decides whether creams, antibiotics or other treatment are needed.

Nausea medicines

Afatinib has minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Routine infusion premedication is not required. Agree any as-needed anti-sickness medicine with the team, after checking interactions and your medical history.

Medicine interactions

Give pharmacy a list of all medicines, herbal products and supplements. Some P-gp inhibitors need separated dosing; strong inducers such as St John’s wort can reduce afatinib exposure. Ask for a written individual schedule before adding or changing medicines.

Pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding

Avoid pregnancy. Women who could become pregnant need adequate contraception during treatment and for at least 1 month after the final dose. Do not breastfeed during treatment. Discuss fertility and future pregnancy plans before starting.

When to get urgent help

Do not wait for the next appointment.

Call urgently

Call urgently for new or worsening breathlessness, cough or fever; severe diarrhoea, inability to drink or much less urine; severe or persistent abdominal pain; blistering or peeling skin; or yellow skin/eyes. Eye pain, light sensitivity or blurred vision needs prompt assessment. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or swelling of the tongue/throat needs emergency help. Use your unit’s temperature and 24-hour contact instructions.

What studies tell us

In studies of selected EGFR-mutated advanced lung cancers, afatinib delayed cancer progression compared with cisplatin/pemetrexed. The overall survival comparison in that trial was not statistically significant. A separate comparison with gefitinib also did not show a statistically significant overall-survival difference. Ask how the study population and alternatives match your situation.

Your own decision

Ask how the study population matches your situation and discuss alternatives, harms and remaining uncertainty.

Questions to ask

Is there a planned week off?

No. These tablets are continuous daily treatment. A hold for side effects is a clinical decision.

What if I miss a dose or vomit?

Take at the same time daily without food: leave at least 3 hours after food before the dose and wait at least 1 hour afterwards before eating. Swallow with water. If missed, take it the same day unless the next dose is due within 8 hours; then skip. Never double a dose or repeat a vomited dose. Ask pharmacy for the approved water-dispersion method if swallowing is difficult.

Can I adjust the dose myself?

No. Keep the prescription and contact plan. Your team authorises every hold, restart or dose change.

Do I need all the tests every day?

No. Your team schedules tests and reviews. Contact them between appointments if symptoms occur.

Explain the plan back: Can you explain how to take your tablets, what to do after a missed or vomited dose, which tests are next and which symptoms mean you should call today?
