When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

Help control advanced urothelial cancer previously treated with platinum chemotherapy and a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor.

Antibody-drug conjugate (targeted chemotherapy)

Enfortumab vedotin

Padcev
Delivers a cell-division inhibitor to cells bearing nectin-4

An antibody attaches to a protein called nectin-4 and delivers a chemotherapy payload that disrupts cell division. Healthy skin, nerves and other tissues can also be affected. This is not a checkpoint immunotherapy medicine.

Nectin-4 attachment↓Payload delivered↓Cell division disrupted

What to watch for
Report any rash early, especially blistering, fever or mouth/eye/genital sores; also report thirst, frequent urination, numbness or new breathing symptoms.

Advanced urothelial cancer · after platinum and immunotherapy

Days 1, 8 and 15 · every 28 days

Individual prescribed course

Enfortumab vedotin is given on days 1, 8 and 15 of a 28-day cycle. The starting dose is 1.25 mg/kg, capped at 125 mg per dose. The team may hold, reduce or stop treatment for side effects. Treatment continues while appropriate according to benefit and tolerance.

Severe skin reactions may occur early, including the first cycle. High glucose, lung inflammation, neuropathy and eye problems need prompt assessment; a week without an infusion is not a guaranteed symptom-free week.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm diabetes/glucose advice, a skin and eye-care plan, baseline nerve symptoms and contact numbers.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

The infusion runs over 30 minutes through a carefully checked intravenous line. Tests, review and observation add to the visit time. Tell the nurse immediately if there is pain or swelling around the line.

STEP 3

Access and pump care

Suitable intravenous access is needed. There is no routine take-home pump. Report pain, redness or swelling around the infusion site immediately.

STEP 4

Home support

Follow the prescribed low-risk anti-sickness plan. Ask about moisturiser, skin care and artificial tears for dry eyes. Check glucose as directed, including if not previously diabetic. Premedication with an antihistamine, antipyretic and intravenous steroid is considered for subsequent infusions after a prior infusion reaction; it is not an automatic routine prescription for everyone.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

EV-301 · 2021 primary report

EV-301 randomised 608 people previously treated with platinum and a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor. Median overall survival was 12.88 months with enfortumab versus 8.97 months with investigator-selected chemotherapy (hazard ratio 0.70, 95% confidence interval 0.56–0.89).

These group medians do not predict an individual lifespan. The result concerns later-line monotherapy and must not be substituted for evidence about first-line enfortumab/pembrolizumab.

Sources: trial · nccp

Questions worth asking

What is the treatment trying to do?

Help control advanced urothelial cancer previously treated with platinum chemotherapy and a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor. This page covers enfortumab alone after prior platinum and checkpoint immunotherapy. It is distinct from first-line enfortumab plus pembrolizumab, which has a different schedule and risk context.

When do I attend?

Enfortumab vedotin is given on days 1, 8 and 15 of a 28-day cycle. The starting dose is 1.25 mg/kg, capped at 125 mg per dose. The team may hold, reduce or stop treatment for side effects. Treatment continues while appropriate according to benefit and tolerance.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.
