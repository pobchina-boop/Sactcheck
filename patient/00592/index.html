InfusionDay 1

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

Atezolizumab 840 mg is given by intravenous infusion on Day 1 every 2 weeks (14 days). The first infusion lasts about 60 minutes; later infusions may last 30 minutes if tolerated. The visit includes checks and preparation. Adjuvant: maximum 12 months. Advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) maintenance: until progression or unacceptable toxicity. ES-SCLC requires completed induction; this suite contains no induction chemotherapy.

Tests and checks

Blood count, kidney/liver tests and glucose before every 2-week treatment cycle; thyroid every 3–6 weeks; other hormones when clinically indicated. Symptoms need review during and after treatment.

Between treatment visits

The diagram shows one reference interval; appointments, treatment duration and monitoring are individual. Immune effects may occur at any time.

Immune effects can happen on any day and after treatment ends. Carry your immunotherapy alert card and tell any healthcare team that you receive atezolizumab.

Selected effects, not every possible effect or a prediction. Blood-count, skin and line markers are schematic. Number and text carry meaning; colour is decorative.
Contact the oncology team immediately for new or worsening breathlessness, chest pain, persistent diarrhoea, severe tummy pain, yellow skin/eyes, much less urine, severe headache, marked weakness, confusion, unusual thirst or passing much more urine, or a spreading/blistering rash. For severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or other immediately dangerous symptoms, seek emergency care. Do not wait for the next infusion.
