When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Tell staff immediately about an infusion reaction. After leaving, seek urgent help for swelling, wheeze, faintness or breathing difficulty. Report spreading rash, infected nails, eye pain, visual change, severe diarrhoea or inability to drink. Mouth and skin effects from radiation may continue after its final session.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How each part of treatment works

Cetuximab is combined with radiotherapy for selected locally advanced head and neck cancer when platinum chemotherapy is unsuitable. The team discusses the reason for choosing this treatment and the alternatives.

EGFR-targeted antibody

Cetuximab

Erbitux
Blocks a growth signal at the cell surface

Cetuximab binds to EGFR, a receptor involved in growth and repair. It helps radiotherapy work against selected head and neck cancers. Healthy skin also uses EGFR, which helps explain acne-like rash, dryness and nail changes.

EGFR receptor blocked↓Growth messages reduced↓Radiotherapy combination

What to watch for
Tell the nurse immediately about itching, flushing, swelling or breathing difficulty. Report rash, painful nails, eye pain or new breathlessness.

Sources: nccp · erbitux

Local treatment

Radiotherapy

Radiotherapy
Damages cancer cells in the planned treatment area

Radiotherapy directs radiation at a carefully planned area. It is delivered on its own appointment calendar. Effects depend on which tissues receive radiation and can build up during the course or continue after it finishes.

Treatment area planned↓Repeated local treatment↓Recovery and late-effect review

What to watch for
Report difficulty swallowing, weight loss, mouth pain, skin breakdown or problems managing saliva.

Day 1: Cetuximab visit

The first cetuximab infusion runs over 120 minutes; later infusions usually run over 60 minutes. Premedication and observation add to visit time. Your team confirms the observation period; no early discharge is promised after two doses. Report new rash, eye, bowel or breathing symptoms before treatment.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Confirm the exact regimen, treatment aim, baseline tests, transport, support medicines and actual urgent contact numbers. Bring any hearing concerns, swallowing problems and medicine list.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

The first cetuximab infusion runs over 120 minutes; later infusions usually run over 60 minutes. Premedication and observation add to visit time. Your team confirms the observation period; no early discharge is promised after two doses.

STEP 3

Two departments, one calendar

Radiation appointments are separate from medicine visits. Keep one calendar showing both departments and tests; tell the team promptly about a cancelled session.

STEP 4

Practical help

Tell the team about travel, cost, work, caring responsibilities, communication or reading needs. Ask whether a support person or interpreter can attend and how treatment changes will be explained.

STEP 5

Leaving the unit

Check the next visit and blood-test dates, support medicines and who to call if unwell. Have the team replace example phone numbers before using the wallet card.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

One written medicine plan

Ask for one schedule showing prevention medicines, rescue treatment and what to do if symptoms persist. Do not add, double or restart medicines from an old prescription.

Premedication, skin and minerals

Premedication helps reduce infusion reactions; the team also provides a skin-care plan. Keep mineral blood-test appointments, including monthly checks for two months after the final dose. Report delayed reactions even after earlier infusions were tolerated.

Food, fluids and weight

A dietitian can tailor food texture, supplements and calorie or protein needs. Tell the team early if eating or drinking becomes difficult; do not wait for substantial weight loss. Fluid targets depend on kidney and heart health.

Oral and dental care

Use the mouth-care plan provided by the team and tell them about ulcers, thrush, bleeding or pain. Inform your dentist about cancer treatment. Before any dental procedure, confirm timing with the oncology team.

Swallowing and communication

A speech and language therapist can assess swallowing and advise exercises or safer food and drink textures. Follow the individual assessment; a general guide cannot decide which textures are safe for you.

Fertility, pregnancy and medicines

Discuss fertility and pregnancy precautions before treatment and the medicine-specific duration of contraception afterwards. Ask about breastfeeding. Bring a full medicine, supplement and allergy list; check before adding over-the-counter or herbal products.

Sources: nccp · oral · nutrition · hse

Understanding your diagnosis

Keep your pathology and agreed treatment goal with your records.

Site, type and stage

Ask which part of the head or neck the cancer started in, what the biopsy shows and how far it has spread. Site, stage, previous treatment and your health affect the options. HPV/p16 testing is relevant to some sites; it is not the same test as PD-L1.

Locally advanced squamous head and neck cancer in people ineligible for platinum-based treatment. This guide should match the named treatment on your prescription.

What tumour tests mean

Your team explains any HPV/p16, Epstein–Barr virus or other tumour tests relevant to your cancer site. Not every head and neck cancer needs the same tests; the medicine safety checks are a separate matter.

Treatment choices and your priorities

The consent conversation explains the boundaries of the plan.

Benefit and alternatives

The team discusses the reason for choosing this treatment and the alternatives. Ask how alternatives compare for your health, goals and previous treatment. A pause is a treatment decision

The clinician may delay, change or stop treatment because of symptoms, test results or your priorities. Record which component is affected and what is needed before restarting. Do not restart from an old prescription.

Combining treatment approaches

Radiotherapy treats a planned local area while medicine supports the cancer treatment. The radiation prescription, number of sessions and late-effect plan are agreed separately.

Treatment reference · 05

Mouth, teeth and jaw care

Dental preparation and gentle daily care reduce avoidable problems.

Before treatment

Arrange dental assessment before head and neck radiation. The dental and oncology teams decide what treatment is needed and how much healing time is required; do not schedule extractions independently.

Sources: oral

During treatment

Follow the prescribed brushing, fluoride, rinse and pain-relief advice. Report ulcers, infection, new bleeding, difficulty wearing dentures or worsening jaw opening. Avoid starting a new mouthwash without checking if the mouth is sore.

Sources: oral

After head and neck radiation

Dry mouth can increase dental problems and some radiation effects last. Keep regular dental care and tell any future dentist that you have had head and neck radiation before invasive treatment. Your radiation/dental team plans any procedure.

Sources: oral

Treatment reference · 06

Eating, drinking and maintaining strength

The aim is adequate intake and comfort during treatment.

Recognising an intake problem

Record weight and report food or drink becoming harder to manage. Pain, taste change, dry mouth or nausea can each need a different response; a dietitian can adapt the plan to you.

Sources: nutrition · hse

If ordinary food is not enough

The team may suggest food fortification, supplements or tube feeding when intake or swallowing safety requires it. A tube is a support option, not a judgement about how well you have tried to eat. Training and follow-up are needed.

Sources: oral · nutrition

Fluid needs are individual

Ask for a realistic drinking plan that accounts for kidney/heart problems and any feeding tube. Persistent vomiting, dizziness, markedly reduced urine or inability to drink needs urgent contact.

Sources: nccp · hse

Treatment reference · 07

Swallowing, voice and communication

A speech and language therapist can help protect function.

Swallowing assessment

Report coughing or choking with food or drink, a wet-sounding voice after swallowing, food sticking or repeated chest infections. The team may need to assess aspiration risk and safe textures.

Sources: oral

Exercises and rehabilitation

Do swallowing or jaw exercises only as taught for your situation. Treatment and recovery can change what is comfortable and safe. Ask for reassessment when symptoms change.

Sources: oral

Communication needs

Tell the team about changes in speech, voice or hearing. Ask for written instructions, communication aids, an interpreter or help involving a support person.

Sources: head-neck

Daily life and emotional support

Energy and activity

Balance rest with manageable activity according to your ability and team advice. Ask for help if weakness, falls, sleep difficulty or pain interferes with daily life.

Treatment reference · 09

Recovery, assessment and longer-term care

Treatment completion and recovery are different milestones.

Continuing symptoms

Electrolytes, magnesium and calcium monthly for two months after the final cetuximab dose. Continue skin, dental, nutrition, swallowing and radiation follow-up. Scans and examinations follow the individual cancer plan; no universal scan date or tumour-marker test is required by this regimen.

Sources: nccp

Cancer assessment

Your team sets examinations and imaging around the disease, treatment and symptoms. Record who arranges each test and who explains the result. An unbooked scan is not automatically due at a fixed week because a general guide says so.

Information for other clinicians

Keep the regimen name, last treatment date, ongoing medicines and relevant alert card. Tell clinicians about earlier immune treatment or head and neck radiation even after the course has ended.

Understanding the evidence

The Bonner trial compared radiotherapy with or without cetuximab in locally advanced squamous head and neck cancer.

The study supports adding cetuximab to radiation in an appropriate setting. It did not compare cetuximab-radiotherapy with cisplatin-radiotherapy and does not establish that the treatments are equivalent.

Sources: nccp · bonner

Questions worth asking

What is this treatment trying to achieve for me?

Cetuximab is combined with radiotherapy for selected locally advanced head and neck cancer when platinum chemotherapy is unsuitable. The team discusses the reason for choosing this treatment and the alternatives.
