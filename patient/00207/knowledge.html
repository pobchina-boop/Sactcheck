Understand your treatment

How your medicines work

For selected bowel cancer that has spread, cetuximab aims to control the cancer after previous treatments. For selected locally advanced head-and-neck cancer when platinum chemotherapy is unsuitable, it is combined with radiotherapy. Your team confirms which pathway applies.

EGFR-targeted antibody

Cetuximab

Erbitux
Blocks growth messages at the cell surface

Cetuximab attaches to EGFR on the outside of cells and blocks growth messages. In bowel cancer, a validated RAS wild-type result helps identify whether this approach is suitable. In head-and-neck cancer, weekly cetuximab may be combined with radiotherapy under a separate treatment plan. Healthy skin also uses EGFR, so skin and nail effects need early care.

EGFR receives a growth signal↓Cetuximab blocks the receptor↓Some growth signals are reduced

What to watch for
Report rash or sore nails early. Tell the nurse immediately about infusion symptoms. Eye pain, new breathing problems or severe diarrhoea need prompt advice. Blood tests check minerals during treatment and for two months afterwards.

Read the whole cycle as text

Day 1: Treatment and checks

The team reviews blood tests, symptoms and your prescription before treatment. Premedicines and observation are part of cetuximab visits. Know the contact number and the next planned visit.

Day 2–3: Care between visits

Follow the skin-care or bowel-symptom plan provided. There is no pump to remove. Call early for symptoms; do not wait for the next appointment.

Day 4–7: Recovery and the next review

The team uses symptoms, tests and your priorities to decide what happens next. Bring questions and a symptom record if useful; this is optional.

Practical treatment guide

Infusion visits and care between treatments

Cetuximab on Day 1. No home chemotherapy pump. A repeating 7-day cycle. The number of cycles depends on benefit, side effects and your treatment plan. For head-and-neck radiotherapy: start cetuximab one week before radiation, then continue weekly during radiation until it ends.

STEP 1

Treatment phases and appointments

One larger first infusion is followed by weekly infusions. Bowel-cancer treatment continues while helping and tolerated. For head-and-neck treatment, cetuximab starts one week before radiotherapy and continues until radiation treatment finishes.

STEP 2

Checks after treatment

Monthly electrolytes, magnesium and calcium for two months after the last cetuximab dose; assign responsibility before treatment ends. Response assessment follows the consultant plan; head/neck radiation follow-up is coordinated separately.

STEP 3

Access and practical support

Day-unit IV access; no home fluorouracil pump. Head/neck pathway requires a separate radiation appointment and nutrition/swallowing support plan.

Do I need a port?

A peripheral cannula or existing central line may be used. A port is not automatically needed; the team considers your veins and overall treatment plan.

If the schedule changes

Ask the unit to confirm the next visit and which medicine is being changed. Do not copy dates from another regimen.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

First cetuximab dose

Skin care and sun protection under local EGFR policy. First two doses require post-infusion observation; delayed-reaction education. Monthly electrolytes/Mg/Ca for two months after last dose.

Subsequent weekly cetuximab

Premedication continues for subsequent infusions. Head/neck RT requires separate mucosal, skin, swallow and nutrition support. Post-treatment monthly electrolytes/Mg/Ca for two months.

Treatment reference · 01

Your treatment aim

The setting changes what benefit means.

Why this option?

Cetuximab alone is an option for RAS wild-type bowel cancer that has spread after oxaliplatin and irinotecan have failed, when irinotecan cannot be tolerated. A separate indication combines weekly cetuximab with radiotherapy for locally advanced head-and-neck cancer; the radiation team confirms suitability, including the current NCCP catalogue criteria. Your team explains the aim, alternatives and how benefit will be reviewed.

Sources: nccp

A plan that can change

Ask whether the aim is to reduce recurrence risk, control cancer, relieve symptoms or support a local treatment. Your preferences and priorities matter.

Sources: nccp

Separate head-and-neck evidence

A randomised trial compared radiotherapy plus weekly cetuximab with radiotherapy alone and found improved local control and survival. It did not compare cetuximab with cisplatin; it cannot establish that cetuximab is equivalent to cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy.

Sources: hntrial

Before the first treatment

Checks reduce avoidable risk.

Blood tests and medicines

Review blood counts, kidney/liver tests, symptoms, allergies and all medicines and supplements. Tell the team about red-meat allergy or tick bites before cetuximab because these can be associated with infusion-reaction risk.

In selected previously treated bowel cancer, cetuximab improved disease control and survival compared with supportive care. Separate head-and-neck evidence supports adding it to radiotherapy in the appropriate setting.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

How is my treatment chosen?

Cetuximab alone is an option for RAS wild-type bowel cancer that has spread after oxaliplatin and irinotecan have failed, when irinotecan cannot be tolerated. A separate indication combines weekly cetuximab with radiotherapy for locally advanced head-and-neck cancer; the radiation team confirms suitability, including the current NCCP catalogue criteria. Your team explains the aim, alternatives and how benefit will be reviewed.

Do I need to enter dates to understand the cycle?

No. The graph explains a general pattern. Your hospital arranges appointments; the guide and its safety information are available without entering anything.

Will I have a pump at home?

There is no home fluorouracil pump in this regimen. Your team chooses a cannula or central line based on your veins and wider treatment plan.

What happens if a dose is delayed?

The team checks the cause and may delay, reduce, change or stop one medicine. Ask which component changes and when you should next attend; do not restart yourself.

How will I know whether it is helping?

Scans, symptoms and clinical review assess benefit. A rash, side effect or good day alone cannot show whether the cancer is responding.

What about radiotherapy?

For the head-and-neck indication, cetuximab starts one week before radiation and continues weekly alongside it. The graphic below shows the relationship. Colorectal cetuximab alone does not include a routine radiation course.

What about fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding?

Discuss fertility preservation and reproductive plans before treatment. Ask for the contraception and breastfeeding instructions for every medicine; the whole regimen may require longer precautions than one medicine alone.

Why is RAS testing mentioned?

RAS testing selects cetuximab treatment in colorectal cancer. It is not the selection test for the separate head-and-neck radiotherapy indication. Confirm the cancer-specific plan with your team.

Which checks continue when treatment changes or finishes?

Monthly electrolytes, magnesium and calcium for two months after the last cetuximab dose; assign responsibility before treatment ends. Response assessment follows the consultant plan; head/neck radiation follow-up is coordinated separately.
