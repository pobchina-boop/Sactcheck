My overall treatment

56 days: lomustine once D1; procarbazine D8–21; vincristine IV D8 + D29.

  1. Six planned cycles after radiotherapyDoses, starts and stopping are confirmed by the team; a card stamp is a record, not approval.

A typical 56-day cycle

Day 1–7One lomustine dose on Day 1
Day 8–21Daily procarbazine and first vincristine
Day 22–29Procarbazine finished; Day 29 infusion
Day 30–56Recovery before the next cycle

Timing may change after review. Side effects can occur on any day; there are no guaranteed safe days.

Between treatments

  • Lomustine is taken once, not daily. Procarbazine ends on Day 21; review before the Day 29 infusion.
  • Delayed low counts can occur after capsule days. Report fever, bleeding, bowel or nerve symptoms early.

Keep the three medicine days separate. Have the anti-sickness, bowel, food/alcohol and interaction instructions ready.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Call 112 or 999 for a new seizure, sudden weakness or speech difficulty, loss of consciousness, severe breathing difficulty or uncontrolled bleeding. If you have an established seizure rescue plan, follow it and its emergency instructions. Contact the team promptly for a worsening headache, repeated vomiting, new cough or breathlessness, unusual bruising, yellow skin/eyes or symptoms that prevent drinking. Do not restart held chemotherapy without instructions.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
