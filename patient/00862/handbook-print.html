My treatment handbook

Niraparib (Zejula) tablets

Your treatment and how the medicines work

These tablets help delay the return or growth of ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer after it has responded to platinum chemotherapy.

Niraparib · Zejula

PARP inhibitor. Niraparib blocks proteins that help repair damaged DNA. This can slow the growth of some cancer cells. Report unusual bruising, bleeding, fever, headaches or racing heartbeat.

What the research found

PRIMA studied first-line maintenance after platinum response. The risk of progression or death was lower with niraparib than placebo in the homologous-recombination-deficient group (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.31–0.59). NOVA separately studied maintenance after platinum-sensitive relapse.

The first-line and relapse studies enrolled different populations. Delayed progression is a group result and does not establish a survival benefit for an individual.

Your tablet schedule

Daily treatment at home

Swallow the tablets whole with water at the same time each day, either with a light meal or at least one hour before or two hours after food. If you miss a dose or vomit after it, take the next scheduled dose. Do not take an extra tablet.

The NCCP lists blood pressure at week 2, monthly for 6 months and then every 3 months. The European product information calls for at least weekly readings for the first 2 months, monthly during the first year and periodically thereafter. Agree a written monitoring plan with your team before starting.

Common effects and what to do

Nausea and constipation

Ask for an anti-sickness plan before starting. Let the team know early if constipation is affecting eating or drinking.

Tiredness

Keep track of changes in what you can manage. A blood test can help identify anaemia.

Serious effects: when to call

Low blood counts

Unusual bleeding, infection or new breathlessness needs urgent assessment. Persistent abnormalities may need assessment for rare bone marrow disorders including MDS or AML.

High blood pressure

Report severe headache, confusion or changes in vision urgently. Call 112 or 999 for a seizure, collapse, chest pain or severe breathing difficulty.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Fever, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness or a severe headache? Call oncology now. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Practical support

At home

Follow your prescribed anti-sickness plan. Bedtime dosing may help nausea. Speak to the team before changing the dose or stopping.

Other medicines

Show your team all medicines, including blood thinners, aspirin and supplements. Do not stop prescribed medicines on your own.

Family planning

Niraparib can harm a developing baby. Discuss contraception, fertility and breastfeeding before starting.

Planning your visits

Prescription and supply

Check tablet strength and dose on the latest label. Arrange repeat supplies before running out. Ask what to do if the team pauses treatment.

Home monitoring

Bring your blood pressure readings, tablet list, symptoms and dates of any missed doses to each review.

Preparing for your oncology discussion

  • Can you show when your next blood count and blood pressure check are due, and explain what to do after a missed tablet? Review your personal plan with the team before leaving.
  • Who do I call outside clinic hours? Ask the team to complete both numbers on the wallet card and agree a backup contact.
