Tepotinib monotherapy

Tepotinib blocks the MET growth signal in selected lung cancers with a MET exon 14 skipping change. In advanced cancer the aim is to control the cancer and its symptoms. The tumour test and previous treatment help determine whether it is suitable.

My schedule at a glance

Your treatment pattern

Daily treatment with food

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

The usual starting dose is 450 mg once daily: two 225 mg tablets taken together with food. Treatment is continuous, without a planned rest week, while it is helping. Follow your prescribed dose; the team may use one 225 mg tablet or pause treatment for side effects.

Tests and checks

Confirm the MET result, previous treatment, dose, other medicines and emergency contact. Check blood count, electrolytes, kidney and liver function; electrocardiogram (ECG) if clinically indicated. Discuss pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding, and perform a pregnancy test when applicable. Repeat blood count, electrolytes, kidney and liver tests at the team’s agreed reviews. ECG is used when clinically indicated. Report new lung symptoms, swelling, diarrhoea or vomiting between visits. A rise in creatinine needs assessment because the medicine can change the test result without necessarily damaging the kidneys. Scans follow the Consultant’s plan.

Between treatment visits

Contact the oncology team urgently for new or worsening cough, breathlessness or fever; marked swelling or sudden weight gain; severe diarrhoea, repeated vomiting or inability to drink; yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or severe abdominal pain; or palpitations or fainting. Carry your treatment details

Advanced MET exon 14 skipping lung cancer National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) 00823a covers tepotinib on its own for adults with advanced non-small cell lung cancer carrying a confirmed MET exon 14 skipping change, after immunotherapy and/or platinum chemotherapy. It is not a first-line or adjuvant treatment guide.

Print, cut out and fold. Follow your treating hospital’s contact plan.
