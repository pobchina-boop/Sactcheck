Your practical treatment plan

Know the next step.

Taking treatment

The usual starting dose is 450 mg once daily: two 225 mg tablets taken together with food. Treatment is continuous, without a planned rest week, while it is helping. Follow your prescribed dose; the team may use one 225 mg tablet or pause treatment for side effects.

Take at the same time each day with food and swallow whole. If a dose is missed, take it when remembered on the same day unless the next dose is due within 8 hours; then skip it. Do not double a dose. Ask your pharmacist if the timing is uncertain or if you vomit; do not take a replacement without advice. If swallowing is difficult, ask about the approved water-dispersion method.

Checks and follow-up

Confirm the MET result, previous treatment, dose, other medicines and emergency contact. Check blood count, electrolytes, kidney and liver function; ECG if clinically indicated. Discuss pregnancy, contraception and breastfeeding, and perform a pregnancy test when applicable. Repeat blood count, electrolytes, kidney and liver tests at the team’s agreed reviews. ECG is used when clinically indicated. Report new lung symptoms, swelling, diarrhoea or vomiting between visits. A rise in creatinine needs assessment because the medicine can change the test result without necessarily damaging the kidneys. Scans follow the Consultant’s plan.

Support between reviews

Swelling and weight changes

Report swelling early and discuss changes in weight, movement or breathing. The team will assess the cause and agree treatment; do not start a water tablet or change tepotinib yourself.

Diarrhoea and fluids

Agree a diarrhoea treatment plan before starting. Begin the prescribed anti-diarrhoeal medicine at the first loose stool as instructed, and maintain fluids if able. Persistent diarrhoea, weakness or reduced urine needs prompt advice.

Nausea medicines

Tepotinib has minimal-to-low oral emetic risk. Agree an as-needed anti-sickness plan with the team, including medicine-interaction and heart-rhythm checks; no routine infusion premedication is specified.

Medicine review

Ask the pharmacist to check all prescribed medicines, non-prescription medicines and supplements before changes. Tepotinib can affect medicines including dabigatran, digoxin, rosuvastatin and metformin. Supportive medicines may also affect heart rhythm; individual advice is needed.

Pregnancy, fertility and breastfeeding

Avoid pregnancy during treatment. Effective contraception is needed during treatment and for at least one week after the last dose; women using hormonal contraception should also use a barrier method. Men with a partner who could become pregnant should use barrier contraception for the same period. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for at least one week afterwards. Human fertility effects are not known; discuss fertility before starting.
