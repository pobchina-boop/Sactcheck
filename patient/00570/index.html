Understand the treatment. Keep the plan manageable.

Lorlatinib aims to control ALK-positive advanced lung cancer, including disease in the brain where present. Your oncology team explains the likely benefit, treatment line and alternatives for your situation.

ALK tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Lorlatinib

Blocks abnormal ALK growth signals
ALK-positive cancer identified↓Abnormal ALK signalling blocked↓Cancer growth may be controlled, including in the brain

Lorlatinib blocks the abnormal ALK protein that helps some lung cancers grow. It can reach the brain, an important site of disease in ALK-positive lung cancer. It is selected using an ALK test. It can also affect healthy tissues, so blood tests and symptom checks remain important.

Report thinking, mood, speech, breathing or heart symptoms promptly and attend lipid, glucose and other monitoring.

Support throughout treatment

Taking and replacing tablets

If a dose is missed, take it when remembered if the next dose is at least four hours away; otherwise skip it. Do not take two doses together. If you vomit after a dose, do not take another tablet; take the next dose at the usual time. These instructions follow the current product leaflet.

Cholesterol and other medicines

Cholesterol or triglycerides may rise without symptoms. Attend blood tests and take any prescribed lipid-lowering medicine. Ask the pharmacist before starting or stopping any medicine, supplement or herbal product, including St John’s wort. Do not change the lorlatinib dose yourself.

Sickness and daily wellbeing

This oral regimen has minimal to low sickness risk. Use anti-sickness medicine only according to your own plan and report persistent vomiting or poor intake. A standard chemotherapy steroid or anti-sickness course is not automatically needed.

Thinking, mood and practical support

Ask someone you trust to help notice changes in thinking, mood, speech or behaviour, if you wish. Report changes early. Lorlatinib can affect driving and machine use, especially when CNS effects are present; discuss whether it is safe for you.

Pregnancy, fertility and feeding

Lorlatinib may harm an unborn baby and make hormonal contraception less effective. Women who could become pregnant need highly effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for at least 35 days afterwards; discuss any unavoidable hormonal method and barrier protection. Men with partners who could become pregnant need effective contraception including a condom during treatment and for at least 14 weeks afterwards; condoms are also needed with a pregnant partner. Discuss fertility preservation before treatment. Do not breastfeed during treatment; agree when it can safely resume with the team.

When to get help

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.
Contact your treating team promptly: Contact your oncology team promptly for changes in memory, thinking, mood, speech or behaviour, hallucinations, new or worsening breathlessness, cough, fainting, a slow or irregular heartbeat, severe abdominal pain, marked thirst or frequent urination. Severe confusion, unsafe behaviour, severe breathing difficulty or collapse needs emergency help. Do not wait for the next appointment.
