When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call oncology immediately for fever, shaking chills, suddenly feeling unwell, unusual bleeding, new breathlessness, very little urine, severe rash or vomiting that prevents drinking. Follow your hospital’s temperature and contact instructions. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse or seizures: call 112/999. Any new confusion or unusual sleepiness, blood in urine, low urine output or infusion-related faintness needs immediate assessment.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

The two medicines damage tumour genetic material through different mechanisms to help control sarcoma. The intended role, chance of benefit and how this fits with surgery, radiotherapy or trials depend on the diagnosis and previous treatment.

Chemotherapy

Ifosfamide

A liver-activated medicine damages genetic material

The liver converts ifosfamide into active compounds that crosslink deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA), the genetic material, preventing normal cell division. Other breakdown products can damage the bladder, kidneys or brain. Mesna reduces bladder injury; it does not prevent kidney injury or brain toxicity.

Liver activates the medicine↓DNA crosslinks damage tumour cells↓Mesna protects the bladder only

What to watch for
New confusion, unusual sleepiness, hallucinations, unsteadiness, seizures, blood in urine or reduced urine needs immediate assessment.

Sources: nccp · ifosfamide-pi

Chemotherapy

Etoposide

Prevents repair of breaks in genetic material

Etoposide interferes with topoisomerase II, an enzyme that controls breaks in DNA, the genetic material. Damaged tumour cells cannot divide normally. The bone marrow and other healthy tissues are also affected.

DNA repair is interrupted↓Dividing cells are damaged↓Slow infusion and blood-count checks

What to watch for
Tell the nurse immediately about dizziness, flushing, rash, chest tightness, breathlessness or feeling faint during infusion.

Sources: nccp · etoposide-pi

Individual prescribed course

Etoposide 100 mg/m² is infused over 60 minutes, followed by ifosfamide 1,800 mg/m² over 60 minutes, on each of days 1–5. Courses repeat every 21 days for up to seven cycles while benefit and tolerability justify treatment. Mesna is given before each ifosfamide infusion and at 4 and 8 hours measured from its START.

National guidance lists this treatment for Ewing sarcoma that has returned, and also for osteosarcoma. Your team will confirm whether it is suitable for your diagnosis and previous treatment. The regimen guidance gives no numerical age limit. Etoposide use for these diagnoses is unlicensed in Ireland; your team must explain this, document your consent and follow the hospital’s medicines policies.

Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenBefore starting and before each 21-day cycle; extra testing follows fever, bleeding or abnormal results. Ifosfamide product information additionally specifies counts before each administration and appropriate intervals afterwards; the team reconciles the daily course monitoring.

Assess infection risk, anaemia and bleeding risk.

Kidney and liver tests

WhenBefore starting and before each cycle. Kidney filtration and tubular function also need assessment during and after ifosfamide, with timing intensified for abnormal results or symptoms.

Assess drug handling and detect organ injury.

Neurological assessment

WhenBefore EVERY ifosfamide dose and urgently for new confusion, sleepiness, hallucinations, unsteadiness or seizures.

Detect early ifosfamide brain toxicity.

Urine checks for blood

WhenBefore each ifosfamide dose and every 8 hours on treatment days; report blood or urinary pain immediately.

Detect bladder injury despite mesna.

Fluid balance and weight

WhenMonitor fluid balance during treatment and record weight daily. Prescribed hydration begins before ifosfamide and continues for 24 hours after the final ifosfamide infusion ends.

Detect low urine output, fluid retention and dehydration.

Tumour, symptom and function review

If part of your plan
WhenBefore continuation at the specialist-set review points; scan timing depends on the individual Ewing sarcoma or osteosarcoma treatment plan.

Assess benefit separately from treatment safety.

Sodium, potassium and phosphate

WhenAt baseline and as regular tests with treatment-cycle reviews; repeat during treatment or recovery when results, fluid balance or symptoms require.

Detect electrolyte losses including ifosfamide tubular injury.

Blood pressure and etoposide reaction monitoring

WhenMonitor during infusion, especially the first 15 minutes. Tell staff immediately about dizziness, flushing, breathlessness or feeling faint.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the visit

Confirm mesna, hydration, white-cell growth-factor support and the complete five-day anti-sickness plan. Review interactions including aprepitant with ifosfamide. The source identifies this etoposide indication as unlicensed in Ireland: the patient must be informed and consented under hospital policy, with acknowledgement by the local Drugs and Therapeutics Committee or equivalent.

STEP 2

Time in the unit

The daily sequence includes etoposide, protective mesna, then ifosfamide, with further mesna at the prescribed times. Hydration starts before the first ifosfamide dose and continues for 24 hours after the last dose ends. Monitoring and supportive treatment make attendance longer than the drug infusion times.

STEP 3

Intravenous access and care

The team chooses appropriate intravenous access and explains its care. Report pain, burning, redness or swelling during treatment immediately. If a port or peripherally inserted central catheter is used, confirm dressing, flushing, infection precautions and eventual removal arrangements. Never change pump settings or disconnect an infusion yourself.

STEP 4

Home support

This combination has high overall nausea/vomiting risk and requires white-cell growth-factor support for every cycle, timed by local prescription. Mesna reduces bladder injury but does not protect the kidneys or brain. Pharmacy must review anti-sickness interactions, particularly aprepitant, and specify all delayed and rescue treatment.

STEP 5

Next appointment

Confirm the next treatment, tests and any scan before leaving.

Treatment reference · 05

Your treatment decision

Read the general treatment discussion alongside the individual medicine risks.

General chemotherapy risks

Discuss low blood counts with serious infection or sepsis, anaemia and bleeding; nausea or vomiting; mouth soreness, appetite or taste changes, diarrhoea or constipation; fatigue, hair, nail and skin changes; changes in sexual wellbeing; allergic reactions and intravenous-line complications; blood clots, including clots in the legs or lungs; fertility, pregnancy and breastfeeding effects; and the possibility of lasting organ injury, rare later cancers or death from serious treatment complications. The likelihood and severity differ between medicines and people. Read this general discussion together with every medicine-specific risk.

Sources: nccp

Historical phase II ifosfamide/etoposide study in recurrent tumours of children and young adults

A historical single-arm study showed tumour shrinkage in some previously treated patients, including small groups with Ewing sarcoma and osteosarcoma. The lack of a comparison group and very small disease-specific groups limit the estimate of benefit for today’s individual patient.

Of 124 enrolled patients, 77 were evaluable for response. Mixed tumour types, small subgroups and short follow-up limit interpretation. The trial’s longer course must not replace the current national maximum of seven cycles. Response does not establish improved survival.

Sources: trial · nccp

When do I attend?

Etoposide 100 mg/m² is infused over 60 minutes, followed by ifosfamide 1,800 mg/m² over 60 minutes, on each of days 1–5. Courses repeat every 21 days for up to seven cycles while benefit and tolerability justify treatment. Mesna is given before each ifosfamide infusion and at 4 and 8 hours measured from its START.

Does a delay mean treatment has failed?

No. Blood counts, organ function and symptoms can make a delay or dose change safer. Your team decides whether and when to give the next dose.

Who do I contact if unwell?

Use your verified oncology daytime or out-of-hours number. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Severe symptoms require 112/999.

Can treatment restart when a blood test improves?

Only when the treating team confirms a new plan. A previous serious reaction, stopped medicine or required protective treatment still matters.
