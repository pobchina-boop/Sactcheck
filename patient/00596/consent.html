1. Treatment and purpose

Ifosfamide and etoposide for Ewing sarcoma or osteosarcoma · NCCP 00596 v2

Etoposide 100 mg/m² is infused over 60 minutes, followed by ifosfamide 1,800 mg/m² over 60 minutes, on each of days 1–5. Courses repeat every 21 days for up to seven cycles while benefit and tolerability justify treatment. Mesna is given before each ifosfamide infusion and at 4 and 8 hours measured from its START.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Ifosfamide · Chemotherapy

New confusion, unusual sleepiness, hallucinations, unsteadiness, seizures, blood in urine or reduced urine needs immediate assessment.

Etoposide · Chemotherapy

Tell the nurse immediately about dizziness, flushing, rash, chest tightness, breathlessness or feeling faint during infusion.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss the individual aim, realistic benefit and uncertainty, treatment burden, alternatives, serious and lasting risks, fertility, personal priorities and the option to decline or reconsider. National guidance lists this treatment for Ewing sarcoma that has returned, and also for osteosarcoma. Your team will confirm whether it is suitable for your diagnosis and previous treatment. The regimen guidance gives no numerical age limit. Etoposide use for these diagnoses is unlicensed in Ireland; your team must explain this, document your consent and follow the hospital’s medicines policies. The national source states that this use of etoposide is an unlicensed indication in Ireland. Your clinician must explain the reason, evidence, uncertainties and alternatives, obtain consent under hospital policy, and confirm acknowledgement by the local Drugs and Therapeutics Committee or equivalent.
