Treatment context
This combination aims to control hepatocellular carcinoma, a cancer that starts in the liver, when it is advanced or cannot be removed and no previous systemic treatment has been given. Atezolizumab helps immune cells recognise cancer; bevacizumab blocks a signal that supports blood-vessel growth.
Schedule
Atezolizumab is given first on Day 1 as an IV infusion or a subcutaneous injection into the thigh, followed by bevacizumab through a vein. Treatment is planned every 21 days while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable.

General treatment discussion

Possible short-term, long-term, unexpected and unlisted effects; benefits are not guaranteed.
Severe complications can require hospital admission and can rarely be fatal.
Infection / sepsis, allergic or infusion reactions and leakage outside a vein (extravasation) should be discussed where applicable to the medicines and route.
Pregnancy, contraception, breastfeeding, sexual health and fertility preservation should be discussed where relevant.
Treatment can be delayed, changed or stopped if risks outweigh benefit. Consent is voluntary and can be withdrawn.

2. Regimen / agent-specific material risks

Atezolizumab

PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitor
Immune inflammation of lungs, bowel, liver, kidneys or skin
Hormone changes, including thyroid, adrenal or pituitary problems and diabetes; replacement may be long term
Rare serious heart, muscle, nerve or brain inflammation
Infusion/injection reactions; delayed immune effects after stopping

Bevacizumab

Anti-VEGF antibody
Raised blood pressure; rarely hypertensive crisis or PRES
Protein in urine and kidney injury, including nephrotic syndrome
Bleeding, including serious bleeding from varices
Arterial or venous blood clots
Poor wound healing, bowel perforation or fistula; rare jaw problems
Infusion or allergic reactions
