Atezolizumab + bevacizumab for HCC

This combination aims to control hepatocellular carcinoma, a cancer that starts in the liver, when it is advanced or cannot be removed and no previous systemic treatment has been given. Atezolizumab helps immune cells recognise cancer; bevacizumab blocks a signal that supports blood-vessel growth.

How your medicines work

PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitor

Atezolizumab

Helps immune cells recognise and attack cancer
PD-L1 blockade→Immune response increases→Cancer and healthy tissues may be affected

Blocking PD-L1 can release a brake on the immune response. This may control cancer but can also cause inflammation in healthy organs during treatment or after it stops.

Carry the immunotherapy alert card. Report new bowel, breathing, skin, hormone, heart, liver, kidney or neurological symptoms promptly.

Anti-VEGF antibody

Bevacizumab

Blocks a signal for blood-vessel growth
VEGF signal blocked→Tumour vessel growth reduced→Blood vessels and healing need monitoring

Bevacizumab binds VEGF, reducing signals that help tumours develop blood vessels. It can also affect normal blood vessels, blood pressure, kidney filtration and wound healing.

Report bleeding, severe headache, chest or abdominal pain, leg swelling, reduced urine or poor wound healing. Tell the team before surgery or dental procedures.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before travelling

Confirm blood/urine test arrangements, blood-pressure checks, transport and the 24-hour number. Tell the team if you are unwell before attending.

STEP 2

Time at the unit

IV atezolizumab is usually infused over 60 minutes initially and may take 30 minutes later if tolerated. The alternative 1875 mg thigh injection takes about seven minutes. Bevacizumab is still an IV infusion: usually 90 minutes first, then 60 and later 30 minutes if tolerated. Checks, preparation and observation make the whole visit longer.

STEP 3

At home

Keep a medicines list, symptom notes and the immune alert card. Follow your own hospital contact plan; the pocket card complements the medicine alert card.

STEP 4

Procedures and collections

Tell the team about planned surgery or dental procedures and ask for clear timed urine-collection instructions if needed.

Atezolizumab

  • Immune inflammation of lungs, bowel, liver, kidneys or skin
  • Hormone changes, including thyroid, adrenal or pituitary problems and diabetes; replacement may be long term
  • Rare serious heart, muscle, nerve or brain inflammation
  • Infusion/injection reactions; delayed immune effects after stopping

Bevacizumab

  • Raised blood pressure; rarely hypertensive crisis or PRES
  • Protein in urine and kidney injury, including nephrotic syndrome
  • Bleeding, including serious bleeding from varices
  • Arterial or venous blood clots
  • Poor wound healing, bowel perforation or fistula; rare jaw problems
  • Infusion or allergic reactions

Support around treatment

Varices and bleeding

Screening and any required variceal treatment must be reviewed before starting. Call urgently for black stools or blood; vomiting blood or heavy bleeding needs emergency help.

Blood pressure and urine

Blood pressure and urine-protein checks are part of each cycle. Continue prescribed blood-pressure medicines and ask before changing them. A positive urine dipstick may need a timed urine collection.

Immune alert card

Carry the supplied immunotherapy alert card during treatment and afterwards. New symptoms can need urgent treatment; do not wait for routine blood tests.

Support medicines

Both medicines have minimal sickness risk and routine preventive anti-sickness medicines or premedication are not usually required. The team individualises symptom treatment; do not self-treat new significant diarrhoea without advice.

Surgery, dental work and healing

Tell oncology before planned procedures and tell your dentist about bevacizumab. Wounds must heal and the team must agree an interruption/restart plan.

Pregnancy prevention and breastfeeding

Discuss fertility and pregnancy prevention. Atezolizumab requires effective contraception for five months after the last dose and bevacizumab for six months; use the longer interval when both apply. Do not breastfeed during treatment or for at least six months after bevacizumab.

Do not wait for the next appointment
