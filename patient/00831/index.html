Atezolizumab + bevacizumab for HCC

This combination aims to control hepatocellular carcinoma, a cancer that starts in the liver, when it is advanced or cannot be removed and no previous systemic treatment has been given. Atezolizumab helps immune cells recognise cancer; bevacizumab blocks a signal that supports blood-vessel growth.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

Atezolizumab is given first on Day 1 as an intravenous (IV) infusion or a subcutaneous injection into the thigh, followed by bevacizumab through a vein. Treatment is planned every 21 days while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable.

Tests and checks

full blood count (FBC), renal/liver profile, glucose, thyroid function tests (TFTs), oesophageal-varices screening, dipstick protein and blood pressure (BP)/cardiac assessment. Consider echocardiogram (heart ultrasound) (ECHO) after chest-wall radiation or anthracycline; international normalised ratio (blood-clotting test) (INR) if indicated. FBC, renal/liver profile, glucose and dipstick protein before every cycle. Blood pressure before each cycle and after treatment; review clinical toxicity and component-specific decisions. TFTs every six weeks; other tests and imaging according to clinical need and Consultant plan.

Between treatment visits

This is a reference schedule. It does not identify safe days or predict when side effects will occur.

Use your own oncology unit’s 24-hour contact plan. Urgent symptoms override the diary; do not wait for the next appointment.

Selected effects, not every possible effect or a prediction. Blood-count, skin and line markers are schematic. Number and text carry meaning; colour is decorative.
Call your oncology team immediately for new breathlessness, persistent diarrhoea, blood or black stools, yellow eyes, dark urine, severe tiredness or dizziness, thirst and frequent urination, unusual weakness, reduced urine or a spreading rash. Tell every healthcare professional that you receive immunotherapy, even after it stops. Vomiting blood, heavy bleeding, sudden severe abdominal or chest pain, stroke symptoms, seizure, collapse or major breathing difficulty need 112 / 999.
