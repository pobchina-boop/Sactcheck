1. Treatment and purpose

Atezolizumab + bevacizumab for HCC · NCCP 00831 v2a

Atezolizumab is given first on Day 1 as an IV infusion or a subcutaneous injection into the thigh, followed by bevacizumab through a vein. Treatment is planned every 21 days while it is helping and side effects remain acceptable.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Atezolizumab · PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitor

Carry the immunotherapy alert card. Report new bowel, breathing, skin, hormone, heart, liver, kidney or neurological symptoms promptly.

Bevacizumab · Anti-VEGF antibody

Report bleeding, severe headache, chest or abdominal pain, leg swelling, reduced urine or poor wound healing. Tell the team before surgery or dental procedures.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss expected benefit and uncertainty, alternatives including not proceeding, liver disease and variceal bleeding, immune toxicity that may be delayed or need lifelong hormone replacement, vascular/kidney/bowel/healing risks, fertility and pregnancy prevention, IV versus thigh administration and your right to reconsider.
