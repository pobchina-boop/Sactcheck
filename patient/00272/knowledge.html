When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report breathlessness, swelling, rapid weight gain, palpitations or persistent diarrhoea; tell staff immediately about a reaction during treatment.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

The medicine in this card

Trastuzumab under the skin helps control HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer. It can be used alone or with specified cancer medicines when the full NCCP indication is met.

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

Targets HER2 growth signals

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

HER2 on cell surface↓Antibody binds↓Growth signalling reduced

What to watch for
Keep echo appointments. Report breathing changes, swelling, allergic symptoms or a painful / spreading injection-site reaction.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Trastuzumab 600 mg under the skin every 21 days, without a loading dose, while beneficial and tolerated. Any taxane or endocrine partner follows a separate prescription and monitoring plan.

STEP 1

Before your visit

Confirm the dose, symptoms, required blood results and echo due window.

STEP 2

Access and administration

SC trastuzumab needs no IV line for this medicine. Another medicine may still need a cannula or port.

STEP 3

Treatment and observation

Injection over 2–5 minutes into alternate thighs. Observation is 30 minutes after the first injection and 15 minutes after subsequent injections, subject to approved local policy.

STEP 4

After the visit

Contact the unit to rearrange a missed dose. SC doses remain at least 3 weeks apart; no loading dose. This medicine does not routinely require a take-home pump.

Injection site

Alternate thighs and avoid tender, bruised, red or hardened skin. Tell the nurse about a persistent or worsening local reaction.

Visit length

Injection over 2–5 minutes into alternate thighs. Observation is 30 minutes after the first injection and 15 minutes after subsequent injections, subject to approved local policy.

Missed or delayed visit

Contact the unit to rearrange a missed dose. SC doses remain at least 3 weeks apart; no loading dose.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Support for this medicine

Trastuzumab alone has minimal emetogenic risk. Routine chemotherapy antiemetics, steroids and growth factors are not added automatically. Previous reactions require a separate plan.

Blood and heart appointments

FBC, renal and liver profile every 6 weeks (around C3, C5, C7 if on time). Echo / MUGA every 12 weeks; around C5 for uninterrupted 21-day cycles.

Local treatment and endocrine therapy

Surgery and radiotherapy are coordinated by the MDT. When endocrine therapy is indicated, it has its own schedule and bone-health plan.

Pregnancy / contraception

Discuss pregnancy, fertility and contraception before treatment. Report pregnancy promptly; do not breastfeed during treatment or for 7 months afterwards. The team confirms the medicine-specific contraception duration.

Cardiac checks

Echo / MUGA every 12 weeks; around C5 for uninterrupted 21-day cycles. Baseline cardiac assessment includes ECG in EMA trastuzumab information. Further ECGs follow symptoms / cardiac plan; an ECG does not replace an echo.

After stopping

EMA trastuzumab information: cardiac assessment every 6 months after stopping, until 24 months; after anthracyclines in early disease, yearly to 5 years or longer if function falls.

The wider pathway

Coordinate cancer medicines with local treatment and follow-up.

Scans and markers

Response imaging commonly every 2–4 months, adapted to sites, disease course and treatment. A new symptom can need earlier imaging. Tumour markers may supplement assessment, never determine a treatment change alone.

Sources: early · metastatic · markers

Radiotherapy relationship

Radiotherapy is used for a defined site or symptom when indicated. The oncology and radiation teams agree whether this medicine continues or pauses; do not infer a concurrent schedule from this card.

Sources: early · metastatic

Bone health

If an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression is planned, assess fracture risk and DEXA need. Repeat timing depends on results and bone treatment; this is not an automatic HER2-therapy scan.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

How long will I receive treatment?

Trastuzumab 600 mg under the skin every 21 days, without a loading dose, while beneficial and tolerated. Any taxane or endocrine partner follows a separate prescription and monitoring plan.

When is my next echo?

Echo / MUGA every 12 weeks; around C5 for uninterrupted 21-day cycles. Existing HER2 exposure and delays affect cycle numbering; the team should give the actual due window.

How does radiotherapy fit?

Radiotherapy is used for a defined site or symptom when indicated. The oncology and radiation teams agree whether this medicine continues or pauses; do not infer a concurrent schedule from this card.

Does a tumour marker replace a scan?

No. In metastatic disease it may add information to symptoms and imaging. It should not alone determine treatment changes. In symptom-free early disease it is not routine recurrence screening.

Try explaining the plan back: What is the purpose of your treatment? Which heart or blood test is due next, and which team arranges and reviews it?
