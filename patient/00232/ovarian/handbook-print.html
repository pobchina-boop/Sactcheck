IV vinorelbine for platinum-refractory ovarian cancer

Your treatment and how the medicines work

Vinorelbine is being offered to slow the growth of platinum-refractory advanced ovarian cancer. Your team will review symptoms and scan results with you to decide whether to continue.

Vinorelbine · IV infusion

Vinca alkaloid chemotherapy. Vinorelbine disrupts the tiny structures a cell needs to divide. This can stop cancer cells multiplying. Healthy blood-forming cells, gut cells and nerves can also be affected. Use the treating unit’s 24-hour emergency number and local fever threshold. Contact immediately for fever/chills, sudden illness, breathlessness/chest pain, uncontrolled vomiting, severe abdominal pain/obstruction symptoms, bleeding or severe allergy. Do not wait until the next visit.

What the research found

Investigators observed tumour responses with single-agent vinorelbine on days 1 and 8.

The study included a broader platinum-resistant population and had no control group. It cannot estimate benefit specifically for an individual with platinum-refractory disease.

Common effects and what to do

Bowel

Constipation can be severe. Use the bowel plan agreed with your team and report abdominal pain or swelling.

Nerves and energy

Tingling, weakness or fatigue can affect daily tasks. Tell the team before your next dose.

Serious effects: when to call

Blood counts and infection

Low white cells can lead to serious infection. Call immediately for fever or chills.

Bowel obstruction

Severe constipation with cramping, vomiting or swelling needs urgent review.

Leakage from the vein

Vinorelbine can injure surrounding tissue. Tell the nurse immediately about burning, pain or swelling.

Contact your oncology service urgently. Call immediately for fever, chills or feeling suddenly unwell. During the infusion, report any pain or leakage around the IV line straight away. If you have severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure, call 112 or 999. Follow the 24-hour contact instructions from your treating hospital.

Planning your visits

Appointments

IV vinorelbine on days 1 and 8 of a 21-day cycle. Week three is a break. Your team reviews benefit and tolerability before repeating the cycle.

At the day unit

The nurse gives vinorelbine into a vein over 15 minutes and then flushes the line with saline. Tell them immediately if the site stings, burns or swells.

What to bring
