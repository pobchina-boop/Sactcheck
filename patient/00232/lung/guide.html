Vinorelbine IV · non-small-cell lung cancer

Vinorelbine chemotherapy can help control non-small-cell lung cancer and its symptoms. The team weighs possible benefit against side effects, previous treatments and your priorities, and reviews whether to continue.

Non-small-cell lung cancer · IV monotherapy · NCCP 00232 v5 · Document revised 4 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

My overall treatment

30 mg/m² on Days 1 and 8 every 21 days. Consultant may consider 25 mg/m² for poor marrow reserve; the dose is individualised after toxicity. Repeated 21-day cycles until progression/unacceptable toxicity; team review determines continuation.

  1. TreatmentDays 1 and 8; repeat every 21 days. Duration agreed with your team.
  2. ReviewYour team reviews benefit and side effects, then agrees whether to continue, pause or change treatment.

Timing may change after review. Side effects can occur on any day; there are no guaranteed safe days.

Between treatments

  • Vinorelbine is planned on Days 1 and 8; there is no routine dose in the third week.
  • Constipation and numbness may build up. Follow your bowel plan and report changes before dosing.

Bring your medicine list and report new symptoms before dosing. Confirm the prescription, test appointments and daytime / out-of-hours contact plan.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

A picture of side effects

Match each symptom box to its numbered body location. Tell your team about new symptoms.

1Blood counts and infection
Vinorelbine

Low blood counts can cause infection, tiredness, bruising or bleeding. Fever, chills or sudden illness: call oncology immediately.

3Tingling, numbness or weakness
Vinorelbine

Nerve effects may change sensation, grip, walking or bowel movement. Report changes early because the next dose may need to wait or be reduced.

5Uncommon serious symptoms
Vinorelbine

New chest pain, sudden or worsening breathlessness, or severe allergic symptoms need urgent medical help. Previous heart disease and interacting medicines need review before treatment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
