1. Treatment and purpose

Pembrolizumab 400 mg + carboplatin AUC 5 / paclitaxel → dose-dense AC · NCCP 00860 v2

Four 21-day cycles: paclitaxel on Days 1, 8 and 15; carboplatin AUC 5 on Day 1. Pembrolizumab 400 mg on Day 1 of cycles 1 and 3 only (every six weeks). Four further cycles (cycles 5–8): doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide on Day 1 every 14 days. Pembrolizumab 400 mg on Day 1 of cycles 5 and 8 only; the six-week clock continues across the change in chemotherapy. G-CSF support is required for every dose-dense AC cycle; the team prescribes its product and timing separately. The operation is planned after recovery; the pathology result helps decide the next treatment. After surgery: pembrolizumab 400 mg every 42 days for five doses, beginning six weeks after cycle 8 Day 1, subject to recovery, surgery and the oncology plan. Progression preventing definitive surgery or unacceptable pembrolizumab toxicity changes this plan; postoperative treatment is not automatic.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Pembrolizumab · Immunotherapy

The immune system can also inflame healthy organs. New cough, diarrhoea, unusual weakness, severe headache, thirst or a spreading rash need prompt advice. Effects can start during treatment or after it finishes.

Carboplatin · Platinum chemotherapy

Keep blood-test appointments. Fever, unusual bleeding, repeated vomiting or infusion symptoms need prompt advice.

Paclitaxel · Taxane chemotherapy

Report tingling or numbness before the next weekly dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Doxorubicin · Anthracycline chemotherapy

Report pain or burning at the infusion site immediately. New breathlessness or chest symptoms need prompt review. Red urine for a short time can be a drug-colour effect; pain, clots or persistent bleeding still need advice.

Cyclophosphamide · Alkylating chemotherapy

Use the prescribed anti-sickness and hydration plan. Report fever, painful urination or blood in the urine.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss treatment aim, alternatives, expected benefit, common and serious risks, fertility, pregnancy precautions and the full sequence. Immune effects can be delayed or permanent; explain the postoperative option and when it is not appropriate. The treating team completes the individual consent discussion; this guide does not determine personal benefit.
