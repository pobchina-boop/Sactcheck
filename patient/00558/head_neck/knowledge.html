When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Contact oncology immediately for new breathlessness, chest pain, diarrhoea, severe tummy pain, yellow skin or eyes, much less urine, severe headache, vision changes, marked weakness, confusion, unusual thirst or a spreading or blistering rash. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse or seizure is an emergency.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

Your treatment medicines and modalities

Pembrolizumab aims to control head and neck cancer that has returned or spread, relieve symptoms and help some people live longer. Benefit and side effects are reviewed for you.

Immunotherapy

Pembrolizumab

Keytruda
Blocks an immune brake

Pembrolizumab is an antibody that blocks PD-1, a control switch on immune T cells. Cancer cells can use this switch to reduce immune attack. Blocking it can help the immune system recognise and attack cancer. The same increased immune activity can inflame healthy organs, which is why new symptoms need attention even after treatment ends.

Immune cell↓PD-1 blocked↓Cancer attacked

What to watch for
Report new breathing, bowel, skin, hormone, heart or nerve symptoms promptly; these may be immune inflammation.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Immune alert card and treatment holds

Carry your immune alert card during and after pembrolizumab. Tell any clinician that you have received immunotherapy. Do not restart a held treatment or change prescribed steroids or hormone replacement yourself.

Eating and weight

Tell the team early if swallowing, appetite, pain or taste changes reduce intake. A dietitian can agree practical food, drink or feeding support with you. Do not wait for substantial weight loss.

Mouth and dental care

Agree a mouth-care plan and tell your dentist about previous or current cancer treatment. Arrange oncology advice before dental procedures, particularly after head and neck radiation.

Swallowing and communication

Ask for speech and language therapy if swallowing or speech is difficult. Use the food textures, exercises and communication support advised for you; a general guide cannot establish safe swallowing.

Medicines and fertility

Bring prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines and supplements for review. Discuss fertility preservation and breastfeeding before treatment. Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least four months after the final dose. Tell the team promptly about a possible pregnancy. Do not breastfeed without specific advice from the team.

Tests and review

Blood safety tests, tumour tests and scans answer different questions.

Before the first dose

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests, glucose and thyroid tests; review autoimmune disease, transplants, previous immune side effects and all medicines. Confirm the tumour PD-L1 combined positive score; this is a tumour test, not a routine blood test.

During the course

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests and glucose before every 6-week cycle. Thyroid tests every 3–6 weeks; cortisol when clinically indicated.

Response assessment

Your team agrees examinations and scans to assess cancer response. No universal scan interval or blood tumour-marker panel is assumed here.

Evidence and further information

Use evidence to support a personal treatment discussion.

What studies can tell us

KEYNOTE-048 supports pembrolizumab alone in the PD-L1-positive first-line recurrent or metastatic population. It compared treatment groups, not an individual prognosis. The trial used 200 mg every three weeks; the six-week schedule is not a separate randomised head and neck survival comparison.

Sources: nccp · trial

Your individual decision

Group results cannot predict what will happen for you. Ask which benefits matter for your situation and how the team will decide whether treatment remains worthwhile.
