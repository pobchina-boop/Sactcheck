When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new breathlessness, chest symptoms, severe diarrhoea, inability to drink, jaundice, a painful swollen leg or a severe rash promptly. You do not need to identify which medicine caused the symptom.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

How the medicines work

For eligible high-risk node-positive early breast cancer, adding abemaciclib to endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence after surgery.

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Abemaciclib

Verzenios
Slows the cell-division checkpoint

Abemaciclib inhibits CDK4 and CDK6 proteins. These proteins help cells move towards division. It is combined with endocrine treatment to reduce recurrence risk in eligible high-risk early breast cancer.

CDK4/6 checkpoint↓Checkpoint inhibited↓Cell cycling slows

What to watch for
Start the prescribed diarrhoea rescue plan at the first loose stool and contact the team. Breathlessness, a swollen painful leg, fever or jaundice need prompt assessment.

Sources: nccp · abema

Hormone-directed treatment

Your endocrine treatment

Reduces the hormone signal

The chosen endocrine medicine either reduces oestrogen production or blocks its action. An aromatase inhibitor may need ovarian suppression before menopause. The exact drug and duration are prescribed separately.

Hormone signal↓Signal reduced or blocked↓HR-positive growth reduced

What to watch for
Tell the team about joint pain, hot flushes, vaginal symptoms and mood changes. The bone-health and clot-risk discussion depends on the drug chosen.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Abemaciclib is taken twice daily continuously, with endocrine treatment, for up to two years if appropriate. A 28-day block is shown for orientation; there is no planned tablet-free week. Endocrine treatment usually continues longer.

STEP 1

Before starting

Confirm the exact tablet strength, endocrine partner, blood-test and ECG plan where relevant.

STEP 2

At home

Take tablets whole at the prescribed times. Abemaciclib is continuous twice daily; ribociclib has a planned 7-day break. Follow the schedule for this card only.

STEP 3

If a dose is missed or vomited

Do not double up or take a replacement dose. Follow the medicine instructions and ask the oncology pharmacist if uncertain.

STEP 4

Before the next supply

Confirm symptoms and required test results have been reviewed. Ask whether the prescription changes; do not restart a held medicine yourself.

Interactions and storage

Check all new medicines and supplements with oncology/pharmacy, avoid grapefruit, store as labelled and keep away from children. Return unwanted tablets to the pharmacy.

Endocrine and ovarian-suppression visits

An aromatase inhibitor is generally a daily tablet. Fulvestrant, when selected for metastatic treatment, has loading injections on Days 1, 15 and 29 then monthly. Ovarian-suppression injections have their own prescription and visit schedule.

No infusion pump

The CDK4/6 medicine is taken orally. An implanted port or infusion pump is not required for these tablets; other treatment may have separate access needs.

Understanding the evidence

monarchE: seven-year invasive disease-free survival

The treatment setting, trial population and endpoint matter when interpreting benefit.

Show trial results and what the numbers mean

A phase III trial of high-risk, node-positive HR-positive/HER2-negative early breast cancer compared two years of abemaciclib plus endocrine therapy with endocrine therapy alone.

Abemaciclib + endocrine therapyEndocrine therapy alone

Seven-year invasive disease-free survival was 77.4% versus 70.9% (6.5 percentage points difference). This composite endpoint is not the same as overall survival and does not predict an individual outcome. The 2025 primary overall-survival report also found an overall-survival benefit. Treatment eligibility still requires the NCCP high-risk and timing criteria.

Questions worth asking

How does this fit around surgery and radiotherapy?

Abemaciclib is taken with endocrine treatment after surgery and other indicated local/systemic treatment. Abemaciclib is planned for two years; endocrine treatment commonly continues beyond that.

Do I need to enter dates?

No. Both charts explain the expected sequence and general intervals without any dates or personal information. Your hospital arranges actual appointments.

Which tests should I expect?

The treatment journey lists baseline and ongoing checks, why they matter and what to ask. It does not mark tests as booked or completed.

What happens if treatment is delayed?

Your team may delay, reduce, change or stop a medicine. Ask which component changes and what happens to the next review; do not restart independently.

What about fertility, pregnancy or breastfeeding?

Discuss fertility preservation and reproductive plans before treatment. The team should explain contraception and breastfeeding restrictions for every medicine, including how long precautions continue afterwards.

How should I use trial numbers?

Trial results describe groups with defined eligibility. Ask how well those patients and their treatment match your situation; the figures are not an individual prediction.

Which checks are baseline, and which happen during treatment?

NCCP 00619 specifies baseline ECG as clinically indicated. It does not mandate the ribociclib Cycle 1 Day 14 ECG schedule. Abemaciclib is continuous twice daily: there is no routine seven-day break.
