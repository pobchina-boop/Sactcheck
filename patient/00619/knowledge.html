Adjuvant abemaciclib + endocrine therapy

The usual abemaciclib starting dose is 150 mg twice daily; a lower dose or altered schedule may be prescribed. Abemaciclib is taken twice daily continuously for up to two years with endocrine therapy, unless recurrence or unacceptable toxicity requires an earlier change. A four-week dispensing block is not a rest cycle. Endocrine treatment normally continues beyond abemaciclib.

How this medicine supports your treatment

For eligible high-risk node-positive early breast cancer, adding abemaciclib to endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence after surgery.

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Abemaciclib

Slows the cell-division checkpoint
CDK4/6 checkpoint↓Checkpoint inhibited↓Cell cycling slows

Abemaciclib inhibits CDK4 and CDK6 proteins. These proteins help cells move towards division. It is combined with endocrine treatment to reduce recurrence risk in eligible high-risk early breast cancer.

Start the prescribed diarrhoea rescue plan at the first loose stool and contact the team. Breathlessness, a swollen painful leg, fever or jaundice need prompt assessment.

Hormone-directed treatment

Your endocrine treatment

Reduces the hormone signal
Hormone signal↓Signal reduced or blocked↓HR-positive growth reduced

The chosen endocrine medicine either reduces oestrogen production or blocks its action. An aromatase inhibitor may need ovarian suppression before menopause. The exact drug and duration are prescribed separately.

Tell the team about joint pain, hot flushes, vaginal symptoms and mood changes. The bone-health and clot-risk discussion depends on the drug chosen.

Your treatment aim

For eligible high-risk node-positive early breast cancer, adding abemaciclib to endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence after surgery.

Who this treatment is for

HR-positive/HER2-negative node-positive early breast cancer at high recurrence risk: four or more positive axillary nodes, or one to three with tumour at least 5 cm and/or grade 3. The NCCP timing, prior-treatment and other exclusions remain applicable; this is not a node-negative or metastatic regimen.

NCCP 00619 v4a · official regimen

Schedule and duration

The usual abemaciclib starting dose is 150 mg twice daily; a lower dose or altered schedule may be prescribed. Abemaciclib is taken twice daily continuously for up to two years with endocrine therapy, unless recurrence or unacceptable toxicity requires an earlier change. A four-week dispensing block is not a rest cycle. Endocrine treatment normally continues beyond abemaciclib. At completion of the planned two-year abemaciclib course, continue the separately agreed endocrine and follow-up plan.

Checks before and during treatment

Baseline tests and ongoing tests have different purposes.

Before the first dose

FBC, renal, liver and bone profile; ECG as clinically indicated. Confirm node-positive high-risk eligibility, definitive surgery and timing: within 16 months of resection and up to 12 weeks of endocrine treatment after the last non-endocrine treatment before starting. Assess VTE history, bowel function, all medicines, endocrine partner, ovarian suppression if using an AI before menopause, and bone/reproductive health.

NCCP 00619 v4a · official regimen · EMA Verzenios current product information; checked 2026-10-25

During treatment

FBC, renal, liver and bone profile every two weeks for the first two months, monthly for the next two months, then as clinically indicated. More frequent liver monitoring for grade 2 or worse liver abnormalities. ECG is driven by clinical need; no routine ribociclib Day-14 ECG schedule. Early and ongoing diarrhoea review, hydration/intake, infection, clots, breathlessness and adherence; prompt action between appointments. Creatinine can rise through inhibited tubular secretion without a true fall in filtration; assess the clinical context and alternative renal measures if needed.

Living with treatment

Have the prescribed antidiarrhoeal treatment available before starting. Begin the agreed plan at the first loose stool, increase fluids as advised and contact the team early; persistent symptoms or dehydration may require a treatment hold.

What the studies tell us

Adding a planned course of abemaciclib to endocrine therapy reduced the chance of invasive cancer returning in eligible high-risk, node-positive early breast cancer. Longer follow-up also showed an overall-survival benefit. These group results do not predict your own outcome.

How to interpret this

Study results describe groups with defined treatment and eligibility. Ask about your own likely benefits, important harms, alternatives and remaining uncertainty.

Do I need to enter dates?

No. This map shows general timing without personal information. Your hospital arranges actual appointments; ask who books each test and who reviews its result.

What if a dose is missed or treatment is held?

Take abemaciclib twice daily at regular times with or without food. Swallow whole; do not split, chew or crush. Avoid grapefruit. Do not replace a missed or vomited dose. A clinical hold and restart require the treating team’s instructions. A hold may affect one medicine rather than the whole combination; confirm each component.

What does the evidence mean for me?

Adding a planned course of abemaciclib to endocrine therapy reduced the chance of invasive cancer returning in eligible high-risk, node-positive early breast cancer. Longer follow-up also showed an overall-survival benefit. These group results do not predict your own outcome.

What happens after treatment changes or ends?

At completion of the planned two-year abemaciclib course, continue the separately agreed endocrine and follow-up plan. Keep the separately agreed disease follow-up, endocrine and bone-health plan.
