Adjuvant abemaciclib + endocrine therapy

For eligible high-risk node-positive early breast cancer, adding abemaciclib to endocrine therapy reduces the risk of recurrence after surgery.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

The usual abemaciclib starting dose is 150 mg twice daily; a lower dose or altered schedule may be prescribed. Abemaciclib is taken twice daily continuously for up to two years with endocrine therapy, unless recurrence or unacceptable toxicity requires an earlier change. A four-week dispensing block is not a rest cycle. Endocrine treatment normally continues beyond abemaciclib.

Tests and checks

full blood count (FBC), renal, liver and bone profile; electrocardiogram (ECG) as clinically indicated. Confirm node-positive high-risk eligibility, definitive surgery and timing: within 16 months of resection and up to 12 weeks of endocrine treatment after the last non-endocrine treatment before starting. Assess VTE history, bowel function, all medicines, endocrine partner, ovarian suppression if using an aromatase inhibitor (AI) before menopause, and bone/reproductive health. FBC, renal, liver and bone profile every two weeks for the first two months, monthly for the next two months, then as clinically indicated. More frequent liver monitoring for grade 2 or worse liver abnormalities. ECG is driven by clinical need; no routine ribociclib Day-14 ECG schedule. Early and ongoing diarrhoea review, hydration/intake, infection, clots, breathlessness and adherence; prompt action between appointments. Creatinine can rise through inhibited tubular secretion without a true fall in filtration; assess the clinical context and alternative renal measures if needed.
