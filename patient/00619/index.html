How your medicines work

CDK4/6 inhibitor

Abemaciclib

Slows the cell-division checkpoint
CDK4/6 checkpoint→Checkpoint inhibited→Cell cycling slows

Abemaciclib inhibits CDK4 and CDK6 proteins. These proteins help cells move towards division. It is combined with endocrine treatment to reduce recurrence risk in eligible high-risk early breast cancer.

Start the prescribed diarrhoea rescue plan at the first loose stool and contact the team. Breathlessness, a swollen painful leg, fever or jaundice need prompt assessment.

Hormone-directed treatment

Your endocrine treatment

Reduces the hormone signal
Hormone signal→Signal reduced or blocked→HR-positive growth reduced

The chosen endocrine medicine either reduces oestrogen production or blocks its action. An aromatase inhibitor may need ovarian suppression before menopause. The exact drug and duration are prescribed separately.

Tell the team about joint pain, hot flushes, vaginal symptoms and mood changes. The bone-health and clot-risk discussion depends on the drug chosen.

3Bone density and fracture riskAt AI/OFS initiation and then by risk

Assess fracture risk and whether a baseline DEXA bone-density scan is needed when starting an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression/treatment-induced menopause. It may already have been done before this regimen.

Ask / act: Ask when bone health was last assessed and whether a repeat scan or bone medicine is needed.

4Breast surveillanceAfter early breast treatment

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Abemaciclib is taken twice daily continuously, with endocrine treatment, for up to two years if appropriate. A 28-day block is shown for orientation; there is no planned tablet-free week. Endocrine treatment usually continues longer.

STEP 1

Before starting

Confirm the exact tablet strength, endocrine partner, blood-test and ECG plan where relevant.

STEP 2

At home

Take tablets whole at the prescribed times. Abemaciclib is continuous twice daily; ribociclib has a planned 7-day break. Follow the schedule for this card only.

STEP 3

If a dose is missed or vomited

Do not double up or take a replacement dose. Follow the medicine instructions and ask the oncology pharmacist if uncertain.

STEP 4

Before the next supply

Confirm symptoms and required test results have been reviewed. Ask whether the prescription changes; do not restart a held medicine yourself.

Interactions and storage

Check all new medicines and supplements with oncology/pharmacy, avoid grapefruit, store as labelled and keep away from children. Return unwanted tablets to the pharmacy.

Endocrine and ovarian-suppression visits

An aromatase inhibitor is generally a daily tablet. Fulvestrant, when selected for metastatic treatment, has loading injections on Days 1, 15 and 29 then monthly. Ovarian-suppression injections have their own prescription and visit schedule.

No infusion pump

The CDK4/6 medicine is taken orally. An implanted port or infusion pump is not required for these tablets; other treatment may have separate access needs.

Support around treatment

Diarrhoea plan

Have the prescribed antidiarrhoeal medicine available. Start the agreed plan at the first loose stool and call early.

Daily tablets

Take the prescribed dose at regular times. Do not double up after a missed or vomited dose.

Baseline and ongoing checks

Baseline FBC, renal, liver and bone profile. Baseline ECG as clinically indicated. Bloods every two weeks for two months, then monthly for two months, then as indicated. No routine ribociclib-style Day 14 ECG schedule.

Endocrine and bone care

Confirm endocrine/OFS treatment, bone-health assessment and the longer-term follow-up plan.
