Phesgo + docetaxel

Phesgo and docetaxel on Day 1 every 21 days. Docetaxel is planned for at least six cycles; it may stop after 6–8 cycles or toxicity. Phesgo may continue alone while beneficial and tolerated.

Source reconciled; independent clinical, pharmacy and patient/PPI review pending. Selected effects; not a complete medicine leaflet.

Your treatment, at home.

Important medicine-specific effects

Docetaxel

  • Low blood counts, infection and sepsis
  • Hair loss, mouth soreness and fatigue
  • Numbness / tingling; sometimes lasting
  • Fluid retention and nail changes
  • Allergic infusion reaction

Pertuzumab

  • Diarrhoea and dehydration
  • Injection / allergic reaction
  • Heart pumping-function reduction
  • Rash and fatigue

Trastuzumab

  • Heart pumping-function reduction / heart failure
  • Injection or allergic reaction
  • Diarrhoea and fatigue
  • Rare serious lung effects

Make the plan practical

Antiemetic plan

Taxane treatment uses a low-risk antiemetic plan; Phesgo alone uses a separate minimal-risk plan.

Premedicines

Docetaxel: prescribed dexamethasone starts the day before each dose. Ask for one reconciled steroid schedule.

Appointments and results

Echo before treatment and every 12 weeks (around cycle 5 if on time), including maintenance. Bloods before each cycle. Ask who books and reviews each test; report a missing appointment.

Phase transition

After chemotherapy stops, use the agreed Phesgo maintenance plan (NCCP 00785 when eligible). Keep cardiac and response checks; chemotherapy steroids, antiemetics and growth factors do not automatically continue.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain needs emergency help.
Contact oncology immediately for temperature ≥37.5°C or ≤36°C, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your own hospital’s instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.
