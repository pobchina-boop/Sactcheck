Weekly paclitaxel + trastuzumab → trastuzumab continuation: the whole pathway

Adjuvant · HER2-positive early breast cancer

Paclitaxel weekly for 12 doses with trastuzumab every 3 weeks; continue trastuzumab to complete one year of HER2 treatment when appropriate.

What each check is for

1Blood tests and treatment reviewFBC, kidney/liver tests → Before each weekly chemotherapy dose → According to clinical status / ongoing treatment → Review unresolved toxicity → Only as clinically indicated

Results may change whether a dose is given or held.

Ask / act: Confirm the booking and who reviews the result before treatment.

Sources: NCCP 00815

2Heart testsBaseline echo / LVEF → Every 12 weeks during HER2 treatment → Continue the same surveillance clock → Completion assessment / risk review → Follow-up by cardiac risk

ECG measures rhythm; echo measures pumping function. They are different checks.

Ask / act: Keep the planned appointments and report symptoms between them.

Sources: NCCP 00815 · ESC

3Imaging and markersReview operative pathology → No routine CT series if well → Routine surveillance plan → Confirm first mammogram timing → Annual mammography; symptoms prompt assessment

The purpose and frequency depend on early versus advanced disease.

Ask / act: Ask which test is next and what decision its result will inform.

Sources: EARLY · BONE

4Bone health with endocrine treatmentAt endocrine / ovarian-suppression planning; repeat by risk

Low oestrogen can affect bone strength.

Ask / act: Ask whether DEXA, fracture-risk assessment or bone protection is appropriate.

Sources: BONE · NOGG

5Who arranges and reviews each check?At initiation and every handover

A recommended test can be missed if responsibility is unclear.

Ask / act: Ask the team to document the booking owner, result reviewer, due window and escalation route.

Sources: NCCP 00815

At the next review, ask

  • Which treatment phase am I in, and what happens next?
  • Which test is expected next, who arranges it, and who explains the result?
  • Who should I contact if an expected appointment has not arrived?
