When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Understand your treatment

Your medicines and their different roles

Adjuvant treatment aims to reduce recurrence; confirm the exact treatment setting and sequence with your team.

Taxane chemotherapy

Paclitaxel

Taxol (conventional paclitaxel)
Locks the cell division machinery

Cells use tiny structures called microtubules to separate their chromosomes when dividing. Paclitaxel stabilises these structures so they cannot rearrange normally, interrupting cell division. It can also affect nerves and other healthy tissues.

Microtubules form↓Structures remain locked↓Division is interrupted

What to watch for
Report tingling or numbness before the next weekly dose, especially if it affects walking or hand use. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, chest or throat tightness, itching, dizziness or breathing changes during infusion.

Sources: nccp

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

IV infusion or locally approved subcutaneous injection every 3 weeks
Targets HER2 growth signals

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

HER2 on cell surface↓Antibody binds↓Growth signalling reduced

What to watch for
Keep echo appointments throughout HER2 treatment. Report breathlessness, swelling, palpitations or a reaction; chemotherapy side effects are tracked separately.

Sources: nccp

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Paclitaxel weekly for 12 doses with trastuzumab every 3 weeks; continue trastuzumab to complete one year of HER2 treatment when appropriate. The team selects IV trastuzumab or locally approved subcutaneous trastuzumab; these are alternative formulations, not additional medicines.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Blood tests, medicines review and the consent discussion come first. Confirm baseline and planned ongoing cardiac assessment.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Paclitaxel is an IV infusion. Trastuzumab may be IV or, if locally approved and prescribed, a subcutaneous injection. Staff confirm formulation, observation and access needs at every phase change.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit time and transport

First visits can take longer because of loading doses, premedicines and observation. Ask the unit about expected time, transport and any escort needed after sedation or line insertion.

Phase-specific visit pattern

Paclitaxel weekly for 12 doses with trastuzumab every 3 weeks; continue trastuzumab to complete one year of HER2 treatment when appropriate. The team selects IV trastuzumab or locally approved subcutaneous trastuzumab; these are alternative formulations, not additional medicines. No take-home infusion pump is part of this schedule. Port de-accessing after a visit is different from removal of the implanted port.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Anti-sickness plan

Follow the prevention and rescue instructions prescribed for this schedule. Call if vomiting prevents drinking.

Infection and bleeding

Know the local urgent-contact number. Fever, shivering, sudden illness or unusual bleeding needs prompt advice even between visits.

Medicine review

Show the oncology pharmacist all prescribed, over-the-counter and herbal medicines. Ask before starting anything new.

Premedicines belong to the active phase

Paclitaxel reaction medicines and AC anti-sickness medicines have different purposes. They are not automatically continued during trastuzumab-only treatment.

Questions worth asking

Why is this schedule being recommended?

This adjuvant option uses weekly paclitaxel with three-weekly trastuzumab for selected HER2-positive, node-negative cancers no larger than 3 cm. It avoids an anthracycline, but still requires heart monitoring and review for chemotherapy effects.

Which days are treatment days?

Paclitaxel weekly for 12 doses with trastuzumab every 3 weeks; continue trastuzumab to complete one year of HER2 treatment when appropriate. The team selects IV trastuzumab or locally approved subcutaneous trastuzumab; these are alternative formulations, not additional medicines.

Who arranges the next tests?

Your treating service arranges and reviews tests. If instructions or appointments are missing, contact the team; the map does not book tests.

What if a dose is missed or I feel unwell?

Contact the unit for a revised plan. Do not double tablets, shift treatment dates or assume that an omitted dose should be made up.

Try explaining the plan back: Which medicines are due on which days? What symptoms mean you should call today? Which tests come next and who arranges them?
