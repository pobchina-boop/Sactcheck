When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Call your oncology team urgently for fever, shaking chills, feeling suddenly unwell, unusual bruising or bleeding, marked weakness, new or worsening cough or breathlessness, a new painful swollen limb, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or inability to keep fluids down. Follow your own temperature and contact plan; do not wait for the next appointment. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse, seizures or stroke symptoms: call 112/999. Severe new abdominal pain, major bleeding, worsening wounds or severe headache/visual change also need urgent assessment; tell the infusion nurse immediately about symptoms during a visit.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

Understand your treatment

How your medicine works

Help sustain cancer control after first-line chemotherapy, using olaparib to target impaired deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) repair and bevacizumab to limit signals for new blood-vessel growth.

Targeted therapy: PARP inhibitor

Olaparib

Lynparza
Olaparib blocks poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP)-associated DNA repair and can trap PARP on DNA. Homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) means an important DNA-repair pathway is already impaired, through a BRCA change or another qualifying pattern of genomic instability. Further disruption can make cancer cells less able to survive. Normal blood-forming cells can also be affected, so blood counts remain important.

What to watch for
Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy.

Targeted antibody: VEGF inhibitor

Bevacizumab

Avastin or the prescribed biosimilar
Bevacizumab binds vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), a signal that encourages new blood vessels to form. Blocking this signal can limit blood-vessel growth that supports cancer. VEGF also matters in normal tissues, so blood pressure, urine protein and wound healing need attention.

What to watch for
Tell the team about headache, visual changes, bleeding, new swelling, severe abdominal pain, wound problems or planned surgery. Keep blood-pressure medicines and urine checks as prescribed. No routine bevacizumab premedication is needed for everyone; previous reactions change the individual plan.

Olaparib + bevacizumab

Maintenance

Individual maintenance plan; separate medicine clocks

The medicines have separate treatment clocks. Bevacizumab is given for a maximum of 15 months in total, counting doses with the earlier chemotherapy. Olaparib has a two-year disease-status review: complete treatment at two years if no cancer is visible on scans; if disease remains, the consultant may consider further benefit from continuing. Either medicine may stop earlier for progression or unacceptable side effects. Finishing bevacizumab does not automatically finish olaparib.

Practical treatment guide

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before the first dose

Confirm the validated HRD result, response after first-line platinum-taxane chemotherapy with bevacizumab, and recovery. Check full blood count, kidney/liver profile, blood pressure, urine protein, medicines and pregnancy testing where relevant; review heart health, bleeding/clots, operations and wound healing.

STEP 2

How maintenance fits after chemotherapy

Maintenance follows the first platinum-taxane and bevacizumab phase. The regimen specifies 6–9 platinum-taxane cycles and bevacizumab in the final chemotherapy cycles, with an interval-surgery allowance. Your team confirms the exact previous-treatment and recovery requirements before this combination starts.

STEP 3

A practical home routine

Swallow the prescribed tablets whole with or without food, once in the morning and once in the evening. Do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. The usual starting dose is two 150 mg tablets each time (300 mg twice daily); your own dose may be lower. Check both tablet strength and tablet count after every prescription change. If you miss a dose, take the next normal dose at its usual time; do not double up. Do not substitute olaparib capsules for tablets. Avoid grapefruit juice and check other grapefruit or bitter-orange products with your pharmacist. Bevacizumab needs an intravenous cannula or suitable existing access. The team explains how long the visit and checks will take.

STEP 4

The written support plan

Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy. Give the pharmacist a complete list of prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and herbal products. Some antibiotics, antifungals, seizure medicines and St John’s wort can change how much olaparib reaches your body. Even anti-sickness treatment needs checking: aprepitant can increase olaparib exposure. Do not start a borrowed support medicine, change the olaparib dose or restart a paused course yourself.

STEP 5

If treatment is paused

Ask why it is paused, which tests are next and when the team will contact you. Only the prescriber can confirm the restart dose. Do not take catch-up tablets.

STEP 6

Reviewing benefit and the next step

The medicines have separate treatment clocks. Bevacizumab is given for a maximum of 15 months in total, counting doses with the earlier chemotherapy. Olaparib has a two-year disease-status review: complete treatment at two years if no cancer is visible on scans; if disease remains, the consultant may consider further benefit from continuing. Either medicine may stop earlier for progression or unacceptable side effects. Finishing bevacizumab does not automatically finish olaparib. The consultant sets symptom reviews, scans and any relevant CA-125 testing. Agree the total bevacizumab exposure and the separate two-year olaparib decision; safety blood/urine tests do not replace cancer assessment.

STEP 7

Genetic results and family questions

BRCA1 and BRCA2 help repair damaged DNA. A germline change is present in the body’s cells and may have implications for relatives. A tumour-only change does not by itself show that it is inherited. Ask which test you had, what the result means and whether genetic counselling is appropriate. A variant of uncertain significance is not automatically a treatment-qualifying result.

STEP 8

Before leaving the clinic

Confirm the next tests, review, tablet supply and verified daytime/out-of-hours contact numbers. Ask what to do if a result or prescription has not arrived.

STEP 9

Infusion time

The first bevacizumab infusion is usually 90 minutes. If tolerated, the second may take 60 minutes and later infusions 30 minutes. Checks, preparation and observation add to the visit; your unit confirms the actual schedule.

STEP 10

Operations and wounds

Tell the team before any planned operation, dental procedure or new wound. Bevacizumab affects healing; a passed surgery date does not mean a wound is healed. Continue prescribed blood-pressure treatment and bring any requested readings. Blood thinners and all new medicines need review because bleeding/clotting risks must be considered together.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Your prescribed sickness plan

Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy.

Check every new medicine

Give the pharmacist a complete list of prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and herbal products. Some antibiotics, antifungals, seizure medicines and St John’s wort can change how much olaparib reaches your body. Even anti-sickness treatment needs checking: aprepitant can increase olaparib exposure. Do not start a borrowed support medicine, change the olaparib dose or restart a paused course yourself.

Blood counts and extra support

Blood counts check red cells that carry oxygen, white cells that help fight infection and platelets that help stop bleeding. Low counts can lead to a pause, a lower prescribed dose, a transfusion or further tests. Persistent or unexplained changes sometimes need specialist bone-marrow tests to investigate myelodysplastic syndrome or acute myeloid leukaemia (MDS/AML). These are serious conditions; their risk differs by treatment setting and previous therapy. A treatment pause is not itself a diagnosis. There is no automatic white-cell injection, antibiotic or steroid course for everyone.

Eating, drinking and daily life

Ask for help early if nausea, mouth soreness, taste changes or poor appetite affect eating. Keep the team informed about weight loss or difficulty drinking. Fatigue may need a dose or blood-count review. Do not drive or use machinery if dizzy, weak or very tired.

A treatment pause

A prescribed pause can give blood counts or symptoms time to improve. It does not mean treatment has failed. Keep the agreed tests and ask exactly when the team will review restarting. Never take catch-up tablets.

Support for your wider treatment

Tell the team before any planned operation, dental procedure or new wound. Bevacizumab affects healing; a passed surgery date does not mean a wound is healed. Continue prescribed blood-pressure treatment and bring any requested readings. Blood thinners and all new medicines need review because bleeding/clotting risks must be considered together.

Fertility, contraception and breastfeeding

Follow the olaparib pregnancy-prevention advice, including two reliable complementary methods where relevant. Bevacizumab also requires effective contraception during treatment and for six months after its last dose; use the later applicable end date. Do not breastfeed during either treatment, for at least six months after the last bevacizumab dose, and for one month after the last olaparib dose—whichever date is later. Discuss fertility before treatment and tell the team immediately if pregnancy is possible.

Blood-pressure and urine follow-up

Continue only the blood-pressure medicines prescribed for you. Ask what readings or symptoms need contact and whether home measurement is wanted. Urine protein may have no obvious symptoms, so keep the tests even when feeling well.

Infusion support

Bevacizumab has minimal emetic potential. Routine steroid or antihistamine premedication is not required for everyone; the team changes the plan if there has been a previous reaction. Report flushing, rash, chest tightness, breathing difficulty or faintness during the infusion immediately.

The treatment setting

This pathway requires a validated homologous recombination deficiency (HRD)-positive result, defined by a qualifying BRCA1/2 change and/or genomic instability, and response after first-line platinum chemotherapy with bevacizumab. HRD can be positive without a BRCA mutation. The team also checks histology, previous cycles, fitness and no previous PARP-inhibitor treatment.

These are serious conditions; their risk differs by treatment setting and previous therapy. A treatment pause is not itself a diagnosis.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Cancer response

The consultant sets symptom reviews, scans and any relevant CA-125 testing. Agree the total bevacizumab exposure and the separate two-year olaparib decision; safety blood/urine tests do not replace cancer assessment.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Other tests

Bevacizumab adds blood-pressure, urine-protein and heart-risk assessment. An echo may be considered after anthracyclines or chest-wall radiotherapy; follow any additional individual heart plan. Warfarin requires its own international normalised ratio (blood-clotting test) (INR) checks.

Sources: nccp · smpc-bevacizumab

Treatment reference · 04

Prescribed support and daily life

Use the medicine plan supplied by your own oncology team.

Nausea and stomach support

Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Interactions

Give the pharmacist a complete list of prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins and herbal products. Some antibiotics, antifungals, seizure medicines and St John’s wort can change how much olaparib reaches your body. Even anti-sickness treatment needs checking: aprepitant can increase olaparib exposure. Do not start a borrowed support medicine, change the olaparib dose or restart a paused course yourself.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Wider care

Tell the team before any planned operation, dental procedure or new wound. Bevacizumab affects healing; a passed surgery date does not mean a wound is healed. Continue prescribed blood-pressure treatment and bring any requested readings. Blood thinners and all new medicines need review because bleeding/clotting risks must be considered together.

Sources: nccp · smpc

Fertility and pregnancy prevention

Follow the olaparib pregnancy-prevention advice, including two reliable complementary methods where relevant. Bevacizumab also requires effective contraception during treatment and for six months after its last dose; use the later applicable end date. Do not breastfeed during either treatment, for at least six months after the last bevacizumab dose, and for one month after the last olaparib dose—whichever date is later. Discuss fertility before treatment and tell the team immediately if pregnancy is possible.

Symptoms that need action

A new symptom can have several causes; contact the team so it can be assessed.

When to call

Call your oncology team urgently for fever, shaking chills, feeling suddenly unwell, unusual bruising or bleeding, marked weakness, new or worsening cough or breathlessness, a new painful swollen limb, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or inability to keep fluids down. Lungs and blood clots

New or worsening cough, breathlessness, chest pain or a painful swollen limb can signal infection, lung inflammation or a clot. Do not assume these symptoms are routine fatigue.

Liver and blood changes

Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, unexplained severe fatigue, infections, bruising or bleeding need prompt advice. Persistent symptoms still matter during a treatment break or after treatment ends.

Abdominal pain, wounds and bleeding

Severe new abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, a swollen abdomen, major bleeding or a wound that is deteriorating requires urgent contact. Tell the team about planned surgery or dental procedures before they happen.

Severe headache, new visual disturbance, confusion, seizures or stroke symptoms need urgent assessment. These may relate to severe hypertension, a clot or PRES; do not try to diagnose the cause yourself.

Evidence and your choices

Trial results describe groups and should support a discussion about your priorities.

PAOLA-1: adding olaparib to bevacizumab maintenance

In the HRD-positive subgroup, median time without progression or death was 37.2 months with olaparib plus bevacizumab versus 17.7 months with placebo plus bevacizumab. These subgroup results support the selected pathway; a median is a group midpoint, not a deadline or an individual forecast.

Sources: paola1-2019 · smpc

What the comparison cannot tell us

The trial did not include an olaparib-only maintenance arm, so this comparison does not prove that the combination is better than olaparib alone. HRD subgroup analyses were not included in the trial’s multiple-testing procedure. Results cannot be transferred to HRD-negative disease or a later relapse.

Ask about expected benefit, side effects, alternatives including symptom-focused care, and choosing to decline or stop treatment. Decisions can be revisited as your health or priorities change.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the aim of maintenance treatment?

Help sustain cancer control after first-line chemotherapy, using olaparib to target impaired DNA repair and bevacizumab to limit signals for new blood-vessel growth. Maintenance follows the first platinum-taxane and bevacizumab phase. The regimen specifies 6–9 platinum-taxane cycles and bevacizumab in the final chemotherapy cycles, with an interval-surgery allowance. Your team confirms the exact previous-treatment and recovery requirements before this combination starts.

How long will I take olaparib?

The medicines have separate treatment clocks. Bevacizumab is given for a maximum of 15 months in total, counting doses with the earlier chemotherapy. Olaparib has a two-year disease-status review: complete treatment at two years if no cancer is visible on scans; if disease remains, the consultant may consider further benefit from continuing. Either medicine may stop earlier for progression or unacceptable side effects. Finishing bevacizumab does not automatically finish olaparib.

How do I take it and what if I miss a dose?

Swallow the prescribed tablets whole with or without food, once in the morning and once in the evening. Do not chew, crush, dissolve or divide them. The usual starting dose is two 150 mg tablets each time (300 mg twice daily); your own dose may be lower. Check both tablet strength and tablet count after every prescription change. If you miss a dose, take the next normal dose at its usual time; do not double up. Do not substitute olaparib capsules for tablets. Avoid grapefruit juice and check other grapefruit or bitter-orange products with your pharmacist.

Which checks should I expect?

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests every three weeks for the first 12 months, then as clinically indicated. Check urine protein and blood pressure before each bevacizumab cycle; monitor blood pressure after treatment as advised. Review symptoms, wounds, medicines and tablet supply; extra tests may be needed. The consultant sets symptom reviews, scans and any relevant CA-125 testing. Agree the total bevacizumab exposure and the separate two-year olaparib decision; safety blood/urine tests do not replace cancer assessment.

Which symptoms mean calling today?

Call your oncology team urgently for fever, shaking chills, feeling suddenly unwell, unusual bruising or bleeding, marked weakness, new or worsening cough or breathlessness, a new painful swollen limb, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, or inability to keep fluids down. Follow your own temperature and contact plan; do not wait for the next appointment. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, collapse, seizures or stroke symptoms: call 112/999. Severe new abdominal pain, major bleeding, worsening wounds or severe headache/visual change also need urgent assessment; tell the infusion nurse immediately about symptoms during a visit.

Does a tumour result mean the change is inherited?

BRCA1 and BRCA2 help repair damaged DNA. A germline change is present in the body’s cells and may have implications for relatives. A tumour-only change does not by itself show that it is inherited. Ask which test you had, what the result means and whether genetic counselling is appropriate. A variant of uncertain significance is not automatically a treatment-qualifying result.

Why do I need urine and blood-pressure tests?

Bevacizumab affects blood-vessel signalling and can raise blood pressure or cause protein to leak through kidney filters. These problems may not cause obvious symptoms, so tests matter even when you feel well.
