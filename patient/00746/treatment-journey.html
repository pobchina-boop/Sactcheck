Tests in your plan

Full blood count

WhenBefore treatment and every three weeks for the first 12 months, then as clinically indicated; earlier for symptoms or abnormal results.

Check red cells, infection-fighting white cells and platelets; persistent unexplained changes may need specialist tests.

Kidney and liver profile

WhenBefore treatment and every three weeks for the first 12 months, then as clinically indicated.

Check organ function and whether the prescription needs review.

HRD and BRCA result

WhenA validated qualifying result is needed before treatment. Discuss any inherited implications with the team.

HRD describes impaired repair of deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) breaks. A qualifying BRCA change or validated genomic-instability result can establish HRD for this pathway; a BRCA-wild-type result does not automatically mean HRD-negative.

Symptoms, scans and selected CA-125 tests

If part of your plan
WhenThe consultant sets symptom reviews, scans and any relevant CA-125 testing. Agree the total bevacizumab exposure and the separate two-year olaparib decision; safety blood/urine tests do not replace cancer assessment.

Assess cancer control in the whole clinical picture, separate from safety blood tests.

Pregnancy testing where relevant

If part of your plan
WhenBefore starting if you could become pregnant; repeat testing as advised.

Avoid exposing an unborn baby to olaparib.

Blood pressure

WhenBaseline, before each cycle and after treatment as advised; home checks if requested.

Detect and manage a bevacizumab effect on blood vessels; symptoms matter as well as readings.

Urine protein

WhenBaseline and before bevacizumab cycles; repeat/quantitative tests according to the team.

Check whether bevacizumab is affecting the kidney filters; abnormal dipsticks can require a24-hour urine collection.

Heart assessment and selected echo

If part of your plan
WhenBaseline history/examination; echo when indicated, rather than a universal serial echo timetable.

Review cardiovascular risk; a heart ultrasound may be appropriate after anthracycline treatment or chest-wall radiotherapy.

international normalised ratio (blood-clotting test) (INR) if taking warfarin

If part of your plan
WhenWeekly until the warfarin dose is stable, then before each cycle; other anticoagulants need their own review.

Check the effect of prescribed warfarin and bleeding risk.

The whole treatment journey

Olaparib and bevacizumab maintenance · the whole treatment journey

Homologous recombination deficiency (HRD)-positive advanced ovarian, fallopian-tube or primary-peritoneal cancer after first-line chemotherapy with bevacizumab

Take the prescribed olaparib dose morning and evening every day, including between infusion visits. Bevacizumab is given into a vein on Day 1 of each 21-day interval after the checks. The two medicines have separate stop dates.
