1. Treatment and purpose

Olaparib and bevacizumab maintenance · NCCP 00746 v2

The medicines have separate treatment clocks. Bevacizumab is given for a maximum of 15 months in total, counting doses with the earlier chemotherapy. Olaparib has a two-year disease-status review: complete treatment at two years if no cancer is visible on scans; if disease remains, the consultant may consider further benefit from continuing. Either medicine may stop earlier for progression or unacceptable side effects. Finishing bevacizumab does not automatically finish olaparib.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Olaparib · Targeted therapy: PARP inhibitor

Agree a written anti-sickness plan with your team, including what is prescribed regularly, what is for rescue, and when to call if it is not working. NCCP classifies oral olaparib as moderate-to-high sickness risk, but a fixed intravenous chemotherapy anti-sickness cycle does not apply. Some people need prescribed support and others do not need routine prevention. A stomach-acid medicine may be considered for symptoms under local policy.

Bevacizumab · Targeted antibody: VEGF inhibitor

Tell the team about headache, visual changes, bleeding, new swelling, severe abdominal pain, wound problems or planned surgery. Keep blood-pressure medicines and urine checks as prescribed. No routine bevacizumab premedication is needed for everyone; previous reactions change the individual plan.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss the expected maintenance benefit, uncertainty and alternatives. Olaparib risks include blood-count/marrow problems, lung inflammation, liver injury and clots. Bevacizumab adds high blood pressure, urine protein/kidney injury, bleeding/clots, wound-healing problems, perforation/fistula, infusion reactions, PRES, heart and fertility effects. Follow the olaparib pregnancy-prevention advice, including two reliable complementary methods where relevant. Bevacizumab also requires effective contraception during treatment and for six months after its last dose; use the later applicable end date. Do not breastfeed during either treatment, for at least six months after the last bevacizumab dose, and for one month after the last olaparib dose—whichever date is later. Discuss fertility before treatment and tell the team immediately if pregnancy is possible.
