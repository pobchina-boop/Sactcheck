Your practical treatment plan

Know the next step.

Taking treatment

One infusion on Day 1 of a 21-day cycle. The next infusion is normally three weeks later. A delayed appointment is rescheduled by the team; patients do not calculate catch-up doses.

The first infusion takes 90 minutes. Later infusions may take 30 minutes if the previous infusion was tolerated. Allow extra time for assessment, anti-sickness medicines and any observation. A cannula or an existing central line can be used according to the clinical plan; there is no home pump.

Checks and follow-up

Before the first dose: full blood count, kidney and liver tests, an echocardiogram or MUGA scan to measure heart pumping function, and a pregnancy test where relevant. The team checks the HER2 result, previous treatment, lung history and current breathing symptoms, and agrees how disease response will be assessed. Blood count, kidney and liver tests before each 21-day cycle. Review breathing, fatigue, nausea, intake and other symptoms at each visit. Repeat cardiac imaging every 12 weeks; assess new breathing symptoms immediately rather than waiting for the next planned scan.

Support between reviews

Prevent nausea before it starts

This regimen has high emetic risk. An antiemetic combination is prescribed before every infusion, with an agreed plan for delayed symptoms and breakthrough nausea. The dedicated NCCP trastuzumab-deruxtecan pathway should be checked by the prescriber and pharmacist.

Lung-safety plan

Carry the patient alert card and report new cough, fever or breathlessness promptly. Steroids used for suspected lung inflammation are prescribed and monitored by the clinical team; do not start them yourself from this information.

Blood-count support

Low blood counts can cause infection, anaemia or bleeding. The need for G-CSF or transfusion support depends on results, symptoms and previous complications; it is not automatic for every cycle.

Eating and energy

Small frequent meals and early treatment of nausea can help maintain intake. Tell the team about weight loss, constipation or diarrhoea so that support can be tailored.
