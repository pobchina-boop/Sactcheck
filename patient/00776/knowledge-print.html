Understanding your treatment

Why this treatment is offered, what its medicines do, and how the agreed plan fits your care.

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu) · every 3 weeks
National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) 00776

How this medicine supports your treatment

Aim to control cancer growth, reduce cancer-related symptoms and prolong disease control. Individual benefit and duration vary; trial averages cannot predict one person's outcome.

HER2-directed antibody–drug conjugate

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu)

Uses HER2 as a docking target to deliver a topoisomerase-I inhibitor payload
Antibody attaches to HER2↓Cancer cell takes the linked drug inside↓Released payload damages deoxyribonucleic acid (the cell’s genetic material) (DNA) during cell division

Enhertu links an antibody that recognises HER2 with deruxtecan, a cell-killing medicine. After uptake into the cell, the payload inhibits topoisomerase I and interferes with DNA replication. The released payload can also affect nearby tumour cells. This targeting does not spare every healthy cell, which is why blood counts, nausea, lungs and heart function still need attention.

New cough, breathlessness or fever needs urgent contact even between cycles. Keep the 12-weekly heart-scan plan and use the anti-sickness medicines as prescribed.

The exact treatment being discussed

Who this covers

NCCP00776a: unresectable/metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer after at least one anti-HER2 regimen. NCCP00776b: unresectable/metastatic HER2-low breast cancer after chemotherapy for metastatic disease, or recurrence during or within six months after adjuvant chemotherapy.

NCCP 00776 v3b · official regimen · EMA Enhertu product information · safety and monotherapy scope reviewed 23 November 2026

Dose and administration

Usual starting dose 5.4 mg/kg intravenously on Day 1 every 21 days; prescribed reductions are 4.4 then 3.2 mg/kg. The first infusion takes 90 minutes. Later infusions may take 30 minutes if the previous infusion was tolerated. Allow extra time for assessment, anti-sickness medicines and any observation. A cannula or an existing central line can be used according to the clinical plan; there is no home pump.

Duration and decisions

Treatment continues while it controls the cancer and side effects remain acceptable. This advanced-disease regimen has no automatic one-year completion date. A good scan does not by itself instruct a treatment break.

Individual safety and source differences

Enhertu is different from trastuzumab and trastuzumab emtansine (Kadcyla); they are not interchangeable. Carry the supplied Enhertu lung-safety alert card. The team may pause treatment while investigating suspected lung inflammation; symptomatic drug-related interstitial lung disease (ILD) requires permanent discontinuation under the protocol.

Evidence for HER2-positive and HER2-low advanced breast cancer

This explains the study setting and its meaning. Ask the team how the evidence applies to you.

Randomised open-label trials enrolled different prior-treatment populations. The DESTINY-Breast03 2024 update reports investigator-assessed progression-free survival and must not be labelled as the original primary-endpoint analysis. In that HER2-positive trial, all-grade drug-related ILD/pneumonitis was 16.7% at the longer follow-up; this is not a pooled estimate for both trials. Population medians are not individual forecasts.

Emergency: 112 / 999. Severe chest pain, major breathing difficulty, collapse or stroke symptoms need emergency help.

Do not wait until the next infusion or scan. Also report fever or feeling acutely unwell, unusual bleeding, inability to keep fluids down, chest pain or new ankle swelling. Checks throughout treatment

Blood tests assess treatment safety. Scans assess disease or investigate symptoms. Echo and electrocardiogram (ECG) answer different questions.

Before treatment

Before the first dose: full blood count, kidney and liver tests, an echocardiogram or MUGA scan to measure heart pumping function, and a pregnancy test where relevant. The team checks the HER2 result, previous treatment, lung history and current breathing symptoms, and agrees how disease response will be assessed.

During treatment

Blood count, kidney and liver tests before each 21-day cycle. Review breathing, fatigue, nausea, intake and other symptoms at each visit. Repeat cardiac imaging every 12 weeks; assess new breathing symptoms immediately rather than waiting for the next planned scan.

Heart checks

NCCP specifies echo/MUGA before treatment and every 12 weeks. With uninterrupted three-week cycles, week 12 is approximately before cycle 5 and week 24 before cycle 9. Delays do not reset the elapsed-time monitoring clock. Signs of cardiac impairment can require 4–8-weekly checks; an abnormal result meeting the treatment-hold criteria requires repeat assessment within three weeks.

Bone health

Enhertu alone does not require a routine bone-density scan schedule. DEXA, bone medicines or bone scans are added for another treatment, fracture risk, symptoms or disease assessment when indicated.

Your wider treatment plan

Additional treatments depend on the confirmed setting and indication.

Advanced-disease scans

Response assessment in advanced breast cancer is generally every two to four months, adapted to disease dynamics, sites, symptoms and treatment. Less frequent monitoring may suit indolent disease. New symptoms or suspected progression need earlier assessment. The team chooses the imaging method; this is not an automatic three-month CT chest/abdomen/pelvis order.

Tumour markers

If informative, particularly when elevated at baseline or disease is not readily measurable, tumour markers may supplement clinical assessment and imaging. A marker rise alone should not trigger a treatment change. There is no universal fixed marker timetable.

Pregnancy, fertility and contraception

Where relevant, use effective contraception during treatment and for at least seven months after the last dose for women, or four months for men with a partner who could become pregnant. Do not breastfeed during treatment and for seven months after the last dose. Discuss fertility before starting.

Aprepitant and hormonal contraception

If you use hormonal contraception, aprepitant can make it less reliable. Use a suitable non-hormonal backup during aprepitant treatment and for two months after its final dose; your team or pharmacist can advise. The longer Enhertu precautions still apply. Ask the pharmacist to check all medicines for interactions.

Evidence depends on the population

Different studies answer different questions; do not compare their results as if populations and follow-up were identical.

DESTINY-Breast04: previously treated HER2-low advanced breast cancer

557 patients with HER2-low metastatic breast cancer after one or two previous chemotherapy lines; 494 had hormone-receptor-positive disease. T-DXd versus physician's-choice chemotherapy. Blinded-central-review PFS in the hormone-receptor-positive cohort; PFS and OS in all patients were key secondary endpoints. In the HR-positive cohort, median PFS was 10.1 versus 5.4 months (HR 0.51). In all patients, median PFS was 9.9 versus 5.1 months (HR 0.50) and median OS 23.4 versus 16.8 months (HR 0.64). These are the original 2022 analysis, also reproduced in EMA SmPC §5.1. The HR-negative subgroup was much smaller. The trial does not establish benefit for HER2 IHC0 tumours or every earlier treatment setting. Drug-related ILD/pneumonitis occurred, including fatal cases; benefit must be discussed alongside pulmonary risk.

Practical support

Prevent nausea before it starts

Enhertu has high emetic risk, so prevention covers treatment day and delayed sickness. The national plan uses aprepitant, ondansetron and dexamethasone, and lists metoclopramide for subsequent days. The prescriber specifies the metoclopramide days and duration and any separate breakthrough plan. Follow your issued prescription; the generated script is a review template.

Lung-safety plan

Carry the patient alert card and report new cough, fever or breathlessness promptly. Steroids used for suspected lung inflammation are prescribed and monitored by the clinical team; do not start them yourself from this information.

Blood-count support

Low blood counts can cause infection, anaemia or bleeding. The need for granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (white-cell growth support) (G-CSF) or transfusion support depends on results, symptoms and previous complications; it is not automatic for every cycle.

Eating and energy

Small frequent meals and early treatment of nausea can help maintain intake. Tell the team about weight loss, constipation or diarrhoea so that support can be tailored.

Indigestion support, if needed

A PPI or H2 blocker may be prescribed for indigestion or reflux. It is conditional, not automatic, and does not replace anti-sickness medicines. The prescriber selects its agent, dose and duration. Keep any such medicine already present in your local prescription.

Warning signs: know when to call

These priority warnings sit alongside the full anatomical map. You do not need to decide whether treatment caused a symptom before contacting the team.

New cough, breathlessness or fever

Contact oncology immediately, even if mild or after earlier well-tolerated cycles. Enhertu can cause lung inflammation (ILD or pneumonitis), which can be fatal. Do not wait for the next infusion or routine scan. Severe breathing difficulty: call 112 / 999.

Fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell

Low white blood cells can make infection serious. Call oncology now using your hospital’s temperature instructions, even if the temperature is normal but you feel acutely unwell. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.

New swelling, breathlessness or heart symptoms

Report new ankle swelling, breathlessness lying flat, unusual tiredness or a racing heart promptly. Keep the heart-scan appointments. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty or collapse: call 112 / 999.

Unusual bruising or bleeding

Report unexpected bruises, tiny red or purple spots, nosebleeds or bleeding gums. Low platelets can affect clotting. Heavy or persistent bleeding, vomiting blood or black stools needs urgent medical assessment.

Vomiting, diarrhoea or poor fluid intake

Call the team if vomiting continues despite your prescribed anti-sickness medicines, fluids will not stay down, diarrhoea is troublesome or urine is reduced. Report severe abdominal pain, blood in stools, dizziness or fainting urgently.

Carry the official Enhertu lung-safety alert card

Show it to every healthcare professional you see, including outside your usual hospital. Keep it with your SACTCheck treatment card.

Common effects and practical support

Nausea, vomiting and reduced appetite

Nausea can begin on treatment day or be delayed. Take the scheduled prevention medicines even when you feel well, following your issued prescription. Follow any additional breakthrough-sickness plan your team gives you. Small frequent meals and regular drinks may help; call early if intake is falling.

Tiredness and low red blood cells

Fatigue may affect usual activities. Pace yourself, balance rest with gentle activity and tell the team how it affects daily life. Anaemia may add breathlessness, dizziness or palpitations; new or worsening symptoms need review.

Low white cells and platelets

Blood tests can show a change before you notice symptoms. Infection may cause fever or shivering; low platelets may cause bruising or bleeding. Report the warning signs promptly. Do not use a normal temperature to dismiss feeling acutely unwell.

Constipation or diarrhoea

Treatment and anti-sickness medicines can alter bowel habit. Ask for a plan before constipation becomes uncomfortable. Record changes and keep drinking if able. Contact the team for persistent or troublesome symptoms, pain, bleeding or reduced urine.

Sore mouth and altered taste

A sore mouth, ulcers or taste changes can make eating difficult. Use the mouth-care advice from your team and a soft toothbrush. Ask for help early if pain, sores or swallowing problems limit food or fluids.

Hair thinning or loss

Hair thinning or loss can occur. Ask about scalp care, headwear and other practical support. Do not assume that the degree of hair loss tells you how well treatment is working.

Why these support medicines are used

Enhertu has high emetogenic risk: nausea and vomiting can be significant, so prevention is planned before treatment and for the following days. This risk category does not predict your personal chance of symptoms after prevention. The national Enhertu schedule uses medicines that act through different pathways.

These explanations follow the written support plan. Your issued prescription confirms the actual medicines, doses, days and any personal changes. Scheduled prevention and rescue treatment have different jobs.

Aprepitant

Blocks an NK1 nausea-signal pathway and helps prevent delayed sickness.

When: The written support plan gives it before the infusion on Day1 and on Days2–3. Follow your issued prescription.

If you use hormonal contraception, aprepitant can make it less reliable. Use a suitable non-hormonal backup during aprepitant treatment and for two months after its final dose; your team or pharmacist can advise. The longer Enhertu precautions still apply. Ask the pharmacist to check all medicines for interactions.

Ondansetron

Blocks a different sickness pathway (5-HT3/serotonin signalling), helping prevent early nausea and vomiting.

When: The written support plan includes a dose before treatment on Day1. Additional doses depend on your personal prescription.

It can cause constipation and headache. The team reviews heart-rhythm risks and interacting medicines.

Dexamethasone

This steroid strengthens nausea prevention and helps control delayed sickness. It is supportive treatment here.

When: The written support plan includes it before the infusion and on Days2–4. Take it with food and follow the timing on your prescription.

It may cause indigestion, poor sleep, mood changes or raised blood sugar. If you have diabetes, agree a glucose plan. Do not extend, double or change the course yourself. Steroids used to treat lung inflammation are a separate clinician-managed treatment.

Metoclopramide

Helps prevent or control sickness in the days after your infusion.

When: The national reference lists 10 mg by mouth three times daily on subsequent days. Your prescriber confirms the exact days, short-course duration and any personal changes. Use it only when needed if your own prescription specifically says so.

It can cause sleepiness, restlessness or abnormal movements. Seek advice about these effects, avoid driving if affected, and do not continue beyond the prescribed short course.

An acid-reducing medicine, if prescribed

A proton-pump inhibitor (PPI) or H2 blocker can help with indigestion or reflux. It does not replace anti-sickness prevention.

When: This is conditional support under national guidance. The team chooses the medicine, dose and duration if needed.

Keep any medicine already included in your local prescription. Check with the pharmacist before adding over-the-counter treatment.
