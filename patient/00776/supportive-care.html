Additional phase-specific support

  • This regimen has high emetic risk. An antiemetic combination is prescribed before every infusion, with an agreed plan for delayed symptoms and breakthrough nausea. The dedicated National Cancer Control Programme (NCCP) trastuzumab-deruxtecan pathway should be checked by the prescriber and pharmacist.
  • Carry the patient alert card and report new cough, fever or breathlessness promptly. Steroids used for suspected lung inflammation are prescribed and monitored by the clinical team; do not start them yourself from this information.
  • Low blood counts can cause infection, anaemia or bleeding. The need for granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (white-cell growth support) (G-CSF) or transfusion support depends on results, symptoms and previous complications; it is not automatic for every cycle.
  • Small frequent meals and early treatment of nausea can help maintain intake. Tell the team about weight loss, constipation or diarrhoea so that support can be tailored.
  • NCCP NCIS v8 Table 1 lists metoclopramide three times daily on subsequent days without PRN. The prescriber completes its exact days and duration. Olanzapine is not part of the dedicated trastuzumab-deruxtecan default row and requires an individual prescribing decision.
  • Consider a prescribed H2 blocker or proton-pump inhibitor (PPI) for dyspepsia, as permitted by NCCP NCIS v8 Table 1 footnote a. This is conditional support: choose the agent, dose and duration after review; preserve any applicable local prescription and do not infer an automatic order.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and prescribed support-medicine durations. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.
