HER2-directed antibody–drug conjugate

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (Enhertu)

Uses HER2 as a docking target to deliver a topoisomerase-I inhibitor payload
Antibody attaches to HER2↓Cancer cell takes the linked drug inside↓Released payload damages DNA during cell division

Enhertu links an antibody that recognises HER2 with deruxtecan, a cell-killing medicine. After uptake into the cell, the payload inhibits topoisomerase I and interferes with DNA replication. The released payload can also affect nearby tumour cells. This targeting does not spare every healthy cell, which is why blood counts, nausea, lungs and heart function still need attention.

New cough, breathlessness or fever needs urgent contact even between cycles. Keep the 12-weekly heart-scan plan and use the anti-sickness medicines as prescribed.

What each check is for

1Blood testsBefore the first dose: full blood count, kidney and liver tests, an echocardiogram or MUGA scan to measure heart pumping function, and a pregnancy test where relevant. The team checks the HER2 result, previous treatment, lung history and current breathing symptoms, and agrees how disease response will be assessed. Blood count, kidney and liver tests before each 21-day cycle. Review breathing, fatigue, nausea, intake and other symptoms at each visit. Repeat cardiac imaging every 12 weeks; assess new breathing symptoms immediately rather than waiting for the next planned scan.

Checks safety before continuation.

Ask / act: Agree the due window, booking team and result reviewer. Contact the team if a test is overdue.

2Heart checksNCCP specifies echo/MUGA before treatment and every 12 weeks. With uninterrupted three-week cycles, week 12 is approximately before cycle 5 and week 24 before cycle 9. Delays do not reset the elapsed-time monitoring clock. Signs of cardiac impairment can require 4–8-weekly checks; an abnormal result meeting the treatment-hold criteria requires repeat assessment within three weeks.

Checks cardiac safety when the agent or clinical risk requires it.

Support throughout treatment

Prevent nausea before it starts

This regimen has high emetic risk. An antiemetic combination is prescribed before every infusion, with an agreed plan for delayed symptoms and breakthrough nausea. The dedicated NCCP trastuzumab-deruxtecan pathway should be checked by the prescriber and pharmacist.

Lung-safety plan

Carry the patient alert card and report new cough, fever or breathlessness promptly. Steroids used for suspected lung inflammation are prescribed and monitored by the clinical team; do not start them yourself from this information.

Blood-count support

Low blood counts can cause infection, anaemia or bleeding. The need for G-CSF or transfusion support depends on results, symptoms and previous complications; it is not automatic for every cycle.

Eating and energy

Small frequent meals and early treatment of nausea can help maintain intake. Tell the team about weight loss, constipation or diarrhoea so that support can be tailored.
