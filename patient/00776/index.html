My overall treatment

Warning signs: know when to call

These priority warnings sit alongside the full anatomical map. You do not need to decide whether treatment caused a symptom before contacting the team.

New cough, breathlessness or fever

Contact oncology immediately, even if mild or after earlier well-tolerated cycles. Enhertu can cause lung inflammation (ILD or pneumonitis), which can be fatal. Do not wait for the next infusion or routine scan. Severe breathing difficulty: call 112 / 999.

Fever, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell

Low white blood cells can make infection serious. Call oncology now using your hospital’s temperature instructions, even if the temperature is normal but you feel acutely unwell. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment.

New swelling, breathlessness or heart symptoms

Report new ankle swelling, breathlessness lying flat, unusual tiredness or a racing heart promptly. Keep the heart-scan appointments. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty or collapse: call 112 / 999.

Unusual bruising or bleeding

Report unexpected bruises, tiny red or purple spots, nosebleeds or bleeding gums. Low platelets can affect clotting. Heavy or persistent bleeding, vomiting blood or black stools needs urgent medical assessment.

Vomiting, diarrhoea or poor fluid intake

Call the team if vomiting continues despite your prescribed anti-sickness medicines, fluids will not stay down, diarrhoea is troublesome or urine is reduced. Report severe abdominal pain, blood in stools, dizziness or fainting urgently.

Carry the official Enhertu lung-safety alert card

Show it to every healthcare professional you see, including outside your usual hospital. Keep it with your SACTCheck treatment card.

Common effects and practical support

Nausea, vomiting and reduced appetite

Nausea can begin on treatment day or be delayed. Take the scheduled prevention medicines even when you feel well, following your issued prescription. Follow any additional breakthrough-sickness plan your team gives you. Small frequent meals and regular drinks may help; call early if intake is falling.

Tiredness and low red blood cells

Fatigue may affect usual activities. Pace yourself, balance rest with gentle activity and tell the team how it affects daily life. Anaemia may add breathlessness, dizziness or palpitations; new or worsening symptoms need review.

Low white cells and platelets

Blood tests can show a change before you notice symptoms. Infection may cause fever or shivering; low platelets may cause bruising or bleeding. Report the warning signs promptly. Do not use a normal temperature to dismiss feeling acutely unwell.

Constipation or diarrhoea

Treatment and anti-sickness medicines can alter bowel habit. Ask for a plan before constipation becomes uncomfortable. Record changes and keep drinking if able. Contact the team for persistent or troublesome symptoms, pain, bleeding or reduced urine.

Sore mouth and altered taste

A sore mouth, ulcers or taste changes can make eating difficult. Use the mouth-care advice from your team and a soft toothbrush. Ask for help early if pain, sores or swallowing problems limit food or fluids.

Hair thinning or loss

Hair thinning or loss can occur. Ask about scalp care, headwear and other practical support. Do not assume that the degree of hair loss tells you how well treatment is working.

Why these support medicines are used

Enhertu has high emetogenic risk: nausea and vomiting can be significant, so prevention is planned before treatment and for the following days. This risk category does not predict your personal chance of symptoms after prevention. The national Enhertu schedule uses medicines that act through different pathways.

These explanations follow the written support plan. Your issued prescription confirms the actual medicines, doses, days and any personal changes. Scheduled prevention and rescue treatment have different jobs.

Aprepitant

Blocks an NK1 nausea-signal pathway and helps prevent delayed sickness.

When: The written support plan gives it before the infusion on Day1 and on Days2–3. Follow your issued prescription.

If you use hormonal contraception, aprepitant can make it less reliable. Use a suitable non-hormonal backup during aprepitant treatment and for two months after its final dose; your team or pharmacist can advise. The longer Enhertu precautions still apply. Ask the pharmacist to check all medicines for interactions.

Ondansetron

Blocks a different sickness pathway (5-HT3/serotonin signalling), helping prevent early nausea and vomiting.

When: The written support plan includes a dose before treatment on Day1. Additional doses depend on your personal prescription.

It can cause constipation and headache. The team reviews heart-rhythm risks and interacting medicines.

Dexamethasone

This steroid strengthens nausea prevention and helps control delayed sickness. It is supportive treatment here.

When: The written support plan includes it before the infusion and on Days2–4. Take it with food and follow the timing on your prescription.

It may cause indigestion, poor sleep, mood changes or raised blood sugar. If you have diabetes, agree a glucose plan. Do not extend, double or change the course yourself. Steroids used to treat lung inflammation are a separate clinician-managed treatment.

Metoclopramide

Helps prevent or control sickness in the days after your infusion.

When: The national reference lists 10 mg by mouth three times daily on subsequent days. Your prescriber confirms the exact days, short-course duration and any personal changes. Use it only when needed if your own prescription specifically says so.

It can cause sleepiness, restlessness or abnormal movements. Seek advice about these effects, avoid driving if affected, and do not continue beyond the prescribed short course.

An acid-reducing medicine, if prescribed

A proton-pump inhibitor (PPI) or H2 blocker can help with indigestion or reflux. It does not replace anti-sickness prevention.

When: This is conditional support under national guidance. The team chooses the medicine, dose and duration if needed.

Keep any medicine already included in your local prescription. Check with the pharmacist before adding over-the-counter treatment.

Medicines and the plan at home

Bring your full medicine list, including over-the-counter treatments, supplements and contraception. Ask the oncology pharmacist before adding or stopping anything. Discuss fertility, pregnancy precautions and breastfeeding before treatment. Keep symptom notes and bring your passport to review; urgent symptoms need a call immediately.
