When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new breathing symptoms, severe diarrhoea, inability to drink, painful swollen limbs, unusual bleeding, or worsening numbness. Tell staff immediately about infusion-site pain or an allergic reaction.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

How the medicines work

The aim and duration depend on the primary cancer and exact NCCP indication. Confirm the cancer-specific plan before treatment; this shared medicine schedule does not replace that discussion.

Taxane chemotherapy

Docetaxel

Disrupts the cell’s division machinery

Docetaxel stabilises tiny structures called microtubules. Cells need these to separate their chromosomes during division; disrupting the process can stop cancer cells multiplying. Healthy dividing cells are also affected.

Microtubules stabilised↓Division disrupted↓Cell growth affected

What to watch for
Take prescribed steroid premedication. Report fever, lasting numbness, swelling, sore nails or infusion symptoms.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Docetaxel 75 mg/m² by IV infusion on Day 1 every 21 days. Duration depends on the cancer and treatment aim: NSCLC has a source maximum of six cycles; other indications require an explicitly agreed course, including prostate treatment. This differs from the 100 mg/m² card.

STEP 1

Before the first visit

Blood tests, relevant cardiac assessment, medicines review, fertility discussion and the consent conversation come first.

STEP 2

Plan venous access

A cannula, PICC or implanted port may be used. If a port is chosen, a trained team inserts it in a separate procedure with local anaesthetic and sometimes sedation; the team explains fasting, blood-thinner and wound instructions.

STEP 3

Infusion day

Staff check the line, give prescribed premedicines and treatment, and observe for reactions. Tell them immediately about burning, pain, rash or breathlessness.

STEP 4

Home and next review

This regimen does not routinely need a take-home infusion pump. Staff disconnect the infusion; a port is de-accessed when appropriate. Keep line-care and next-visit instructions.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Visit time and transport

First visits can take longer because of loading doses, premedicines and observation. Ask the unit about expected time, transport and any escort needed after sedation or line insertion.

Phase-specific visit pattern

Infusion and observation times differ by drug and first versus later doses; the unit confirms the full visit duration, including blood review and preparation.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

The correct phase prescription

Taxanes use low-risk antiemetic support; hypersensitivity premedication is a separate requirement.

Premedicines and growth factors

Docetaxel dexamethasone is taken for three days starting the day before treatment. Ask the team to reconcile this with antiemetic steroids.

Tests and appointments

The team should name the booking owner, due window and person reviewing each required test. Tell them if an expected appointment has not arrived.

After chemotherapy

Discuss recovery, fertility, rehabilitation and the next treatment or surveillance step.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What changes between phases?

Docetaxel 75 mg/m² by IV infusion on Day 1 every 21 days. Duration depends on the cancer and treatment aim: NSCLC has a source maximum of six cycles; other indications require an explicitly agreed course, including prostate treatment. This differs from the 100 mg/m² card.

When are heart checks due?

No routine fixed-cycle echo or ECG is mandated for this regimen alone; assess symptoms and individual cardiac risk. Test timing follows the treatment, clinical findings and local pathway.

Where do surgery and radiotherapy fit?

Surgery and radiation follow your actual cancer-specific treatment plan. This shared taxane card does not impose an adjuvant breast sequence.

Do I need routine CT scans or tumour markers?

Agree response imaging and any tumour markers for the actual primary cancer, disease sites and treatment aim. The metastatic breast 2–4-month example is not a universal interval for lung, prostate, gastric, ovarian or bladder cancer.

Does a port mean a pump at home?

No. This regimen has no routine take-home infusion pump. A port is an access device; the team removes the needle after the visit when appropriate and arranges device removal separately.

Try explaining the plan back: Which phase are you in, what changes next, and who is arranging and reviewing your next blood test, echo or scan?
