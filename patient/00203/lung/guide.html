Docetaxel alone for previously treated non-small-cell lung cancer

To help control previously treated non-small-cell lung cancer and relieve symptoms.

Lung · previously treated advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) · NCCP 00203 v7 · Document revised 4 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

My overall treatment

Docetaxel by drip on Day 1, repeated every 21 days, for up to six cycles. Treatment may stop earlier if it is not helping or side effects are unacceptable. Your team confirms the prescribed course.

Between treatments

  • Day 2: finish prescribed steroid. Between visits, watch for infection, bowel changes, numbness or swelling; timing varies.
  • Before another cycle: blood tests and symptom review. Delays or early stopping may be needed.

Take the prescribed steroid from the day before each infusion. Tell the team if any doses were missed. Bring your medicine list and report symptoms since the last visit. Ask when your next blood tests and cancer review will be.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
