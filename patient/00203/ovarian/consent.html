Treatment and purpose

Docetaxel can slow the growth of epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer that has returned or is progressing. Your team reviews symptoms and scans with you to decide whether to continue. 00203e primary peritoneal, 00203f epithelial ovarian and 00203g fallopian tube cancer after relapse or progression. These uses are outside the Irish product licence.

Treatment schedule. One docetaxel infusion over one hour on day 1 of a 21-day cycle. Treatment continues while it is helping and side effects remain manageable.

Treatment discussion and decision

Docetaxel every three weeks for recurrent ovarian, tubal or peritoneal cancer

Benefits, alternatives and individual risks

Some participants with previously treated ovarian cancer had tumour shrinkage with docetaxel. This was a small, single-arm study. It cannot show a survival advantage or establish the best dose or schedule for you.

The prescriber will discuss the medicines on your individual plan. Discuss the possibility of persistent nerve symptoms, infection, hair loss and a severe infusion reaction.

Discuss other treatment options, symptom-focused care, fertility, contraception and the effects that matter most to you. You can ask for more time and change your decision.

Options discussed, personal risks and questions

Urgent contact Call the 24-hour oncology number immediately for fever, chills, bleeding or feeling suddenly unwell. Tell the nurse immediately about flushing, breathlessness, dizziness, throat tightness or pain at the infusion site. Emergency services: 112 or 999.
