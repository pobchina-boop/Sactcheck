My schedule and checks

Know the plan before your next visit.

Durvalumab 10 mg/kg every 14 days

Durvalumab is given after definitive chemoradiotherapy to reduce the chance of lung cancer returning or worsening and improve the chance of longer survival. This is part of a treatment pathway that aims for long-term control and possible cure; it cannot guarantee cure.

This shows treatment timing, not guaranteed symptom-free days. Your team confirms the active phase and dates.

Durvalumab is infused on Day 1 of each 14-day cycle. The course lasts up to 12 months (26 cycles), and can finish earlier if the cancer progresses or side effects make continuation unsuitable. The first dose should start within 42 days after chemoradiotherapy finishes.

Tests and checks

Before starting: blood count, kidney and liver tests, blood sugar, thyroid function, hepatitis B/C screening and the tumour PD-L1 result. Blood count, kidney/liver tests, blood sugar and thyroid tests continue regularly during treatment. The team sets the timing of blood tests and scans and adds tests when symptoms or results need checking.

Between treatment visits

This example shows one 14-day durvalumab cycle after concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CRT) has finished. It is not a dated appointment calendar; the actual prescription and clinical decisions determine visits.

Use the current prescription and your oncology team’s emergency plan. This educational guide does not determine whether a dose can be given.

Selected effects, not every possible effect or a prediction. Blood-count, skin and line markers are schematic. Number and text carry meaning; colour is decorative.
Contact the oncology team promptly for new or worsening cough, breathlessness or chest pain; diarrhoea, blood in stools or significant abdominal pain; yellow skin/eyes or dark urine; reduced urine; a spreading or blistering rash; unusual weakness, severe headache, dizziness, marked thirst or passing much more urine. Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, confusion, seizures, rapidly worsening weakness or difficulty swallowing need emergency assessment. Do not wait for the next infusion.
