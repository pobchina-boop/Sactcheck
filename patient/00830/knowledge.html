Understand your treatment

How this hormone treatment works

This is androgen deprivation therapy (ADT): it lowers testosterone to help control prostate cancer. With radiotherapy, the aim may be cure; in advanced disease, it is part of ongoing cancer control.

GnRH receptor antagonist

Relugolix

Orgovyx
Blocks the signal that makes testosterone

Relugolix blocks gonadotrophin-releasing hormone (GnRH) receptors in the pituitary gland. This lowers the signal to the testes and reduces testosterone without the initial surge seen with GnRH agonist injections. It does not normally need an anti-androgen solely to prevent a starting flare.

GnRH receptor blocked↓Signal to testes reduced↓Testosterone falls

What to watch for
If treatment is interrupted for more than 7 days, contact the team before restarting: a new loading dose is normally needed. Some interacting medicines require a different dose or separation by at least 6 hours. Do not change the dose yourself; ask pharmacy to check every new medicine.

Read the whole review window as text

Day 1: First day: loading dose

Take 360 mg only as prescribed when starting or when your team directs a reload. Confirm the dose and interactions.

Day 2–28: Daily maintenance tablets

Usual dose 120 mg daily, including weekends. Continue beyond this review window. Swallow tablets whole, with or without food, at about the same time daily. If no more than 12 hours late, take the missed dose when remembered; if more than 12 hours late, skip it and resume the next day. Never double the dose.

Practical treatment planning

Agree the arrangements with your oncology team.

STEP 1

Before starting

Full blood count, kidney and liver tests, blood glucose and a bone blood profile. Review heart disease, blood pressure, diabetes, mood, falls and fracture risk, sexual health, medicines and the treatment goal. An ECG (heart tracing) is also a baseline NCCP test.

STEP 2

Taking or receiving treatment

Swallow tablets whole, with or without food, at about the same time daily. If no more than 12 hours late, take the missed dose when remembered; if more than 12 hours late, skip it and resume the next day. Never double the dose.

STEP 3

Tests and reviews

Repeat blood count, kidney and liver tests and blood glucose as arranged. Bone blood tests and ECG are repeated when clinically indicated. PSA, testosterone and scans follow the individual cancer plan.

STEP 4

Specific instructions

If treatment is interrupted for more than 7 days, contact the team before restarting: a new loading dose is normally needed. Some interacting medicines require a different dose or separation by at least 6 hours. Do not change the dose yourself; ask pharmacy to check every new medicine.

STEP 5

Leaving with a clear plan

Confirm the exact next dose or appointment, duration and verified daytime and out-of-hours contacts.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Sickness support

This treatment has minimal sickness risk. Routine preventive anti-sickness medicines are not usually required; agree a plan if symptoms develop.

Bone and muscle health

Discuss fracture risk, bone-density testing when indicated, calcium intake, vitamin D and suitable resistance and aerobic exercise. Supplements and bone medicines are individual decisions.

Sexual health, mood and hot flushes

Tell the team which effects matter most. Support is available for sexual function, sleep, mood and vasomotor symptoms.

Treatment-specific instructions

If treatment is interrupted for more than 7 days, contact the team before restarting: a new loading dose is normally needed. Some interacting medicines require a different dose or separation by at least 6 hours. Do not change the dose yourself; ask pharmacy to check every new medicine.

Medicine review

Bring all prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines and supplements. Ask pharmacy before starting or stopping anything.

Urgent help

Call your team promptly for new or worsening symptoms, severe low mood or problems taking treatment. New leg weakness, loss of bladder or bowel control, severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure needs emergency assessment: call 112/999.

Treatment reference · 04

Treatment goals and choices

Your goal

This is androgen deprivation therapy (ADT): it lowers testosterone to help control prostate cancer. With radiotherapy, the aim may be cure; in advanced disease, it is part of ongoing cancer control.

Sources: nccp · smPC

Additional treatment

In metastatic hormone-sensitive cancer, many suitable patients benefit from an additional androgen-pathway medicine, and sometimes chemotherapy. Radiotherapy can have a different role in localised disease, selected metastatic disease or symptom relief. Your specialist explains the options for your setting.

Sources: nccp · smPC

Deciding together

Discuss expected benefit, uncertainty, side effects, alternatives including surgical castration where appropriate, and the option of declining treatment. No one duration fits every setting.

Sources: nccp · smPC

What the evidence can tell you

Trials support the discussion but do not predict an individual outcome.

In the HERO trial, sustained testosterone suppression from Day 29 through Week 48 occurred in 96.7% receiving relugolix and 88.8% receiving leuprorelin. This was a hormone-suppression endpoint, not a comparison proving longer survival. The trial does not establish that this is the best treatment for every person.

Prepare for the conversation

Questions worth asking

What is the aim and how long is this planned for?

This is androgen deprivation therapy (ADT): it lowers testosterone to help control prostate cancer. With radiotherapy, the aim may be cure; in advanced disease, it is part of ongoing cancer control.

Do I need another cancer medicine or radiotherapy alongside this?

Ask how this hormone treatment fits the plan for your disease. Additional hormone-pathway medicines, chemotherapy and radiotherapy have different roles; they are not automatically needed in every setting.

When is my next dose and what if I miss it?

If treatment is interrupted for more than 7 days, contact the team before restarting: a new loading dose is normally needed. Some interacting medicines require a different dose or separation by at least 6 hours. Do not change the dose yourself; ask pharmacy to check every new medicine.

How can we protect bone, heart, sexual and mental health?

Ask for a plan covering exercise, bone and fracture risk, blood pressure and glucose, sexual symptoms, mood and sleep. Tell the team about changes early so support can be offered.

Who can I contact urgently?

Use the verified daytime and out-of-hours numbers supplied by your treating hospital. For severe breathing difficulty, collapse, a seizure or severe chest pain, call 112 or 999. Do not use the example numbers in this draft.

Try explaining the plan back: Explain your starting dose and usual schedule. Explain what to do after a missed dose or appointment. Name your next tests and your urgent contact route.
