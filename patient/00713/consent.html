1. Treatment and purpose

Nivolumab, ipilimumab, pemetrexed and carboplatin · NCCP 00713 v4

The first cycle lasts 42 days: all four medicines on Day 1, then nivolumab, pemetrexed and carboplatin on Day 22. Chemotherapy ends after those two visits. From Cycle 2, nivolumab continues on Days 1 and 22 and ipilimumab on Day 1 of each 42-day cycle. Nivolumab every 21 days; ipilimumab every 42 days. No further chemotherapy. Immune treatment continues while appropriate until the cancer progresses, side effects require stopping, or up to 24 months without progression. Plans can change after review.

Review the treatment journey, tests and phase transitions with the patient.

2. Risks and supportive care

Nivolumab · Immunotherapy

New breathing, bowel, skin, hormone or other symptoms need prompt advice during or after treatment.

Ipilimumab · Immunotherapy

Report diarrhoea, tummy pain, rash, severe tiredness, headache or other new symptoms promptly. Keep your immune alert card.

Pemetrexed · Chemotherapy

Low blood counts, sore mouth, rash, sickness and kidney problems can occur.

Carboplatin · Chemotherapy

Report fever, bleeding, nausea, hearing changes or numbness. Tell the nurse immediately about possible allergy during the infusion.

These are selected discussion prompts, not a complete risk list. Discuss serious, potentially life-threatening and lasting effects; tailor the conversation to comorbidities and the patient’s priorities. Discuss treatment aims, alternatives, expected benefit and uncertainty, short-term and lasting harms, and effects on fertility, contraception, pregnancy, work and daily life. Your priorities and questions belong in the decision.
