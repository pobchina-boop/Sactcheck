Additional phase-specific support

  • Docetaxel 75 mg/m² and cisplatin 100 mg/m² on Day 1, with fluorouracil 1,000 mg/m²/day continuously over Days 1–4. Repeat every 21 days for 3 cycles unless progression or unacceptable toxicity develops.
  • granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (white-cell growth support) (G-CSF) may be used. After complicated neutropenia, the source recommends prophylactic coverage in subsequent cycles (example Days 6–15); select product, dose and timing under the actual prescription. This is not the all-cycle mandatory wording of 00315.
  • Docetaxel premedication is dexamethasone 8 mg orally twice daily for 3 days starting the day before docetaxel; at least 3 doses should precede treatment unless contraindicated. If oral premedication was missed, the source permits dexamethasone 20 mg intravenous (IV) at consultant discretion. This is not automatic permission to proceed. Reconcile the total steroid with antiemetic dexamethasone; do not add both schedules blindly.
  • Prescribe cisplatin pre- and post-hydration and electrolytes using current renal/cardiac status and local policy. The national source suggests saline/magnesium with optional potassium, but a reference list is not an individual fluid prescription. Monitor intake, urine output, renal function and electrolytes.
  • Document dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (a medicine-processing enzyme) (DPD) phenotype and/or genotype before first fluorouracil exposure unless previously tested. Complete deficiency precludes fluorouracil; partial deficiency needs an individual starting-dose plan. Renal impairment can elevate uracil and misclassify phenotype; consider genotype. A normal result does not exclude all severe toxicity.
  • Docetaxel premedication is dexamethasone 8 mg orally twice daily for 3 days starting the day before docetaxel; at least 3 doses should precede treatment unless contraindicated. If oral premedication was missed, the source permits dexamethasone 20 mg IV at consultant discretion. This is not automatic permission to proceed. Reconcile the total steroid with antiemetic dexamethasone; do not add both schedules blindly.
  • Arrange dental/oral assessment, mouth care, dietetic and swallowing support according to need. Review weight, oral intake, pain and hydration throughout chemotherapy and radiotherapy; no automatic feeding-tube decision is generated.
  • Preserve the CUH high/moderate and docetaxel proforma adjuncts where applicable: omeprazole, local nystatin/chlorhexidine mouth care, breakthrough antiemetics and loperamide. These are historical local reference items requiring current-policy and patient-specific review, not universal prophylaxis or an additional duplicate prescription.

Before signing: verify allergies, current medicines, interactions, renal / hepatic function, QT risk, sedation, prior response, total steroid exposure and the rescue-medicine duration. Do not duplicate medicines from another phase.

Additional phase-specific support

  • If there is no progressive disease and marrow function is adequate, the source describes weekly carboplatin AUC 1.5 with radiotherapy for 7 weeks, followed by consideration of surgery. NCCP 00323 says start 3–8 weeks (Days 22–56) after the START of induction cycle 3. Its carboplatin cross-reference says 00322, whereas the matching current regimen is 00332. The 00332 indication text says after COMPLETION of induction, while its treatment paragraph says after START of cycle 3. Resolve the date origin and correct route with the treating team before setting patient dates.
  • Confirm the separate 00332 prescription; no induction docetaxel/cisplatin/fluorouracil, granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (white-cell growth support) (G-CSF) or hydration automatically carries forward.
  • NCCP gives no routine G-CSF or additional supportive-care prescription for this weekly phase. Do not carry over induction G-CSF, docetaxel premedication or cisplatin hydration automatically.
  • Arrange dental/oral assessment, mouth care, dietetic and swallowing support according to need. Review weight, oral intake, pain and hydration throughout chemotherapy and radiotherapy; no automatic feeding-tube decision is generated.

These are historical local reference items requiring current-policy and patient-specific review, not universal prophylaxis or an additional duplicate prescription.
