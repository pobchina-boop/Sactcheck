TCF induction followed by carboplatin and radiotherapy

This chemotherapy is given before definitive treatment to shrink or control the head and neck cancer. The team explains how induction fits your overall treatment aim and why this particular schedule is appropriate.

Head and neck cancer: induction chemotherapy · NCCP 00323 v7b · Document revised 10 November 2026

Your personal plan: this is the standard regimen schedule. Your team confirms your prescribed doses, cycles, treatment changes and appointments in your written plan.

This guide covers NCCP 00323 induction and explains the next treatment stage. Doses and dates are confirmed by your team. Later treatment needs its own agreed prescription; do not carry induction medicines or support instructions into it automatically.

My overall treatment

Every 21 days for 3 planned induction cycles.

Between treatments

  • Confirm whether your prescription uses one continuous 96-hour fluorouracil infusion or four daily 22-hour infusions; the unit writes down connection, change and disconnection times.
  • Report fever or sudden illness promptly. Follow the written support plan and do not wait for the next visit if symptoms worsen.

Bring your medicine list, appointment calendar and verified urgent contacts. Check steroid and growth-factor instructions.

When to get help

Call 112 / 999 for severe breathing difficulty, severe chest pain, collapse or a seizure.

Call oncology immediately: temperature 37.5°C or above, 36°C or below, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell, even with a normal temperature. Follow your hospital’s temperature instructions. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Do not wait for the next appointment.

What to watch for

You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

A picture of side effects

Match each symptom box to its numbered body location. Tell your team about new symptoms.

1Hair and scalp
Docetaxel

Hair thinning or loss can occur. Ask about scalp care and practical support.

3Mouth and throat
Docetaxel + 5-FU

Soreness or ulcers (mucositis) can make eating and drinking difficult. Contact the team for pain or poor intake.

5Blood and infection
Docetaxel + cisplatin + 5-FU

Low white cells can cause serious infection; low platelets cause bruising or bleeding; anaemia causes fatigue. Call for fever or sudden illness.

7Stomach and bowel
All three chemotherapy drugs

Nausea and vomiting can occur; 5-FU may cause severe diarrhoea. Persistent symptoms can cause dehydration and need prompt help.

9Hands, feet and nerves
Docetaxel + cisplatin

Numbness or tingling (peripheral neuropathy) can persist. Report difficulty with buttons, walking or balance before another dose.

12345678910
2Ears and hearing
Cisplatin

Ringing or hearing loss can persist (ototoxicity). Report a change before the next dose.

4Heart and chest
5-FU

Chest pain can signal a heart effect (cardiotoxicity). Seek urgent assessment during or after the infusion.

6Kidneys and fluids
Cisplatin

Kidney injury and mineral changes can occur. Prescribed fluids and blood tests help protect you; report reduced urine or inability to drink.

8Skin, nails, hands and feet
Docetaxel + 5-FU

Docetaxel may affect nails and retain fluid; 5-FU may cause painful palms or soles (hand–foot syndrome). Report swelling, pain or skin breakdown.

10Later radiation area
Radiotherapy: later phase

Dry mouth, taste change, sore skin and swallowing difficulty can build up. Some dental, saliva and swallowing effects last; follow the radiation care plan.

Selected effects, not every possible effect or a prediction. Blood-count, skin and line markers are schematic. Number and text carry meaning; colour is decorative.

Call oncology immediately for fever, shivering, sudden illness, unusual bleeding, persistent vomiting, severe diarrhoea, severe mouth pain or inability to drink. Chest pain during or after fluorouracil, sudden confusion, or unexpectedly early severe symptoms needs urgent assessment even if DPD testing was normal. Report reduced urine, hearing change, ringing in the ears or new numbness promptly. Severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, collapse or a seizure is an emergency: call 112 or 999.

Keep the record accurate

The team dates and initials each recorded treatment unit when complete. A cycle is complete after its final planned dose; a weekly-treatment stamp records that weekly dose. Delays, omitted doses or a changed plan need a note or a replacement card. A stamp does not approve the next treatment. Keep any medicine-specific alert card too.
