Treatment context
This chemotherapy is given before definitive treatment to shrink or control the head and neck cancer. The team explains how induction fits your overall treatment aim and why this particular schedule is appropriate.
Schedule
Docetaxel 75 mg/m² and cisplatin 75 mg/m² are given on Day 1. Fluorouracil 1,000 mg/m² per day runs continuously over 96 hours on Days 1–4; the source also allows a separate 22-hour infusion on each of those four days. Induction repeats every 21 days for 3 planned cycles unless cancer progression or unacceptable side effects require a change.

2. Regimen / agent-specific material risks

Docetaxel

Taxane chemotherapy
Low blood counts, infection and sepsis
Hair loss, mouth soreness and fatigue
Numbness / tingling; sometimes lasting
Fluid retention and nail changes
Allergic infusion reaction

Cisplatin

Platinum chemotherapy
Low blood counts and serious infection
Nausea and vomiting
Kidney injury and mineral changes
Hearing loss or tinnitus, which can persist
Nerve damage, which can persist
Infusion reaction and effects on fertility

Fluorouracil

Antimetabolite chemotherapy
Low blood counts and serious infection
Mouth and throat soreness
Diarrhoea and dehydration
Painful palms or soles
Heart problems including chest pain
Rare severe neurological or early excessive toxicity

Discuss the individual aim, expected benefit, alternatives and uncertainty, including the role of induction before definitive treatment. Later surgery, radiation or chemoradiation needs its own consent discussion.
