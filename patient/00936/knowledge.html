When to get help

Emergency: call 112 / 999

Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, seizure or severe chest pain: call emergency services. Do not wait for an online answer.

Contact your oncology service immediately

Temperature 37.5°C or higher, 36°C or lower, shivering or suddenly feeling unwell — even with a normal temperature. Report new breathlessness, jaundice, marked bleeding, severe diarrhoea, inability to drink, chest symptoms or a severe rash promptly.

Follow the urgent contact instructions from your treating hospital. If you cannot reach the team, seek urgent medical assessment. Outside clinic hours, use your instructed out-of-hours pathway or the emergency department.

This page does not diagnose symptoms or decide whether it is safe to stay at home. You do not need to know which medicine caused a symptom before calling.

Understand your treatment

Your medicines and their different roles

The three medicines work together to control previously treated HER2-positive advanced breast cancer. Treatment is reviewed regularly for benefit, symptoms and side effects.

HER2 tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Tucatinib

Tukysa · oral targeted treatment
Blocks HER2 signals inside cells.

Tucatinib reduces signalling from the internal part of HER2. It works alongside trastuzumab and capecitabine, which act in different ways. Its tablet schedule continues through the capecitabine break.

Reach inside the cell↓Block HER2 signalling↓Reduce growth signals

What to watch for
Report diarrhoea early, especially with poor intake. Liver tests and a full medicines review are important.

Sources: nccp · tukysa

Oral chemotherapy

Capecitabine

Xeloda
Becomes fluorouracil in the body

The body converts capecitabine into fluorouracil, which disrupts DNA and RNA processes needed for cell growth. Tablets are active chemotherapy; taking them at home still requires a clear monitoring and interruption plan.

Swallowed tablet↓Converted to fluorouracil↓Cell growth disrupted

What to watch for
Stop tablets and contact the team for significant diarrhoea, painful mouth ulcers, hand-foot soreness affecting activities, fever or dehydration. Chest pain needs urgent assessment. Do not restart until instructed.

Sources: nccp · xeloda

HER2-targeted antibody

Trastuzumab

Targets HER2 growth signals

Trastuzumab binds to the HER2 protein on cells and helps reduce HER2-driven growth and recruit immune activity. HER2 testing identifies the cancers for which this approach is used.

HER2 on cell surface↓Antibody binds↓Growth signalling reduced

What to watch for
Attend planned echoes. Report breathlessness, ankle swelling, rapid weight gain or infusion symptoms.

Sources: nccp · herceptin

Repeated 21-day cycles

The capecitabine break does not mean a tucatinib break. Your team may hold or adjust one medicine without applying the same change to all three. Trastuzumab route is agreed separately; the fixed SC dose must not be treated as an IV loading or maintenance dose.

The order depends on the indication and your recovery. The whole-treatment map shows how local treatment and ongoing medicines relate to each other.

Practical treatment guide

Your practical treatment plan

Tucatinib twice daily every day; capecitabine twice daily on Days 1–14 then off on Days 15–21; trastuzumab every 21 days by IV infusion or a locally approved under-the-skin injection. Continue while beneficial and tolerated, with clinician-approved changes.

STEP 1

Before dispensing

Confirm blood tests, interactions, the exact tablet strengths and the plan for missed doses.

STEP 2

Taking tablets

Tucatinib twice daily every day; capecitabine twice daily on Days 1–14 then off on Days 15–21; trastuzumab every 21 days by the agreed IV or locally approved SC route. Continue while beneficial and tolerated, with clinician-approved changes.

STEP 3

Review before restarting

The team confirms blood results, side effects and the next prescription. Never double a missed dose or restart held treatment without advice.

STEP 4

Trastuzumab visit

Trastuzumab every three weeks with staff observation. IV treatment may use a cannula, PICC or port; the locally approved SC alternative uses an injection under the skin; no routine take-home infusion pump.

STEP 5

Confirm the trastuzumab route

Your team confirms IV infusion or the locally approved under-the-skin injection. The visit length, observation and missed-dose instructions differ; ask before switching. The injection has no loading dose.

Missed or vomited dose

Do not take an extra dose. Follow the medicine-specific instructions and ask the team if uncertain.

Safe handling

Swallow tablets whole, keep them in their original packaging away from children, and return unused tablets to pharmacy.

Practical arrangements

Confirm who supplies the tablets, who arranges tests, who reviews results and your urgent contact route.

Port insertion and removal

The line team explains placement, dressing care and when normal activities can resume. A port is accessed with a special needle during treatment. Removal of the implanted device is a separate planned procedure when no longer needed; do not remove or adjust it yourself.

PICC care and urgent line symptoms

A PICC usually needs regular dressing and flushing appointments even between treatments. Fever, redness, swelling, pain, leakage or damage requires prompt advice. Your hospital supplies the exact care instructions.

Make the plan practical

Support around the medicines

Support for this phase

Obtain a clear diarrhoea action plan and separate instructions for each tablet. Anti-sickness medicines and interactions require individual review.

Bloods and cardiac checks

FBC, renal, liver; DPD testing before capecitabine → Extra tests if symptoms / toxicity → Pre-cycle bloods and symptom review. Symptoms need assessment between visits.

Visits and practical support

Ask about travel, work, fertility, menopause, exercise and help at home. The team arranges and reviews clinical checks; the patient should not have to calculate the monitoring timetable.

Follow-up and bone health

If an aromatase inhibitor or ovarian suppression is prescribed, assess fracture risk and baseline DEXA as indicated. Repeat timing depends on the result, risk and bone treatment; it is not an automatic scan for everyone.
