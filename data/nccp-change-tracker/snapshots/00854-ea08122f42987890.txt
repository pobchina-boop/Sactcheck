NCCP National SACT Regimen

 Decitabine and Cedazuridine (Inaqovi®) Therapy
 INDICATIONS FOR USE:
 Regimen HSE approved
 INDICATION ICD10 Code reimbursement
 status*
 As monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with newly diagnosed C92 00948a N/A
 acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) who are ineligible for standard induction
 chemotherapy
 * This applies to post 2012 indications

 TREATMENT:
 The starting dose of the drugs detailed below may be adjusted downward by the prescribing clinician, using their
 independent medical judgement, to consider each patient’s individual clinical circumstances.

 Decitabine/cedazuridine (Inaqovi®) is to be taken orally once daily on Days 1-5 of each cycle.

 Cycle frequency and duration:
 Each cycle is 28 days.
 Treatment is to be continued for a minimum of 4 cycles or until disease progression or unacceptable
 toxicity. A complete or partial response may take longer than 4 cycles.

 Day Drug Dose Route Cycle
 1-5 Decitabine/cedazuridine 35mg/100mg once daily PO All cycles
 Tablets must be swallowed whole with water at approximately the same time each day. Food is not to be consumed 2 hours
 before and 2 hours after taking treatment in order to avoid a risk for lack of efficacy. Tablets must not be chewed, crushed,
 broken or divided.
 Substitution with an IV decitabine product within a cycle is not recommended.
 Decitabine/cedazuridine is commonly available as 35mg/100mg film-coated tablets.

 Missed or vomited dose:
 • If the patient misses a dose within 12 hours of the time it is usually taken, the patient must take
 the missed dose as soon as possible and resume the normal daily dosing schedule
 • If the patient misses a dose by 12 or more hours, the patient must wait and take the missed
 dose the following day at the usual time and then extend the dosing period by one day for
 every missed dose to complete 5 daily doses for each cycle
 • If the patient vomits following dosing, no additional dose is to be taken that day. The next dose
 must be taken at the usual time and resume the normal daily dosing, with no extension of the
 dosing period

 ELIGIBILITY:
 • Indication as above
 • Adequate organ function

 CAUTIONS:
 • History of severe congestive heart failure or clinically unstable cardiac disease
 • History of interstitial lung disease (ILD)
 • Risk of differentiation syndrome
 • Patients with severe renal impairment
 • Patients with hepatic impairment or those who develop signs and symptoms of hepatic
 impairment

 EXCLUSIONS:
 • Hypersensitivity to decitabine, cedazuridine or any of the excipients
 • Pregnancy
 • Breastfeeding

 PRESCRIPTIVE AUTHORITY:
 The treatment plan must be initiated by a Consultant Haematologist working in the area of haematological
 malignancies.

 TESTS:
 Baseline tests:
 • Full blood count (FBC), renal and liver profile
 • Pregnancy test for women of childbearing potential
 • Hepatitis B virus* (HBV) [HBV sAg, HBV sAb, HBV cAb], hepatitis C virus (HCV), human
 immunodeficiency virus (HIV), cytomegalovirus (CMV) serology [IgG] and additional screening
 as clinically indicated
 *Refer to Regimen Specific Complications for information on Hepatitis B reactivation
 • Bone marrow examination and cytogenetics as baseline
 • ECHO, ECG as clinically indicated

 Regular tests:
 • FBC, renal and liver profile prior to each cycle and as clinically indicated
 • Bone marrow examination as clinically indicated

 Disease monitoring:
 Disease monitoring should be in line with the patient’s treatment plan and any other test/s as directed by
 the supervising Consultant.

 DOSE MODIFICATIONS:
 • Any dose modification should be discussed with a Consultant
 Haematological:
 • Patients are to be treated with a minimum of 4 cycles of treatment with active disease, unless
 there is disease progression or unacceptable toxicity
 • Persistent severe neutropenia and febrile neutropenia have to be managed with supportive
 treatment (as per local policy)
 • Refer to Table 1 below for recommendations of treatment modification in haematological
 toxicity in the absence of active disease
 • Refer to Table 2 below for recommended dose reductions for myelosuppression

 Management of adverse events:
 Subsequent treatment cycles must be delayed for the following non-haematologic adverse events and
 resumed at the same or reduced dose upon resolution:
 • Serum creatinine ≥ 180mmol/L
 • Serum bilirubin ≥ 2 times the upper limit of normal (ULN)
 • Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) ≥ 2 times ULN
 • Active or uncontrolled infection

 Dose adjustments for all other Grade ≥ 3 adverse reactions should follow institutional guidelines.

 SUPPORTIVE CARE:
 EMETOGENIC POTENTIAL:
 • As outlined in NCCP Classification Document for Systemic Anti-Cancer Therapy (SACT) Induced Nausea and Vomiting Available
 on the NCCP website

 Decitabine/cedazuridine: Minimal-low (Refer to local policy).

 Nausea and vomiting may occur during treatment. Administration of standard antiemetic therapy prior to
 each dose should be considered to minimise nausea and vomiting.

 For information:
 Within NCIS regimens, antiemetics have been standardised by the Medical Oncologists and Haemato-oncologists and
 information is available in the following documents:
 • NCCP Supportive Care Antiemetic Medicines for Inclusion in NCIS (Medical Oncology) - Available on the NCCP website
 • NCCP Supportive Care Antiemetic Medicines for Inclusion in NCIS (Haemato-oncology) - Available on the NCCP website

 PREMEDICATIONS:
 None usually required.

 OTHER SUPPORTIVE CARE:
 • G-CSF support may be required (refer to local policy)
 • PJP prophylaxis may be required (refer to local policy)
 • Antiviral prophylaxis may be required (refer to local policy)
 • Antifungal prophylaxis may be required (refer to local policy)
 • Transfusions for anaemia or thrombocytopenia (refer to local policy)
 • Women of child-bearing potential must use effective contraceptive measures and avoid
 becoming pregnant while being treated with decitabine/cedazuridine and for 6 months
 following completion of treatment. Men should use effective contraceptive measures and be
 advised to not father a child while receiving decitabine/cedazuridine, and for 3 months
 following completion of treatment
 • Decitabine/cedazuridine has moderate influence on the ability to drive and use machines.
 Patients should be advised that they may experience undesirable effects, such as anaemia
 during treatment. Therefore, caution should be observed when driving a car or operating
 machinery

 • Myelosuppression: Fatal and serious myelosuppression can occur with treatment. Complete
 blood cell counts must be obtained prior to initiations of treatment, prior to each cycle, and as
 clinically indicated to monitor response and toxicity. Growth factors and anti-infective
 therapies must be administered for treatment or prophylaxis as appropriate. The next cycle
 must be delayed and resumed at the same or reduced dose as recommended (see Tables 1 and
 2 above). Patients must be monitored for signs and symptoms of infection and treated
 promptly
 • Respiratory, thoracic and mediastinal disorders: Cases of ILD (including pulmonary infiltrates,
 organizing pneumonia and pulmonary fibrosis) without signs of infectious aetiology have been
 reported in patients receiving IV decitabine. Patients with acute onset or unexplained
 worsening of pulmonary symptoms must be carefully assessed to exclude ILD. If ILD is
 confirmed, appropriate treatment must be initiated
 • Cardiac disease: Patients with a history of severe congestive heart failure or clinically unstable
 cardiac disease were excluded from clinical studies and therefore, the safety and efficacy of
 decitabine/cedazuridine in these patients has not been established. Cases of cardiomyopathy
 with cardiac decompensation, in some cases reversible after treatment discontinuation, dose
 reduction or corrective treatment, have been reported in the postmarketing setting with IV
 decitabine. Patients, especially those with a history of cardiac disease, must be monitored for
 signs and symptoms of heart failure
 • Differentiation syndrome (retinoic acid syndrome): Cases of differentiation syndrome have
 been reported during the post-marketing period with IV decitabine. Differentiation syndrome
 may be fatal. Treatment with high-dose IV corticosteroids and haemodynamic monitoring must
 be considered at first onset of symptoms or signs suggestive of differentiation syndrome.
 Treatment must be temporarily discontinued until symptoms resolve, and if resumed, caution
 is advised
