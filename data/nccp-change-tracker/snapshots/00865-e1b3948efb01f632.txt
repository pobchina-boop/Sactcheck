 Teclistamab Monotherapy
 INDICATIONS FOR USE:
 Regimen HSE approved
 INDICATION ICD10 Code reimbursement
 status*
 As monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed and C90 00865a ODMS
 refractory multiple myeloma, who have received at least three prior 01/03/2025
 therapies, including an immunomodulatory agent, a proteasome inhibitor
 and an anti-CD38 antibody and have demonstrated disease progression on
 the last therapy.
 * This applies to post 2012 indications

 TREATMENT:
 The starting dose of the drugs detailed below may be adjusted downward by the prescribing clinician, using their
 independent medical judgement, to consider each patients individual clinical circumstances.

 Teclistamab is administered at a dose of 1.5mg/kg by subcutaneous (SC) injection ONCE WEEKLY every 28
 days, preceded by step-up doses of 0.06 mg/kg and 0.3 mg/kg in cycle 1, to reduce the risk of cytokine
 release syndrome (CRS).

 For patients who have a complete response or better for a minimum of 6 months, consideration can be
 given to reducing the dosing frequency to 1.5mg/kg SC every TWO weeks (See NCCP regimen 00927
 Teclistamab Monotherapy – Two Weekly Maintenance). Consideration may also be given to MONTHLY
 dosing at the discretion of the prescribing Consultant, noting that this would be an unlicensed dosing
 posology.

 The recommended dosing schedule (including dose titration) is shown in tables 1 and 2 below. Treatment
 should be continued until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

 Due to the risk of cytokine release syndrome (CRS) treatment should be initiated in the inpatient setting.
 All patients must be monitored for signs and symptoms of CRS daily for 48 hours after administration of all
 doses within the step-up dosing schedule, and those who have previously experienced a grade 2 or higher
 CRS.

 In the event of CRS and/or Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS), tocilizumabi
 and emergency equipment must be available for each patient. The treatment centre must have access to
 additional doses of tocilizumabi within 8 hours.

 Facilities to treat anaphylaxis MUST be present when systemic anti-cancer therapy (SACT) is administered.

 PREMEDICATIONS:
 • Pre-medication with corticosteroids, antihistamines and antipyretics must be administered 1 to 3
 hours before each dose of the teclistamab step-up dosing schedule (see Table 1) to reduce the risk
 of cytokine release syndrome, as suggested in Table 9
 • Administration of premedication may also be required prior to administration of subsequent doses
 of teclistamab for the following patients:
 o Patients who repeat doses within the teclistamab step-up dosing schedule due to dose
 delays (Table 3), or
 o Patients who experienced CRS following the previous dose (Table 6)

 Table 9: Suggested premedications prior to teclistamab infusion
 Drug Dose Route
 dexAMETHasone 16mg PO, 60 minutes prior to infusion
 Chlorphenamine 10mg IV bolus, 60 minutes prior to infusion
 Paracetamol 1g PO, 60 minutes prior to infusion

 OTHER SUPPORTIVE CARE:
 • Immunoglobulin replacement is recommended for all patients receiving teclistamab as they will
 quickly become hypogammaglobulinaemic when on treatment with an anti-BCMA bispecific
 antibody
 • Antiviral prophylaxis is recommended for prevention of herpes zoster reactivation (refer to local
 policy)
 • Tumour lysis prophylaxis (Refer to local policy)
 • Anti-bacterial prophylaxis (Refer to local policy)
 • Anti-fungal prophylaxis (Refer to local policy)
 • Consider PJP prophylaxis (Refer to local policy)

 ADVERSE EFFECTS:
 • Please refer to the relevant Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for details.
 • Teclistamab is subject to additional monitoring. Healthcare professionals are asked to report
 any suspected adverse reactions.

 REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS:
 • CRS: including life-threatening or fatal reactions, may occur in patients receiving teclistamab.

 Treatment should be initiated with teclistamab according to the step-up dosing schedule (Table 1)
 to reduce risk of CRS, and pre-treatment with corticosteroids, antihistamine and antipyretics should
 be administered prior to each dose of the step-up dosing schedule to reduce risk of CRS. Patients
 who experience CRS following their previous dose should be administered pre-treatment
 corticosteroids, antihistamine and antipyretics prior to the next dose of teclistamab. Patients
 should be counselled to seek medical attention should signs or symptoms of CRS occur. At the first
 sign of CRS, patients should be immediately evaluated for hospitalisation. Treatment with
 supportive care, tocilizumabi and/or corticosteroids should be instituted, as outlined in Table 6 and
 7 above. Treatment with teclistamab should be withheld until CRS resolves.
 • ICANS: Serious or life-threatening neurologic toxicities, including ICANS may occur following
 treatment with teclistamab, patients should be monitored for signs or symptoms of neurologic
 toxicities during treatment and treated promptly. Patients should be counselled to seek medical
 attention should signs or symptoms of neurologic toxicity occur. At the first sign of neurologic
 toxicity, including ICANS, patients should be immediately evaluated and treated based on severity
 as outlined in Table 8 above. For ICANS and other neurologic toxicities, treatment with teclistamab
 should be withheld as indicated in Table 6 above. Due to the potential for ICANS, patients should
 be advised not to drive or operate heavy machinery during the teclistamab step-up dosing schedule
 and for 48 hours after completing the teclistamab step-up dosing schedule (Table 1) and in the
 event of new onset of any neurological symptoms.
 • Hepatitis B virus reactivation: Hepatitis B virus reactivation can occur in patients treated with
 medicinal products directed against B cells, and in some cases, may result in fulminant hepatitis,
 hepatic failure, and death. Patients with evidence of positive HBV serology should be monitored
 for clinical and laboratory signs of HBV reactivation while receiving teclistamab, and for at least six
 months following the end of teclistamab treatment. In patients who develop reactivation of HBV
 while on teclistamab, treatment with teclistamab should be withheld as indicated in Table 6 and
 manage per local institutional guidelines.
