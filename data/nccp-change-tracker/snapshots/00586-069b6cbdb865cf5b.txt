NCCP National SACT Regimen

 Capivasertib and Fulvestrant Therapy
 INDICATIONS FOR USE:
 Regimen HSE approved
 INDICATION ICD10 Code reimbursement
 status*
 In combination with fulvestrant for the treatment of adult patients with C50 00947a N/A
 oestrogen receptor (ER) positive, HER2 negative locally advanced or
 metastatic breast cancer with PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN-alteration following
 recurrence or progression on or after an endocrine-based regimen.
 In pre- or peri-menopausal females, therapy should be combined with a
 luteinising hormone releasing hormone (LHRH) agonist.
 * This applies to post 2012 indications

 TREATMENT:
 The starting dose of the drugs detailed below may be adjusted downward by the prescribing clinician, using their
 independent medical judgement, to consider each patient’s individual clinical circumstances.

 Capivasertib is administered by mouth twice daily on Days 1-4, 8-11, 15-18 and 22-25 of each cycle (i.e. 4
 days on treatment followed by 3 days off treatment each week).

 Fulvestrant is administered intramuscularly (IM) on Days 1 and 15 of cycle 1, and on Day 1 of each cycle
 from cycle 2 onwards.

 • In pre- or peri-menopausal women, capivasertib in combination with fulvestrant should be
 combined with a LHRH agonist.
 • For men, administration of a LHRH agonist according to current clinical practice standards
 should be considered.

 Cycle frequency and duration:
 Each cycle is 28 days.
 Treatment should continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

 ELIGIBILITY:
 • Indication as above
 • ECOG 0-2
 • Adequate organ function

 CAUTIONS:
 Capivasertib:
 • Pre-existing diabetes mellitus or pre-diabetes
 o All patients should be informed about capivasertib’s potential to cause hyperglycaemia and should
 immediately contact their clinician if hyperglycaemia symptoms occur (See Regimen Specific
 Complications)
 • Patients with risk factors for hyperglycaemia or diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
 • History of rash, dermatitis or skin-drug reactions
 • History of chronic diarrhoea
 • Moderate hepatic impairment
 • Patients receiving bisphosphonates or RANK-ligand inhibitors
 • Co-administration of strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors

 Fulvestrant:
 • Caution should be taken while administering fulvestrant at the dorsogluteal injection site
 o Site related events including sciatica, neuralgia, neuropathic pain, and peripheral
 neuropathy have been reported
 • Mild-moderate hepatic impairment

 EXCLUSIONS:
 • Hypersensitivity to capivasertib, fulvestrant or any of the excipients
 • Severe hepatic impairment
 • Severe renal impairment
 • Pregnancy
 • Breastfeeding

 PRESCRIPTIVE AUTHORITY:
 The treatment plan must be initiated by a Consultant Medical Oncologist.

 Co-administration with strong and moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors
 • Co-administration of capivasertib with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors should be avoided. If co-
 administration cannot be avoided, the dose of capivasertib should be reduced to 320 mg twice
 daily (equivalent to a total daily dose of 640 mg).
 • Capivasertib dose should be reduced to 320 mg twice daily (equivalent to a total daily dose of
 640 mg) when co-administered with moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors.
 • After discontinuation of a strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor, capivasertib dosage (after 3 to
 5 half-lives of the inhibitor) that was taken prior to initiating the strong or moderate CYP3A4
 inhibitor should be resumed.

 REGIMEN SPECIFIC COMPLICATIONS:
 • Please refer to the relevant Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for details.

 Capivasertib:
 • Hyperglycaemia: Severe hyperglycaemia, associated with DKA and with fatal outcomes
 occurred in patients treated with capivasertib. DKA can occur at any time during capivasertib
 treatment. Patients with a history of diabetes mellitus may require intensified anti-diabetic
 treatment and should be closely monitored. Consultation with a diabetologist or a healthcare
 professional experienced in the treatment of hyperglycaemia is recommended for patients
 with diabetes. Before initiating treatment with capivasertib, patients should be informed about
 capivasertib’s potential to cause hyperglycaemia and requested to immediately contact their
 healthcare professional if hyperglycaemia symptoms (e.g. excessive thirst, urinating more
 often than usual or greater amount of urine than usual, or increased appetite with weight loss)
 occur. In a setting of additional co-morbidities and treatments (e.g. dehydration,
 malnourishment, concurrent chemotherapy/steroids, sepsis), the risk of hyperglycaemia
 progressing to DKA may be higher. DKA should be considered as one of the differential
 diagnoses in the event of additional non-specific symptoms such as nausea, vomiting,
 abdominal pain, difficulty breathing, fruity odour on breath, confusion, unusual fatigue, or
 sleepiness. In patients where DKA is suspected, capivasertib treatment should be interrupted
 immediately. If DKA is confirmed, then capivasertib should be permanently discontinued. In
 addition to the recommended management of hyperglycaemia described in Table 4,
 counselling on lifestyle changes is recommended for patients with baseline risk factors and
 those that develop hyperglycaemia during treatment with capivasertib.
 • Diarrhoea: Diarrhoea has been reported in the majority of patients treated with capivasertib.
 Clinical consequences of diarrhoea may include dehydration, hypokalaemia and acute kidney
 injury, which have all, together with cardiac arrhythmias (with hypokalaemia as risk factor)
 been reported during treatment with capivasertib. Based on the severity of diarrhoea,
 capivasertib dosing may be interrupted, reduced, or permanently discontinued (Refer to Table
 5). Advise patients to start anti-diarrhoeal treatment at the first sign of diarrhoea, increase oral
 fluids if diarrhoea symptoms occur while taking capivasertib. Maintenance of normovolemia
 and electrolyte balance is required in patients with diarrhoea to avoid complications related to
 hypovolemia and low electrolyte levels.
